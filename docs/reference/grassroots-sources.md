# Grassroots Sources & the Badge

*The record kept by sheriffs, planners, club secretaries and local papers.*

Grassroots is a **way we know**, not a topic. Any page in any department can rest on local or homegrown sources, and when it does, its article record carries the teal <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> badge. This page defines the term, lists every page that wears the badge (built automatically), and catalogs the outlets and archives we have used. New outlets are added each research round. Industry media still appear in the wiki, but they do not earn the badge on their own.

Most of what is written about hot rodding comes from national magazines and trade press. The other record is police chiefs writing for other police, planners surveying small-town clubs, a club's own history page, a local paper's obituary, a historical-society talk. These sources are uneven and often promotional, so each page says what it is and flags the doubts.

## What counts as grassroots
- **Counts:** local newspapers and local obituaries; oral histories and interviews recorded by local historians; club newsletters, club histories and club-run track pages; municipal, county and police records; historical-society pages and talks; historical markers; family accounts and captions; local-history sites and blogs; enthusiast forum recollections (flagged as anonymous or unverified).
- **Does not count by itself:** national magazines, trade press, NHRA or hall-of-fame features, auction copy, encyclopedias. A metro daily such as the *Los Angeles Times* counts when it covers its own region, as it does for Modesto and Inyokern.
- **Why the caution:** local sources are often the only record of small events, but they are usually unsigned, promotional, or retold from memory. Pages say so, and flag single-sourced and conflicting claims.
