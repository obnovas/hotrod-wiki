# Contributing: Source Standards

This wiki treats hot rod history as detective work. Every claim has to show where it came from.

## Rules
1. **Cite every factual claim** with a footnote that has a URL (or a full print citation). Add each new source to the [Bibliography](../bibliography.md) with a type tag.
2. **Never invent** facts, quotes, dates or identifications. A gap goes into the [Research Trail](../research-trail.md), not the article.
3. **Quote exactly.** Name the speaker and the channel, for example "Isky in a 1994 interview, via Dave Wallace."
4. **Prefer lesser-known first-hand material:** oral histories, club newsletters, obituaries, local papers, family captions and forum first-person accounts. Mainstream summaries are only a last resort.
5. **Images:** use public domain or openly licensed images only, with creator, date, source and license in every caption and on [Image Credits](image-credits.md). Copyrighted photos are linked out, never copied. If an identification is uncertain, say so in the caption.

## Inline markers
| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **⚠ Conflict** | Sources disagree. Every version is shown with its source, and a working conclusion is stated only if the evidence supports one. |
| ◐ | Single-sourced, or the source is weak (auction copy, promotional HOF text, an unsourced post). Treat it as provisional. |
| ~~struck~~ / "resolved" | An earlier conflict, resolved in the noted round. The reasoning is kept. |

## Source types (bibliography tags)
| Tag | Type | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| `[primary]` | Patents, period programs, timing slips, letters, census and directory records | Highest for what it directly records |
| `[oral]` | Interviews, oral histories, first-person forum or list posts, family recollections | Vivid, but memory drifts. Dates especially need corroboration |
| `[obit]` | Obituaries | Good for vital dates when several agree |
| `[museum]` / HOF | Hall of fame and museum bios | Useful, but often promotional and repeated |
| `[mag]` | Magazines and websites, including archive retrospectives | Secondary |
| `[ref]` | Reference works such as Coachbuilt, Kustomrama and encyclopedias | Secondary or tertiary |
| `[corp]` | Company histories | Self-interested |

## Confidence levels (article record box)
- **High:** the core facts are confirmed by several independent sources or by primary documents, and no major conflicts are open.
- **Medium:** the core story rests on one or two good sources (often oral history plus one secondary), and some conflicts are flagged.
- **Low:** it rests mainly on single, weak or late sources. It is a scaffold for further research.

"Last verified" is the date of the most recent research round in which the article's sources were re-read or extended.

## Scholarly anchor
Robert C. Post's *High Performance: The Culture and Technology of Drag Racing, 1950–1990* (Johns Hopkins University Press, 1994; rev. 1996) is the academic benchmark (WorldCat: https://search.worldcat.org/title/28212724). Post was a Smithsonian curator (JHU Press author page: https://www.press.jhu.edu/books/authors/robert-c-post). **We have not yet read it page by page.** Until then, don't cite it for specific claims. It is the next text to check our drag-racing chronology against.
