# "Let's Call Ourselves The Undertakers": How Lowrider Clubs Spread Across Texas (El Paso to Corpus Christi, 1970s– )

*A body shop on the border, matching black-and-gray Chevys, a hearse at the back of the line, a club named by a police escort, and a letter from Del Rio*

!!! quote "In their own words"

## The body shop on the border

In California, lowriding had a boulevard and, by the late 1970s, a magazine. In Texas it moved the slow way, town by town, through a body shop, a park, a family that moved, and letters mailed to that magazine. One of the first clubs got its name from a police escort that mistook it for a funeral.[^lrm02]

The low cars had been in Texas a long time. Américo Paredes recalled that after the war in Crystal City, people met at the Dairy Queen to see whose car was low enough to knock over a cigarette pack.[^tsha] *Lowrider*'s history says lowered "shorts," as some Texans called them, had rolled Texas roads for at least 50 years, that hydraulics had already reached the state, and that the record gets clearer in the early 1970s.[^lrm02] The Handbook of Texas says El Paso's Nevarez and Salazar brothers, early craftsmen, showed in national custom shows that added El Paso to the circuit in the early 1970s.[^tsha]

Armando "Mando" Santillan was 12 when his family moved from East Los Angeles to El Paso in 1970. "Lowriders were barely starting," he said. A club called the Exclusives was already known for riding on 14-inch rims lifted from '57 Chevys.[^lrm02] Santillan started hanging out at the neighborhood body shop, George and Son's, run by the Salazar family, and in 1972 he met Richard Salazar there. The group drove Chevys, Fords and Pontiacs; nobody cared about the make.[^lrm02]

## A funeral to Ascarate Park

One day the group stood around Richard Salazar's black 1964 Impala with a charcoal gray top. "It looked so sharp, we painted all of the cars the same black with the gray, plus gangster whitewall tires," Santillan said. "One guy couldn't afford a car, so he had a hearse. We decided to cruise Ascarate Park, and we put him last."[^lrm02] Ascarate, with its horseshoe-shaped road, was the place to cruise in El Paso.[^lrm02]

"We started to cruise, and two police officers thought we were a funeral. They began to escort us, stopping traffic for us," Santillan said. "When we got to the park, they gave us tickets because they stopped the traffic! We went to court and won. After it was over with, Richard said, 'Let's call ourselves The Undertakers.'"<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>[^lrm02] Other El Paso clubs followed, among them New Breed, Image, Lords, Browns and Destini, with jackets and plaques, and women's social clubs too.[^lrm02] Later, Benny Ramos and his two brothers moved to El Paso and asked to start a chapter of the Imperials, which the history calls lowriding's oldest club. "Then we said, well, if you guys want to start another chapter, you had better go by our rules," its president, Jesse Valadez, said. "And everything worked out."[^lrm02][^tsha]

## Town to town

The style went east. In Odessa, almost 200 miles away, Nick Hernandez had been watching lowriders cruise for years, and in 1973 he started a car and bicycle club, Taste of Latin. His idea, the history says, was to use each member's strength, metalwork or bondo, upholstery or paint, so that every car came out clean.[^lrm02] In 1978 two younger members, the brothers Abel and David Leal, moved with their family to Corpus Christi, bringing their raked, chromed bikes. They met local cruisers like Joe Ramos, who had a '65 Chevy with a tilt front end, and started Taste of Latin–Corpus Christi. "It was slow," David Leal remembered. "We used to get a lot of stares and bad looks, but I started making friends who were interested in lowriding." Within six months other clubs were joining them on Port Avenue and Ocean Drive.[^lrm02]

*Lowrider* was still a California magazine in 1978, but copies reached the border town of Del Rio. That year three women there, Sylvia Ramirez and the sisters Mary Jane and Veronica Mojica, wrote in: "We organized 21 cars to enter our annual Fiesta de Amistad parade. We called our club Border Riders de Del Rio."[^lrm02] The magazine's staff came to Texas to see, and with San Antonio lowrider George Velasquez set up a permanent Texas bureau.[^lrm02]

In Laredo, Jesus "Chuy" Martinez founded the Brown Impressions in 1979. *Lowrider* later called it the first lowrider club in Texas.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^bi09][^lrm02] In Houston, Westheimer Road and Memorial Park filled with clubs like the Finest Few, whose Nick Ochoa drove a '64 Impala called "Red Devil," painted at Garcia's Body Shop. Houston's hydraulics came by mail from Art Lee in Arizona, with instructions for the local mechanics.[^lrm02] By the late 1970s, the history says, hundreds of clubs were cruising from Corpus Christi to Dallas and El Paso.[^lrm02] In San Antonio, Victor Stewart and two friends co-founded 1st Impressions.[^ksat23]

The clubs lasted. When the Brown Impressions held their 30th-anniversary show at the Laredo Civic Center in 2009, more than 100 lowriders and more than 1,000 spectators came, and the club had chapters across Texas and as far off as Rochester, Minnesota, and Okeechobee, Florida. Laredo's mayor proclaimed a week in its honor.[^bi09] In Austin, *Austin American-Statesman* reporter Emiliano Tahui Gómez said the style arrived in the 1970s with people from California and other parts of Texas and the Southwest, and that in the early 1980s the city's police hired someone from the East Side as a go-between with the lowriders, to talk over the infractions they were being ticketed for.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>[^ts24] In 2023 San Antonio parked three lowriders and some bikes in the airport's Terminal B baggage claim, curated by Clint Westwood, who calls himself a lowrider liaison.[^ax23] Victor's son, Stacy, still hits the hydraulics on his own car downtown.[^ksat23] "These cars are meant to be seen. They're not meant to be kept in the garage," said Joe De La Rosa of Lone Star Lowriders.[^ksat23] In Texas the cars got seen one town at a time, and in El Paso the first time a police escort saw them, it thought it was leading a funeral.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Which was the first lowrider club in Texas?** *Lowrider*'s 2009 story says the Brown Impressions, founded by Jesus "Chuy" Martinez in 1979, "was the first Lowrider Club in Texas."[^bi09] The magazine's own 2002 history puts El Paso's first lowrider clubs in the early 1970s, with the Exclusives already known in 1970, the Undertakers named in 1972 or soon after, and Odessa's Taste of Latin in 1973.[^lrm02] The Handbook of Texas quotes Richard Salazar saying Los Angeles lowriders founded an early El Paso club, the Imperials, and San Antonio's 1st Impressions is called one of that city's iconic clubs.[^tsha][^ksat23] "First" may mean first to call itself a lowrider club; we have not found the clubs' own founding papers. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **The funeral escort.** The hearse, the escort, the tickets and the court win are Mando Santillan's story in *Lowrider*'s history, told about 30 years later.[^lrm02] He gives no year for the cruise beyond meeting Salazar in 1972. An El Paso municipal court record or the *El Paso Times* would date it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Austin's go-between.** Gómez said "I believe in the early '80s" the police hired a go-between; he named no one and no year.[^ts24] The Austin Police Department's records, or Gómez's own *Statesman* stories, would name the person. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
