# "This Is a Rat Rod Show": The Rats Nest Run-In and the Rat Rod Rendezvous (Saugerties, New York, and Mahtowa, Minnesota, 2008 or 2009–2023)

*A builder told "not now, not ever," a club behind a meat market, a beer keg for a gas tank, and 4,000 miles to Las Vegas*

## "Not now, not ever"

Rat rods got shows of their own because some car shows did not want them. Two of those shows began in small towns at about the same time, one behind a body shop in the Hudson Valley and one beside a country store in northern Minnesota. Both were built for the car that did not fit, and both said so.

Peter Duvaloois owned the Rats Nest Shop on Kings Highway in Saugerties, New York. "Shiny cars are not my forte," he told Donna Kessler of the *Times Herald-Record*. "I see it as, drive for an hour, clean them for three." Some years earlier he had built himself a showpiece and never drove it anywhere, for fear of scratching or wrecking it. "Rats are really back-to-basics," he said. "You scrounge around or trade for parts, and what you can't find, you make yourself."[^thr14] He built a 1946 Chevy truck for himself and a rat rod for his wife, Cathy, and at shows he told people to climb in and rev the engine.[^thr14]

Not every show wanted them. "I was told at a car show, 'We don't need you, not now, not ever,'" he said. After that he decided to put on a show where pre-1965 rat rods and kustoms were welcome.[^thr14] The first Rats Nest Run-In was held on the property behind his shop, and it outgrew the space. He moved it to the Blackthorne Resort in East Durham and made it three days.[^thr14] By 2014, its fifth year, it had more than 15 rockabilly bands, burlesque and sideshow acts, a tattoo artist, and trophies for "Best Rodz, Kustom and Sickles." "I'm looking for the homemade and the creative to come to this show," he said.[^thr14] In 2016, its seventh year, the trophies were custom-painted rat traps. Kessler handed out the paper's first trophy to a 1941 tow truck that Kevin and Theresa Strumpf of Fort Salonga had spent three years building, with a diesel under the hood. The show also gave two students in a regional welding and automotive program $750 gift certificates.[^thr16]

## A beer keg and a Boy Scout canteen

In Mahtowa, a northern Minnesota town on Historic Highway 61 just off I-35, the rat rodders were the Peckerhead Rat Rod Club.[^nnn] Roger Rentola, a heavy equipment mechanic in Cloquet for years, built his truck out of several trucks, vans, cars and a school bus, with a beer keg, a cow horn, a couple of street signs and an antique Boy Scout canteen, which served as the radiator's overflow tank.[^pj10] "I've been repairing cars, trucks and tractors for years, along with toasters and anything else my boss wanted to throw at me," he said.[^pj10] The gas tank was a Pabst keg, and the shifter handle matched it. He checked the fuel with an old broom handle. The floor was road signs, black arrows on gold, pointing straight ahead. His son, Jeremy, made the "flying eyeball" on the exhaust pipe.[^pj10] "Every gas station is a car show," Rentola said. "You can figure on a half hour stop at least."[^pj10]

Bryan Dagel restored vintage vehicles for a living. "Roger had the only rat rod there," he remembered. "There was a crowd standing around, just looking and talking. I thought, 'Hey, I can do this.'" He started from a 1949 F-100 frame and a 1965 Impala engine, and made a church pew into a holder for the two kegs in the bed that served as his gas tanks. "There's not so much ego or keeping up with the Joneses," he said.[^pj10] As the paper put it, a rat rod had to be street legal, with turn signals, brake lights, headlights and wipers, and its owner should drive it to the show instead of trailering it.[^pj10]

On Sunday, September 5, 2010, they held the second annual Rat Rod Rendezvous, from 10 to 2, next to TJ's Country Store in downtown Mahtowa.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^pj10] They had advertised in the Twin Cities and taken flyers to shows in St. Cloud, and had no idea how many would come.[^pj10] By 2012 the Rendezvous registered more than 200 rat rods.[^pj13]

## 4,000 miles of rust

On Labor Day 2012, ten rat rods left downtown Mahtowa for Las Vegas, with Rentola driving the chase car as guide and the publisher of *Rat Rod Magazine*, Steve Thaemert, riding along with a camera. They went through South Dakota, Nebraska, Colorado and Utah, crossed into California "just to say they'd done it," showed at Lake Las Vegas in Henderson, Nevada, and came home through New Mexico. Three were from Canada, one from Nebraska, two from Wisconsin, several from around Mahtowa, plus "two deaf guys" from southern Minnesota. They picked up four more on the way. The only breakdown was one alternator.[^pj13][^dnt13] The film of the trip, *4,000 Miles of Rust*, premiered at the Lake Theater in Moose Lake in March 2013, for $5.[^pj13][^dnt13] The magazine, then based in Shakopee, had grown close to the club. "The magazine has small-town roots to begin with," Thaemert said. "It's kind of like family now."[^dnt13]

In 2023 the Rendezvous held its 15th annual show, still at TJ's, now the TJ's Country Corner Store and Meat Market, where Renee and Glenn Gaffney had T-shirts for the rat rods and a 1950 Buick for a fountain out front. Saturday was for the shiny cars, and Sunday for the rusty ones. "If your hand can't slide off the paint job you know it sticks to it, then you got good patina," said Rentola, a co-founder.[^nnn] The man with a Jack-and-Coke truck had wanted to know if he would be shunned. He was not, because this was a rat rod show.
