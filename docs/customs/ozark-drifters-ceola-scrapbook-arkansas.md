# Washing the Signs: The Ozark Drifters, Dick Ceola's Truck and the Auto-Rama, Northwest Arkansas (1960–1964)

*A club that, according to the Ceola family scrapbook, sentenced its own speeder, and an indoor show with a wet floor*

## Washing the signs

The Ozark Drifters of Fayetteville were a small Arkansas club, and their one claim here is that they kept their own paperwork. Dick Ceola of Springdale saved a scrapbook, an album with his own notes, show flyers, newspaper clippings and the club's 1963 NHRA charter renewal. Kustomrama credits them to Nicholas Coe and the Ceola family.[^kk][^truck] What they show is a club working on its reputation.

One photograph shows a member, Jerry Gideon, washing traffic signs on North College Avenue. Gideon had been fined for speeding. The club decided the court's fine "wasn't sufficient punishment," Kustomrama says, so he was put to washing street signs. The picture ran in the local paper.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> A second photo's caption says the washing was "helping build a good reputation for the club." A third photo's caption reads, "Safety comes first - You have been assisted by a member of the Ozark Drifters Kar Klub."[^kk]

In April 1963, members made the paper again when they carried a 1934 Ford two miles, from North Leverett Street to East Mountain Street. The page does not say whose car it was or why.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Open question: see Open questions">?</a></sup>[^kk]

## A truck, and a show that kept growing

Ceola started building a 1930 Ford Model A truck in 1960, and it was done in 1962. The body was channeled over the frame, the fenders and running boards came off, and it sat lower front and back behind a 1932 Ford grille. The back got a rolled pan, an inset club plaque and 1939 Ford taillights.[^truck] At the 1962 Fourth of July parade in Springdale it was towed behind Ceola's 1959 Ford convertible. A photo caption calls it "Dickey Celoa and his 3500.00 dollar street rod," and Kustomrama's text says $3,000 "supposedly" went into it.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^truck]

The club put on its first Auto-Rama, an indoor show, at the National Guard Armory in Springdale in December 1962. Ceola's truck, fire-engine red with a white firewall and a white rolled and pleated interior, took first in the full entry custom class and was judged the entry with the best exterior appearance. It was also picked best of show in an audience poll.[^truck] The local paper's clippings are in the scrapbook, and one spells his name "Dick Cola."[^truck] A caption on the People's Choice photo reads: "The big one didn't get away, all five feet of it go to Dicky for peoples choice of entire show."[^truck] Harry and Larry Arrington of Camden, Ark., drove their 1934 Ford five-window coupe, "Thor," to the same show and won Best Street Rod, Best Display and Greatest Distance driven.[^arr]

The second Auto-Rama, in 1963, moved to the Springdale Roller Rink. As Kustomrama summarizes the *Northwest Arkansas Times*, the show drew cars from Arkansas, Missouri, Oklahoma and Texas and thousands of onlookers over Saturday and Sunday. The club thought the second show was better than the first, the same page says, partly because of the new rink. It rained both days. Kustomrama, not the newspaper, puts the trouble this way: the hardwood floor didn't like the water the cars carried in, and neither did the owner of the rink. Twenty-three owners and groups got awards. A 1957 Ford four-door from Sherman, Texas, won Radical Custom, and an Amarillo, Texas, entry took farthest distance, best paint, best engine and best display. The Ozark Drifters won best club display, and Ceola's truck won early pickup.[^rama]

## A club under another name

Sometime in 1963–64 the Ozark Drifters became the Road Angels of Springdale, and about then Ceola replaced the truck's flathead with a small-block Chevy with six carburetors and an early alternator.[^truck] The last photograph the family has of the truck is from about 1968, with white headers along the cab. It was sold sometime in the 1970s. The family has been trying to trace it since.[^truck] Kustomrama calls it Ceola's "pride and joy."[^truck]

A fine the club thought was not enough, a show with a wet floor, a truck that has not turned up: all of it is known because one member kept his clippings, and his family shared them.
