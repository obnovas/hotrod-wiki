# "Scraping the Sky": Slamfest, Scrapin' the Coast and the Gulf's Slammed-Truck Shows (Tampa, Fla., and Biloxi, Miss., 1991–2026)

*A mini-truck club in an outlet-mall parking lot, a Biloxi show named for dragging frames, and why both are still around*

!!! abstract "Article record"

## About 320 trucks in a parking lot

The slammed-truck shows of the Gulf began as one kind of car and lasted by taking in every kind. A mini-truck club's parking-lot show in Tampa reached its 28th year in 2019. A Biloxi show named for frames scraping the pavement now jokes that it is scraping the sky.

Slamfest's first year was 1991. About 20 members of the Mini Madness car and truck club put it on, and about 320 vehicles came to a Belz Factory Outlet parking lot in Tampa.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> The name, the club told *Street Trucks*, came from "no real reason other than the club's passion for 'slammed' vehicles." Club members still ran it themselves 28 years later. Hardcore Minis, from around Orlando, was the one club known to have supported every show since the first.[^st19]

By 2019 the club counted about 10,000 people over two days. "We have sheriff's deputies at the show each year and we get praised for the people in our scene," the club said. It gave a farthest-traveled award, won that year by Todd Robinson of the Acrophobia club, from Canada. Drop Em Wear was its longest-standing vendor. Between Slamfests the club ran a one-night cruise and show in Clearwater, Doin it in the Dark. Joe Greeves, who had been freelancing since his first article ran in a truck magazine in 1974, lived in St. Augustine and called it a home show.[^st19]

## Scrapin' the Coast

Biloxi's show started in 2002.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^wx19][^wl21] "We wanted to bring something to the Gulf Coast that meant something to our area," founder Eric Kendrick told WXXV in 2024. "So, we started it 22 years ago and it's been going on ever since."[^wx24] *The Gazebo Gazette* puts the start with "a crew of car enthusiasts jamming out to Ludacris in 2002."[^gg25]

The show lives at the Mississippi Coast Coliseum, with cars inside the convention center and a burnout contest and a hydraulics contest outside. Organizer Myles Holcomb explained the hoppers in 2019: "It's where the trucks and cars bounce up and down. They usually tear something up and the crowd really loves that."[^wx19] Mark Wise brought side-by-sides from Gator Powersports in Mandeville, Louisiana. Asked what people said when they saw them, he answered, "Honestly, I can't say that on TV."[^wx19]

In 2021 the traffic jam on Highway 90 was an irritation, WLOX said, and the one in the coliseum parking lot was an inspiration. Narleski Young had shown his 1953 Chevy pickup at both Cruisin' the Coast and Scrapin' and would not pick between them. Terrance Hudson of the X-Rated Car Club of Mobile used his truck to promote autism awareness for a little cousin. Dirk Bercegeay had the show on his bucket list but no show car, and did not plan to get one: "My wife would kill me if I bought a project."[^wl21]

## Scraping the sky

In 2023 Shane Lester, who runs Precision Paint and Body in Long Beach, got his 1984 GMC C10 ready. His wife, Natalie, had found the truck: "I actually found it in a parking lot and me and my husband Shane chased it on foot." Holcomb, who helped start the show 21 years before, said it had grown from just scraping into flashy show cars of every style. "There's a joke going around now that we're scraping the sky because we have a lot of big trucks that are starting to show face," he said. "The guys that are buying the big trucks are towing the scrapers, so there's something for everybody out there." Around 2,000 vehicles were expected.[^wl23]

Some of the new trucks brought the law. In April 2024 Mississippi passed House Bill 349, from Rep. Fred Shanks, banning squatted vehicles with the front fenders four or more inches higher than the rear. At the 2025 show the *Gazette* saw "a constant blur of red and blue police lights"; one woman said she had been stopped seven times in one day.[^gg25]

In June 2026 the 24th show had lifted and squatted trucks, Jeeps, side-by-sides, motorcycles, classics and more mini trucks than in years. Bo Brannan of Chicken Shine in Mobile, a spectator for years before he was a vendor, said, "As they took in the ATVs and side-by-sides and trucks, not only just the lowriders anymore, the participants became more broad." Julio César Olivas came from Midland, Texas, for his 10th. The show also opened Draggin Metal Alley, a hall for the lowriders, under the motto "the rides may be low, but the standards high." From the stage at the awards, the announcer said, "It's hard for some shows to get beyond five or six years, maybe get to ten years."[^gg26] Both shows got past that by keeping the low trucks in the middle and letting everybody else in around them.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Was the first Scrapin' the Coast in 2002 or 2003?** WXXV (2019) and WLOX (2021) say the show has been around since 2002, and Kendrick told WXXV in 2024 that "we started it 22 years ago" and that it has gone on every year.[^wx19][^wl21][^wx24] *The Gazebo Gazette* says the same start, in 2002, in both its 2025 and 2026 stories.[^gg25][^gg26] The 2003 date is not another paper's date. It is that paper's own arithmetic: the same pieces call 2025 "The 23rd annual" and the June 2026 show the 24th, and look ahead to a 25th in 2027, which counts back to 2003 if a show was held every year. WXXV and WLOX do not say 2003. A skipped year, perhaps after Hurricane Katrina in 2005, would square the count with 2002; no source we read says a year was skipped. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **How big was the first Slamfest?** The 1991 date, the 20 members, the 320 vehicles and the Belz Factory Outlet lot are all from the club's own answers in *Street Trucks*.[^st19] A Tampa paper of 1991 would confirm them. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
