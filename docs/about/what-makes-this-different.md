# Below the Local Level

*What this archive is for, and why it isn't trying to be a hall of fame.*

The SEMA Hall of Fame has a rule that tells you a lot about how hot rod history usually gets kept. To be nominated, a person's contributions "must extend beyond the local level and reach throughout the national and/or international level."[^sema] That is a sensible rule for a hall of fame. It is also a pretty good description of everything this archive goes looking for.

In 1955 an Escanaba, Michigan city policeman and his partner ran a hot rod club from the room above their auto shop, and a member who got a moving-violation ticket was suspended for 30 days ([The Cop and the Auto Shop](../drag-racing/escanaba-dragns.md)). In Ames, Iowa, an Iowa State mathematics professor had the official title of **track steward** ([The Professor's Drag Strip](../drag-racing/ames-drag-strip-nightcrawlers.md)). Outside Salem, Ohio, a silo became the timing tower ([Quaker City](../drag-racing/quaker-city-salem.md)). None of those people will ever make a hall of fame ballot, and they shouldn't have to. They are the local level. This is where the hobby actually lived on a Saturday night, and it is the beat we cover.

## What we do differently

**1. We read the hometown paper first.** Most hot rod history comes down to us through the national magazines, the sanctioning bodies and the companies, and those sources are indispensable. We go to them last. First we read the small-town daily, the high school paper, the city commission minutes, the obituary, the club newsletter and the historical marker. Articles built mainly on that kind of record carry the teal <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> badge, and every local outlet we have used is listed on [Grassroots Sources](../reference/grassroots-sources.md).

**2. We show our work, including the parts we haven't solved.** Every factual claim has a footnote you can click. When sources disagree, we print both versions and mark it **⚠ Conflict**. When we only have one source, we mark it **◐**. Each article states its own confidence level. The rules are on [Source Standards](../reference/source-standards.md). We would rather tell you a date is disputed than hand you a clean story that might be wrong.

**3. We connect the parking lot to the parts catalog.** A club strip in Ohio, a cam grinder's shop foreman in Florida and a speed-parts company in Memphis are often the same story told in three towns. The company profiles trace what each firm grew out of, and the [Web of Influence](../companies/web-of-influence.md) draws those lines. Every one of them has to point to a page that supports it, or the site refuses to build.

**4. We go well past Southern California.** The dry lakes and Santa Ana matter, and they are here. But so are Twin Falls, Idaho; Sedalia, Missouri; Stroudsburg, Pennsylvania; Kaukauna, Wisconsin; and Malden, Missouri. Hot rodding was a national pattern of local fights over who got to use the airport runway, and we are mapping it one town at a time in [Drag Racing & Strips](../drag-racing/index.md) and [Places & Scenes](../places/index.md).

**5. The investigation is open.** The [Research Trail](../research-trail.md) is our working log: what each round found, what got corrected, what's still open and which archives turned us away. Readers can send a memory, a correction, a source or a suggestion from the end of any article, and the [Contribute](../contribute.md) page explains exactly what happens to it.

## How we fit with the others

We didn't invent hot rod history, and we couldn't do this without the people who kept it. The halls of fame honor the careers. The museums keep the cars. The foundations recorded the pioneers on camera. The magazine archives are the record of the industry, and the forums and strip encyclopedias hold thousands of memories. Our sources come from all of them, and we cite them by name. The [Landscape](landscape.md) page is a directory of all of them, with what each does best.

| | Halls of fame | Museums | Oral-history foundations | Magazine archives | Forums & fan encyclopedias | **This archive** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Built around** | Inductees and careers | Cars and artifacts | Recorded interviews | What the industry published | Members' posts and entries | Local stories, linked to the industry |
| **Who's in it** | National figures | Significant machines | Pioneers, mostly well known | Stars, builders, products | Anyone who posts | Clubs, strips, towns and the people in them |
| **How sources are shown** | Biographies | Labels and catalogs | The interview itself | The page itself | Varies; often first-person | A footnote on every claim, with conflicts and confidence flagged |
| **Local newspapers** | Rarely | Sometimes, in libraries | Rarely | Rarely | Sometimes | First stop |
| **Can you correct it?** | No | By contacting staff | By contacting staff | No | Yes, in the thread | Yes, from any page, with a public log |
| **Where it's strongest** | Recognition | Seeing the real thing | Hearing the voices | Depth and photos | Breadth and memory | Context, connection and checkable sourcing |

Some things we can't do as well as they can. We have no cars, no photo vault, and no hour-long interviews with the founders. Our image use is limited to public-domain and openly licensed material. Some of the best newspaper archives are closed to us. On sheer coverage, [Drag Strip List](https://dragstriplist.com/) has profiled more than 1,450 strips,[^dsl] and we have a few dozen. We say so because it's true, and because it tells you where to look next.

## The mission

> **To find, verify and tell the local stories of American hot rodding — the clubs, strips, cruise nights and small shops below the hall-of-fame level — and to show how they fed the people and companies everyone remembers. Every claim is cited, every doubt is shown, and anyone who was there can help set the record straight.**
