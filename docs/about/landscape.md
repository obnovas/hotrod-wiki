# The Landscape: Who Else Keeps Hot Rod History

*A directory of the halls, museums, archives and communities that keep this history. We rely on all of them.*

Nobody tells this story alone. Below are the main places where American hot rod and drag racing history is collected, honored or argued over, with what each one is and what it does best. Descriptions come from each organization's own pages, read on 2026-10-22. If something here is out of date, [tell us](../contribute.md). To see how this archive fits among them, read [Below the Local Level](what-makes-this-different.md).

## Halls of fame

**SEMA Hall of Fame.** Run by the Specialty Equipment Market Association, the aftermarket's trade group. It was created in 1969, and its first inductee was Paul Schiefer, the flywheel and clutch pioneer. It honors people who built the specialty-equipment *industry*. Nominees need contributions that reach beyond the local level to the national or international one, at least 10 years in the industry (15 preferred), and they must be 50 or older or deceased.[^sema][^semahof] *Best for:* the business founders and leaders of the aftermarket. [sema.org/sema-hall-of-fame](https://www.sema.org/sema-hall-of-fame)

**International Drag Racing Hall of Fame.** Part of the Don Garlits Museum of Drag Racing, founded in 1990 by Don and Pat Garlits. Inductees include drivers, crew chiefs, builders, track owners, promoters and manufacturers. A board of selectors chooses six each year from a candidate list of more than 500, and the ceremony is held in Gainesville the week of the Gatornationals.[^idrhof] *Best for:* the whole cast of drag racing, track operators included. [garlits.com/hall-of-fame](https://garlits.com/hall-of-fame/)

**Motorsports Hall of Fame of America.** A 501(c)(3) hall covering every kind of racing, with a drag racing category. It moved from the Detroit area to Daytona International Speedway in 2016 and holds its museum there; the 2027 ceremonies are its 39th.[^mshf][^mshfmove] *Best for:* drag racers in the context of all American motorsport. [mshf.com](https://www.mshf.com/)

**Grand National Roadster Show Hall of Fame.** Inducted each year at the Grand National Roadster Show in Pomona, the long-running show home of the America's Most Beautiful Roadster award. It honors builders, painters and shop owners in the show and custom world.[^gnrs] *Best for:* the show-car and builder community. [rodshows.com hall of fame](https://hotrodshows.com/grand-national/hall-of-fame/)

**Darryl Starbird's National Rod & Custom Car Hall of Fame.** Long housed in Afton, Oklahoma, it merged into the Museum of American Speed, and its collection moved to Lincoln, Nebraska, in 2022.[^starbird] *Best for:* rod and custom builders, especially in the middle of the country.

## Museums

**Wally Parks NHRA Motorsports Museum** (Pomona, California). An independent nonprofit since 1998, on the Fairplex grounds beside the drag strip where the NHRA held its first event in 1953. It holds dragsters, land speed cars, customs and hot rods, and it also runs the California Hot Rod Reunion and the Nostalgia Nationals.[^nhram] *Best for:* the cars and artifacts of organized drag racing, and its living reunions. [nhramuseum.org](https://nhramuseum.org/)

**Speedway Motors Museum of American Speed** (Lincoln, Nebraska). Founded in 1992 by "Speedy" Bill and Joyce Smith of Speedway Motors, now about 150,000 square feet of engines, vehicles and speed equipment. Its Leroy Byers Memorial Library collects books, magazines, catalogs, newspapers and event programs on the high-performance industry.[^moas][^moasdisp] *Best for:* the engines and parts themselves, and a deep research library. [museumofamericanspeed.org](https://www.museumofamericanspeed.org/)

**Don Garlits Museum of Drag Racing** (Ocala, Florida). Garlits's own museum, with his Swamp Rat cars and the International Drag Racing Hall of Fame. It is open daily.[^idrhof] [garlits.com](https://garlits.com/)

**Petersen Automotive Museum** (Los Angeles). Its rotating Vault holds more than 300 vehicles. The museum's digital archive is digitizing about a million images from the archive of Petersen Publishing, the house that published *Hot Rod*, in partnership with SEMA and MotorTrend Group, and commercial licensing goes through MotorTrend.[^petvault][^petarch] *Best for:* period photography from the magazines that defined the hobby. [petersen.org](https://petersen.org/) · [archive.petersen.org](https://archive.petersen.org/pages/home.php)

**Smithsonian National Museum of American History** (Washington, D.C.). Holds drag racing objects in the national collection, including Don Garlits's Swamp Rat XXX.[^nmah] Its former curator Robert C. Post wrote *High Performance*, the standard scholarly history of drag racing.[^post] *Best for:* drag racing as American history, at the national level. [americanhistory.si.edu](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1276317)

**The Henry Ford / Benson Ford Research Center** (Dearborn, Michigan). Its archives include Ford Motorsports records from 1957 to 1968 and the Dave Friedman photo collection, which covers Ford drag racing at the NHRA Nationals and Winternationals in the 1960s.[^thf1][^thf2] *Best for:* the factory side of 1960s racing. [findingaids.thehenryford.org](https://findingaids.thehenryford.org/xtf/data/pdf/2009-158_DaveFriedmanCollection.pdf)

**Revs Institute** (Naples, Florida). A research library and archive with a large searchable digital photo library and archival finding aids. Visits are by appointment.[^revs] Its collections lean toward road racing and sports cars more than hot rodding. [library.revsinstitute.org](https://library.revsinstitute.org/)

## Foundations and oral history

**American Hot Rod Foundation (AHRF).** Founded in 2002 by Steve and Carol Memishian to document and preserve hot rodding and land speed racing history. Its Pioneers series of filmed interviews includes Alex Xydias, Ed Iskenderian, Stu Hilborn and Barney Navarro, and its online library sorts photos by donor collection, decade and venue.[^ahrf][^hemmings] *Best for:* hearing the pioneers tell it themselves. [ahrf.com](https://ahrf.com/)

*A note on "AHFA":* we found no hot rod organization by that name. The initials mostly belong to the American Home Furnishings Alliance.[^ahfa] The American Hot Rod Foundation (AHRF) is almost certainly the one people mean.

**Quarter Mile Foundation / Project 1320.** Set up to collect and archive drag racing history and the related aftermarket, with a focus on first-person oral histories of the pioneers.[^qmf] [quartermilefoundation.org](https://quartermilefoundation.org/)

**Hagerty Drivers Foundation: National Historic Vehicle Register.** Documents historically significant American vehicles in the Library of Congress. The register includes the McGee Roadster, the 1932 Ford that ran the dry lakes and made the cover of *Hot Rod* in 1948. Hagerty Media also publishes hot rod history features.[^hagerty][^mcgee] *Best for:* the documented history of individual cars. [driversfoundation.org/register](https://www.driversfoundation.org/register)

## Associations

**National Street Rod Association (NSRA).** Grew out of the first Street Rod Nationals, held in Peoria, Illinois, in 1970 and organized by a *Rod & Custom* group led by editor Tom Medley. It is an events organization more than an archive. Its Street Rod Nationals, now in Louisville, reached their 57th year in 2026.[^nsra1][^nsra2] Our own account of Peoria is in [1970s: Peoria & the Street Rod Resurgence](../eras/1970s-street-rod-nationals.md). [nsra-usa.com](https://nsra-usa.com/)

## Magazine archives

**HOT ROD Digital Archives.** Since November 1, 2022, MotorTrend has offered more than 900 issues of *Hot Rod*, from January 1948 through December 2021, free on MotorTrend+, as PDFs or in a text format.[^hrarch] *Best for:* what the industry's flagship magazine said, month by month. [hotrod.com](https://www.hotrod.com/events/hot-rod-magazine-archive-release)

***Rod & Custom* and other titles.** Back issues are scattered. Some are sold digitally, and individual issues turn up on the Internet Archive.[^rc] The Petersen archive above holds much of the photography.

## Encyclopedias, databases and communities

**Kustomrama.** An open, wiki-style encyclopedia of traditional hot rods and customs, run from Norway by Sondre Kvipt. Anyone can contribute, and readers are asked to send information, photos and corrections.[^kr] *Best for:* customs, builders and individual cars, with lots of photos. [kustomrama.com](https://kustomrama.com/wiki/)

**The H.A.M.B. (The Jalopy Journal).** Ryan Cochran's traditional hot rod message board, running since 1997, with more than 500,000 discussions.[^hamb][^hambnow] It is an enormous, searchable store of first-person memory, and we cite H.A.M.B. posts as oral sources when someone who was there tells it. [jalopyjournal.com](https://www.jalopyjournal.com/forum/)

**Drag Strip List.** Compiled by Mel Bashore: more than 1,450 U.S. and Canadian drag strips, many long closed, with histories researched mainly from newspapers, reader-submitted memories, and a "Then … and Now" feature locating lost strips on aerial photos.[^dsl] It is the closest thing to this archive in method, and it covers far more strips than we do. *Best for:* finding out whether a strip existed, where, and when. [dragstriplist.com](https://dragstriplist.com/)

**Draglist.com.** Bill Pratt's drag racing database, online since 1996, with more than 129,000 racer listings from 1950 to today and thousands of archived stories.[^draglist] *Best for:* who raced what, and when. [draglist.com](https://www.draglist.com/about/)

**NHRA's "ghost tracks" columns and the lost-strip community.** Phil Burgess's *National Dragster* Insider columns collected readers' memories and coordinates for closed strips.[^ghost] A Facebook group, Lost and Forgotten Dragstrips, feeds an interactive map of abandoned strips.[^map] [nhra.com](https://www.nhra.com/news/2008/back-ghost-track-trail)

**Standard 1320 Drag Racing.** An online group of former racers and fans devoted to drag racing's golden era. Around 2000 its members came up with the idea of scanning complete runs of *Drag News* onto CD-ROM. Dennis Holding of the Frantic Four paid for the project, and sets from Don Garlits's own collection were among those scanned.[^s1320]

**Collectors and historians.** Much of drag racing's paper and photo record survives in private hands. The Wallace Family Archives, Dave Wallace's collection of magazines and photographs, is credited in *Hot Rod* and Competition Plus, and historians such as Larry Davis (*Gasser Wars*, 2003) have written the class histories.[^wallace][^gasser]
