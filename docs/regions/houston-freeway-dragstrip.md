# Houston Freeway Dragstrip (Dickinson): Purpose-Built on the Gulf Freeway

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** local history site (quotes Chronicle / Garlits), NHRA ghost-tracks, forum recollections · **Last verified:** 2026-10-14 (Round 9) · **Open issues:** Exact closing date (1987 vs "unknown"); Don Gay purchase year · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "A group of the West Coast fuel drag racers are doubting the times you've been setting there in the East, so everybody is figuring that it's time for a real showdown..."

    — Invitation letter to Don Garlits from a Freeway Drag Strip promoter, July (year in Garlits's memoir context ~1958), quoted via houstonfreewaydragstrip.com. [Voices](../voices/injection-and-tin.md)

The strip south of Houston on the east side of **I-45 / Gulf Freeway** near **Dickinson, Texas** (today roughly I-45 and 16th St / FM 646) was one of the Gulf Coast's purpose-built quarter-miles. Local history pages call it, over time, **Freeway Drag Strip**, **Houston International Speedway / Raceway / Dragway**, **South Texas Dragway**, and related tower names. It ran as an NHRA Division 4 track and closed in the late 1980s; a Walmart later occupied the old starting-line area.[^hfd][^nhra]

## Before the strip
Houston-area street racing fed the track: Road Kings activity, unfinished Westbury subdivision pavement, Richmond & Post Oak, the Greater Houston Timing Association, and events at the Hitchcock blimp base and Ellington Field (local site: 20,000+ at an Ellington event). ◐ *Club chronology is enthusiast-compiled.*[^hfd]

Land for the strip was donated by the **Fingers Furniture** family, per the same local history site. ◐[^hfd]

## Opening and the 1958 showdown
- Opens **fall 1957**—described as the region's first known **purpose-built** drag strip (as opposed to airports).[^hfd]
- Houston Chronicle Magazine, **March 1, 1959** ("The Flag Says Go"): starter Tim Evers; Bobby Langley's Scorpion II and Lin Huiet among those "zooming over 160 mph." ◐ *Quoted on the history site; Chronicle PDF not yet read here.*[^hfd]
- Don Garlits's memoir (quoted on the history site) recounts a July invitation to a California/Florida/Texas fuel showdown at Freeway: Pete Ogden in a Palamides streamliner, Jack Ewell in Kamboor's Jado Special, Red Case, and Texas star Bobby Langley in Scorpion II—Garlits against the field.[^hfd]

## Improvements, names, and notes
- Local site: **Don Gay** purchased the track in **1960** and added lights, Christmas tree, timing, stands, PA. ◐ Gay (the "Texas Teenager" Funny Car racer) later died June 30, 2007, age 60 (League City)—obituary facts as relayed on the site.[^hfd]
- Tower and sanctioning names shifted (NHRA / AHRA labels appear on different-era photos). Treat the name list as a photo-caption census, not a legal chain of title.[^hfd]
- **Kent Chatagnier** (Beaumont, "Kent's Speed Shop" fueler): local historians and Standard 1320 researchers argue he ran **197–201+ mph** at Houston in **1960** under then Drag News rules, later "forgotten" when record-keeping was reset. ◐ *Disputed historiography; flag, do not assert as NHRA-recognized first 200.*[^hfd]
- NHRA.com "Ghost tracks" (2008): by **2000** the site was largely demolished; reader note that a Walmart sat on the old starting line.[^nhra]
- Closing: Yellow Bullet forum recollection says end of **1987**, with Houston Raceway Park (Baytown) opening **1988**. Other secondary text says "unknown date." ⚠ Prefer **1987** as working date, marked single-sourced to forum memory until a Chronicle closing notice is found.[^yb][^hfd]
