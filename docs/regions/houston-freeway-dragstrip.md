# Houston Freeway Dragstrip (Dickinson): Purpose-Built on the Gulf Freeway

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> local history site (quotes Chronicle / Garlits), NHRA columns (2008 ghost tracks; 2015 Gay family profile; 2001 Top 50 Snow bio), forum recollections · **Last verified:** 2026-10-15 (Round 10) · **Open issues:** exact closing date (1987 vs "unknown"); exact year the Gay family bought the strip (by 1961 at the latest); ⚠ buyer was Carl and Marie Gay, not Don, per NHRA (Round 10 correction) · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "A group of the West Coast fuel drag racers are doubting the times you've been setting there in the East, so everybody is figuring that it's time for a real showdown..."

    — Invitation letter to Don Garlits from a Freeway Drag Strip promoter, July (year in Garlits's memoir context ~1958), quoted via houstonfreewaydragstrip.com. [Voices](../voices/injection-and-tin.md)

The strip south of Houston on the east side of **I-45 / Gulf Freeway** near **Dickinson, Texas** (today roughly I-45 and 16th St / FM 646) was one of the Gulf Coast's purpose-built quarter-miles. Local history pages call it, over time, **Freeway Drag Strip**, **Houston International Speedway / Raceway / Dragway**, **South Texas Dragway**, and related tower names. It ran as an NHRA Division 4 track and closed in the late 1980s; a Walmart later occupied the old starting-line area.[^hfd][^nhra]

## Before the strip
Houston-area street racing fed the track: Road Kings activity, unfinished Westbury subdivision pavement, Richmond & Post Oak, the Greater Houston Timing Association, and events at the Hitchcock blimp base and Ellington Field (local site: 20,000+ at an Ellington event). ◐ *Club chronology is enthusiast-compiled.*[^hfd]

Land for the strip was donated by the **Fingers Furniture** family, per the same local history site. ◐[^hfd]

## Opening and the 1958 showdown
- Opens **fall 1957**—described as the region's first known **purpose-built** drag strip (as opposed to airports).[^hfd]
- Houston Chronicle Magazine, **March 1, 1959** ("The Flag Says Go"): starter Tim Evers; Bobby Langley's Scorpion II and Lin Huiet among those "zooming over 160 mph." ◐ *Quoted on the history site; Chronicle PDF not yet read here.*[^hfd]
- Don Garlits's memoir (quoted on the history site) recounts a July invitation to a California/Florida/Texas fuel showdown at Freeway: Pete Ogden in a Palamides streamliner, Jack Ewell in Kamboor's Jado Special, Red Case, and Texas star Bobby Langley in Scorpion II—Garlits against the field.[^hfd]

## Who bought it
- **Correction (Round 10):** our local source says **Don Gay** bought the track in **1960** and added lights, a Christmas tree, timing, stands and a PA.[^hfd] ◐ NHRA's Phil Burgess says instead that Don's **parents, Carl and Marie Gay**, owners of **Gay Pontiac** on the Gulf Highway in Dickinson, bought "nearby Freeway Drag Strip, which they rechristened **Houston Drag Raceway**." It later became Houston Int'l Speedway, then Houston Int'l Raceway.[^gay] Don, who died June 30, 2007, at 60, was only about 13 in 1960. We therefore credit the **Gay family (Carl and Marie)** as buyers. The improvements may well be theirs too. The purchase came **no later than 1961**, when Carl booked Hayden Proffitt there (below). The exact year is still open.

## The Gay family years (Round 10)
- **Booking the factory cars:** in **1961** Carl Gay booked **Hayden Proffitt** and his Mickey Thompson–owned, 421-powered aluminum **Pontiac Catalina** factory experimental. Don was 14, legal driving age in Texas then. "Within a year" he had an A/Stock Catalina and an A/Modified Production Catalina wagon, and younger brother **Roy** ran a B/Stock GTO.[^gay]
- **1964:** Don, still a Dickinson High student, won **A/Stock class at the Indy Nationals**. That year Carl booked **Arnie Beswick**'s blown GTO, and the family decided to build their own.[^gay]
- ***Infinity*:** with lead mechanic **James Osteen** they took a **'65 GTO** off the lot, moved the front wheels forward four to five inches, and added a fiberglass front end, hood and doors. The name came from the opening of TV's *Ben Casey*. It debuted **July 3, 1965, at the family track** and lost to "Dyno Don" Nicholson that day when the transmission broke.[^gay] *Infinity II* (1966) had a **Jay Howell** tube chassis. The series ended with ***Infinity V***, a 1970 Firebird.[^gay]
- **A home for touring racers:** "They'd all stay at our house," Don recalled. "We'd say, 'Hey, my mom has a two-story house. Why don't you stay?' We'd sit by the swimming pool on Friday night and drink and talk."[^gay] See [Voices](../voices/strip-owners-and-door-slammers.md).
- **The Gay–Snow connection:** Fort Worth's [Gene Snow](../people/gene-snow.md) made what NHRA calls Funny Car's first 200-mph pass, **200.88 mph**, "at Don and Carl Gay's track in Dickinson, Texas." ⚠ It is dated variously to Aug. 17, 1968, or 1969.[^snow]
- **Roy Gay** was runner-up to [Eddie Schartman](../people/eddie-schartman.md) at the 1967 Manufacturers Championships at OCIR. He was killed in a 1972 motorcycle accident.[^gay]
- **Next generation and the next track:** in the late 1980s the Gay family and the **Angel brothers** (Greg, Gary and Glenn) founded **Houston Raceway Park in Baytown**, on an old rice farm.[^gay] On Oct. 6, 1988, Gene Snow ran the first sub-five-second pass at an NHRA event there (see [Gene Snow](../people/gene-snow.md)). This supports the Yellow Bullet memory below that the Dickinson track closed about 1987.

## Names, records and closing
- Tower and sanctioning names shifted (NHRA / AHRA labels appear on different-era photos). Treat the name list as a photo-caption census, not a legal chain of title.[^hfd]
- **Kent Chatagnier** (Beaumont, "Kent's Speed Shop" fueler): local historians and Standard 1320 researchers argue he ran **197–201+ mph** at Houston in **1960** under then Drag News rules, later "forgotten" when record-keeping was reset. ◐ *Disputed historiography; flag, do not assert as NHRA-recognized first 200.*[^hfd]
- NHRA.com "Ghost tracks" (2008): by **2000** the site was largely demolished; reader note that a Walmart sat on the old starting line.[^nhra]
- Closing: Yellow Bullet forum recollection says end of **1987**, with Houston Raceway Park (Baytown) opening **1988**. Other secondary text says "unknown date." ⚠ Prefer **1987** as working date, marked single-sourced to forum memory until a Chronicle closing notice is found.[^yb][^hfd]
