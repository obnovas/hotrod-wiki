## JEGS: Columbus, Ohio, 1960
Jeg Coughlin was "frustrated by the unavailability of quality car parts in his Midwest hometown." In 1960 he opened **JEGS Automotive** in a 4,000 sq ft Columbus shop, "half garage, half parts store," building engines part-time and hunting down parts to sell.[^jegs] He turned the business over to his four sons in the early 1990s.[^jegs] Search summaries mention earlier borrowed gas-station space and a Bonham Avenue building, then an 11th Avenue store. ◐ *Columbus Dispatch pages were paywalled; not verified.*[^disp]

## The Slo-Pokes of Peoria
The club that hosted the 1970 Nationals ([Peoria](../eras/1970s-street-rod-nationals.md)). **Spelling varies:** "Slo-Pokes" (Rod & Custom, Peoria Magazine), "Slo Poks" (Modern Rodding).[^rc][^pm][^mr] Peoria Magazine reports 1,200 participants under the banner "All roads lead to Peoria." Chuck McCarty credits the club with introducing him to cruise-ins in the '60s.[^pm]

- **⚠ Best-car puzzle:** Andy Brizio complained that "a guy wins the Best Car Award with a car that got there on a trailer."[^rc] Modern Rodding identifies the Best Overall winner as **Orv Elgie** ('37 Ford sedan delivery), with a plaque showing his 8,000-mile drive California–Ontario–Peoria.[^mr] These may be different awards. Unresolved.
- Modern Rodding: "Technically the National Street Rod Association wasn't created until after the first event."[^mr]

## The Torrid Torkers (Buffalo, NY)
Jim Oddy joined the club at 16 in 1957, per the International Drag Racing Hall of Fame list.[^idr] Other club details (a '36 Chevy, cruises to Canadian strips) come only from a search summary. ◐ *Needs a direct source.*

## Kentucky / NSRA
The Kentucky Motorsports Hall of Fame "NSTRA" history page is mainly about the NSRA's Louisville era: the first Louisville Nationals in 1988, a record 25 consecutive years through 2022, and the Kentucky Safety Team ranked #1 nationally in 1986.[^ky] The "NSTRA" in the page name seems to refer to the NSRA's state program, not a drag-racing body. ◐
