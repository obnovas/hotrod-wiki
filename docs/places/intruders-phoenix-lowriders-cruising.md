# "We're the Slowest Ones": The Intruders and Cruising in Phoenix (Phoenix, Ariz., 1975 or 1976–2026)

*A lowrider club MotorTrend says was named for a soul group, and a city rule that counts to three*

!!! abstract "Article record"

## Three times past the same point

Phoenix has a rule that counts. In a posted no-cruising area, a driver who passes the same police traffic control point three times within two hours, and knowingly adds to the congestion, can be ticketed on the third pass. The Intruders, by Jimmy Arbizu's account the first club for lowriders only, have cruised under that rule, and one of them says they are the slowest ones on the road.

The cruise used to be Central Avenue. When Phoenix police cracked down on the teenagers coasting up and down it on Friday and Saturday nights in the late 1970s and early 1980s, the kids moved to Metrocenter, the mall on the west side, and circled it for hours.[^nt20] "If the cops saw you go past three times, you got a ticket," Randy Slack remembered.[^nt20] Silva, a lowrider who cruised Metro on Friday nights with his friends, said police singled them out. "You'd go around once and they'd pull you out of the line and try to ticket you," he said. His older brother had a chromed-out Bonneville, and the police would be on him as soon as he showed up.[^nt20]

The rule is in the city code. No one may drive past a traffic control point "three times within a two-hour period in or around a posted no cruising area" so as to knowingly add to congestion, block streets or parking lots, or get in the way of businesses. The signs have to say so. Nothing counts until the third pass, the fine is up to $250, and an area is posted only when the council passes a resolution for it.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^pcc]

## The Intruders

The Intruders were started by Carlos Zarate, the club's first leader, and took their name from the soul group of the 1960s and 1970s.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^mt21] The club dates itself to 1975 by one account and 1976 by most.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^mt21][^dd22][^gg18] In 1976 it had more than twenty members, among them Joe Taos, Gerald Barela, Tony Barela and Mark Orosco. In 1994 Tony Barela and his nephew Gerald started it up again with ten others. Other Arizona clubs had lowriders, the president, Jimmy Arbizu, said, but the Intruders were the first to have lowriders only. They used to cruise Grand Avenue downtown, and he said they never broke the cruising law doing it.[^dd22]

Arbizu had run the club for 17 years by 2021, with about sixty members. He and his father built cars together at his father's house, and when they ran out of room they made the garage bigger. When MotorTrend's *Lowrider Garage* came to his house in Avondale, the club rounded up about 40 cars on a weekday.[^mt21] His own '51 Chevy pickup, with its 235 straight six on dual pipes, and his '66 Impala convertible were in the club's lineup when the Intruders were the featured club at the Goodguys Spring Nationals at WestWorld in Scottsdale in 2018, with Tino Hernandez's '35 Dodge, Andy Reynoso's '66 Impala and William Martinez's '51 Chevy.[^gg18]

Gabe Lopez's father won his mother over with a 1959 Chevy convertible. Lopez got his own first Impala, a 1974, in his early twenties. His father took him to the dog races, where Lopez had three numbers in his head, and his father placed the winning bet; Lopez bought the Impala with the winnings. Lopez likes bombs, the rounded cars of the 1930s and 1940s that run on original parts and no hydraulics. They got the name, he said, from the air-raid sirens they cruise with, which mean "here we come." His 1935 Chevy Master took third in its class at the Arizona Super Show.[^dd22] The club's September picnic brings out hundreds of cars. Talking about the club's free events, David Tapia put it simply: "We're the slowest ones, usually the low riders are going under the speed limit."[^abc15]

## Last cruise at Metro

Metrocenter closed on June 30, 2020. Tracy Smith, who graduated from Thunderbird High in 1986, set up a last cruise on Facebook, expecting maybe 10 friends. Nearly 15,000 people came on the mall's last day of business, and a second "final" cruise that Saturday drew an estimated 18,000-plus. One man had pasted a giant copy of the ticket he got for cruising Metro some 40 years before on the side of his car.[^nt20]

The Intruders marked fifty years in 2026.[^azpbs26] That year the Tempe History Museum put up "Ranfla Vida," an exhibit of Arizona lowriding; ranfla is slang for car, its curator, Marco Albarran, explained, and vida is life. His own club jacket, from his hometown of Somerton near Yuma, hung in a glass case. The cruising ban was still in effect in Phoenix and other Valley cities.[^kjzz26] The rule still counts to three, and the Intruders, by their own account, are still going under the speed limit.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Were the Intruders founded in 1975 or 1976?** MotorTrend's *Lowrider Garage* (2021) says the club was "established in Arizona way back in 1975."[^mt21] *Downtown Devil* (2022), *Goodguys* (2018), ABC15 and Arizona PBS (2026) say 1976, and Arizona PBS dates the 50th anniversary to 2026.[^dd22][^gg18][^abc15][^azpbs26] A founding in late 1975 and a first full year in 1976 would fit both; no source says so. The front-matter `years` span is `[1975, 2026]`. That field is `[first, last]`, two integers, and 1975 is the earlier side of the dispute, the year MotorTrend gives, so the span covers 1976. It does not decide the question. The title says 1975 or 1976. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Is the club named for the soul group?** Only MotorTrend says "The Intruders came up with their name from the popular Soul music group from the 60's and 70's."[^mt21] It does not name a song or a member who chose it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **When did Phoenix pass its cruising rule, and where was it first posted?** The code section lists Ordinances G-2750 and G-2872 and an amendment, G-4285, "passed, 2000," but gives no dates for the first two.[^pcc] The *New Times* places the crackdown on Central Avenue in the late 1970s and early 1980s and the three-lap rule at Metrocenter.[^nt20] The council minutes for the first ordinance would date it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
