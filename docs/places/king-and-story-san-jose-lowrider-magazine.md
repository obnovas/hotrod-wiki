# The Last No Cruising Sign: King and Story, Lowrider Magazine and San Jose's Ban (1974–2022)

*A cruising corner that got its own magazine, a 36-year ban, and the councilman who took the last sign down*

## A magazine from a Willow Street storefront

San Jose gave its cruise a national magazine, and then it outlawed cruising for 36 years. The man who finally took the last "No Cruising" sign down had been four years old when the ban went up.

The cruise started downtown. Police pushed it out to the unincorporated East Side, and it settled near the corner of Story and King roads.[^sjsu] The New Style Car Club, founded in 1974, is remembered as San Jose's first formal lowrider club.[^ulc] Cruising from sundown to sunup was a common weekend thing there, the San Jose lowrider council says: the parking lots on the corners filled with lowriders, and thousands of cars packed the short strip of road.[^ulc]

Sonny Madrid grew up with it. Born in Yuma, Arizona, in 1945, he came to San Jose with his family in the 1960s and went to James Lick High, San Jose City College and San Jose State, where he promoted dances under the name Lowrider Associates. He wrote for several local papers, and he had an idea for a magazine that put his love of custom cars beside art, satire, fashion and politics.[^sjpl] In January 1977 he and two other San Jose State students, Larry Gonzalez and David Nunez, pooled their money and put out the first *Lowrider* from a small storefront on Willow Street.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^sjsu][^lat00] At first the editors and ad salesmen delivered every issue themselves to stores around the state, which ate a week of each month.[^lat00]

## King and Story

The city became what the library calls northern California's cruising capital.[^sjpl] A KCRA reporter, John Gibson, visited in 1978 to report on the scene at Story and King, "purported to be the 'Low Rider capital of the world.'"[^ulc] The first National Lowrider show was held that year at the Santa Clara County Fairgrounds.[^ulc] Andy's Hydraulics opened one of the first hydraulic supply shops for lowriders, and the council credits it with being first to hit back bumper and to flip a car over, in 1978.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^ulc] Biney Ruiz of 5 Star Productions put music acts and car exhibits together in the same show.[^ulc]

The magazine sold slowly until it put bikini-clad models on its covers at the end of 1979. Imitators followed. Madrid gave up ownership in 1984 over money troubles, and in December 1985, taken over by its printer, *Lowrider* folded.[^sjpl][^lat00] Madrid moved to Visalia, promoted car shows and wrote now and then for *Streetlow*.[^sjpl] A group of loyalists, co-founder Larry Gonzalez among them, brought *Lowrider* back in June 1988 and moved it to Southern California, and the first issue sold 20,000 copies in a week. By 2000 it averaged about 210,000 copies a month, most of them sold at the newsstand.[^lat00]

## The ban

San Jose banned cruising in 1986.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^nbc][^ao] The city code defined cruising as "the repetitive driving of any motor vehicle past a traffic-control point in congested traffic," and signs went up marking the zones.[^sjpd] "So in downtown, if you miss your turn and you get lost and you go around a couple times you can essentially be in violation of this ordinance," Peralez said later. "It was very, very broad."[^sjs]

Peralez grew up cruising downtown with his parents. His first car, at 16, was a 1965 Chevy Impala, forest green, an SS, and within a year he joined the Impalas Car Club. He was the only kid at Cupertino High who drove a lowrider. "I can't recall how many times I was pulled over by the police," he said. "Maybe 30 to 40 times through the years." The police never found anything in his car, he said, but they always found out he was a scholar athlete and, later, a math major at San Jose State. He became a police officer himself.[^ao][^sjs]

David Polanco, who grew up around the cars in the late 1970s, got his first custom car, a '69 Chevelle he admits was "ugly," at 19. He left the scene to raise his family and came back when his kids went off to college. One of his cars now is an Impala painted aqua azure, and outside a supermarket in Almaden a little boy walking by told his mother, "Mom, it looks like water!" "That was the biggest compliment that kid could give me for my paint job," Polanco said.[^ao]

## Taking the sign down

By 2022 Peralez was on the city council, and he found the city had not written a cruising ticket in 20 years.[^nbc][^ao] On Tuesday, June 28, 2022, the council voted unanimously to repeal the ban; Police Chief Anthony Mata had proposed allowing cruising only at permitted events instead.[^nbc][^sjs] The police bulletin to all officers dates the repeal of the code chapter to August 9, 2022, and tells them they can no longer use it as grounds to stop anyone.[^sjpd]

Later that year hundreds of lowriders filled the City Hall rotunda and East Santa Clara Street for the "End of Cruising Ban" event, and the city's last "No Cruising Zone" sign came off its pole on East Santa Clara.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^abc7] Peralez took it down himself, standing on a small ladder beside a 1941 Chevy Special Deluxe, and waved it in the air.[^ao] "It's a historic day! It is a historic day!" said Tim Carrasco, a solo rider.[^abc7] Dulce Fernandez of the lowrider council told the crowd, "Let's be proud, let's be safe, and let's keep it low and slow!"[^abc7] The town that gave cruising its own magazine had made it legal again, and the man holding the sign had never known it any other way.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did the ban start in 1986 or the early 1990s?** NBC Bay Area (2022) says the council repealed "a ban on cruising implemented in 1986," and *Atlas Obscura* (2022) calls it "a 36-year prohibition."[^nbc][^ao] Peralez's memory of being four when it became illegal fits 1986.[^abc7] *San José Spotlight* (July 1, 2022) says the ban "was implemented in the early 1990s to curb gang violence."[^sjs] The 1986 ordinance itself, in the city's records, would settle it; the Spotlight's date may describe when enforcement began. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Nunez or Nunes?** San José State's exhibit and the *Los Angeles Times* (2000) name the third founder David Nunez; the lowrider council's history page spells it David Nunes.[^sjsu][^lat00][^ulc] The page uses Nunez, the spelling in two sources. The council also says the Willow Street office was at No. 282.[^ulc] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Andy's Hydraulics' firsts, and the landmark?** That Andy's was "known to be the first to hit back bumper and flip a car over in 1978" is the lowrider council's account alone.[^ulc] So is its statement that the original *Lowrider* office "is now a historical landmark"; we did not find the city's designation record, and the page does not state it as fact. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did the last sign come down?** ABC7's report, published September 1, 2022, says the celebration and the sign's removal took place "on Wednesday," which would be the last day of August, and that the council had voted "in June."[^abc7] *Atlas Obscura* (November 2022) describes the same scene, Peralez on a ladder taking down the last sign, as a gathering "in June."[^ao] The police bulletin dates the code's repeal to August 9, 2022.[^sjpd] We follow ABC7, which reported the day it happened, but have not found a city notice of the event. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
