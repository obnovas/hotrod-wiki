# Cruising & Hangouts: Overview

*Before the strips, and alongside them, the street was the showroom.*

This section documents the **cruise strips, drive-ins, parking-lot meets and street-racing spots** from about 1940 to 1990 that we can source. Revivals after 1990 are noted briefly. Where a spot had no documented wider influence, we record it as a **regional repeat gathering**.

## How cruising was policed or ended

Every city found its own way to shut the party down. Here is the scorecard:

| City | Tool used | When | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Woodward (MI) | Unspecified laws limiting cruising and long-term parking; drive-in "lot cops"; insurance and the oil crisis | Late 1960s to 1970s | Faded. The specific ordinances aren't identified [◐](woodward.md#q-2 "Single source so far: see Open questions on Woodward Avenue") |
| Burbank (CA) | Police tolerance, then raids on the River Road (told years later) | 1950s | Racing moved to the strips |
| Van Nuys (CA) | LAPD street closures and barricades | 1980 to 1983 [⚠](van-nuys.md#q-1 "Sources disagree: see Open questions on Van Nuys Boulevard") | Ended; monthly revival from 2010 |
| East LA (CA) | Sheriff's weekend roadblocks (Atlantic to Eastern), then a 1997 county ordinance | Mar or Aug 1979 [⚠](whittier-blvd.md#closure-two-dates "Sources disagree: see Open questions on Whittier Boulevard") | Ended for about 15 years; revival and new enforcement in 2017 |
| Modesto (CA) | Anti-cruising ordinance (no passing the same spot more than once every 4 hours) plus anti-loitering | Mar 27, 1990 ("banned" in 1993 per a marker [⚠](modesto.md#q-1 "Sources disagree: see Open questions on Modesto")) | Moved off McHenry; now sanctioned festivals |
| Houston (TX) | Roadblocks; turns off side streets banned | About 1986 | "Vanished in about a month"; the scene moved elsewhere |
| Albuquerque (NM) | 2005 ordinance; license-plate tracking at checkpoints | 2006–2018 | Repealed; club cruise program |
| Twin Falls (ID) | Coroner's jury, newspaper editorial, then heavier fines from the police judge; the city voted to lease a strip site | 1957–1960 | Club formed; no evidence the strip was built [◐](twin-falls-main-avenue.md#q-4 "Single source so far: see Open questions on Main Avenue, Twin Falls") |
| California statewide | **AB 436** (Alvarez et al.), Stats. 2023 ch. 803: amends Vehicle Code 21100 to remove local authority to regulate cruising; repeals 24008 (low-frame ban)[^ab436] | Chaptered Oct 13, 2023; effective **Jan 1, 2024** | Old local cruising ordinances unenforceable (LAist; LA City Attorney memo Oct 2024)[^lacity] |

The statewide change came through Assembly Bill 436 (authors Alvarez, Ramos and Luz Rivas; Chapter 803, Statutes of 2023), which the Governor approved on Oct 13, 2023, and which took effect Jan 1, 2024. It removed the Vehicle Code authorization for local cruising ordinances and repealed the statewide low-frame height prohibition in section 24008.[^ab436] Lowrider organizations were among its sponsors, according to the Assembly analysis.[^ab436] Los Angeles's City Attorney reported in October 2024 that the city's own cruising ordinance could no longer be enforced.[^lacity]

A pattern runs through the table. California cities and counties mostly used **physical closures** (barricades and roadblocks) first and **pass-limit ordinances** later. These defined "cruising" as passing a checkpoint a set number of times within a set window: 2 times in 6 hours in LA County, 1997; once per 4 hours in Modesto, 1990; 3 times in 2 hours in Albuquerque, 2005. Woodward and Houston ended through enforcement and changing conditions. The sources rarely say whether the dated measures were formally repealed. Sources are on each page.
