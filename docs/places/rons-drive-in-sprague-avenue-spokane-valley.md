# "I Don't Know Anything About Frying a Hamburger": Ron's Drive-In and Sprague Avenue, Spokane Valley (1958– )

*A produce vendor who had never cooked a burger built a drive-in on the family's old farm, and hot rodders have been parking there for sixty years*

    "I think Ron's and cruising go together. Nothing fits this location better than guys and street rods."

    — Denny Hegewald, owner of Ron's Drive-In, to Sam Francis, *The Spokesman-Review*, August 23, 1997.[^sr97]

## A produce man and a model

Ron's Drive-In is a hot rod hangout that was built by a man who knew nothing about restaurants. Joe Genova was a produce vendor on Spokane's North Side, facing competition from a big grocery store, when he decided to try something new. He had an eighth-grade education. "I don't know anything about frying a hamburger," his sister, Helen Ronayne, remembered him saying when he opened. "But he had the land and he had the smarts and the head to make it go."[^obit04]

The land was the Genova family homestead, one block east of Pines Road on Sprague Avenue, where his immigrant Italian parents had farmed when he was a child. He tore down the family house and built a life-sized version of a model he had made in his basement: a 30-by-30-foot stainless steel kitchen ringed with glass windows. The drive-in opened in 1958.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> Some people were sure it would not last, his son Ron said, because the freeway had just opened. Joe named it for his eldest son, and for 37 years began each day in the kitchen picking and slicing tomatoes himself.[^obit04]

## Saturday nights, and a raw onion

On Saturday nights the lot filled with teenagers. A burger, Coke and fries cost less than 50 cents. Bonita Cox, who worked the counter in the 1970s, said it "reminded me of 'Happy Days'": kids came in after football games and stayed until 1 a.m., an hour after closing. When they got out of hand, Genova walked out of the kitchen into the crowd. "Go find yourself a girlfriend," he would say, and everyone listened.[^obit04] The *Spokesman-Review*'s obituary credited him with "simultaneously introducing Spokane Valley to fast-food hamburgers and teenagers to cruise night."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^obit04]

The owner who bought it from Genova in 1995, Denny Hegewald, had his own hot rod days. He bought a purple Model T roadster in 1969 for $600. "The girls liked it," he told the paper. He remembered that back in the 1950s the cruising guys ordered "pure raw onion burgers" from Ron's, and gave a joking reason: "So the guys could go home on Friday nights and cover up their beer."[^sr97] A man named Jim Burke told the paper he had been "cruising at Ron's since I was in high school," and had bought his 1940 Chevrolet just before starting at Spokane Falls.[^sr97]

By the summer of 1997 the lot was a Thursday-night rendezvous, sponsored by the Valley's Old Irons Auto Club. Members cruised in from 6 p.m. to about 9, typically 50 cars, and Ron's gave them discounted burgers and fish sandwiches. That week the National Street Rod Association was holding its 14th annual convention in Spokane, with about 1,200 pre-1949 cars at the Spokane Interstate Fairgrounds and 8,000 to 10,000 visitors expected, and "plenty of convention-goers" came to the lot.[^sr97]

## Still on Thursdays

The original drive-in gave way to a building with indoor seating in 1979, and Genova sold the business in 1995.[^obit04] In November 1997 the *Spokesman-Review* reported that a second Ron's had opened four miles west, in the former Burger Basket building.[^sr97b] Genova died on August 30, 2004, at 89. Ron said customers from Alaska had ordered burgers and had them flown north to friends.[^obit04] The Inland Northwest Car Club Council's 2026 cruise-night list shows Thursday at Ron's Drive Inn, 12502 E. Sprague, "Directed by Riverside City Cruisers."[^cc26]

The Top Hat Drive-In, at 2101 E. Sprague, had been the teenagers' place from the 1940s into the 1960s, with uniformed carhops, and it closed in the late 1960s.[^th25] A produce man who knew nothing about frying a hamburger built a drive-in on the family's old farm, and the Valley's hot rodders made it theirs. By the 2026 list, they still park there on Thursdays.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **1958 or 1957?** The 2004 obituary says Genova "opened the business in 1958 and operated it for 37 years."[^obit04] The *Spokesman-Review*'s November 27, 1997 business item says "the original Ron's opened 40 years ago," which would be 1957.[^sr97b] Hegewald's memory of 1950s cruisers ordering onion burgers fits either year. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Ron's introduce teenagers to cruise night?** The line is the obituary writer's, Tom Lutey's, not a quote from the family. Spokane's Top Hat Drive-In, at 2101 E. Sprague, was a teenagers' hangout from the 1940s into the 1960s, which suggests teenagers on Sprague did not wait for Ron's.[^th25] Whether any of them were *cruising*, in the sense of a rolling circuit, none of our sources says. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
