# Companies & Shops

*Garage-born speed shops, and the aftermarket names they grew into.*

Nearly every company here began with one guy who needed a part that didn't exist: a header he couldn't afford, a gasket that wouldn't stay put, a bellhousing that wouldn't let a clutch fly into the grandstand. The stories are mostly about what happened next, and about the grassroots (clubs, strips, cruise spots, small shops) that made the demand.

*Short on time?* The [Quick Answers](../reference/quick-answers.md#companies-and-parts) give the short version of the big questions about companies and the parts business, each with a link to the full story.

**New: [The Web of Influence](web-of-influence.md)**, a map of who fed whom. Every line is backed by a wiki page and a verbatim phrase from it.

## The Big Names

The brands every catalog carries, told from their first garage, first ad or first customer.

- [Holley](holley.md): a bicycle-motor shop in Bradford, Pa., and the carburetor that followed everybody; now the collector of other brands.
- [Hurst-Campbell](hurst-campbell.md) <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span>: a two-car garage in Abington, Pa., and the Bucks County magazines that watched the plant go up.
- [Crane Cams](crane-cams.md) <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span>: a Florida machinist's son who ground his own cam, and refunded the little guys at the Nationals.
- [COMP Cams and Racing Head Service](comp-cams-rhs.md): three Oldsmobile tinkerers, a Memphis basement and Sun Studio's corner.
- [Summit Racing](summit-racing.md): a Corvette, a donut-shop basement and two small magazine ads.
- [Mickey Thompson](mickey-thompson.md): the Times pressman who dug the post holes for Lions himself.
- [Edelbrock's Slingshot](edelbrock-slingshot.md): how a Breawood Garage mechanic became a manufacturer.
- [JEGS](jegs.md): a Columbus racer who opened his own speed shop in 1960 and grew it into a mail-order giant.

## How the profiles are built

Each profile ends with a **Connect the dots** section that lists the wiki pages (people, strips, clubs, regions) tied to that company, with a one-line reason for each. Where a source does not document a tie, we say the link is only place or time. Conflicting sources are marked ⚠ and single-sourced claims ◐, and most "firsts" are credited to the person who claimed them. Not yet profiled, for lack of good sources: Offenhauser, Cragar, American Racing, Mallory, Weiand's later years and JEGS; see the [Research Trail](../research-trail.md).
