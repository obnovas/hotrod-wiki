# Logghe Stamping: Fraser's Flip-Top Chassis Shop

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium–High · **Source types:** MMSHOF bio, Garlits Museum HOF, enthusiast press · **Last verified:** 2026-10-14 (Round 9) · **Open issues:** Company founding year 1946 vs 1947 · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "I said, 'What the hell is this?' It was a dragster with a body on it and the driver in the back seat..."

    — Eddie Schartman on his first Logghe flip-top Comet, Dec 1965 (NHRA.com, 2016). [Context](../people/eddie-schartman.md)

Gene Logghe was burned in the brothers' own dragster at **Ubly Dragway** on a **187 mph** run in **1962**. Their father then forbade them to drive diggers.[^mms][^gar]

They kept building them. The **Logghe Stamping Company** of Fraser, Michigan, turned a Big Three bracketry shop into the chassis house that defined early Funny Cars. Brothers **Ron** and **Gene Logghe** built the first series of tube-frame, coil-over, one-piece flip-top cars for Lincoln-Mercury in 1966. Customers included [Eddie Schartman](../people/eddie-schartman.md), Dyno Don Nicholson, Jack Chrisman, Connie Kalitta, Gene Snow, [Al Bergler](../people/al-bergler.md), and many others.[^mms][^gar]

## Morris Logghe's shop

Morris Logghe, a Belgian immigrant, founded Logghe Stamping as an automotive bracketry company serving Detroit's Big Three. **⚠ From the detective's notebook: the founding year.** The Michigan Motor Sports Hall of Fame says **1947** in Fraser; Hot Rod's family feature says **1946**, starting in a home garage before moving to a Fraser plant.[^mms][^hr]

Ron and Gene began working for their father in **1955** (Gene about 17, Ron about 15).[^mms] They first tried sports-car racing, then attended the drag races at the long-defunct strip in **New Baltimore, Mich., in 1958**, where they met local racer Connie Kalitta.[^mms][^gar]

## From Kalitta rails to Competition Products

In **1961** Kalitta asked them to build a dragster chassis, their first customer chassis. Success brought more orders; they added clutch cans, steering parts, and axles.[^gar]

Partnership with employees **Jim Marsh** and **Roy Steffey** produced the injected Chevy digger nicknamed **"the Giant Killer."** MMSHOF: in **1964** it became the first non-supercharged car into the sevens. ◐[^mms]

In **1965** Maynard Rupp drove their blown Chrysler **"the Prussian"** (tuned by Steffey) to what Garlits Museum calls the first NHRA points championship for that team. ◐[^gar]

They formed a **Competition Products** division for race cars and parts. Garlits Museum: the shop later turned out **over 600** race cars (Funny Cars, diggers, Pro Stocks, Altereds, Bonneville cars, even go-karts). ◐[^gar] *Car Craft* named Logghe Funny Car Chassis Builder of the Year **1967–70**. ◐[^gar]

## The 1966 flip-top Comets

Lincoln-Mercury's **Al Turner** (with [Fran Hernandez](../people/fran-hernandez.md) in the factory program) asked for cars that could beat Chrysler's altered-wheelbase match racers. The Logghes delivered a **chromoly tube chassis**, **ladder-bar** rear, **four-corner coil-overs**, and a **one-piece flip-top** fiberglass body, first for Nicholson and Schartman on normally aspirated **427 SOHC** engines, then a blown SOHC car for Chrisman.[^mms][^gar][^hr]

Ron Logghe told MMSHOF they "literally introduced the coil-over design to the racing industry." ◐ *A strong claim; treat as self-attribution.*[^mms] [Al Bergler](../people/al-bergler.md) did the aluminum tinwork on Logghe cars.[^mms]

Schartman won NHRA's first Funny Car eliminator at the **1966 World Finals** in Tulsa in a Logghe Comet.[^nhra]

Demand outran the shop. Wikipedia (citing secondary histories) notes other builders (Don Hardy, Ronnie Scrima, Dick Fletcher) joined the chassis trade as Logghe could not fill every order. ◐ *Verify against period ads before treating as settled.*[^wp]

## Later

Racing chassis work at Logghe Stamping was **discontinued in 1975**.[^mms] The stamping company itself closed in **2013**.[^gar]

Ron Logghe died **May 27, 2020**; Gene died **August 2021** (MMSHOF).[^mms][^gar] ⚠ Garlits Museum bio still said Gene "resides in Michigan" at the time of that page's text; the MMSHOF update is the stronger death record.

Inducted MMSHOF **2010**; Garlits Museum / IDRHOF class of **2021**; NHRA Hot Rod Reunion honorees **2006**.[^mms][^gar]
