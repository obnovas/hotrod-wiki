# "A Show of Our Own": The Daytona Turkey Run (Daytona Beach, Fla., 1974– )

*A hot rod turned away from a holiday parade, a four-year-old in tears, and a Thanksgiving show that ended up in the Speedway's infield*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** High (the founding is told the same way by the founder to a local paper and by the event's own history; the sources differ on the son's name and age and on two dates) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> the founder in his own words to the *Hometown News* (Volusia County, 2026); the event's own history and its 2026 cancellation notice; *Motortopia* (2025); a Daytona Beach hotel's history blog (2025) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-25 (Round 50) · **Open issues:** ⚠ Jim at four or Tim at five; ⚠ Bellair Plaza in 1975 or 1976; ⚠ the spring show's count · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

## Turned away in Ormond Beach

What sets the Turkey Run apart is how it started. The event calls itself the largest combined car show and swap meet in the country, and it began because a parade said no to one hot rod and a small boy cried about it.[^tr][^htn26]

Stu Sarjeant and his son tried to enter their 1923 Ford T-bucket in the 1973 Ormond Beach AACA Gaslight Parade, which the event's history says was sponsored by the local Jaycees. They were turned away, "because hot rods were not permitted."[^tr] *Motortopia* adds that the T-bucket had a 289 Ford V8.[^mt25] The boy started crying, and he asked his father if they could have their own parade the next Thanksgiving.[^tr] Sarjeant told the *Hometown News* in 2026: "My four-year-old son started crying. And we decided right then and there, we would have a show of our own."[^htn26] The event's history and the hometown paper name the boy Jim, at four. *Motortopia* calls him Tim, at five.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^tr][^htn26][^mt25]

The next year, 1974, they did it. The first one, which the *Hometown News* says was billed as a "fun rally and rod run," drew 45 cars to a Howard Johnson hotel near Bellair Plaza in Daytona Beach.[^htn26][^tr] *Motortopia* says it was called the Turkey Rod Run back then.[^mt25] In 1975 Sarjeant and his friends Rick Finzer and Olin Hopes founded the Daytona Beach Street Rods to run it.[^tr] The show moved across the street into Bellair Plaza, added live bluegrass and started the charity raffles it still holds.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^mt25][^mx25] A Daytona Beach hotel's history of the show says the club had only five members at the time.[^mx25]

## Plenty of cars, and a place with space

It kept outgrowing its lots. In 1982 it moved to Seabreeze High School and added a swap meet.[^tr][^htn26] By 1987 it had more than 900 cars and moved to the Daytona Beach airport, in the Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University parking lot by the *Hometown News* account.[^tr][^htn26] Two years later it moved again.[^mx25]

The partner was a local public body. The Daytona Beach Racing and Recreational Facilities District was created in 1955 to fund and build a racetrack, and the International Speedway Corporation built the Speedway in 1959. The District has leased it the land ever since.[^tr] In 1989 the Street Rods and the District joined up, and the show moved into the Speedway's infield with more than 1,500 show cars and 250 swap-meet vendors.[^tr] "We had plenty of cars and needed space," Sarjeant said. "The District had space and needed cars. It was love at first sight!"[^htn26] It is still split that way. The club runs the show cars, and the District runs the swap meet, the car corral and the operations.[^htn26] The word "Rod" came out of the name once the show took in every kind of car, *Motortopia* says.[^mt25] In 1990 the organizers started a second show in March.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^mt25][^mx25]

Rick Finzer was its longtime event director. "For more than 50 years, he lived and breathed the Turkey Run," the *Hometown News* wrote.[^htn26] The event director now, Gregg Pellicer, has been a club member for 26 years. "When I first got into this, it was mostly the really old hot rods of the 1930s and '40s," he said in 2026. "Now it's evolved."[^htn26] The club counts more than 125 members, from 16 to 90 years old, who volunteer to put on the show.[^tr] The event says more than $23 million in proceeds has gone to charities and local programs over the years.[^tr] Sarjeant, 81 in 2026, said the club picks its charities itself. "We vet every charity," he said. "We know where it's going. We deliver the checks ourselves."[^htn26]

The 53rd fall show was set for November 25 through 29, 2026.[^htn26] On November 20 the organizers canceled it. Historic rainfall and flooding had made the grounds unsafe. Vendors' spaces moved to November 2027, and the raffle drawing was set for a livestream on November 28.[^trhome] Sarjeant's son, the boy who cried at the parade, is still part of the Turkey Run.[^htn26] It started as a show of their own, for a hot rod that wasn't allowed in.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Was the boy Jim, at four, or Tim, at five?** The event's own history says "his four-year-old son Jim," and the *Hometown News* quotes Sarjeant on his "four-year-old son" and names him Jim.[^tr][^htn26] A Daytona Beach hotel's history also says Jim, four.[^mx25] *Motortopia* says "his five-year-old son, Tim."[^mt25] Three accounts agree on Jim at four; we have not seen a period source. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did the show move into Bellair Plaza in 1975 or 1976?** *Motortopia* says the founders moved it across the street in 1975, the year the club formed.[^mt25] The hotel's history says "By 1976, the newly formed Daytona Beach Street Rods club, with only five members at the time, helped expand the event" and then describes the move.[^mx25] The event's own history does not date the move, and the *Hometown News* lists Bel Air Plaza as the first of the new homes without a year.[^tr][^htn26] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When was the first spring show?** *Motortopia* and the hotel's history say the spring show began in 1990; the hotel's history adds that it drew more than 800 cars that first year.[^mt25][^mx25] The *Hometown News* called the March 27–29, 2026, show the "36th Annual Spring Daytona Turkey Run."[^htn26] Counting from 1990, the 2026 show would be the 37th. A year without a spring show would square the two, but no source we have read says there was one. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
