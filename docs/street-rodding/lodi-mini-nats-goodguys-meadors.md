# Come Get Stuck in Lodi: Nor-Cal Early Iron, the Mini-Nats and How Goodguys Began (California, 1955–2015)

*A club's weekend at the fairgrounds, a women's spark plug contest one boy remembers, and a yellow Deuce that broke down on the way home*

## A club in Fremont

Goodguys, which now puts on shows across the country, did not start as a company. It started as a car club's weekend at a county fairgrounds in Lodi, California, in 1973, with the founder and his wife writing the entries down by hand. The family kept running it from their kitchen table long after it grew.[^aw][^st]

Gary Meadors grew up in Dinuba, a small town in the Central Valley. He built his first hot rod there in 1955, a '47 Plymouth, and slammed it to the ground right away; Goodguys' feature remembers the cut-off ends of the coil springs "glowing red on the garage floor."[^leg] He went into sales. On weekends he wrote for hot rod magazines as "Gary Goodguy."[^aw] In the early 1970s he and Larry Westervelt, an airline mechanic, lived down the street from each other in Newark, and both were among the first members of Nor-Cal Early Iron, a club Meadors helped start in neighboring Fremont for family hot rodders.[^fcw][^sema15][^aw]

## Lodi, 1973

In 1973 Meadors asked the National Street Rod Association for a regional event in central California. He got the approval and very little in the way of instructions.[^aw] Nor-Cal Early Iron hosted it at the Lodi Grape Festival Grounds as the first West Coast Street Rod Mini-Nats, with Meadors as event director.[^hamb][^sema15] "We picked Lodi over Monterey because the hotel rooms were cheaper," he said later, and he and his wife, Marilyn, hand-recorded all 566 entries, from six states.[^aw]

The people who were there remember it in small pieces. A club member's son, who posts on the H.A.M.B. as HEMI32 and was 11 that year, shared his father's slides in 2018: resto-rods and C-cabs, a Streetkhana competition, and a Women's Spark Plug Changing Contest. As he remembered it, "first lady to change all 8 plugs, jump-in, & start the car was the winner."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^hamb] Another man remembered riding in the box of a Model A pickup with his brother on a poker run, at 10. Another, remembering Lodi in those years, recalled the NSRA rep Jack Stewart, whose red Deuce roadster carried a small keg on the luggage rack lettered "Come get stuck in Lodi."[^hamb] At the 1976 event a visitor's automatic died at Micke Grove on Friday night; "One of the club members had rebuilt my trans for me on Sunday morning!"[^hamb]

The event kept going, and the director changed. HEMI32's list has the Northern California Street Rod Association hosting it from 1974. Meadors directed 1974. Chuck Corsello directed 1975, 1976 and 1977. The 1978 line names no director. Bob Cress directed in 1979.[^hamb] Meadors moved up in the NSRA. From 1978 he ran its Western Street Rod Nationals, mostly in Merced, with HEMI32's father as his "right-hand man," and in 1980 Gary and Marilyn were listed together as the NSRA's Western States event directors.[^hamb]

## A Deuce that broke down on the way home

In 1973 he also bought a '32 Ford Tudor sedan in Paradise, California: full fenders, metallic brown paint, wire wheels. "Naturally, it broke down on the way home," Goodguys' feature says, and *Hot Rod* in November 1987 says the same thing more briefly: he drove it home, and it broke down on the way.[^leg][^hr87] He sorted it out and drove it to the 1973 NSRA Nationals in Tulsa, where it won the "Rod Run" award, though the traction bars fell off on the Streetkhana course. In 1975 he rebuilt it in his garage and painted it Pearl Yellow himself, and in 1977 the family drove it 6,000 miles, to the Canadian Street Rod Nationals in Winnipeg and the Street Rod Nationals, which the feature places in St. Paul.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^leg]

## Goodguys

Then he went out on his own, and the year depends on who is talking. *Autoweek* says that in 1983 he quit his sales job and broke from the NSRA, because he wanted Friday-night vintage drag racing with the shows, and held his first official Goodguys event.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^aw] A Fuel Curve column says he launched the association in 1987.[^fc87] SEMA and *Street Trucks* give both dates: a founding, or a Get-Together at the Alameda County Fairgrounds in Pleasanton, in 1983, and a launch or an incorporation in 1987, when the Western Nationals became the Goodguys West Coast Nationals.[^sema15][^st] Other Fuel Curve pages say founded or incorporated in 1983 and a national series in 1987, or they say 1983 and stop there.[^fcpl][^fcsc][^fcmm][^leg] The first event's name is split the same way: "Goodguys Get Together" in the *Street Trucks* interview, and "the 1983 All-American Get-Together" in the sedan feature.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^st][^leg] A club member's son, posting his father's event list in 2018, has Meadors still the NSRA's Western Nationals director through the 1986 Pleasanton event.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^hamb] That list is color from a boy who was there, not another side of the argument. In the early years the staff was the family, Gary, Marilyn and their sons Marc and Marty, and by *Street Trucks*' account they ran it "right from their kitchen table," taking out several loans against their home in Alamo to build it.[^st] The *Goodguys Gazette* started in 1989 as a bimonthly 40-page black-and-white paper.[^st]

The Deuce got one more rebuild, in 1985: a new chassis by Dick Magoo, a chopped highboy body by Sam Foose and his son Chip, a B&M blower on a fresh 350 small-block.[^leg] It became the Goodguys logo, as drawn by Thom Taylor, and has been since 1987.[^sema15] Why "Goodguys"? He told *Autoweek* it was because they were all good guys.[^aw] Larry Westervelt came back years later, after he retired from the airlines, and ran Goodguys' vintage drag races.[^fcw]

Gary Meadors died December 27, 2015, at his home in Cave Creek, Arizona.[^aw] His son Marc, by then running Goodguys, told that same feature the yellow sedan had covered at least 200,000 miles.[^leg] It is still the car on the logo: the one that broke down on the way home from Paradise, in the year a Fremont club put on its first weekend in Lodi.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did Goodguys really start: 1983 or 1987?** *Autoweek* (2015): "In 1983, Meadors quit his sales job and broke away from the NSRA to hold his first official Goodguys event."[^aw] Fuel Curve is not one year. "Fathers Know Best" (2019) says that in 1987 he launched the Goodguys Rod & Custom Association.[^fc87] The Pleasanton gallery (2017) says Goodguys was officially founded in 1983 and did not start full rod-and-custom festivals until the spring of 1987.[^fcpl] The 2018 scrapbook says Goodguys "was incorporated in 1983 then began their national series of events in 1987."[^fcsc] Marilyn Meadors's 2019 interview says he founded Goodguys in 1983,[^fcmm] and the 2023 sedan feature says "In 1983, Meadors set off on his own and formed Goodguys," with more than 600 cars at the first event, and that the restyled sedan debuted at the "1986 Goodguys West Coast Nationals in Pleasanton."[^leg] A 2020 Fuel Curve column says he "decided to leave NSRA in 1986 and launch Goodguys."[^fcloy] SEMA (2015) gives both: "Meadors founded Goodguys in 1983 in Northern California before branching out to stage a national series of hot-rod and custom-car events beginning in 1987," and Gary and Marilyn "quit their day jobs and launched Goodguys as their own association in 1987."[^sema15] *Street Trucks* gives both: a 1983 "Goodguys Get Together" at Pleasanton, then "started Goodguys in 1983 and incorporated in 1987," and John Drummond's answer that 1987 "was the first full year of the Goodguys show series."[^st] The scrapbook's "incorporated in 1983" and *Street Trucks*' incorporation in 1987 are not the same sentence. HEMI32's 2018 list is supporting color from a club member's son, not an independent side: he has Meadors as NSRA Western Nationals director through Pleasanton in 1986, and the first Goodguys West Coast Nationals in 1987 after Meadors "had left NSRA."[^hamb] The through-1986 detail is q-5. One reading that fits SEMA, *Street Trucks* and several Fuel Curve pages is a first event in 1983 and the association's own launch in 1987. *Autoweek*'s "broke away from the NSRA" in 1983 does not fit the son's list, and the son's list is not a second witness. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **What was the first event called?** "Goodguys Get Together" (*Street Trucks*' interview) or "the 1983 All-American Get-Together in Pleasanton" (the sedan feature, read on Fuel Curve).[^st][^leg] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **The spark plug contest?** HEMI32's photos show a Women's Spark Plug Changing Contest at Lodi in 1973; the rules come from his own memory ("IIRC").[^hamb] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Where were the 1977 Nationals?** Only the Goodguys sedan feature puts the family's 1977 trip at "the Street Rod Nationals in St. Paul, Minnesota." We have not checked it against an NSRA list of host cities.[^leg] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Was Meadors still the NSRA's director through 1986?** Only HEMI32's January 17, 2018 list says so. He was 11 at the 1973 Lodi event, and he writes that his father worked the events with Meadors. The list has Gary Meadors as NSRA Western Nationals event director at Pleasanton in 1986, names no director for the NSRA's 1987 Bakersfield event, and says Meadors had left the NSRA by the first Goodguys West Coast Nationals in Pleasanton in 1987. It is one club member's son's list, used as color beside the obituaries, not as its own side of q-1.[^hamb] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
