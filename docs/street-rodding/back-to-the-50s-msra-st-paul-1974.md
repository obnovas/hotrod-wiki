# A Tune-Up for the Nats: How Back to the 50's Began (St. Paul, Minnesota, 1974)

*A shopping-center lot, a cruise down University Avenue, and a theater crowd that walked out of the movie into the real thing*

## The club that had to host the Nationals

Back to the 50's, one of the biggest car shows of its kind anywhere, was never planned as a show. It began as practice. In 1974 the Minnesota Street Rod Association was about to host the Street Rod Nationals and was nervous about it, so it tried a Friday night out first, and the night was such fun that it never stopped.[^hh50][^lc]

The association itself began with talk. In 1967 car clubs from St. Paul and Minneapolis met at the Leaning Tower of Pizza to hear an idea from Jerry Johnson, who became member No. 1: why not bring the best of Minnesota street rodding together under one umbrella?[^hh50] At one of the first meetings there, Kenny Venberg of Mankato doodled a circle design on a napkin, and it became the club's first logo in 1968. "It was suppose to be maroon and gold, but turned out more red and yellow," he said.[^msra] That year Terry Nystrom was serving in Vietnam when a letter came from his mother with a newspaper ad for the new club. He wrote right back telling her to send the $5 membership fee immediately.[^cc18]

By 1970 the MSRA was helping hold the first nationwide Street Rod Nationals, at Peoria ([The NSRA and the early Nationals](nsra-early-nationals.md)).[^hh50] In 1971 its members built a C-cab truck and gave it away at the Nationals in Memphis, and the club's new logo that year, its first to go on a windshield, made a point of it.[^msra][^cc18] Then the NSRA chose St. Paul for the 5th Nationals in 1974, at the State Fairgrounds. The MSRA was "a little nervous" about hosting something that big, a member wrote later, and the board suggested a test run the month before.[^hh50] "It was all new to us," the 2012 look-back says.[^lc]

## Friday night, June 21, 1974

Gene Sonnen and his girlfriend, Red, get the credit. Sonnen wanted to show her what cruising had been like in the '50s. The idea came up at a membership meeting, and the club decided to give it a whirl, as a tune-up for the Nats and a time to have some fun. The date was Friday night, June 21, 1974.[^lc]

They gathered in the Midway Shopping Center lot on University Avenue, the same avenue the Twin Cities had cruised for years ([Lake Street and University Avenue](../places/lake-street-university.md)).<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> Jerry Johnson wrote it up for the club magazine, the *LineChaser*, the next month. Some members dressed in '50s styles, and more than 2,500 handbills for the Nationals went to the people who came to look.[^lc] Johnson sent the cars out of the lot in groups of two or three to control traffic, and they "overwhelmed the 'Avenue' from every direction." People sat in lawn chairs on the curbs, young people stood on the center island to get closer, and many of the rods made the traditional pass through Porky's Drive In.[^lc] The Midway Commerce and Civic Association sponsored the night with the club.[^lc]

Then came the part the members still tell. *American Graffiti* was playing at the Grandview Theater, at Grand and Fairview. The club split into four groups and came at the theater from different directions, timed for when the film let out at 9:27 p.m. Members on the sidewalk handed out handbills to "the totally unsuspecting theater patrons," who walked out of a movie about cruising into the real thing.[^lc] One couple just stood there staring, Johnson wrote. One man pointed at Sterling Ashby's custom and said, "Look at the 'Pharaohs' Merc'." A note in the reposted report adds that the car was built by Bailon Customs in Northern California in 1956 for Sonny Morris ([Joe Bailon](../people/joe-bailon-candy-apple.md)).[^lc] Afterward the rods went back to University Avenue, or into Porky's for Coke and onion rings or a "Twinburger," or over to Minneapolis's Lake Street "to give them a little taste of the evening."[^lc]

One member had cut it close. Gary Magner was working in Los Angeles the week before and had ordered chrome Tru Spoke wire wheels for his '34 Ford. He picked them up south of LAX on Thursday, tied two boxes into one, stuffed his laundry in around the wheels, and checked all four as luggage on Western Airlines at no charge. On Friday morning he was at the tire dealer, and that night he drove into the Midway lot on what he thinks was the first set of chrome Tru Spoke wire wheels in Minnesota.[^lc]

## From a parking lot to the Fairgrounds

Johnson called the night "probably the best event that has happened to the Association since it was formed back in '67," and members were already asking to do it again.[^lc] They did. By 1976 the club had tried several venues and activities, including some heads-up drag racing at Twin City Speedway, cruise parades from the fairgrounds into St. Paul, and a dance at Brook's Dance Hall in Inver Grove Heights. The old Midway lot couldn't hold the cars anymore, and they spilled into the neighboring streets.[^cc18] The event moved to the Minnesota State Fairgrounds.[^cc18]

By 2017 the MSRA counted 14,000 active members, and Back to the 50's averaged 12,000 pre-1965 cars, staffed entirely by volunteers.[^hh50] In 2018 a longtime member put the volunteer count at over 2,000. Nystrom, home from Vietnam by the first one, still had the 1938 Ford sedan he called Sunkist.[^cc18] The year rule stuck too: no 1965 or newer cars, because "we simply have maxed out the entire fairgrounds."[^hh50]

The show that fills a state fairgrounds every June started with a club that needed practice, a man who wanted to show his girlfriend the '50s, and a theater crowd that got the real thing on the sidewalk.

## Open questions & disputed details

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How many cars came the first night?** Johnson's July 1974 *LineChaser* report counted "More than 250 cars" during the one-hour parking-lot show. The members' 2012 look-back says "One hundred fifty cars showed up," and the MSRA's history page and ClassicCars.com say about 125–150.[^lc][^msra][^cc18] The 1974 count is the one written closest to the night. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
