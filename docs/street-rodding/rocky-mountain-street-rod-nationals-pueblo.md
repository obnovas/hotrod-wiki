# "Wave to the Rodders": The Rocky Mountain Street Rod Nationals (Pueblo, Colo., 1984 or 1985– )

*A national association's regional meet, a steel town that rolled out the welcome, and a mechanic who went home and built a car*

!!! abstract "Article record"

## A meet the town adopted

Plenty of towns host a car show. What stands out in Pueblo is how hard the town worked at it. The Rocky Mountain Street Rod Nationals belonged to a national association, but for decades the local chamber of commerce threw the kickoff party, sponsored the parade and gave its own award, and the local paper used its editorial page to tell readers to wave.[^pc99][^sm16][^pc93]

The National Street Rod Association decided to bring a regional meet to Pueblo in the mid-1980s.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^sm16][^pc06] Roger Jetter, writing in 2016, guessed that the Colorado State Fairgrounds staff did not quite know what to make of the request when they were told there might be 500 to 700 cars. "The actual count in 1985 was 792 entries, exceeding all expectations," he wrote.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^sm16] The meet has been at the fairgrounds ever since.[^pc06]

Dave Baca was in the crowd at the first one. He was a Pueblo auto mechanic who had fallen for cars at 8. As he told it, his father, working on a 1948 Crosley, told him to take the motor across the street and take it apart, and he took it apart and rebuilt it.[^pc08] He went home from the street rod show with a plan. A friend gave him a pile of parts for a 1939 Chevrolet, and he built it into a rose-colored coupe. "I worked night and day and I finally had it ready to show in 1989," he said. "It's not the greatest but it's something I built all by myself."[^pc08]

## The fourth weekend of June

By 1993, which the *Chieftain* called the ninth year, the meet drew 1,555 cars and close to 10,000 people, according to Pat Newell, the NSRA's special events director. Cars came from as far away as New Bern, North Carolina. The awards show who was in the room. The Pueblo Chamber of Commerce chose "The Pueblo Pick," a 1932 Ford from Hobbs, New Mexico. Pueblo police gave an award for appearance and safety to a 1934 Ford from Montrose. The host club, Colorado Springs Rods and Customs, chose its own pick, and Gayle Dorschner of Englewood won the Lady Rodders Pick in her 1933 Plymouth.[^pc93]

The *Chieftain* welcomed the rodders in print. In 1999, the 15th straight year in Pueblo, its editorial quoted the NSRA's own website: "No city hosting an NSRA event does more to welcome the rodders than does Pueblo, Colorado." The NSRA praised the chamber's lakeside kickoff party, and the paper pointed out that the association held national events in 10 other cities. The meet, it said, had grown from a start of 500 or 600 to an expected 2,000 or more, and the chamber's president, Rod Slyhoff, put its value to the town at $4 million. That morning about 100 street rods would parade down Elizabeth and through the Union Avenue Historic District to the fairgrounds. "Wave to the rodders as you see them driving around the area," the paper told its readers.[^pc99] In 2006 the paper called it "a big event that people look forward to every year in Pueblo," with nearly 2,100 pre-1949 cars expected.[^pc06]

Baca stayed with it. In 2008, at 66 and retired after 55 years as a mechanic, he showed his coupe and a 1948 Ford pickup he had cut down into a roadster, and he worked his 12th year as one of the meet's safety inspectors. "We check the tires and the brakes, do a 23-point inspection to make sure the vehicles are safe to drive," he said. The parade that year left from the North Side Kmart.[^pc08]

The meet changed with the hobby. In 2010 the NSRA opened it to cars newer than 1948 on a 30-year rolling rule, and that year set the record, 2,194 entries. In 2016 there were 2,089. The chamber still hosted the Thursday barbecue at Lake Pueblo State Park and sponsored the 100-car parade. That year the "Streets of Rods" display was moved out from behind the fairgrounds' "Sheep, Swine and Goat" building, where Jetter thought it had seemed almost hidden, to an exhibition building closer to the crowds.[^sm16]

Colorado builders took turns in the spotlight. In 2023, the 38th meet, hosted with the chamber and Visit Pueblo, the featured builder was Kevin Zerby of Kevin's Hot Rod Service in Cañon City.[^koaa23] In 2024 it was Marshall Sanders of Hot Rods by Marsh in Woodland Park.[^nsra24] The parade still rolled in. "It's fun to do the parade for the community, so all the kids and parents can come out and see," Rodney Leffler told KOAA that June. "We try to throw candy out for the kids on the street as we're going to the fairgrounds."[^koaa24] The meet came from a national association, but the welcome came from Pueblo.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Was the first meet in 1984 or 1985?** The *Chieftain*'s 2006 editorial says the meet has been held at the fairgrounds "since the event's inception in 1984," and Dave Baca told the paper in 2008 that he attended "the inaugural event in Pueblo in 1984."[^pc06][^pc08] Roger Jetter wrote in 2016 that the NSRA decided to host the event "Thirty-two years ago," but gave the first count as "The actual count in 1985."[^sm16] The numbering points to 1985: the *Chieftain* called 1993 the 9th, 1999 the "15th consecutive year," 2006 the 22nd and 2008 the 24th, and *Street Muscle* called 2016 the 32nd.[^pc93][^pc99][^pc06][^pc08][^sm16] (The numbering later slips by a year, with the 38th in 2023 and the 39th in 2024, so at least one year seems to have been skipped.)[^koaa23][^nsra24] The front-matter `years` span is `[1984, 2024]`. That field is `[first, last]`, two integers, and 1984 is the earlier side of the dispute, the earlier year a source gives, so the span covers 1985. It does not decide the question. The title says 1984 or 1985. A June 1984 or 1985 *Chieftain* would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How big was the first meet?** The *Chieftain* wrote in 1999 that the meet started "with 500-600."[^pc99] Jetter gave "792 entries" in 1985 and said the fairgrounds had been told to expect 500 to 700.[^sm16] The difference may be the first-year question again, or a forecast against a count. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
