# Beast III: Three Days Without Sleep

*Chet Herbert's streamliner, 1952*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** secondary, auction/collection · **Last verified:** 2026-10-12 (Round 2) · **Open issues:** Some details single-sourced · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

*Kustomrama compiles this from* Merchants of Speed*, Sports Car Digest, and* Rod & Custom.[^k]

The body arrived after the 1952 Nationals had started. Volunteers assembled the car at **Chrisman & Sons Garage**, Compton. Herbert stayed up three or four days and finally had to be belted into his wheelchair so he wouldn't fall out.[^k]

## Drawn in a wind tunnel

Herbert teamed with Caltech wind-tunnel consultant **Rod Schapel**, who had never seen a streamliner or Bonneville. A 1/10-scale aluminum model was wind-tunnel tested, and the fiberglass body was laid up over a male mold lofted in Schapel's backyard. The mold was cut up afterward, so the body is one of a kind.[^k]

## Borrowed power

The car had a partial chassis from Paul Sylvia of Inglewood, 108-in. wheelbase. A planned aluminum Franklin six blew up on the dyno, so Dana Fuller loaned the 1951 Chrysler Hemi from his new Imperial, which happened to be in Herbert's shop for a roller cam. It ran Hilborn-Travers injection and a Vertex magneto.[^k] Herbert, in his wheelchair, was on the cover of Hot Rod in December 1952.[^k]

## The runs

Entered as the "Herbert Cams Special," #666, D/Streamliner, it was push-started by a Chevy coupe and Howard Johansen's Model A in tandem, to 130 mph.[^k]

George Bentley ran 182, 197 and 211 mph, then missed a shift. When he didn't show the next morning, **Art Chrisman** took over and ran 219, 224, 230, and a **235.991 mph** record (238.095 best one-way), making it the fastest single-engine car in America. Chrisman became the 10th member of the 200 MPH Club.[^k]

## The long afterlife

Dana Fuller set diesel records with it in 1953. It was retired in 1955, bought by Ed Hegarty in 2002, unearthed in 2009 by Dave Crouse and Mark Brinker, took second in class at Pebble Beach in 2010, and was donated to the NHRA Museum (exhibit opened 2014).[^k]

**Correction from Round 1:** the 235.991 mph figure is now confirmed by reading the page, and the driver was **Art Chrisman**, not Herbert.
