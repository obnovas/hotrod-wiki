**Owner:** Robert "Bob" Hirohata, Los Angeles. **Builder:** Sam & George Barris, Barris Kustom. **Base:** 1951 Mercury coupe, bought barely used from an older couple.[^revs]

## The build
- Roof chopped about 4 in. in front and 7 in. at the rear, with the B-pillar removed for a hardtop look (tried earlier on Nick Matranga's '39 Merc).[^revs]
- Frenched headlights, '52 Lincoln taillight lenses, peaked and extended hood, handmade grille, flared skirts, and scoops with '52 Chevy grille teeth.[^revs]
- '53 Buick side spears dividing ice green over organic green metallic.[^revs]
- About 5 in. ground clearance: de-arched leafs, lowering blocks, and a C'd rear frame.[^revs]
- Interior by Glen Houser's **Carson Top Shop**, trunk by **Gaylord**, dash striping later by **Von Dutch**. Hirohata made the teardrop knobs himself.[^revs]
- A 1953 Cadillac V8 installed by Dick Lyon (Lyon Engineering) for the drive to Indianapolis.[^revs]

**⚠ Conflict on build time:** Ken Gross (Revs Institute) writes **97 days**;[^revs] a Hagerty video title says **14 days**.[^hag]

Cost was "about $3,500" per George Barris. Hirohata: "I had to sell everything I owned and put my great aunt in hock to pay for the car."[^revs] He won his class at the 1952 Motorama, drove it to Indianapolis in 1953 and took Best Custom there, then advertised it in Hot Rod (May 1955) for $4,900.[^revs]

## Afterlife
Jim McNeil bought it in **1959 for $500** from a used-car lot and kept it for decades. When he took the dash apart he found Hirohata's and Barris's business cards wedged behind the radio speaker: "I never touched them... I wanted to feel their presence."[^revs] Pat Ganahl at Rod & Custom pushed the restoration with original crew (Barris, Frank Sonzogni, Junior Conway). It won its class at Pebble Beach in 2015 and was displayed on the National Mall by the HVA in 2017.[^revs] Bob Hirohata was murdered on May 14, 1981, in Temple City, and the case was never solved.[^revs] ◐
