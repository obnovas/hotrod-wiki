# 1960s: The Drag Rags and the Independent Press

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** secondary (season series), period press via excerpts · **Last verified:** 2026-10-13 (Round 3) · **Open issues:** Some excerpts only · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

Dave Wallace argues that cheap independent tabloids, the "drag rags" controlled neither by a sanctioning body nor by NHRA-leaning Petersen Publishing (where Wally Parks was editorial director until 1963), "recorded the truest accounts to be found" of drag racing's growth.[^cp1]

## Timeline
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1955 | *Drag News* founded (Mar 4) by Dean Brown and Dick McMullen.[^cp1] |
| 1957 | Emery Cook, Cliff Bedwell and Bruce Crower's runs past 160 mph at Lions set off the 1957–63 fuel ban. Drag News, Feb 9, 1957.[^cp1] |
| 1959 | Doris Herbert buys Drag News (April). Raulston's *Drag Racer* runs seven issues.[^cp1] |
| 1960 | Standard 1320 Record List. Garlits credited with 182.18 mph.[^cp1] |
| 1963 | *Drag Sport Illustrated* founded.[^cp65] |
| 1965 | *Drag World* launches (March). Three national weeklies are published from LA at once.[^cp65] |
| 1966 | Drag Sport Illustrated shuts down; Drag World is sold to the AHRA.[^cp67] |
| 1967 | Only Drag News and *Drag Digest* ("the Drag Racer's New Testament") remain; Digest's last archived issues are dated June 1967.[^cp67] |
| 1967 | Orange County International Raceway opens (summer).[^cp67] |

## Breadcrumbs from the rags
- Isky believed a tiny ad in the September 1958 Hot Rod set off the **Gasser Wars**: Howard Johansen's ad on Doug Cook's defection. See [Camgrinder Wars](1950s-camgrinder-wars.md).[^cp1]
- Bob Muravez raced as "Floyd Lippencott Jr.," and the press kept his real name and face out of print for years.[^cp1]
- Pete Millar's *Drag Cartoons* and his National Association For The Advancement Of Flatheads.[^cp1]
- By 1967 Funny Cars were drawing a new generation of fans, while supercharged gassers still held the doorslammer crowd.[^cp67]
- OCIR (1967–83), designed and managed by Mike Jones, is credited with stadium seating, electronic scoreboards and the Manufacturers Meet. The Irvine Company did not renew the lease after 1983.[^cp67]

The Wallace Family Archives offers duplicate issues of many drag rags, per Competition Plus.[^cp67] They are a potential primary source for future rounds.
