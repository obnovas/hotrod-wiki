# 1950s: Santa Ana and the First Commercial Drag Strip

## Routed by bayonets
In the late 1940s Orange County hot rodders raced on abandoned wartime airstrips such as the Navy's **Mile Square**. C.J. "Pappy" Hart recalled: "We used to run off seven, eight abreast... With that many cars running, you'd have to beat somebody!" One Sunday they were run off by a company of U.S. Marines with fixed bayonets.[^mshf]

## The Santa Ana Drags, June 19, 1950
The Orange County Airport manager (today John Wayne Airport) offered an unused strip if Hart paid the airport 10% of proceeds and got insurance. With less than $1,000, Hart, his wife **Peggy**, and two backers opened on **June 19, 1950**; admission was 50 cents, race or watch.[^mshf] Other accounts name the backers as Creighton Hunter and Frank Stillwell.[^search] ◐ *Backer names not yet confirmed from a fetched primary page.*

Hart was emphatic: "I didn't invent drag racing... When I was growing up in Ohio, every stoplight, every night was a race."[^mshf]

## How the rules were invented
- **Quarter mile:** borrowed from horse racing, because "there was a lot of talk in those days that quarter horses were faster than cars," and the public could relate to it.[^mshf]
- **Timing:** stopwatch first, then homemade electric timers.[^mshf]
- **Classes:** stockers split by axle ratio, model year and displacement; "hot-car" classes added when lakes racers showed up, along with the first cash awards. The "Dollar-A-Mile" program paid Top Eliminator $1 per mph.[^mshf]

The strip ran until Hart lost the airport lease in 1959; Hart later managed **Lions Drag Strip (1965–71)**.[^mshf] Chet Herbert's stripped Harley "The Beast" ran 121 and then 129 mph at Santa Ana in 1950, beating every bike and car there.[^herbert]
