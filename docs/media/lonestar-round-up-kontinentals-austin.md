# "Closer to Texas": The Lonestar Round Up and the Kontinentals (Austin, Texas, 2002– )

*A car club named for a music club, a show started so Texans would not have to drive to California, and a rule that the cars be 1963 or older*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** High (the local paper, public radio, the student paper and the show's own pages agree on the moves and the growth; the first year's size differs by source) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> *The Austin Chronicle* (2004 photo essay on the club; 2014 profile of Steve Wertheimer); KUT, Austin's public radio station (2014); *The Daily Texan* (2018), with Wertheimer, Charlie Runnels and Rudy Morales; the show's own About and press pages; *Hot Rod* (Joe Hoppe, 2017) and Hemmings (2022) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-26 (Round 51) · **Open issues:** ⚠ how many cars came the first year; ? what came before the stadium show; ? the show's numbering · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

    — Steve Wertheimer to *The Daily Texan*, April 3, 2018.[^dt18]

## A club named for a club

The Lonestar Round Up is a car show that grew out of a music club. Its hosts, the Kontinentals, are named for the Continental Club on South Congress Avenue in Austin, and they use it as their clubhouse.[^hr17] Their idea was simple: Texas should have a good traditional hot rod and custom show of its own, so its builders would not have to drive to California for one.[^dt18]

Steve Wertheimer took over the Continental Club at the end of 1987 and opened it on New Year's Eve.[^acc14] He was also in a group of car people who drove out to California for the shows. "We thought it would be nice to have some good shows closer to Texas," he told *The Daily Texan* in 2018. He started the Round Up with two friends.[^dt18] Hemmings, whose writers counted the club's members as friends, described its members as "graphic designers and show promotors, club owners and artists."[^hem22] *The Austin Chronicle* said the Round Up began as a simple "show & shine" in front of the Continental Club.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>[^ac04]

The first Round Up as a show was held in April 2002, on a football field in central Austin, House Park Stadium.[^hr17][^kut14][^lsru] There was one rule. The cars had to be American and built in 1963 or earlier. Joe Hoppe wrote in *Hot Rod* that "All 88 fit on the field handily," though big tires and turf did not always get along.[^hr17] Wertheimer has put the first show at 80 cars, and a crowd that was mostly friends.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^kut14][^dt18] Hemmings remembered "a hundred cars and a rockabilly wedding on the makeshift stage between band sets."[^hem22]

## Driven, not trailered

The show moved to the grass at Fiesta Gardens in East Austin for its second and third years. Jimmie Vaughan's 1963 Riviera had a place of honor.[^hr17] By the third year, the *Chronicle* wrote, the Round Up drew people from California, Kansas and Michigan, and it had an attitude: "don't trailer your ride to this joint."[^ac04] The paper's photo essay on the club that spring is full of small proofs of it. Will Muntz drove to De Leon, Texas, in the middle of the night to pick up an oil pump for the Dahlia, Steve Wertheimer's Jim Bruns–built custom, so the club could leave together for Paso Robles, California, at 8 a.m.[^ac04][^hr17] Mercury Charlie wanted to drive to the 2003 show in his own car, so he bought the body of a 1926 Ford T coupe 30 days before it. "Four weeks and no sleep later," the *Chronicle* wrote, he arrived, grinning, and fell asleep.[^ac04] He is Charlie Runnels, and *The Daily Texan* wrote that he has taken part in the Round Up since it began.[^dt18]

The club's cars were their own advertisement. Vaughan's customs were built by Gary Howard, and his Riviera won a Bradley Award. The *Chronicle* credited the abalone flake on Mike Young's 1960 Impala, another Howard car, with reviving metal flake in the custom world.[^ac04] For the fourth through seventh shows the Round Up took over all of Festival Beach. The Kontinentals painted almost all their cars semi-gloss black with red wheels, and people who drove in from far away left the road grime on.[^hr17]

In 2008 the show moved to the Travis County Expo Center, home of the Austin rodeo.[^hr17] KUT counted it in 2014: the 13th Round Up, April 4 and 5, with Wertheimer saying it had grown from 80 cars to "in excess of 1,500."[^kut14] *Hot Rod* counted more than 1,800 in 2017.[^hr17] In April 2018 Wertheimer told the student paper that the most recent show had 2,000 cars and a crowd of thousands from around the world.[^dt18] By then it was a whole week. A barbecue in Taylor opened it on Thursday night. A Friday garage crawl ended at Dell Children's Hospital, where the kids got gifts and a close look at the cars. On Friday night the Round Up cars and the locals cruised Congress Avenue.[^hr17]

The music stayed. "Most car shows are limited to cars, but with my background in the music scene I wanted to add that element in as well," Wertheimer said.[^dt18] Local shops came too. Rudy Morales of Rex Rod and Chassis said his shop set up a booth every year to show its traditional hot rods and motors.[^dt18] The 24th Round Up<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> is set for April 16 and 17, 2027, at the Expo Center, still for 1963-and-earlier cars, "driven in by builders and fans from across the country and around the world," the show says.[^lsrupr] It started with a few friends who were tired of driving to California, and now people drive to Austin.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How many cars came to the first show?** Joe Hoppe wrote "All 88 fit on the field handily."[^hr17] Steve Wertheimer told KUT in 2014, "Eighty cars were at the first show back at House Park Stadium," and told *The Daily Texan* in 2018 that there were 80.[^kut14][^dt18] Hemmings wrote of "a hundred cars."[^hem22] A 2002 program, entry list or *Austin American-Statesman* report would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **What came before the stadium show?** *The Austin Chronicle* wrote in 2004 that "what started as a simple 'show & shine' in front of the Continental Club on South Congress has turned into the Lonestar Round Up."[^ac04] The show's own page and the other accounts start the story at the football stadium in 2002.[^lsru][^hr17] Whether the show-and-shine was an earlier event, and when, is still open. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Why is the 2027 show the 24th?** KUT called the April 2014 show the 13th, which fits a first show in 2002.[^kut14] The show calls the April 2027 show the 24th.[^lsrupr] That count suggests years without a show, but no source we read says which years. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
