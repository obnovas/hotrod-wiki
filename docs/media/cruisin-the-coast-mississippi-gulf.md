# The 2005 Shirts: Cruisin' the Coast (Mississippi Gulf Coast, 1996– )

*An October car show borrowed from Reno to fill hotel rooms, the one year a hurricane took it, and the shirts they wore the year after*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** High (the founding, the counts and the hurricane years come from the coast's own papers and TV station, told year by year since 1996; the sources differ on the first show's length and one hiring date) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> the *Sun Herald* (Biloxi, 2016, 2017, 2021, 2024) and the *Clarion-Ledger*'s reprint of a *Sun Herald* story (2015); WLOX (Biloxi, 2006); the *Ocean Springs Weekly Record* (2026); the executive director to *Scenic 98 Coastal* (2023); the event's own page · **Last verified:** 2026-11-25 (Round 50) · **Open issues:** ⚠ three and a half days or four; ⚠ 1998 or 1999; ◐ the man from Reno · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

## A car show to fill the rooms

Cruisin' the Coast was not started by a car club. Hotel, casino and business people on the Mississippi coast started it in 1996 to fill rooms after Labor Day. Then the car people made it theirs, and the one year a hurricane took it away, they came anyway.[^sh16][^s98][^sh17]

Woody Bailey, whose family ran hotels on the coast, was one of about six to eight people in the first talks. "We were trying to figure out what we could do to extend the tourism season," he said.[^s98][^oswr26] They talked about hot air balloon festivals and boat shows. Then, Bailey told *Scenic 98 Coastal*, a casino executive told them about Hot August Nights, the car show he had seen while working in Reno.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^s98] "We all decided, 'Okay, let's try a car show,'" Bailey recalled. "We came up with the name Cruisin' The Coast."[^oswr26] The casino operators helped underwrite it, and a firm, George McMillan and Associates, ran it the first years.[^oswr26][^s98] The chairman, Chevis Swetman, said in 1996 that it was "going to expand the tourist season too, because September and October are usually pretty thin."[^sh16b]

That first year 374 cars registered, "mostly from the Gulf South," as Bailey put it in 2021. The *Sun Herald* puts the show at three and a half days, and other accounts say four.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^sh16][^sh21][^oswr26] The cars were spread out over three venues, Casino Magic in Bay St. Louis, Rice Pavilion in Gulfport and Point Cadet in Biloxi, to spread out the traffic too.[^sh16b] The *Sun Herald* later wrote that the first event "almost was the last."[^sh21] The next year 997 cars came. In 1998 the show went on the weekend after Hurricane Georges, with 1,854 cars.[^sh24] Gene Oswalt came over from the McMillan firm as executive director and ran it until he died in 2008, and Bailey took over.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^s98][^oswr26] In 2000 the *Sun Herald* started a column called "The Masked Cruiser" to feature cars during the week.[^sh24] It grew to eight days in 2002.[^sh24]

The habits that make it a cruise and not a parking lot came early and stayed. Cars get blessed in Diamondhead and pulled over for a "Cruisin' Citation" in D'Iberville. By late afternoon U.S. 90, Beach Boulevard, is lined with people taking pictures as the cars go by and the sun goes down over the water.[^sh16b][^sh16] The *Sun Herald*'s photographers kept the small scenes. In 2003 twelve-year-old Amy Ardis of Mobile read a novel at Jones Park on the last day. She came every year with her father, who owned a 1963 Impala Super Sport. In 2017 John Mitchell of Diamondhead watched from an easy chair in the bed of his 1978 Chevy pickup. It had belonged to his father, and he had rescued it from the weeds.[^sh24]

## Katrina's year

In 2005 Hurricane Katrina canceled the show. 4,107 cars had already registered. The Cruisin' office was destroyed, and many of its records were lost with it.[^sh24][^oswr26] Many longtime cruisers came anyway, to volunteer or to give money to the recovery.[^sh17] "People who usually came to South Mississippi with their cars instead came as volunteers, brought their tools and helped Coast residents rebuild," the paper wrote in 2016.[^sh16] Cruisers also came in RVs and rented parking spaces from owners whose homes and businesses along U.S. 90 were gone.[^cl15] "We didn't even have a highway to travel on," Bailey said later.[^s98]

The 2006 show was the tenth anniversary, and it went on with bridges unfinished and hotel rooms short.[^sh24] There was no kickoff parade in Long Beach. Becky Burdeshaw, the registration director, told WLOX she was a little nervous. "It's like giving a party and hoping everybody shows up," she said. The organizers did not reprint the merchandise. The shirts and the poster kept 2005's dates, and a logo on the sleeve added an American flag, the 2006 dates and the words "can't beat our cruisin spirit." "It gives everybody a chance to have some 05 stuff as well as 06 stuff," Burdeshaw said. Charly Miller of Gulfport was ready to cruise in his 1957 Ford Fairlane, painted yellow and brown, for the tenth time since 1996. "I'm glad to see it back personally," he said. "I enjoy working with the people, because we all have the same thing in common, we like cars."[^wlox06]

By 2023, twelve car clubs, ten from Mississippi, one from Mobile and one from Slidell, Louisiana, were supplying 600 of the show's 1,200 volunteers, Bailey said, with three full-time staff.[^s98] In 2025, 10,334 vehicles registered, from 42 states and four other countries, a record. Bailey still calls it their "little car show."[^oswr26] The 30th, in October 2026, had gasser racing at Gulfport Dragway on the schedule.[^oswr26] It began as a way to fill hotel rooms. The year it could not happen, the cruisers came to help, and the next year everybody wore 2005's shirts.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Was the first show three and a half days or four?** The *Sun Herald* said "a 3 1/2 -day festival" in 2016, and its 2024 timeline says the leaders "announced a 3 and a half day car show."[^sh16][^sh24] The same paper said in 2021 that "The first event, four days long" almost was the last, and the *Ocean Springs Weekly Record* (2026) says it "lasted four days."[^sh21][^oswr26] An announced three and a half days and a show that ran into a fourth would fit both, but no source says so. The 2024 timeline also puts the first year's crowd at 25,000 to 30,000, from the paper's own 1996 stories.[^sh24] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did Gene Oswalt start, 1998 or 1999?** Bailey told *Scenic 98 Coastal* the firm ran the show "for the first three years," and that Oswalt "did a great job from 1999 until he died in 2008."[^s98] The *Ocean Springs Weekly Record* says Oswalt "became executive director in late 1998."[^oswr26] The two also spell the firm differently: George McMillian and Associates (*Scenic 98*), George McMillan and Associates (*Weekly Record*). <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Who brought Hot August Nights to the table?** Only Bailey's account to *Scenic 98 Coastal* says one casino executive told the group about Hot August Nights from his time working in Reno; it does not name him.[^s98] The *Sun Herald* and the *Weekly Record* say only that the event was patterned after Hot August Nights and that its success "came up during those conversations."[^sh16][^oswr26] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
