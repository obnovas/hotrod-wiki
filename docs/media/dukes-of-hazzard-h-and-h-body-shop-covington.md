# "We Wreck 'Em Just as Fast as We Need 'Em": The Georgia General Lees and H&H Body Shop (Covington, Conyers, Oxford and Norcross, Ga., 1978–2006)

*Five episodes, a three-man body shop, and the orange Charger they could not believe anyone would paint orange*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (the shop's work is told by two of its men, decades later, to a Macon TV station and a fan news blog; the cars' fates come from a museum's own investigation, which a fan author disputes; the jump is in many accounts) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> Henry Holman to WGXA (Macon, 2017); Tony Kelley's talk at the 2007 Covington cruise, reported by BRBTV; BRBTV (2006); the Volo Auto Museum's account of its car, by Jim Suva (*Star Car Central*, 2020); a fan's filming-locations survey (*Horsepower Memories*, 2018); History.com · **Last verified:** 2026-11-24 (Round 49) · **Open issues:** ⚠ 81 or 82 feet; ◐ 76 cars in two months; ⚠ whether Lee 6 was a Warner car or a later replica · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "We thought, who in the world would paint a car orange, put a flag on it and call it General Lee?"

    — Tony Kelley of H&H Body Shop, remembering the first jump car's arrival in late 1978, at the Dukes cruise in Covington, October 2007, as reported by BRBTV.[^brb07]

## Three men in Norcross

*The Dukes of Hazzard* made a 1969 Dodge Charger called the General Lee famous.[^hist] Its first life, in Georgia, was kept going by three men in a Norcross body shop. They took a wrecked car and had it ready for the cameras the next day. And the one General Lee they finished but never got to use is, by a museum's account, the only Georgia car still around.

Warner Bros. built three General Lees in California and sent them to Georgia in November 1978. The first five episodes were shot there, in November and December.[^scc20] Most of it was filmed that winter in and around Covington, Conyers and Social Circle.[^hpm18] Don Schisler was hired as transportation coordinator, and H&H, a body shop owned by Henry Holman, kept the cars running.[^scc20] The shop was in Norcross. "We were in the right place at the right time," Holman said.[^wgxa17]

Tony Kelley was 18 and worked at H&H. "When our first General Lee hit the yard, we'd been waiting on it for a week," he told a Covington crowd in 2007. "That was the first jump car." His job was to take a wrecked car and turn it, overnight, into a clean one ready to film the next day. "There was only three of us in the body shop," he said. People came by in the afternoons just to see what wreck was coming in. One day a stuntman stopped by for gear he had left in a Charger. "He said, 'I'm glad to see there's more than three of you'all, because we wreck 'em just as fast as we need 'em.'"[^brb07]

Holman did most of the paint and Danny Hobbs did the bodywork, and Kelley went between them. The dark blue and red of the flag on the roof took a long time to dry, and sometimes it was still wet when the crew came for the car in the morning. Kelley slept in a corner now and then, or in a car. Boss Hogg's Cadillac was really a gold car, and they had to paint it white. Kelley went to sleep in it and woke up outside the building, not sure where he was. "We were bad about wearing masks," he said.[^brb07] Holman put the shop's General Lee work at six cars for Warner Bros. The shop furnished the show's other cars too: 76 cars in two months, he said.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> "It was just three of us," he said. "We probably drank too much beer and stayed awake too much but we did it."[^wgxa17]

The shop put its own name on one car before sending it back. The production people took it off before filming. Another time Kelley, Holman and the cameraman Doug Smith were driving a Hazzard County sheriff's car back to Conyers on the back roads, out Beaver Ruin Road from Norcross, when real police pulled them over, with a helicopter overhead. Someone had been driving around Metro Atlanta in a fake police car pulling women over. It took an hour and a half, and calls to the Holiday Inn in Conyers where the cast and crew were staying, to sort it out.[^brb07]

## The jump at Oxford

The jump everyone remembers was filmed on Saturday, November 11, 1978, on the campus of Oxford College. A stuntman ran the first General Lee off a dirt ramp and over a police car, 16 feet up.[^hist] The crew shot it on a Saturday to avoid a crowd of students.[^hpm18] The landing totaled the car.[^hist] The jump measured 82 feet by History.com's count and 81 by a fan's survey of the locations.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^hist][^hpm18] It went into the opening credits, and the first episode aired January 26, 1979.[^hpm18] In Covington itself another jump went over the railroad tracks near the town square, where a rail trail runs now.[^hpm18]

After the fifth episode production broke for Christmas, and filming was to go on in January. Warner Bros. moved the show to California instead. Lee 1 and Lee 2 went to the scrap yard on Christmas Eve 1978.[^scc20] Lee 1 had one more part to play first. It came back to the shop a wreck and went out again, painted blue, as the wrecked race car in the episode "Repo Men."[^brb07] Three usable General Lees went west. By Holman's account a fourth was given to Don Schisler, because Warner Bros. owed him money.[^scc20]

## Lee 6

That fourth car was the last one H&H built. Holman found it himself, he said: he was making a beer run to the gas station when a woman pulled up in a Charger, and he asked her if she wanted to sell it. H&H turned it into a General Lee, and it was never used on film. "Got it ready, but didn't use it," Danny Hobbs said.[^scc20] Later the story went around that it was only a parts car Schisler had dressed up as a replica years afterward. When the Volo Auto Museum in Illinois bought it,[^volo] its director, Brian Grams, set out to check. He found the roll bar was from Lee 1. In an old photo of the finished car he spotted a "Bill Hutson for Sheriff" sign in the background, which dated the picture to before late November 1979. And the buyer from 1980 still had Schisler's own ad: "General Lee as owned and built by Warner Bro. for Dukes of Hazzard series, not a replica, only privately-owned General Lee in existence."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^scc20]

Lee 1 turned up too, in a Metro Atlanta junkyard. Travis Bell and a partner found it and bought it,[^scc20] and Bell, an Indianapolis DJ, restored it in 2006.[^hist] It was put back the way it looked leaving the ramp at Oxford, with the college sticker still on the windshield, and the plan was to bring it onto Covington's square on November 11, 2006, the jump's 28th anniversary, with John Schneider at the wheel.[^brb06] Holman kept a trunk lid from Lee 1, one of the deck lids that survived the jump, at his small car museum in Taylor County. Schneider used to come by the shop and take a General Lee out back to a dirt field, where they would see who could come closest to the dumpster without hitting it. "Henry, if he has a wreck and gets hurt his career is over, quit it and get rid of the car," Schneider's mother was always telling Holman.[^wgxa17] Two years before, another Norcross shop had readied the stunt cars for *Smokey and the Bandit*, and Schisler had a hand in that one too; that story is told in [Ghost Cars With No VIN](smokey-and-the-bandit-jonesboro-mcdonough.md).

In California the show went on to use hundreds of General Lees.[^hist][^scc20] The first ones were rebuilt overnight by three men in Norcross. "It's unbelievable that an orange car can be as popular as it is," Kelley said.[^brb07]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Was the Oxford jump 81 feet or 82?** History.com says the jump was "16 feet high and 82 feet long."[^hist] The *Horsepower Memories* location survey says it "measured a distance of 81 feet and launched the General Lee 16 feet into the air," and calls the campus "Oxford University"; it is Oxford College.[^hpm18] Both agree on the date, the height and the police car. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did H&H do 76 cars in two months?** Henry Holman told WGXA in 2017, "We did six cars for Warner Brothers," and "we had to do 76 cars in two months."[^wgxa17] The six fits the museum's count of five General Lees used in Georgia plus the unused sixth.[^scc20] We have no second source for the 76. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Was Lee 6 a Warner Bros. car or a later replica?** The Volo museum's account, written by Jim Suva, cites Holman, John Schisler and Danny Hobbs, the Lee 1 roll bar, the sheriff's sign and Don Schisler's own ad, and calls the car "the only surviving Georgia-era General Lee."[^scc20] The same account says Don Schisler told people for years that it was a Warner parts car he later turned into a replica, and that Jon Holland, author of *Roads Back to Early Hazzard*, still holds that view.[^scc20] We have not read Holland's case in his own words. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
