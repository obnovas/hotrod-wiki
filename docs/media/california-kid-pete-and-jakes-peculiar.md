# Home of The California Kid: A TV Movie Coupe, Pete & Jake's and the Road to Peculiar, Missouri (1972–2025)

*A '34 bought on New Year's Day, a cover that caught a producer's eye, and the parts dealer in Missouri who ended up with the shop and the car*

## A coupe from a Glendora garage

Most movie cars are props. This one became a trademark. A hot rodder built a black '34 Ford coupe for himself, a TV movie borrowed it and gave it a name, and the name went onto the sign of his new parts shop. Then a parts dealer in Missouri who had called that shop after seeing the movie bought the business, and the car came with it. It has lived in Peculiar, Missouri, ever since.

Pete Chapouris told the story himself, on the company's website. In the late 1960s and early '70s he had a T-bucket, built in his parents' garage, and he wanted "a more basic ride," a hot rod that "could have been part of the stories I heard as a kid being passed back and forth between my dad and his pals out in the garage." It had to be a coupe. His two favorites were the Les Hawkins "15 oz. Coupe" and Gary Wagner's chopped, full-fendered street racer, both Hemi-powered '34 Fords.[^pj]

A local drag racer, Randy Troxel, told him about a chopped, full-fendered '34 three-window stashed in a Glendora garage, owned by a man who did not really want to sell it. The deal took two weeks. Chapouris bought it on New Year's Day 1972, moved it to the backyard garage in Temple City, sold the T and kept its drivetrain, wheels and tires.[^pj]

## Built after hours at Blair's

In May 1972 he quit his job at Clayton Manufacturing, where his father had worked, and went to work for Phil Lukens at Blair's Speed Shop in Pasadena as a chassis builder.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^pj][^hem] He and his wife, Carol, had a house, two small children, a '67 VW and a '50 Bel Air, and he remembered that "leaving a secure job and heading out into the hot rod world did not seem right to most people."[^pj] Money was tight. Friends came by when he needed hands. Blair's engine builder, Buddy Lotsberg, built the 302 small-block Ford; Eric Vaughn taught him the louver press; Dale Caulfield did the major bodywork and paint. For a while it was going to be an icebox-white highboy. It ended up full-fendered and black, with flames by Manuel Reyes.[^pj]

Gray Baskerville came to the house on a rainy afternoon to see it, and that visit started Chapouris's friendship with Jim "Jake" Jacobs, who was building a yellow '34 coupe of his own.[^pj][^kcs] Both of them, the American Hot Rod Foundation says, were "bucking the resto-rod style that was popular at the time."[^ahrf] With *Rod & Custom* editor Bud Bryan involved, the two coupes went on the cover of the November 1973 issue.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^pj][^hem][^ahrf][^fc] That "coupes issue," Chapouris wrote, helped the two friends open their business early in 1974, in Temple City.[^pj][^hem] It started as Pete & Jake's Hot Rod Repair and later became Pete & Jake's Hot Rod Parts.[^pja] In April 1974 the coupe was photographed at the Antique Nationals, before anyone had painted "California Kid" on the door.[^ahrf]

## Four days before filming

That June, by Chapouris's account, producer Howie Horowitz was making a TV movie at Universal Studios with Martin Sheen and still had no car, with four days left before filming. He saw the *Rod & Custom* cover. Chapouris got the call, he and Jake drove over the next morning, and by that afternoon the coupe had the part.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^pj]

The studio used it for two weeks and, he wrote, turned "my pristine hot rod into a real stunt 'work horse.'" Jerry Summers did the driving and Hal Needham coordinated the stunts. Jake was hired as the hot rod advisor and "babysat the coupe" for the whole shoot.[^pj] *The California Kid* aired on Wednesday night, September 25, 1974, as ABC's 200th made-for-TV Movie of the Week.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^pj] Afterward a friend looked over the damage and asked if he would do it again. "In a heart beat," he said.[^pj]

The best part, he wrote, was that Universal gave them permission to use "Home of The California Kid" in the Pete & Jake's logo.[^pj] *Hemmings* says the movie brought business to the shop and gave the Ford its name, and that the car "soon became better known than the film itself."[^hem] The foundation says it "inspired Cal' Kid clones to be built all around the world."[^ahrf] The shop's parts went the same direction as the car: at a time when hot rods were getting modern suspensions, *Hemmings* says, Pete & Jake's improved on Henry Ford's original parts instead.[^hem] A decade later the shop launched what the foundation calls the "Fat Attack," with Chapouris's purple-flamed '39 Ford convertible.[^ahrf]

## A dealer in Kansas City

In Missouri, Jerry Slover watched the movie. His son Jason told a Kansas City magazine in 2025 that it "changed his life as well." Jerry bought a 1934 Ford of his own, needed parts, and called Pete & Jake's, "Home of The California Kid." He became friends with Pete and Jake, and in 1975 he became their dealer in Kansas City. He owned Ruskin Auto Parts and a hot rod shop, Ruskin Rod Shop.[^voy] *The Kansas City Star* tells it from the store side: the relationship began in 1975, when Jerry opened an auto parts store and sold hot rod parts along with it.[^kcs]

In 1986 the Slovers bought Pete & Jake's and moved it to Grandview, Missouri, and later to Peculiar.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^kcs][^voy][^pja] The coupe came with the purchase. Jason was 16. His friends, he told the *Star*, "knew it was a cool car, but that was the extent of it. They didn't understand the magnitude of the car."[^kcs] Chapouris went to SEMA as vice president of marketing.[^hem][^ahrf] Pete & Jake's joined with the Super Bell Axle Company in 2004.[^pja]

## The real car

By 2015 the coupe sat inside the shop at 401 Legend Lane, and the question Jason heard most was whether it was the real car. The family took it to about two shows a year; for its 40th anniversary, in 2014, they took it to the National Street Rod Association Nationals in Louisville.[^kcs] The company says it and Jake's Coupe have together covered almost 250,000 miles, and both sit on the showroom floor.[^pja] Jason said people wrote from overseas asking how to find Peculiar so they could see it.[^kcs] Chapouris died on January 6, 2017, at 76.[^hem] In 2025 the business was still family-run in Peculiar, with three generations working there.[^voy]

A hot rodder built a coupe to look like the cars his father talked about. A TV movie gave it a name, the name went on his shop's sign, and the man who answered that sign with a phone call now owns the shop and the car.

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did he leave Clayton for Blair's?** Chapouris's own account says May 1972, after he bought the coupe on New Year's Day that year.[^pj] *Hemmings* says, "In 1971 he left a steady job at Clayton Manufacturing to work at Blair's Speed Shop."[^hem] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How did the producer find the car?** Chapouris wrote that Howie Horowitz saw the *Rod & Custom* cover in June 1974, with four days left before filming.[^pj] Jason Slover told the *Star* the producer "happened to be at a Thanksgiving dinner of one of his relatives and that magazine happened to be there at the house."[^kcs] The two can both be true only if the producer saw the magazine months before he called. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
