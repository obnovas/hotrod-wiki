# Why Limit It Just to Swaps? Holley's LS Fest at Beech Bend (Bowling Green, Kentucky, 2010–2024)

*A junkyard engine, a carburetor company's hometown strip, and a race to see who could swap one in fastest with hand tools*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (the origin is told by one of the Holley people who planned it, years later; the first weekend is in two magazine reports and a forum thread written that night; the crowds and the traffic are in the hometown paper) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> *Bowling Green Daily News* (2019, 2021), with the county public works director, a Holley marketing director and residents; firsthand forum posts from the first night (LS1Tech, September 10, 2010); *Hot Rod* and *Popular Hot Rodding* (February 2011); Holley's own 2024 history and 2010 announcement · **Last verified:** 2026-11-20 (Round 45 citation review) · **Open issues:** ⚠ how long the first engine swap took · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "Why limit it just to swaps? We should invite anyone with an LS engine to this."

    — Tom Tomlinson, then Holley's president and CEO, as Shane Weckerly of Holley remembered it in 2024.[^hl24]

## An engine with no real name

LS Fest is unusual for a reason that is easy to miss. It was a carburetor company, at its own hometown drag strip in Kentucky, that threw the biggest party for the engine that made carburetors optional. And the thing hot rodders were doing to that engine, pulling it out of one car and putting it in another, became an event of its own, timed and won.

The engine came in the 1997 Corvette. The Gen III LS1 went into the Corvette that year, and the big switch from the old small-block to the new family came in 1999, *Hot Rod* wrote in 2011.[^hr11] *Popular Hot Rodding* remembered how it was received at first: the motoring press "snickered at the antiquated cam-in-block two-valve-per-cylinder layout."[^phr11] Hot rodders found it light, small and strong. By 2011, *Hot Rod* said, "the days of scoring an LS drivetrain from a dead Chevy Silverado or F-body car for almost nothing are gone," because everyone had figured out what they were worth, and its staff could not find a recycler who would let a set of ignition coils go for less than 400 dollars.[^hr11] Races just for LS-powered cars, it said, had "popped up all over the country after the first GM Performance Parts LSX Shootout in 2007."[^hr11]

It did not even have a good name. GM called it a Gen III or Gen IV small-block. Hot rodders called it an LS, after the production codes that began with LS1 and LS6. "None of this has any ring to it, like Mouse motor, or Rat motor," David Freiburger wrote, and he asked readers to email *Hot Rod* if they had something better.[^hr11]

## A conference room in 2009

Holley's headquarters are in Bowling Green, Kentucky, where the Corvette plant is too.[^phr11] Shane Weckerly of Holley remembered, in 2024, a day in 2009 when "a bunch of us got together in a conference room at our headquarters to talk about what we could do to make the company more relevant to late model enthusiasts." The company was starting to sell engine swap parts. The first idea was an event for anyone who had swapped an LS into something. Tom Tomlinson, then president and CEO, widened it to anyone with an LS engine.[^hl24]

Holley announced it in February 2010: the first Holley LS Fest, September 10 through 12, at Beech Bend Raceway Park, "Holley's hometown racetrack."[^dz10] [Beech Bend](../drag-racing/beech-bend-raceway-bowling-green.md) had been a racetrack since its stock cars of 1948. There would be a drag race, an autocross, a 0-60-0 speed-stop challenge and a show, and a grand champion for the best overall score. "We are just a bunch of gear-heads that love GM LS engines," Tomlinson said in the announcement. "We will have Beech Bend Raceway to ourselves for three days of everything LS."[^dz10] Weckerly said later that they looked to the Optima Street Car Challenge, and to Pro-Touring, which "was really starting to gain some momentum at that point," for a format that tested more than one skill.[^hl24]

## The first weekend

People started arriving on Friday, September 10, 2010. That evening a member from northwest Louisiana posted on the LS1Tech forum: "I'm here. We drove 10 hrs and it rained a good bit before nashville." A member from Alabama answered that the hotel desk had told him "the Vette museum and Jim Beam closed at 5," and said he had seen "Mighty Mouse" in the parking lot.[^ls1t]

Mighty Mouse was Scott Hamlin's '94 Geo Tracker from Fayetteville, Tennessee. He had swapped at least a dozen LS engines into trucks over the years, *Hot Rod* reported, and he built a Geo this time partly because it fit on his 12-foot lawn mower trailer.[^hr11] Bervil Hillis of Sikeston, Missouri, brought his '94 Silverado work truck, which had already had a carbureted Vortec 305 and a pair of LT1s, and now had an LS1 he had bought on eBay as an accessory donor for another project.[^hr11] Holley remembers about 150 cars at that first LS Fest.[^hl24]

The event the magazines liked best was the Car Craft Engine Swap Challenge. Justin R. Dermody of Williamsburg, Iowa, drove in with an Oldsmobile Cutlass whose small-block was held in with a few finger-tight bolts. His team swapped in a new LS6 crate engine and had it running in 31 minutes and 57 seconds, beat the other teams by a wide margin and got to keep the engine.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^hr11] The rules, as Weckerly later described them: the car is driven into the work area and driven out, with hand tools only, and the winners keep the new engine.[^hl24]

The all-around contest suited a name from the Pro-Touring story. [Mark Stielow](../eras/1990s-pro-touring-garage-engineers.md) scored at or near the top in every part of it in his '69 Camaro, won *Popular Hot Rodding*'s speed-stop challenge and earned a spot in the Optima invitational.[^phr11] Brian Finch, an autocrosser from Hermitage, Tennessee, told *Hot Rod* he had put a second LS3 in his '71 Camaro "after finding out that the first one didn't have enough power to keep up with the Kyle Tuckers and Mark Stielows of the world."[^hr11]

## Bigger than the road

Holley wanted, Weckerly said, "an eight-ring circus," and they talked about Disneyland while they planned it.[^hl24] The crowds came. In November 2019 Holley's marketing director, Bill Tichenor, told the Warren County Fiscal Court that LS Fest had grown from 25,000 visitors to 35,000 in a single year. "It was mayhem this year," he said. "Some people went home (because of the traffic issues)."[^bgdn19] The county's public works director, Josh Moore, hoped to cut the wait into the big events from "five hours or so down to about two hours." All six magistrates voted to widen a 1,650-foot section of Beech Bend Road for $351,820. Not everyone on the road liked it; one resident, Kenneth Deputy, called the plan "ill-advised" and "a waste of taxpayer dollars."[^bgdn19]

By 2021 the county was turning Beech Bend Park Road into a one-way, two-lane road toward the event on Saturday morning, and the city was blocking off part of Wilkinson Trace, near the convention center, "to prevent drag racing that usually accompanies automotive events in the city," the *Daily News* reported.[^bgdn21] In 2024 Holley expected about 2,500 participating vehicles. LS Fest East had spread to the Corvette Museum's NCM Motorsports Park, and on Saturday it took over the Beech Bend amusement park. There were LS Fest West and LS Fest Texas. Weckerly said the quickest engine swap had dropped under 30 minutes, with quick-disconnect front clips and motor mounts.[^hl24]

A carburetor company built a hometown party around the engine people were pulling out of junkyard trucks. The swap itself became the race, and the crowd outgrew the road.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How long did the first engine swap take?** *Hot Rod*, reporting on the 2010 event in its February 2011 issue, says Dermody's team had the LS6 running "in an astonishing 31 minutes and 57 seconds."[^hr11] Weckerly, in 2024: "I think the first winning team took about half a day to do it." He added that the record is now "less than 30 minutes."[^hl24] He may be remembering an earlier rule set or another year; we have not found the 2010 rules. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
