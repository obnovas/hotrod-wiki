# Real Bootleg Cars: *Thunder Road* in Asheville (N.C., 1957)

*A Hollywood star who bought his moonshine cars from moonshiners, a Canton racer's engine, and a theater that sold 23,000 tickets*

## Cars that had done the job

Most movie cars are props. The cars in *Thunder Road* were not. When Robert Mitchum's company came to Asheville in 1957 to make its moonshine-running picture, it went looking for real bootleggers' cars and bought them from the people who drove them.[^mmt2]

Asheville heard in July 1957 that Mitchum was coming to make a movie called *Jack of Diamonds*. It became *The Whippoorwill* and then *Thunder Road*. The story was set in the Tennessee mountains, but it was filmed in and around Buncombe and Transylvania counties.[^wnc18] The company arrived in August, and its casting director set up in the Battery Park Hotel and interviewed locals for more than 150 parts.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^ct18][^wnc18][^mmt2] The ones cast included Frances Koon of the Asheville Community Theatre as the mother of Mitchum's character, and Mitchum's own 16-year-old son, Jim, as his brother.[^ct18][^wnc18] A state ABC agent, Charles Giglia, played one of the villain's henchmen. Towson writes that Giglia held the North Carolina record for stills captured, 1,297 of them.[^mmt2]

Mitchum's script had imagined a 1953 Mercury with an "Offenhauser racing motor," a supercharger and more. That changed when he got to Asheville and found the real thing, according to Heath Towson's history of the filming. Real bootleg cars were hard to buy, Towson writes, because they changed hands so often; a runner with a little extra money tended to trade up.[^mmt2]

## Pender Fox's engine

The lead car became a 1951 Ford wearing a 1949 Ford's hood, grille and fenders. Its engine, the one seen in the movie, was a 312-cubic-inch Ford Y-block with three two-barrel Holley carburetors. Towson writes that it was built by Pender Fox of Canton, a racer known for winning at the Smoky Mountain Speedway in Waynesville.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>[^mmt2] The 1951 Ford and the 1957 Fords were serviced in the evenings at the Sawyer Motor Building, home of Matthews Ford on Coxe Avenue.[^mmt2] Bill Parris, whose family had run moonshine after World War II, remembered the producers buying cars from his father and uncle, and the two men going to Sandy Bottoms, along the French Broad River, to teach the crew to spin the cars without running them into the river.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>[^mmt2]

Hot rods were in the background too. A downtown parking garage at Hoyle's Office Supply, a former Hudson dealership on North Market Street, became the set for the villain's speed and hot rod shop. The store stayed open while they filmed, and customers came in for pencils while the actors walked through.[^mmt3] A 1936 Ford coupe with a supercharged flathead sat in that shop. Towson writes that it later belonged to John Bell of Asheville, who made it into a street rod with a small-block Chevrolet.[^mmt2]

The hard driving went to stuntman Carey Loftin.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^mmt2][^wnc18] For the climax he drove at 75 miles an hour through a barricade of two police cars on Brevard Road and flipped into a bank of transformers.[^wnc18] Towson writes that Loftin had a roll cage put in the 1957 Ford, a front bumper said to be made from a piece of railroad track, 300 pounds of weight strapped in the trunk and only a gallon of gas in the tank. Asked where the car should stop, a cameraman pointed at the ground, and Loftin set his cigarette butt on the spot. After the roll, the butt was lying under the front of the fender.[^mmt2] The car that tumbled down Toxaway Falls went without him.[^wnc18]

The town came out for the result. Between May 9 and May 23, 1958, a record 23,000 people bought tickets at the Imperial Theater downtown.[^wnc18][^mmt3] Mitchum's "The Ballad of Thunder Road" later turned up on *Shut Down*, Capitol's 1963 hot rod compilation with the Beach Boys and Jan and Dean.[^mmt2] Hollywood brought the star and the cameras. The cars came from the people who had driven them for real.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How was the stunt driver's name spelled?** Towson writes "Carey Loftin" throughout.[^mmt2] Frank Thompson in *WNC Magazine* writes that most of the crashes "were piloted by stuntman Cary Lofton."[^wnc18] The film's credits or a 1957 *Citizen-Times* report would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Pender Fox build the lead car's engine?** Only Towson's series says the 312 Y-block was "built by Canton native, Pender Fox."[^mmt2] A Canton or Waynesville paper, or a Smoky Mountain Speedway program, might confirm it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did a local family sell cars and teach the spins?** The car sales and the Sandy Bottoms lessons rest on Bill Parris's memory, as quoted by Towson; we have not found where Parris first told it.[^mmt2] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How many locals worked on the film?** Neufeld writes that the casting director interviewed locals for "more than 150 parts, 29 of them speaking roles."[^ct18] Thompson writes that "Between 150 and 300 locals appeared as extras."[^wnc18] Towson cites a November 1957 *Citizen-Times* report that "close to 500 people were hired for temporary employment in the movie."[^mmt2] The counts may measure different things (parts, extras and all jobs). <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
