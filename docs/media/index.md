# Media & Shows

*How a look and a hobby traveled: by paper, plastic and show hall.*

How did a Barris chop in Lynwood become a look copied in Akron, Wichita and Portland? And how did street rodding get its own magazine? Through paper, plastic and show halls. This department follows each channel: the magazines and books that covered hot rodding, the model kits that sold it to kids, and the indoor shows that gave it a stage. The cars and builders themselves live in other departments (see [Customs & Kustom Kulture](../customs/index.md) and [Street Rodding](../street-rodding/index.md)).

*Short on time?* The [Quick Answers](../reference/quick-answers.md#media-and-shows) give the short version of the big questions about media and shows, each with a link to the full story.

- [Where Lucas Shot It: Petaluma and *American Graffiti* (1972)](american-graffiti-petaluma-1972.md): San Rafael says no after one night, Petaluma says yes, a drive-in where the Pacers met, and the car show the town's own car people built in 2006 (⚠ when Lund took over Hollie's).
- [Home of The California Kid (Temple City to Peculiar, Mo.)](california-kid-pete-and-jakes-peculiar.md): a backyard '34 coupe, a *Rod & Custom* cover, a 1974 TV movie, and the parts dealer who bought the shop and the car (⚠ how the producer found it; ⚠ 1986 or 1987).
- [Why Limit It Just to Swaps? Holley's LS Fest (Bowling Green, Ky.)](ls-fest-beech-bend-bowling-green.md): a carburetor company's hometown meet for the junkyard LS engine, a timed engine swap, and a crowd that outgrew the road (⚠ how long the first swap took).
- [Two Nationals in One Summer: The National Truck-In and Beech Bend (vans, 1973–76)](national-truck-in-vans-beech-bend.md): a *Hot Rod* editor's call for a van meet, a club-hosted party that filled Beech Bend in 1975, and a Denver club that won the name, so 1976 had two Nationals (⚠ the first meet's size; ⚠ which 1976 meet was the fourth).
- [Named, by His Brother's Account, for a Neighbor's Mother: Toby Halicki and Eleanor (Dunkirk, N.Y., to Yukon, Okla.)](eleanor-halicki-gone-in-60-seconds.md): a tow-truck family's son makes *Gone in 60 Seconds* in 1974, dies filming the sequel, and leaves a Mustang that builders copied and a court in 2025 called a prop (⚠ the car's year; ⚠ the tower's height; ◐ who it was named for).
- ["Halloween on Wheels": The Street Machine Nationals at Du Quoin (Ill.)](street-machine-nationals-du-quoin.md): a traveling show lands at a fairground town of 6,000 in 1986, a Pontiac that took Best Street Machine, and a governor's decision in 1998 (⚠ the 1986 count; ⚠ who ended it).
- ["As If They Were Long-Lost Relatives": The First Hot Rod Power Tour and Goodland (Kan.)](power-tour-goodland-kansas-1995.md): a magazine's road trip, two police cruisers on an off-ramp, a vo-tech shop open past midnight and a town that stopped work to watch, 1995–98 (⚠ the first count; ◐ why Mingo became Goodland).
- [Ghost Cars With No VIN: Smokey and the Bandit in Georgia](smokey-and-the-bandit-jonesboro-mcdonough.md): a first week of filming in Jonesboro and McDonough, stunt cars readied at a Norcross race shop, used DeKalb County police cars, and the Bandit Run that came back to Jonesboro for the 40th (⚠ when McDonough was filmed; ⚠ how many Trans Ams).
- ["We Wreck 'Em Just as Fast as We Need 'Em": The Georgia General Lees](dukes-of-hazzard-h-and-h-body-shop-covington.md): *The Dukes of Hazzard*'s five Georgia episodes, a three-man Norcross body shop rebuilding wrecks overnight, the jump at Oxford College, and the one Charger they finished but never used (⚠ 81 or 82 feet; ⚠ Lee 6).
- [The 2005 Shirts: Cruisin' the Coast (Mississippi Gulf Coast)](cruisin-the-coast-mississippi-gulf.md): casino and tourism leaders borrow Reno's idea in 1996 to fill October hotel rooms, Katrina cancels 2005, cruisers come anyway to help, and 2006 goes on in 2005's shirts (⚠ three and a half days or four).
- [*Thunder Road* in Asheville (N.C., 1957)](thunder-road-asheville-bootleg-cars-1957.md): by one local account, Robert Mitchum's company buys real moonshine cars, a Canton racer's Y-block goes in the lead Ford, and a family of former runners teaches the spins (◐ the cars, the engine and the lessons); 23,000 tickets sell at one Asheville theater.
- [A Camaro on a Quarter-Mile Cable: *Vanishing Point* in Cisco, Utah, and Goldfield, Nev. (1970)](vanishing-point-cisco-goldfield-1970.md): Chrysler's white Challengers, the stripped Camaro that hit the bulldozers, a family detoured off I-70 the day of the crash, and the fans who drive the route (⚠ five cars or eight; ◐ the crash date).
- ["Closer to Texas": The Lonestar Round Up (Austin, Texas)](lonestar-round-up-kontinentals-austin.md): a car club named for the Continental Club starts its own show in 2002 so Texans need not drive to California, with bands on the stage, cars 1963 or older and a T coupe built in 30 days to drive there (⚠ the first year's count).
