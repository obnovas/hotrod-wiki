# From Speed Shop to Strip: Julius Hughes and the Atlanta Speed Shop Dragway (Covington, Ga., 1959–91)

*A Georgia Tech hot rodder opens a parts counter, takes over a county strip and puts his shop's name on it*

The day after the crash at the Yellow River Drag Strip in March 1969, a committee of Georgia legislators drove to the other strip in Newton County. Standing on it, the funny car driver Arnie Beswick explained "why he as a driver considered the Speed Shop Strip safer for drivers and spectators."[^cn690306] The strip west of Covington carried the name of an Atlanta parts store, and the same man ran both.

## The Newton County strip (1959–63)

It began as a county amusement company. In September 1959 three petitioners, H. C. Capps, W. A. Kelly and Ben Nell Meyers, asked to incorporate "NEWTON COUNTY DRAG STRIP, INC." to furnish "amusements to the public," including "automobile racing and other amusements thereto such as, horseback riding, skating and various rides."[^cn590910] By April 1961 it had Don Garlits. The *Covington News* placed the strip "just beyond the Hub Junction," quoted the manager, Bennell Myers, on a $500 prize, promised "A local ambulance will be secured for safety purposes to trail the Garlits machine," and added: "The Newton Drag Strip is sanctioned by the National Hot Rod Association."[^cn610330]

## A parts counter in Atlanta

Julius Hughes Jr. was a native Atlantan who, by a memorial page built with his widow's help, "first fell in love with racing while recovering from eye surgery when he was twelve years old," when his mother read him car magazines. At Georgia Tech he started the Georgia Tech Auto Club in 1956, and the club built a gas dragster with Hoyt Grimes' help.[^gdrhughes][^gahof] He raced at national meets in Kansas City and Oklahoma City in a roadster that *Hot Rod* featured.[^gdrhughes][^gahof]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> With Frank Brumby he ran a supercharged '55 Chevy that fans named the "Zot" car, after a painting on its trunk of the anteater from the *B.C.* comic strip "'zotting' a trophy." It won D/Gas at the 1959 Nationals in Detroit.[^gdrhughes]

He "started his speed shop business while attending high school, but officially opened The Atlanta Speed Shop in 1960. According to his widow, Elaine, this was the first speed shop in Georgia."[^gdrhughes]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> The memorial page calls him "arguably, Atlanta's strongest proponent of legal, sanctioned drag racing" and says he "regularly gave free passes to young people for the Atlanta Speed Shop Dragway to urge them to race on a safe NHRA drag strip and not on the street."[^gdrhughes]

## The shop's name on the strip (1964–77)

In 1964 Hughes and Brumby took over the Newton County strip.[^gdrhughes][^gahof]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> The *Covington News* reported it in one line that April: "The Newton Drag Strip has changed its name to Atlanta Speed Shop Dragway."[^cn640416] The Hall of Fame says the renamed strip "became the first NHRA sanctioned drag strip in Georgia."[^gahof]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>

That October came the big one. The *Covington News* billed "The Atlanta $10,000 Drag Race," run by the Atlanta Drag Strip Association, as "the richest drag race ever held in the Southeast," with $2,500 for the winning fuel dragster and $2,000 in Super Stock, entries from California, Detroit and Massachusetts, and "500 cars" expected.[^cn641022] The Forest Park paper called it "the first major drag meet ever to be held in the Southeast."[^fpn641008] The association, the Georgia drag racing historian David Dilbeck later wrote, was made up of strip operators: Hughes and Brumby, Lamar Sexton and Pete Robinson of Cumming Dragway, the Houston brothers, Charles Hardy of Southeastern International Dragway, and "Yellow River Drag Strip's Shug Campbell."[^gdr1964] Paul Sutherland of California won the fuel final at 7.71 and 191.88 miles an hour, and Don Nicholson took Super Stock. Marvin T. Smith's history of the race quotes *Drag News* calling it "by far the biggest and richest race ever held in the Southland."[^gdr1964]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> The race came back every year through 1972.[^gahof]

The strip also booked the factory shows. In July 1965 it had the "Hurst Hemi-Under-Glass," which the *Athens Banner-Herald* called "a rolling Detroit research laboratory," for "a series of exhibition runs—the first in the area," with some 5,000 spectators expected.[^abh650725] (For the company behind the car, see [Hurst-Campbell](../companies/hurst-campbell.md).) That October "Sneaky" Pete Robinson was top eliminator at the second $10,000 race,[^cn651028] and the same year Hughes, driving the nitro-burning "Zot III," won the NHRA Division 2 Top Fuel championship.[^gahof][^gdrhughes]

In the 1970s the strip turns up mostly in the county-board pages. In December 1972 "Mr. Julius Hughes, owner of the Atlanta Speed Shop Dragstrip," asked for a hearing before the commissioners adopted a license fee, and "explained the operation of the dragstrip and the revenues derived from the operation, including expenses involved."[^cn721221] In September 1973 he was back, with Hulon Hodges, calling the fee "unfair and excessive"; the board took no action.[^cn730927a] The same week the strip gave $125 to the high school's Touchdown Club, whose members had parked cars on race day.[^cn730927b] In April 1974 a Stone Mountain driver, Billy Joe Gay, was killed when his Plymouth Road Runner slid sideways for 180 feet past the finish line into a tree; a sheriff's investigator said "it was the first fatality at the dragstrip that he could remember."[^cn740430] In 1977 a request for a county ambulance at the races was tabled for the county attorney to study.[^cn770203]

The strip outlasted all of it. By the memorial page's account it ran until 1991, operated by Harold Springs and Julius Hughes III, "when the lease agreement was nullified by new land owners in court."[^gdrhughes]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-6"><a href="#q-6" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> Hughes kept the shop. He "would continue to operate the Atlanta Speed Shop until his death on February 15, 1992."[^gahof]

For the other strip on Highway 278, and the crash that sent the legislators to this one, see [Yellow River Drag Strip](yellow-river-covington.md).<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-7"><a href="#q-7" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** *The Georgia Racing Hall of Fame page, and the* Pickens County Progress *column that announced his 2014 induction, put Hughes's class win at "the 1955 National Championship Drag Races in Kansas City, MO" and his Oklahoma City win in 1956.[^gahof][^pcp141204] The memorial page has him at "the 1956 National Championship Drag Races held in Kansas City, Missouri, and the Oklahoma City Nationals (1957)," in a roadster featured in* Hot Rod *for August 1957.[^gdrhughes] NHRA dates its first national meet, the 1955 National Championship Drags, to Great Bend, Kan.[^nhra] (see [Great Bend](great-bend-srca.md)), which fits the memorial page's 1956. We have not seen the* Hot Rod *article ("The Georgia Foursome," by the Hall of Fame's account).* <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** *"The first speed shop in Georgia" is Elaine Hughes's account, reported on the memorial page; the Hall of Fame repeats it. We have not found a 1960 opening notice or an earlier Georgia speed shop to test it against.* <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** *The Hall of Fame says Hughes "began management of the Newton County Drag Strip" in 1964; the memorial page says he and Frank Brumby "took over ownership." By 1972 the* Covington News *calls Hughes the "owner." Marvin T. Smith's race history dates the new management and the new NHRA sanction to March 1964.* <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** *The Hall of Fame calls the renamed strip "the first NHRA sanctioned drag strip in Georgia." The* Covington News *of March 30, 1961, already calls the Newton strip NHRA-sanctioned, and Smith's history says the former Newton County Dragway was "also NHRA sanctioned." The claim may mean the first under the new name or the first in some other sense; as written, the 1961 paper contradicts it.* <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** *The* Drag News *quote, the winners and the times come from Smith's draft history, which says it drew on racing papers, the Atlanta papers and interviews. Smith gives Nicholson's Super Stock purse as $2,500 but notes that* Drag News *says $2,000, the figure in the* Covington News *preview. We have not read* Drag News *for October 1964. The Forest Park paper's "$16,000" purse is an OCR misreading of the $10,000 in the* Covington News *and* Oconee Enterprise *notices.[^oe641007]* <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-6" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** *The 1991 closing and the court ruling on the lease come only from the memorial page. A court record or a* Covington News *story from 1991 would settle the date and the parties.* <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-6" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
