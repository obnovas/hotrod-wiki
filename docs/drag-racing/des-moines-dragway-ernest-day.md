# A Farmer's Strip on Grimes Road: Des Moines Dragway (1957–67)

*A farmer built it, it ran eleven years, and the pavement waited thirty-three more*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (the facts come mostly from one local man's history site, built from racers' photos and stories, and from a 2011 trade-press summary of that site; the reasons it was built and closed are one source each) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> a local historian's site (*Des Moines Dragway*, Larry Lathom, 2007–2025); racers' forum posts (the H.A.M.B., 2007–08); a regional magazine quoted in our [Ames](ames-drag-strip-nightcrawlers.md) article (*The Iowan*, 1959); *Dragzine* (2011) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-13 (Round 40) · **Open issues:** ◐ why Day built it and why it closed; ◐ the admission prices; ◐ the jet car and the back fence · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

## A farmer and his son

Des Moines's drag strip was a farmer's project. It ran eleven years and then sat, a strip of blacktop on Grimes Road, for thirty-three more, until the houses came. Its history is being pieced together by one local man who put his phone number on the web and asked people to call.

A farmer named Ernest Day built it at the urging of his eldest son, with plenty of local racer interest, *Dragzine* wrote in 2011.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^dz11] The first day of racing was July 13, 1957, and about three thousand people came the opening weekend.[^dmd50][^dmdh] It was the Greater Des Moines Drag Strip through 1960 and the Des Moines Dragway from 1961 on. It sat at 128th Street, then called Grimes Road, and Aurora, in what is now Urbandale.[^dmdh] Admission was a dollar, a pit pass $1.50, and running your car $2.50.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^dz11]

It was not a club strip, and the clubs noticed. In 1959 Robert Lambert, the math professor who served as steward of Ames's club-run strip, told *The Iowan*: "The only other permanent drag strip installation in Iowa—at Des Moines—is owned by a private investor, not a hotrod club."[^iowan] Which of the two raced first is still open on the [Ames page](ames-drag-strip-nightcrawlers.md#open-questions-disputed-details).

## The jet car and the fence

The national names came through. Art Arfons brought the *Green Monster* jet car on May 13, 1962.[^dmdh] A racer from Sully, Iowa, remembered being there in the early '60s "when a Jet-powered car blew the back fence down."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^hamb] Don Steadman announced from 1962 to 1967.[^dmd50] The local cars were the regulars: Joe and Lee Wray's dragster in 1966, the Firebird Lee bought in March 1967 and raced at Des Moines and Humboldt, Vern Moats's Anglia and '55 Chevy, and Chuck McGuire's '34 Chevy, powered in 1962 by a blown small-block. When McGuire's car was still Studebaker-powered, it had been in *Hot Rod*'s December 1957 issue.[^dmdhome]

Then it stopped. *Dragzine* says that, according to historians, it closed in 1967 when Day simply moved away.[^dz11] The strip was left where it was. The whole strip was still there in 2000, when the Days Run housing development began. The first eighth-mile lasted until June 2003.[^dmdh]

## Larry's phone number

The man keeping its memory is Larry Lathom of Des Moines.[^hamb] His site lists his phone number and says he is "constructing a history of the strip," asking for photos, videos, newsletters, stories and testimonials. He is still hunting three 1967 issues of *Hawkeye Racing News*, and anything about Vern Moats's *Red Baron*.[^dmdhome] When Des Moines's KCCI-TV ran a piece on the strip on August 1, 2007, the response was so big that it took him a month to call everyone back.[^dmdtv] The next day a viewer posted on the H.A.M.B. that he had never known Des Moines had a strip.[^hamb] The same racer from Sully wrote in February 2008 that he still had an original copy of the *Des Moines Dragger* from September 1957.[^hamb]

By 2008 fourteen cars that had raced there had been found in Iowa, and Lathom invited their owners to bring them to a reunion at Eddyville Raceway that July, "even if they don't run and have to be trailered." The schedule had Lou Bredesky's slides of the strip, Denny Reed flagging and Don Steadman announcing again. Dick Hoover of Dallas Center planned to bring the '32 Ford he had raced there, 400 cubic inches with dual quads, and race it.[^dmd50] Ernest Day built the strip. It ran eleven years, the pavement lay there thirty-three more, and the people who raced on it are still being found.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Built for his son, and closed when he moved?** Both come from *Dragzine*'s 2011 summary, which credits its images to the Des Moines Dragway site and gives the closing reason "according to historians."[^dz11] The site's own history page, as we read it, gives neither the builder nor the reason. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did a jet car knock down the back fence?** One racer's memory, and he does not name the car.[^hamb] Arfons's *Green Monster* is the jet car the site records, on May 13, 1962, but we have not tied the two together.[^dmdh] Another poster remembered a scorched billboard or fence, but at Cedar Falls.[^hamb] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **A dollar in, $1.50 for a pit pass, $2.50 to run?** Only *Dragzine* in 2011 gives the prices.[^dz11] Larry Lathom's history page, read live and in its May 19, 2022 Wayback copy, does not.[^dmdh] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
