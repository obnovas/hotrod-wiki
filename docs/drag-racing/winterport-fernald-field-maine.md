## Too far to Sanford

The town of Winterport still owns the field. In 1967 four racers asked for its old abandoned airfield, to reduce the long tow south to Sanford, and Carl Crowley christened the new strip in his 1964 Pontiac GTO. In 2025 a lease fight ended the association's run.

For a racer in eastern Maine in the early 1960s, a drag race meant a long tow south to the airport at Sanford, the strip the [New England Hot Rod Council](new-england-dragway-epping.md) ran from 1955. Carl Crowley of Lamoine began racing there in 1959.[^mvrca] So did Cappy Holt, Gary Jenkins and Gary Sargent. The four "wanted to reduce the travel," the *Bangor Daily News* wrote, so they went to the town of Winterport about its old abandoned airfield.[^bdn10] "They were a little tentative but they let us give it a try and here we are 43 years later," Crowley said in 2010.[^bdn10]

Someone had tried a closer airfield first. Bruce Wheeler, who raced at Sanford until its last summer, remembered drags at the old Norridgewock airfield in 1965. George McDonald, then running Sanford, "had some races at that old airfield using Sanford's Chrondek clocks and PA. As I understood it, the effort required wasn't worth it, and they never ran races there again. But I could be wrong." Wheeler ran his daily-driver '64 GTO there and got its best times, 13.54 at 105.[^dslme]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> Only Wheeler's memory dates that year.

## "It might as well be you"

Winterport Dragway's first race card came in 1967. Holt, whom the paper called a co-owner with Sargent, felt somebody should christen the track with a pass down the quarter mile before the racing began. "And he said it might as well be you," Crowley remembered. He climbed into his 1964 Pontiac GTO and did it.[^bdn10]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> Who owned it when it opened is still open. He spent three years reviving that GTO for the track's 40th anniversary and was set to recreate the run for the 50th, in 2017.[^bdn10][^bdn17]

"When we started the drag strip, we were right in the middle of the muscle car era," Crowley said. "Everybody was buying muscle cars with high horsepower. And they needed a place to race to show how fast they could go. They couldn't race in the streets."[^bdn10] John Gray raced his new 1967 Chevelle SS 396 there in '67, '68 and '69, when it was still a quarter-mile strip. He stored the car in his barn for 28 years.[^dslme] Russ Allen, a Winterport kid, rode his bicycle to the track in the 1960s and came back in 1989 with his '66 Fairlane and won.[^dslme] Jim Watson snuck out to race the Mustang his parents had bought him. "I just hoped I didn't break anything. (The track) was a little rough back then. A guy with two flags started the race. There weren't any lights or anything."[^bdn17]

By the Maine Motorsports Hall of Fame's count, Maine has had four drag strips: Sanford, Norridgewock, Winterport and Oxford.[^mvrca] At Oxford Plains, Larry Stegna raced a Hemi-powered '55 Chevy convertible gasser painted like an Indianapolis pace car.[^dslme] When the Maine Motorsports Hall of Fame took in Crowley in its class of 2023, it counted him among a handful of drivers who had raced and won at all four.[^mvrca]

## The association years

The racers ran it themselves. In 2010 the Winterport Dragway Association, which owned and operated the dragway, was racing an eighth-mile. Its president, Bob Reynolds, counted 200 to 300 spectators on a normal Sunday, and more than 800, with about 80 vintage dragsters, when the Gassah Guys Nostalgia Reunion came to town. Its promoter, Vaughan Stevens of Augusta, said the men who had quit racing 35 years before were building facsimiles of their 1960s and '70s cars. Crowley explained why. "Their houses are paid for, the kids are out of the house and their educations are paid for so the guys are saying to their wives that they want to go back drag racing."[^bdn10]

In the 50th anniversary year, 2017, the strip was repaved, and more than $100,000 was raised or donated for new scoreboards and computers.[^bdn17] "It's probably even more social than it is racing," said Willy Larrabee of Stockton Springs. "If your car breaks and someone else isn't racing, they'll let you use their car."[^bdn17]

## The town's lease

The land was never the racers'. Fernald Field belongs to the town. The *Bangor Daily News* says the federal government had used it as an airport, and *Dragzine* says it was a military airbase named for Roy L. Fernald that the government gave to Winterport in the 1950s.[^bdn25a][^dz25]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> What Fernald Field was is still open. The association leased it year to year, and the town keeps those leases among its public documents.[^town]

In 2025 the arrangement broke down. The association's one-year lease, $20,000, ran out on March 31, and the town manager, Casey Ashey, said it had defaulted by not turning in receipts for the gate and overnight camping. Town officials first proposed $30,000 for the next eight months, with no automatic renewal, so the town could put a five-year lease out to bid for 2026. "Asking $30,000 — a $10,000 increase for eight months, no guarantee of next year — from the commercial business side of that, how do I accept that?" the association's vice president, Gage Reynolds, asked at the April 3 council meeting. After he spoke, the price came down to $25,000, still nearly double the monthly rent.[^bdn25a] The association declined to sign. On the night of April 29 the council withdrew its offer and gave the eight months, at $30,000, to Crystal Lancaster, a drag racer from Androscoggin County who had formed a company to run the track. Not everyone in the room agreed. "I've been here 16 years without a complaint, not one complaint," said Joe Tyler, who owns an auto parts business in town. Lancaster said her mission was the association's: "to save the racing track for the racers, by a racer."[^bdn25b]

Lancaster named the track Fernald Field Dragway. It opened on Sunday, June 8, 2025, with 70 racers and more than 100 spectators, at a time when, *Dragzine* wrote, Maine had only two drag strips still running.[^dz25]

The four had asked for the field to reduce the travel. Crowley christened the strip in the GTO. The town still owns the field. A lease is what ended the association's run.
