# The Track That Milk Built: Green Valley Raceway (Smithfield, Tex., 1960–86)

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> City of North Richland Hills history pages (the Green Valley Raceway Historic Plaza site, run by the city's parks department, and the NRHTXHistory collection); a retired police officer's recollections in a 2019 local sports column; *San Antonio Express-News*, 1975 and 1977, read as OCR on the Internet Archive; a 1968 Texas Folklore Society essay · **Last verified:** 2026-10-27 (Round 24; city page, *Press Box DFW* and newspaper quotes rechecked) · **Open issues:** how many came to see Knievel ⚠; who owned the track after 1972 ⚠; the date of the last race ⚠; why the AHRA left rests on one unnamed source ◐ · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "The best job you could get, especially when they came out with the Funny Cars, was standing up there keeping everybody off the bridge. You had the best seat in the house."

    — Randy Shiflet, retired North Richland Hills police officer, on working off-duty at Green Valley, quoted by John Henry, *Press Box DFW*, 2019.[^pb]

Smithfield was a farm town north of Fort Worth, about 400 people strong by the time North Richland Hills annexed it in 1958, after what the Texas State Historical Association calls a "bitterly contested" campaign and election.[^pb] Two years later one of its dairy farms had a drag strip on it. The city that grew around the farm now keeps the strip's history on its own parks pages, and the stories it tells are a dairyman's gamble, a sanctioning war, a daredevil and a town that finally ran out of patience with the noise.

## A dairy farm on the drag circuit (1960–69)

Bill and Dorothy McClure owned a 400-acre dairy farm north of downtown Smithfield. They decided to turn 250 acres of it into a drag strip, and "In early 1960, after months of planning, contracts and red tape, Green Valley Raceway officially opens." The city's history page adds that it was "Called by some, 'The Track that Milk Built.'"[^gvr] On Thanksgiving Day, in an entry the city page files under 1960, McClure staged a "Race of Champions" with names from both the AHRA and NHRA circuits. The list runs from Art Malone and Bobby Langley to Eddie Hill, J.E. Kristek, Jim Tice and August Hartkopf, a racer San Antonio still tells legends about (see [San Antonio's strips](san-antonio-double-eagle-alamo-dragway.md)).[^gvr] The page calls it "a bold move," one that "helped put Green Valley Raceway on the map."[^gvr]

McClure picked a side early. The same page says he chose AHRA sanctioning over NHRA because "Nitromethane fuel was currently banned on NHRA tracks," and nitro drew crowds.[^gvr] The bet paid: the 1961 season drew 125,000 spectators, 30,000 of them at the AHRA nationals over Labor Day weekend. The strip, its return lane and the parking lots also made a 1.6-mile road course, and an SCCA road race that February drew 6,000.[^gvr]

The Dallas speed-shop crowd had its own name for the place. In 1968 the folklorist Hermes Nye listed it among the strip's slang: "Green Latrine; Green Valley Dragstrip near Ft. Worth." (See [T-Bones and Cheater Slicks](../voices/dallas-folksay-custom-automotive.md).)[^nye25]

The farm kept adding attractions. In February 1965, at the SCCA Polar Prix, Carroll Shelby's team debuted the Mustang GT350, driven by Ken Miles. In 1966 television crews taped drag racing for Channel 5, which the city page calls a first for Texas, and McClure opened a "pure stock" class for showroom family cars. He built what was "touted as 'the world's first heated grandstand'" for the cold early-season meets, and in June three jet dragsters, "Green Monster," "The Bonneville Avenger" and "Exodus," ran side by side.[^gvr]

## Selling the farm, and buying it back (1969–72)

In 1969 McClure sold a 120-acre tract, track included, to American Raceways Inc., a group headed by the West Coast racer and track operator Ron Miller, for a price reported "in the neighborhood of $1,000,000.00." AHRA president Jim Tice agreed to keep the Labor Day championships there. That same year the AHRA's Grand American championships had been moved to Oklahoma City, but "a tornado and 12 inch rain destroyed the track ahead of the race," and the race came back to Green Valley.[^gvr] Two and a half years later McClure took the raceway back "by default" when American Raceways returned it, and he put the repairs it needed at about $50,000.[^gvr]

In 1972 it was sold again, to United Enterprises, Inc., with the race drivers Bill Hielscher and Dennis Toppletz named race coordinators.[^gvr]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> That November a fire burned the timing tower to the ground, and before the 1973 season the track had a new tower with computer and electric timing, a speed shop and a Hall of Fame.[^gvr]

## Hielscher's show (1972–77)

Hielscher had raced before he ran tracks. *Press Box DFW* tells how at 30 he asked his wife Mary for one year to try racing while she supported the family, and credits him with 37 AHRA major championship wins by the time his six-plus professional years were done.[^pb] As a promoter he was, in the words of Jim Hill, who eulogized him in print after his death in 2002, "a one-man PR machine."[^pb]

His biggest show came on Feb. 17, 1974, when Evel Knievel jumped 11 Mack trucks at Green Valley for ABC's *Wide World of Sports*, with Howard Cosell and "Dandy" Don Meredith in the booth.[^gvr][^pb]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> The city page says Hielscher had not got a permit for the TV crew's temporary tower, "so he told his staff to stall them while Knievel made his jump, and afterwards Hielscher paid the fine."[^gvr] Randy Shiflet, then a 19-year-old rookie on a police force of 18 to 20 officers, spent the jump at Cosell's side. "If you weren't working patrol that day, you were working off-duty at Green Valley," he told *Press Box DFW*. After the landing, "The crowd was just overwhelming, swarming Evel."[^pb]

Shiflet remembered ordinary race days too, from the footbridge over the strip (see the quote above).[^pb] In 1975 the track set AHRA records for entries and spectators at the July nationals, and that April it hosted the fourth annual National College Drag Racing Championship, sponsored by the University of Texas at Arlington's intramural department.[^gvr] The AHRA meet was big enough to make the San Antonio paper: "The American Hot Rod Association will hold its national meet Friday through Sunday at Green Valley Race City in Smithfield."[^sa750704]

## The AHRA leaves (1977)

Then the AHRA left. In July 1977 Green Valley held its first NHRA Winston World Championship event, and the city page says that "After 13 years at Green Valley Race City, the American Hot Rod Association decides to move its national event to another facility."[^gvr] The *San Antonio Express-News*, whose city got the meet, gave a reason. "A reliable source said when Bill Hielscher, proprietor of the Fort Worth drag strip, started scheduling numerous National Hot Rod Association (NHRA) events, the AHRA decided to pull the nationals away from Green Valley," the paper's Mike Bruton wrote; the AHRA "seemed to frown on Hielscher's apparent conflict of interests."[^sa770619]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> A San Antonio racer, Buddy Cortinas, put it the racers' way: "It used to be a yearly pilgrimage to Green Valley on Labor Day weekend," and "The race fans at Green Valley were burned out."[^sa770619] The meet went to [Alamo Dragway](san-antonio-double-eagle-alamo-dragway.md#the-nationals-come-to-san-antonio-1977).

## Noise, ordinances and the last race (1977–86)

North Richland Hills was no longer a farm town, and the raceway's crowds tested it. On June 5, 1977, after a day of racing, Willie Nelson played a concert there. The city page says Police Chief Hamp Scruggs enforced the public gathering ordinance and cut the power "after Willie's second song," and angry fans tore up the concession tents and the fence in front of the stage.[^gvr]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> In April 1981 three NRH officers were suspended after staging a drag race at the track in their patrol cars, a stunt meant to improve relations between the raceway and the police: "At the green light, one patrol car took off down the track, and the other broke down at the starting line."[^gvr] That September a free concert and race for the radio station KTXQ drew 55,000 people, with arrests, two stabbings and complaints from neighbors, and the city council unanimously passed a tougher "mass gathering" ordinance with 13 requirements for promoters.[^gvr]

The end came in stages. Two fatal crashes three weeks apart in 1983 brought a negligence lawsuit; in October 1984 the aging road course held its last professional races, a Trans-Am and Can-Am weekend; and in February 1986 the NHRA's Winston series closed out "nearly a quarter century of championship racing."[^gvr] The city page says the final event, "the Last Race," ran on the weekend of June 15, 1986: "After a decade of noise complaints, the owners defiantly closed out the event with jet dragsters running deafening quarter-miles for everyone to revel in."[^gvr]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>

Houses cover the farm now. *Press Box DFW* traced the strip running south-southeast across today's Bursey Road, with the start line near North Tarrant Parkway and the finish near Green Valley Drive; only a bridge and some guard railing are left.[^pb] The city's Green Valley Raceway Historic Plaza, at 7904 N. Tarrant Parkway, asks the people who raced there for their stories.[^gvrhome]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Who owned the track after 1972?** The Historic Plaza page says it was sold to United Enterprises, Inc., with Hielscher and Toppletz as race coordinators. The NRHTXHistory entry says it "was bought by a racecar driver, Bill Hielscher, in 1972," and *Press Box DFW* says the Hielschers owned it in 1974. Hielscher may have been an owner of United Enterprises; we have not seen a deed or corporate record.[^gvr][^nrhtx][^pb] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How many saw Knievel jump?** The Historic Plaza page says "Over 35,000 spectators." Cosell said "Thirty thousand" on the air. *Press Box DFW* writes that "News reports from that day put the crowd closer to half that number," as did the officer at Cosell's side.[^gvr][^pb] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Why did the AHRA leave?** The conflict-of-interest reason comes from one unnamed "reliable source" in one 1977 newspaper story. The city page notes that Hielscher "had scheduled numerous events with NHRA sanctioning" but adds a different reason: "The AHRA had been suffering in recent years, offering fewer cars in each field."[^sa770619][^gvr] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Who pulled Willie Nelson's plug, and when?** The city page names Chief Scruggs and says "after Willie's second song." Shiflet remembered that Nelson "Got one song played and the city manager disconnected the power," and that the promoter who had pulled the permit was arrested.[^gvr][^pb] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When was the last race?** The Historic Plaza page gives the weekend of June 15, 1986; the NRHTXHistory entry says "The last race was in July, 1986."[^gvr][^nrhtx] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
