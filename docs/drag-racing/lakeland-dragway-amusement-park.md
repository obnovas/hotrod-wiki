# A Drag Strip by the Skyride: Lakeland Dragway, Memphis (1960–79)

*Memphis's strip was one ride in a developer's amusement park, and it outlasted the park*

## An insurance man's lake

Memphis's drag strip was not a club's and not a farmer's. It was one ride in a developer's amusement park, alongside a skyride and a paddle-wheeler. The park closed, the strip ran until 1979, and a town grew up where both had been.

The developer was Louis Garner, a World War II veteran and former prisoner of war who sold life insurance in Memphis. In 1958 he visited Ruby Falls, outside Chattanooga, and came home with plans for an amusement park on a lake. The Lakeland Development Corporation was formed on February 13, 1959, with Garner as president and 13 stockholders. It asked for a zoning variance for "750 acres lying in a wooded valley" east of Memphis, to hold "Disneyland-like educational-amusement devices." On July 4, 1959, the public was invited out to picnic on land that was still farms and woods, with no lake yet.[^ll] The dam went up that fall and the lake filled from Scotts Creek, springs and four artesian wells. Garner bought the Brussels World's Fair skyride, and it opened over a half-empty lake in August 1960.[^ll] Garner Lake is still the largest lake in Shelby County.[^rht]

## Dragways Inc. and five local racers

The local histories agree on a date for the strip: July 4, 1960.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^rht][^dm25] The *Daily Memphian* wrote in 2025 that it was "originally owned by the Lakeland Development Corp. that also owned the Lake Land Amusement Park."[^dm25] The company's own record, as Chip Averwater's timeline gives it, has a later start. On February 21, 1961, Garner announced construction of "a 3500 ft drag strip to be leased to Dragways Inc for operation," with a sports-car track and a motorcycle track planned and a go-cart track already running. On January 16, 1963, after two years, Dragways Inc. did not renew its lease, and the company leased the strip "to 5 local race enthusiasts."[^ll] The Shelby County heritage site calls Raymond Godman the strip's visionary.[^rht] Godman's partnership and the strip's first starter are told in [Memphis and the Mid-South](../places/memphis.md#ray-godman-and-lakeland-dragway-196079).

The park grew around the strip. Big bands played the Pepsi Dance Pavilion, and in 1963 one ticket bought every ride for $2.[^ll] Memphis heard the ads: "Sunday! Sunday! Sunday! Lakeland International dragway! I-40 at Canada Road!"[^dm25]

## The flagman and the chute

The racers remembered it as theirs. One wrote on the H.A.M.B. in 2008 that at 14, before it was built, he and a friend rode out on a Triumph motorcycle "just to look where the hallowed spot was going to be." Before Lakeland, he wrote, "we had to go to Halls,Tennessee or Carlise,Ark. Both of these were retired air strips." He remembered a '59 Plymouth with a long-ram engine run by a tugboat captain, a day when three Thunderbolts turned up, and Herb McCandless, a local boy he had watched street-drag a 280 Chevy before he was known as Mr. Four Speed. The flagman was Red Dyer, who, he wrote, also drove Godman's *Tennessee Bo-Weevil*, and Dyer pointed to the local driver last, so you knew the flag was coming up.[^hamb] Another fan, writing in 2010, met Tommy Ivo at Lakeland in 1969, when he was in the seventh grade. Gene Snow and Ivo showed him how to pack a drag chute, and he packed both of their cars' chutes.[^mpc]

In 1970 Monte Hellman brought *Two-Lane Blacktop* through Memphis. The film's long drag strip scene, the '55 Chevy prowling rows of race cars for a spot, was shot at Lakeland International Raceway, and a technical advisor on the film, Jay Wheatley, has a cameo with a Mopar at the start of it.[^hpp08][^l2lb] In 1972 Don Garlits set an AHRA national ET record there of 5.95 seconds, the county heritage site says.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^rht]

The heritage site has the amusement park closing in 1976 or 1977; it gives both years. The strip closed in 1979.[^rht][^dm25] Lakeland itself was incorporated in 1977, the only one of Shelby County's seven towns "to be founded from an amusement park beginnings," and it grew from 612 people in 1980 to more than 12,400 in 2010.[^rht] The track sat silent for nearly twenty years. In 2009 it was torn up for new development.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^l2lb][^rht] Garner had planned a skyride, a lake and a dance hall. The drag strip was one more attraction on the list, and it lasted longer than the park did.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Opened July 4, 1960, or built after February 1961?** The *Rural Heritage Trust* page and the *Daily Memphian* both say the strip opened on July 4, 1960, and this wiki's Memphis page, from other sources, says the same.[^rht][^dm25] The development company's timeline, compiled from its records and ads, has Garner announcing construction of the drag strip on February 21, 1961, and Dragways Inc. leaving after "two years of operation" in January 1963, which fits a 1961 start.[^ll] The company's public picnic on the empty land was on July 4, 1959.[^ll] The Memphis papers for July 1960 and February 1961 would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Garlits's 5.95 at Lakeland in 1972?** Only the county heritage site.[^rht] An AHRA record list for 1972 would confirm it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Houses or industry?** The film-locations study says the track was torn up in 2009 "for a residential development," and a racer on the H.A.M.B. in 2008 relayed a Memphis newspaper clipping saying it would become a subdivision. The heritage site says it was demolished in 2009 "to make way for mixed-use industrial development," with remnants left.[^l2lb][^hamb][^rht] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
