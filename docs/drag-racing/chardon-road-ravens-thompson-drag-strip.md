# Windshields on a Sunday: Chardon's Road Ravens and the Track of Champions at Thompson (1955–61)

*An optometrist's living room full of policemen, a club garage with a television in the lounge, and the Swamp Rat on Ohio's longest straight*

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "Prospective members are expected to prove their interest in promoting safe, courteous and sportsmanlike driving."

    — the Road Ravens Hot Rod Club's membership rule, as the *Geauga Record* printed it (March 1, 1956).[^gr560301]

On a Wednesday evening in February 1956, the law came to Dr. Louis Berta's house in Chardon, the Geauga County seat east of Cleveland. Sheriff John Phelps was there, with Chardon patrolman Joseph Holowaty, Charles White of the Painesville police and Al Hewis of the Ohio State Highway Patrol. So were the officers of the Road Ravens Hot Rod Club, which Berta, a Chardon optometrist, had advised since October: president Earl Stoltz, vice president Bruce Erickson, secretary Walt Asuma, treasurer Tom Adams and sergeant at arms Jim Thrasher. The meeting was "to promote better relations and understanding of the hot rod movement," the *Geauga Record* reported, and they talked about safety, curbing speed and "show off" driving.[^gr560301]

The club had been "fully organized in August, 1955." Two officers of a newly organized club from Painesville, the Goldenrods, sat in "to observe the club procedure." The Ravens were planning a Parents' Night and a dance at the Chardon Roller Rink, and membership was "open to anyone interested in the hot rod movement," so long as he could prove his interest in safe driving. "The Road Ravens are associated with the National Hot Rod Association of Los Angeles, California," the paper added.[^gr560301]

## A garage with a lounge

The sheriff kept the connection. Running for re-election that spring and again in the fall, E. John Phelps listed it in his campaign ads: "I have always been interested in young people and continue to work with them, as I have with the Road Ravens, newly formed organization of boys who seek to cut down traffic accidents and violations."[^gr560503][^gr561101]

By December 1956 the club had its own garage, next to the junior high school auditorium on East Park Street, and it invited the public in for an open house on the 18th, with other area clubs as guests. "The Road Ravens are trying to prove by example that hot rodding can be a beneficial pastime for every teenage boy whose main interest in life is cars," the *Record* wrote. They spent many evenings there working on their rods, and a separate "lounge" area held a television set, a radio and a record player, where members could "share ideas both mechanical and social."[^gr561206]

In March 1957 the paper ran a photograph of Ray Miller and Bruce Erickson wiping a windshield. The Ravens, "a club of young men interested in automobiles," had spent part of a Sunday cleaning the windshields of cars parked in and around Chardon "as a safety measure," with Earl Stoltz, Joe Sears, William Boyles, William Atkins, Fred Quigley, Ronald Balslay, Frank Steinford and William Pomeroy along.[^gr570328]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## The Track of Champions

Just east of Chardon, at Routes 528 and 166 in Thompson Township, Geauga County got a strip of its own.[^gr610817]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> By the summer of 1961 Thompson Drag Strip was running every Saturday night and buying ads in the *Record*. One, in late July, promised "WORLD'S FASTEST DRAGSTERS," with "World Champion DON GARLITS racing his 200 mph SWAMP RAT" on Saturday night and, all day Sunday, the "Drag Master Two-Thing" of Dode Martin and Tom Nelson in an "Exclusive Ohio appearance."[^gr610727]

Garlits came back in August for his "final appearance of the season in Northern Ohio." The *Record*'s story gave the Swamp Rat the official world speed record of 189.45 mph and a Thompson track record of 182.45, said it had been custom built "in his own speed shop in Tampa, Florida," and reported that he had just beaten Art Malone in a Michigan match race at 191 mph. It also explained why he came: "Garlits chose Thompson Drag Strip for his Northern Ohio appearances because Thompson has Ohio's longest straight track, 4,000 feet over-all, allowing more room for safer stops at the end of the 1320-ft. run." There would be "no increase in admission," and the ad beside it, for a $3,000 invitational the next weekend, called the place "The Track of Champions."[^gr610817]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>

## Still racing

Thompson kept racing. Cleveland's gasser crowd ran there in the 1960s ([The Ohio Gassers](../places/ohio-gassers.md)), and by 2014 *The News-Herald* could call it "the oldest continuously running drag strip in Ohio," on 200 acres of pasture, with a weekly bracket program and Wednesday-night grudge racing.[^nh14]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> "It's an old school track," said Ed Kasicki of Aurora, who brought his supercharged 1933 Willys to the Gassers Reunion there. "It's close knit. Everybody knows everybody."[^nh14]

The brothers Tom and Kim Kuhnle bought it in December 2021 and renamed it Kuhnle Motorsports Park.[^hem24] In February 2024 the owners announced that the track was closed and up for lease or sale.[^hem24] By December 2025 it was running nostalgia gasser races and semi-truck racing, and the NHRA welcomed it to its Member Track Network. "When I got my driver's license at 16, Thompson Drag Raceway, now Kuhnle Motorsports Park, was one of the first places I would go," Kim Kuhnle said.[^ds25]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did Thompson open: 1958 or 1960?** Performance Racing Industry (2019) and the NHRA's 2025 announcement say it first opened in 1958 as Thompson Drag Raceway; Hemmings (2024) says it was "founded" in 1958 but "has been in operation since July 24, 1960."[^pri19][^ds25][^hem24] The 2014 *News-Herald* headline counted "56 years," which also points to 1958.[^nh14] The *Geauga Record*'s 1961 pages call it Thompson Drag Strip and "Thompson Speedway Drag Strip" but give no opening date.[^gr610727][^gr610817] The *Record* for 1958–60 should settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Is Thompson Ohio's oldest drag strip?** Performance Racing Industry calls it "the oldest drag strip in the Buckeye State"; *The News-Herald* says "the oldest continuously running"; Hemmings, more carefully, says "one of the longest continuously operated drag strips in Ohio."[^pri19][^nh14][^hem24] Salem's [Quaker City Drag Strip](quaker-city-salem.md) is dated to 1957 by its owners (a date disputed on that page), a year before Thompson's earliest claimed opening, so only the narrower claim, continuous operation, can stand. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Thompson "launch" Don Garlits's career?** Performance Racing Industry says the track "helped launch the career of such drag racing icons as 'Big Daddy' Don Garlits and 'TV' Tommy Ivo."[^pri19] The 1961 *Record* already calls Garlits world champion and the holder of track records at Thompson and 100 other strips, so in 1961 he came as a star, not a beginner.[^gr610817] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **What became of the Road Ravens after 1957?** The *Geauga Record* pages we found end with the windshield Sunday in March 1957;[^gr570328] whether the club raced at Thompson, and when it folded, is not yet known. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
