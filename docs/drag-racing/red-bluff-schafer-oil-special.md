# "A Little Town Near the Oregon Border": the Schafer Oil Special and the Redding Airport Drags (Red Bluff and Redding, Calif., 1968–71)

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> small-city daily (*Red Bluff Daily News*, Red Bluff, Calif.): sports-page photos, meet previews and results, and the sports editor's first-person column, 1968–71, read as scanned page OCR · **Last verified:** 2026-10-22 (Round 22-West) · **Open issues:** the Bakersfield results are the local paper's own account and have not been checked against a national result list ◐; whether Red Bluff ever got its own strip ? · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "Now folks, let's see who it will be, the youngster from Red Bluff, or the old pros."

    — The Bakersfield announcer before the junior fuel final, as heard by *Daily News* sports editor Bob Kennedy, March 5, 1969.[^rb690305]

Red Bluff is the seat of Tehama County, in the upper Sacramento Valley south of Redding. In the late 1960s its small daily had a local drag racing team to follow, a father and son with an oil company name on the car. It also had a sports editor who followed them to Bakersfield to watch them race the biggest names in the sport.

## The Shasta Roadsters' runway (1968)

The team's home strip was a runway. In August 1968 the *Daily News* ran a photo of Jack Schafer making a final check of the fuel system and carburetors on the **Schafer Oil Company Special** before "the annual Labor Day Drag Races Sunday and Monday at the Redding Municipal Airport," sponsored by the **Shasta Roadsters**. The driver, Garvin Tonkin of Red Bluff, said he hoped to reach about 190 mph. More than 40 dragsters were coming "from all over the state," along with stock cars in various classes, and the Northern California Drag Racers Association was holding a points meet the same Monday.[^rb680831] Afterward the paper reported that "Jack and Bob Schafer didn't win much money at Redding, which is unusual for these boys."[^rb680903]

## Bakersfield (1969)

The next February the paper's photo caption said the Schafer Oil Special would "represent the city of Red Bluff" at the 11th annual Bakersfield Fuel and Gas Championships. It was a Chevrolet-powered junior fuel dragster that burned nitromethane and alcohol, and it had already run the quarter mile at the Redding strip "in less than eight second[s]." Jack and Bob Schafer were the owners and Tonkin the driver.[^rb690220]

Sports editor Bob Kennedy followed them south and wrote it up in his column, "Drag Racing World Hears From Red Bluff." He described the car as "a 302 cubic inch Chevrolet engine powered rail owned by the father and son team of Bob and Jack Schafer and driven by Garvin Tonkin," and George Santos had built the engine. It was one of more than 600 entries at the meet, which drew "40,000 plus people," and the field included Mickey Thompson, Don Garlits and Don Prudhomme. The team hoped only to be one of 16 of the 60 junior fuel cars to qualify. Tonkin qualified fourth, at 196.65 mph and 7.77 seconds. On the first day the announcer kept calling Red Bluff "a little town near the Oregon border." After that run, Kennedy wrote, it was the "Dark Horse" from Red Bluff.[^rb690305]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

Kennedy had seen plenty of local racing: "I have been to many drag races in Redding, Vina, and Sacramento, but compared to those, Bakersfield would be like comparing a Cadillac to a Volkswagen." In eliminations Tonkin beat Mike Clancy at 190.67 mph and then Bob Williams at 193.45 mph and 7.85 seconds. Burkey and Neal, the second-fastest qualifiers, red-lighted in the third round. That put the Red Bluff car in the final against Bill Tidwell, and the announcer called it as quoted at the top of this page. "Well, It was the old pros taking it." Tidwell got the jump at the start and won at 195.22 mph and 7.867 seconds. Tonkin ran a quicker elapsed time, 7.829 seconds at 194.80 mph, but finished behind. Tonkin was the only one disappointed: "he was caught napping at the line." The others in the pits, the Schafers, Ruth Tonkin and George Santos, were happy with the result.[^rb690305]

## Back on the runway (1969–71)

Two weeks later the Shasta Roadsters opened their 1969 season at the Redding Municipal Airport drag strip with "racers from California, Oregon, and Nevada," nine eliminator classes, a $2,000 purse and $400 in trophies, and the club expected more than 600 entries.[^rb690314] Don Argee of Sacramento, in a blown Chrysler AA/Fueler, beat Tonkin in the top eliminator final at 197.80 mph. The meet "brought one of the largest crowds in the strip's history."[^rb690317]

The strip was still a shared runway. A May 1970 feature on a sailplane contest at the airport described campers lined up along "the far runway that is used occasionally as a drag strip by the Redding Roadsters."[^rb700525] Red Bluff had no strip of its own. In December 1971 the Red Bluff Jaycees announced that Tehama County Sheriff Lyle Williams would talk to them about "the possibilities of constructing a drag strip and round track in Red Bluff."[^rb711230]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

Northern California clubs also hunted for strips at [Modesto](modesto-century-toppers.md) and in [Marin and Cotati](marin-cotati-strips.md). Redding had a runway the club could borrow, and Red Bluff had a local team that took its runway car to Bakersfield.

## Open questions & disputed details

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** *All the Bakersfield numbers come from Kennedy's column. We have not checked them against a published result list (for example in* Drag News *or* National Dragster*). The same issue's photo caption muddies two of them: it says Tonkin "defeated three other dragsters to qualify for the number-one position," while also repeating the column's "fourth fastest qualifying time," and it gives his best speed of the meet as 195.63 mph, against the column's 196.65 mph qualifying run. The caption has him losing the final "by less than one second"; the column says he was "right behind him at the finish line." "Shasta Roadsters" (1968–69) and "Redding Roadsters" (1970) are probably the same club.* <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
