# The Dragstrip That Didn't Want to Die: Dover Drag Strip, Wingdale, N.Y. (1961–1976)

*By the strip's own account, a gas station owner saw one day of drag racing and bought a farm the next, and fifty years after the gates closed his racers still hold a reunion*

## A day at Montgomery

Dover Drag Strip began with one man watching one meet. Chet Anderson ran a gas station in Brookfield, Conn. In 1959 a friend took him to the airport at Montgomery, N.Y., where hot rodders were running timed quarter-mile races with help from NHRA's Safety Safari. By the strip's own history page, Anderson rode over with Mark Mastriani of the Danbury Modifiers, in Mastriani's '55 Chevy. Mastriani raced. Anderson "disappeared for the day," the page says. He was "checking it all out."[^dds]

The next day he went looking for somewhere to build his own. He had met a man named Frank Marrata at Montgomery and asked him to be partners on a strip in Connecticut. Marrata said no, though the page says he later built Connecticut Dragway at East Haddam.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Open question: see Open questions">?</a></sup>[^dds] Anderson found 144 acres of farmland across the line in Wingdale, N.Y., and bought it. He needed the ground cleared and graded, and that was Joe Archiere's job. Archiere had the heavy equipment.[^dds]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

The track opened on May 14, 1961, and the Danbury Modifiers were its crew and its inspectors.[^dds] Someone had slept at the gate to be first in. By the history page, it was a pair camping in a '59 Pontiac. At daylight somebody thumped on the trunk lid to wake James "Grover" Grove, who became the first paid customer and still has his ticket. The one doing the thumping was Mastriani.[^dds]

## Sunday at Dover

It cost $1 to get in and $3 for a pit pass, a woman who went as a young teenager told NHRA's Phil Burgess in 2008. Afterward the racers went to Josey's Tavern on the Ten Mile River, down the road in Wingdale.[^nhra08] The crew was a small-town one, and the history page names it. Charles Van Muren, "Van," managed first. Tim Hallock took over in 1962, Mastriani ran it until 1965, and Joe Tanner ran it until 1973. Christ Swift was the last manager. George Hosford was the original starter and was known for his acrobatics with the flag. The announcers went through Harry "Blue Goose" Loper, Ralph "Chink" Butera and Gary Teto before Dean "Dino" Lawrence, who the page says called races from 1965 to 1972.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^dds] Frank Rice and Mike Mannion ran and repaired the clocks, and Mannion had married Archiere's niece. The American Legion, not the Lions Club, ran the food stand.[^dds]

Garlits ran Dover four times, and the strip's own photos show it.[^nyt10] Al Baran of Connecticut built a Corvette-powered Model T there in 1967 from a pile of scrounged parts, among them a '49 Ford axle he dragged out of a farmer's field. It ran low 12s at 111 mph. "We had so much fun at Dover," he told the *Times*. "Those were simpler times." Then he was drafted.[^nyt10]

Dover was sanctioned by NHRA for only one year, the history page says. A "precarious drop off the left side of the shut down area and inadequate guard rails wouldn't meet NHRAs insurance standards."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^dds] It did not seem to matter to the crowd. By the same page, "Dover became the place to be for racers from five states."[^dds]

## Fifty years of reunions

The track closed in 1976.[^dds][^nyt10] Lawrence said it was because "the track owners were not keeping up with the competition." They would have had to take "the next step into public relations, advertising and sponsorships."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^nyt10] Lawrence, by then a sign-shop owner in North Carolina, built a website for the strip in 2008. By 2010 it held more than 3,500 historic photos and had 750 members, one of them in New Zealand. Former Dover cars had turned up in Great Britain and Sweden.[^nyt10]

The site was where the retired racers found each other. In 2009 Lawrence held the first Dover reunion race at Island Dragway in New Jersey, and 197 "old Dover guys" came. One brought a car that had not run in 39 years, and its times were as fast as they had been back then.[^nyt10] A Labor Day reunion at the Marcus Dairy car show in Danbury, Conn., was by then a 20-year tradition.[^nyt10] By 2021 the Dover Drag Strip Nostalgia Drags were in their 13th year, three days at Lebanon Valley Dragway in New York, with a car show, an East Coast Fuelers dragster class and a "Dover Reunion Back Up Girl" contest.[^dra21]

A few small pieces of the old strip's story survive in forum posts. One H.A.M.B. member wrote in 2008 that his New Jersey club, the Motormounts of Maplewood, used to volunteer at Montgomery. Its job was to make sure every car in competition had its hubcaps off and its lug nuts tight. The volunteers got in free, and "in 1960, that was a good deal."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-6"><a href="#q-6" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^hamb08] A man from Ridgefield, Conn., wrote that he went to Dover once, in 1961 at age 10, and still has his ticket stub.[^hamb08] The first customer kept his ticket. So did a ten-year-old. A drag strip that closed in 1976 is still being kept alive by the people who raced there.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did Dino Lawrence start announcing?** The strip's history page says he announced "from 65' to 72'."[^dds] Lawrence told the *Times* in 2010 that he was the announcer "from 1967 to 1972," in the reporter's words.[^nyt10] The end year agrees. The start does not. Lawrence is also the history page's author, so the two may simply reflect different memories of the same man. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Anderson's day at Montgomery.** The story of the gas station owner, the '55 Chevy, the Marrata turn-down and the 144 acres is told only on the strip's own history page. A shorter version appears in a 2008 H.A.M.B. post by a man who had been given photographs and a club plaque by Bill Babyak, who wrote a Danbury Modifiers history for a 2004 Dover reunion program. The post does not say that is where its version comes from, and it is not an independent account.[^dds][^hamb08] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Was Dover sanctioned for only one year?** The claim is on the strip's own history page. NHRA's 2008 *National Dragster* column repeats it, and says the claim is "according to the site," so it is not a second source.[^dds][^nhra08] An NHRA track list for 1961–1964 would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Why did Dover close?** Only Lawrence's 2010 statement to the *Times*.[^nyt10] A search excerpt of a *Greenwich Time* piece on Dover memories seemed to mention fuel shortages and insurance, but the article is behind a paywall (HTTP 402) and was not read, so nothing from it is used here. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Who was the man at Montgomery?** The history page spells him "Frank Marrata" and says he later built Connecticut Dragway; the H.A.M.B. post spells him "Frank Moratta."[^dds][^hamb08] This wiki's [Connecticut article](connecticut-clubs-nutmeg-state-timing-association.md) names a Hartford body-shop owner who led the push for that strip as Frank Maratta. They are very likely the same man, but no source we read says so. A Hartford directory or an obituary would fix the spelling. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-6" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **The Motormounts at Montgomery.** One forum post, with no date given for the volunteering beyond the writer's "in 1960, that was a good deal."[^hamb08] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-6" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
