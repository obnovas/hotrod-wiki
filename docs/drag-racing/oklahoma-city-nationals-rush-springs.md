# A Watermelon Farmer's Dragster: Melvin Heath, the Fairgrounds Nationals and Oklahoma's Hometown Strips (1954–1966)

*A national champion from a peanut-and-watermelon farm, an engine from a barn in Marlow, and the Nationals on a strip the Jaycees built*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (Heath's career and the 1957–58 Nationals rest on NHRA and *Hot Rod*; the barn engine, the Enid club and the small strips rest on racers' and relatives' own posts, none yet matched to an Oklahoma newspaper) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> racers' and relatives' recollections (H.A.M.B. forum; *Drag Strip List* memories), an Enid funeral home obituary, a 1957 event program cover (AHRF), an Oklahoma City history blog, NHRA (2016, 2025), *Hot Rod* (July 1958) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-03 (Round 31: 1958 entrants from North Dakota and New Mexico added) · **Open issues:** ⚠ 1957 entry count (450 vs 465); ◐ who built Heath's engine; ◐ the Kagelmann and Lake Murray strips have one recollection each · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "my great uncle george woolever built the engine in it in his barn in marlow, ok."

    — connerj, on Melvin Heath's dragster, in a H.A.M.B. thread of 1957 Oklahoma City photos.[^hambokc]

Rush Springs farmer Melvin Heath won the 1956 Nationals with a homebuilt dragster and became NHRA's second national champion. George Woolever built the engine in a barn in Marlow, a family post says. A drag strip the Jaycees built at the Oklahoma City fairgrounds held the next two Nationals. Around that, Oklahoma raced an airstrip near Jet, a dusty road at Ardmore, and a Sunday runway.

His whole life, Melvin Heath farmed the family place at Rush Springs, in Grady County, raising "everything from peanuts, alfalfa, watermelons, and wheat to cattle." He had an animal science degree from Oklahoma State and, his family says, sat for more than 50 years on the Grady County Soil Conservation Board. He also "had an ingenious mind and a unique ability to take ideas and make them into practical machinery to use on his farm."[^heath]

A dragster is where those skills went. It first appeared in 1954 at the first Drag Safari meet in Caddo Mills, Texas, where Heath won Open Gas and was runner-up in Top Eliminator. Then Heath and his wife, Betty, "spent the next three months improving their race car," a 331-cubic-inch Chrysler Hemi with six Stromberg carburetors on nitro. After a disappointing 1955 Nationals at Great Bend, it all came together in 1956 at the Nationals in Kansas City (see [Kansas City Timing Association](kansas-city-timing-association.md)). Heath, "a known holeshot artist," beat Don Little wire to wire with a 10.49 and became NHRA's second national champion.[^heath] The car is in the Wally Parks NHRA Motorsports Museum in Pomona.

NHRA calls it a "homebuilt dragster."[^heath] Woolever's great-nephew passes on a family story that puts more hands on it. The forum member connerj wrote that "my great uncle george woolever built the engine in it in his barn in marlow, ok," and that Woolever raced a 1941 Lincoln Continental of his own.[^hambokc]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> Only that post says who built the engine. Lined up against that Lincoln, a Tulsa racer answered that Woolever "had leave down pat way before anybody else," and that losing to him was "when i learned the the differnce between driver reaction time and car reaction time."[^hambokc]

## The Nationals at the fairgrounds, 1957–58

Oklahoma City's new state fairgrounds, on the old Sandlot ballpark land west of May Avenue, held its first fair on Sept. 25, 1954. Built with it were grandstands, an oval track and, just south, "a drag strip which hosted the National Championship Drag Races in 1957-1958."[^fatokie] The town's young businessmen got the credit from *Hot Rod*. The strip, 3,800 feet long, "was built by the Jaycees and is operated as their major project of the year," with the junior chamber of commerce and the Okla City Timing Association as hosts. It sat "only two miles from the center of Oklahoma City."[^hr58]

Aug. 30 through Sept. 2, 1957, is the date the cover of the 1957 program gives the Third Annual NHRA National Championship Drags.[^ahrf] The "ample paved pit areas, separate spectator and participant parking, a cafeteria, and more," are what NHRA's history praises, and it counts 450 entrants despite that year's new fuel ban. Some 40,000 people over four days, and 465 cars, is what *Hot Rod* counted.[^firstnat][^hr58]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> The two counts do not agree. The Money Oldsmobile Special, with Buddy Sampson, won at 10.42. Entries in 1958 were capped at 500 and filled a month early. Top Eliminator went to Ted Cyr at 10.04 in his spare car, a blue Chrysler dragster with "For Sale" lettered on its nose in shoe polish.[^firstnat]

Other things were what the people in the stands remembered. "Was pretty much like heaven to a 17 year old kid," one forum member wrote of 1957. Another recalled that the Texas fuel racer Bobby Langley won an award for the best-appearing car and crew, in uniforms his wife, Ruth Ann, had made.[^hambokc] (Langley also raced at [Green Valley Raceway](green-valley-raceway-smithfield.md) and [Houston's Freeway Dragstrip](houston-freeway-dragstrip.md).) The job of driving Paul Grimmett's 1939 Simca with a 1951 Cadillac engine fell to Bill Honea, then 23, in 1958. "when everyone else tried it and couldn't make it go, I finally got up enough nerve to try it." He got it to 120 mph and 11.20 in A/C.[^dslok] Still on the thread is one member's 2016 question. Their father, Bill Mangum, raced a '34 Ford at Oklahoma City in 1957 and won trophies, but "my family has no pictures left." Another member's offer to ask his own dad is the only reply so far.[^hambokc]

A very long way is where some of them came from. Bruce Olson, in late August 1958, flat-towed his Hemi-powered 1932 Ford coupe from Beach, North Dakota, to the fourth Nationals, and ran 13.40 at 107 mph in B Altered Coupe.[^olsonok] Larry Sanchez, president of the Duster's Hot Rod Club at Walker Air Force Base in Roswell, New Mexico, raced "at all the local strips and Oklahoma City Nationals," a club officer remembered, without saying which year.[^dslnmok] (Their home scenes: [North Dakota](north-dakota-minot-borrowed-strips.md) and [New Mexico](new-mexico-timing-association-albuquerque.md).)

Far from the Nationals is where most Oklahoma racing happened. Up in Enid, a member posted a club's clippings in 2017: the Playboys Rod and Custom Club, with the message "if you think hot rodding in the fifties was limited to the west coast check it out." Kagelmann, "a WWII era air strip by the Great Salt Plains near Jet OK." is where its 1957 photos came from. The poster, G V Gordon, grew up a mile south of Vance Air Force Base and rode his bike to the drags there, where he saw Eddie Hill's twin-engine Pontiac dragster run.[^hambenid]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> No race dates are in the clippings. Weekends, his obituary says, Enid used-car dealer Jim Lewis spent "drag racing with friends just outside the Enid city limits" in '55–'57 Chevys and early '60s Corvettes, and he won dozens of trophies. In the 1960s and 1970s he was "one of very few dealers in Oklahoma that sold high performance muscle and collector cars."[^lewis]

Ardmore's first strip ran on Lake Murray Drive, where the city's airport is now. 1957 is Monroe Cameron's first memory of it. "only the starting line was paved," and his best friend's dad "sprayed water on the strip to keep the dust down." Art Shaw, who had an automotive shop on Broadway, was the owner when the strip later moved to Springer, where Cameron first saw racing in 1963.[^dslok]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> On when it opened, there is one recollection, from 1957. Rob Ritter, in the early 1960s, raced on the runway at Clinton-Sherman Air Force Base at Burns Flat, "a Sunday event, 1/4 mile with plenty of shut down room," which he thinks was just called the Burns Flat Drags. In Tulsa, Duane Walker ran a Corvair Monza at Tulsa North Airport, where there was no light system, "just a person with a flag."[^dslok] Sunday, Nov. 6, 1960, is the date a pit pass and a *Duncan Banner* advertisement place a race at Duncan.[^dslok]

Willow Run opened on the east side of Oklahoma City in August 1966, still under construction, and the touring stars were coming to Oklahoma. Opening day, in Doug Waggoner's memory, had Eddie Hill, Mike Sorokin with the Surfers, Bobby Langley and Gene Snow.[^dslok] For the teenagers in Tulsa, though, the street was still where it started. See [The Restless Ribbon](../places/brookside-restless-ribbon-tulsa.md).

A watermelon farmer from Rush Springs became NHRA's second national champion, and the engine, on one family post, came out of a barn in Marlow. The next two Nationals ran on the strip the Jaycees built. The rest of the racing was an airstrip, a dusty road, and a Sunday runway. Heath died on Feb. 17, 2025, at 94, and his services were in Rush Springs.[^heath]

## Open questions & disputed details

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How many cars ran the 1957 Nationals: 450 or 465?** NHRA's 2016 history says 450 entrants; *Hot Rod*'s July 1958 preview says 465 cars were "entered in active competition."[^firstnat][^hr58] The program lists the pre-entries and could settle it.[^ahrf] (The forum member who posted the photos guessed "October of 1957"; the program cover's Aug. 30–Sept. 2 is the date we use.)[^hambokc] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
