# Hao Wela at Kahuku: Oahu's Airfield Drags and the Road to Hawaii Raceway Park (1949–64)

*A timing club without a road, a farmer's runway at the top of the island, airmen racing on Sunday and a kid from Pacific Heights who left for California*

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "We'd put it in Saturday night, and race Sunday. And beat everybody, including that Caddy '32 coupe."

    — LeRoy Noker, an airman at Hickam Air Force Base who raced at Kahuku in 1956–58, as his son Shawn passed his memory to DragStripList.com.[^dsl]

"Pronounced 'how vella,' hao wela is the Hawaiian word for hot rod," *Hot Rod* explained in 2018, adding that drag racing in Hawaii "has been as popular as anywhere in the U.S. since years before Hawaii was a state."[^hr]

The first organized try came in Honolulu. In 1949, Kustomrama records from *Best Hot Rods* (1952), the Hono-Kai Timing Association formed with about 20 members. "A stretch of Navy roadway was set aside for drags," and one member built a timer for quarter-mile clocking. Within three months the club had over 100 members. Then "The authorities refused to cooperate and assign a spot for racing, so the activity ended." In June 1951 the club put on the Honolulu Hot Rod Show at the Civic Auditorium "in order to help gain public favor."[^kr]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> The Jalopy Journal, which found the same small article, called it "A grass roots effort to get the sport going on the islands."[^jj09]

## A farmer's runway at the top of the island

The racers found their spot at the north tip of Oahu. Kahuku Point had been the northernmost of three wartime airfields on that side of the island; after the war, The Jalopy Journal writes, "the property was leased to a farmer." Jimmy Pflueger and Walter Dick, hot rodders who "needed a place to race their cars that didn't result in a jail cell afterwards," made a deal with him, and they and their pals were able to do a little "somewhat legal" drag racing. "By 1957, the idea had taken off and the Hawaiian Motor Timing Association was formed to keep things on the level."[^jj20]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> The local history blog that lists Oahu's tracks gives Kahuku's years as 1954–1964 and describes "two paved runways and a taxiway paralleling the northern runway."[^hon]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>

The Kolea Racing Team, Buddy Hughes, Eddie Sorenson and Pflueger, "ran the first dragster constructed in Hawaii."[^hr] Servicemen raced too. LeRoy Noker, stationed at Hickam Air Force Base from 1956 to 1958, remembered his team's car to the part: "We raced in the A-gas street coupe class. We raced a 1934 Ford coupe with a Chrysler Hemi, six Stromberg 97's on top, a Flame-Thrower ignition, and we built our own headers." It ran a LaSalle transmission and an Iskenderian cam, and "we ran 110 in the quarter at Kahuku." His Hawaiian Timing Association membership card survives.[^dsl]

The strip grew serious enough that mainland racers such as Tommy Ivo and Jim Nelson shipped their cars over, "using the strip as an excuse for a Hawaiian vacation."[^jj20] It also had its cost. In 1958 Henry Lee lost control of his dragster at the far end and hit a tree at 140 mph; he died, "the first recorded casualty of the strip."[^jj20]

## Leong's record and the new park

Roland Leong was born in May 1944 at Queen's Hospital and grew up in Pacific Heights; his first "race car" was the family car.[^sa24] His own summary of Kahuku: "I was young and all we cared about was racing. Set the track record in 1964 at 8:50 @ 180. Fastest at the time. Then won the opening race at Hawaii Raceway Park."[^dsl]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> He moved to California for good in 1964 and became a top fuel owner and tuner, "The Hawaiian."[^sa24]

The new park was Pflueger's. By *Hot Rod*'s account, "in 1964 Jimmy Pflueger gathered a group of Honolulu doctors together and funded the construction of Hawaii Raceway Park."[^hr]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> It went up in an old Ewa sugar cane area at what became Campbell Industrial Park, on land leased from the James Campbell Estate; $225,000 was spent on its construction.[^hon] Pflueger brought in Tommy Greer, Keith Black and Don Prudhomme to make demonstration runs with their dragster, and there Leong and Prudhomme met.[^sa24] "By late 1964, Kahuka [sic] Point Airfield held its last event," The Jalopy Journal writes; a hotel, golf course and wildlife refuge occupy the old field today.[^jj20][^hon]

The new strip had its own regulars. Roger Ramos of the Leeward Racing Club ran a Chrysler-powered '33 Plymouth there and held the A/G record at 115.66 mph in 1964–65, DragStripList's editor found in the Honolulu papers; his daughter Rita (Ramos) Casil wrote that "My dad used to always talk about his race cars and the times he raced."[^dsl]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-6"><a href="#q-6" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>

Pflueger himself, born March 5, 1926, was by 30 "one of Hawaii's foremost sportsmen," won a 1962 Jeep race up Mauna Kea, and in 1969 became the first Honda dealer in the United States. "Jimmy had a love affair with motorized vehicles — a love affair with machines," his nephew said when he died in 2017 at 91. His obituary also records the jail time he served over the deaths of seven people killed by a flood from a Kauai reservoir he altered.[^sa17] Hawaii Raceway Park closed in 2006, when its lease ran out.[^hon]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did racing at Kahuku begin?** *Hot Rod* says "shortly after World War II";[^hr] The Jalopy Journal says that "by 1953" drag racing had reached Oahu and that one of the first organized events was at Kahuku;[^jj20] the Kanehili/Honouliuli list gives 1954.[^hon] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Was Leong's 1964 record 8.50 or 8.58?** Leong himself wrote "8:50 @ 180";[^dsl] The Jalopy Journal says he "pulled an 8.58/180mph slip from the timing booth."[^jj20] His claim to have won the opening race at Hawaii Raceway Park is his alone so far. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did Hawaii Raceway Park open in 1962, 1963 or 1964?** The Kanehili/Honouliuli list gives its "Years of Operation" as 1964–2006,[^hon] and *Hot Rod* says that "in 1964 Jimmy Pflueger gathered a group of Honolulu doctors together and funded the construction" of the park, which dates the funding and building rather than the first race.[^hr] Leong's 2024 obituary says "In 1963, Hawaii businessman and racing promoter Jimmy Pflueger opened Hawaii Raceway Park in Ewa."[^sa24] The Jalopy Journal gives no year; it says only that "not long after Roland's record setting run a new drag strip was completed," which, with its 1964 date for that run, points to 1964.[^jj20] The 1962 date is our inference: a 2015 *Star-Advertiser* story quoted on the history blog says the park closed in 2006 "after 44 years at Campbell Industrial Park."[^hon] The blog's "Years of Operation" lines follow DragStripList's format and may not be independent of it. A Honolulu paper from 1963–64 should settle the opening day. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Were the Hawaiian Timing Association and the Hawaiian Motor Timing Association the same club?** Noker's card, from 1956–58, reads Hawaiian Timing Association;[^dsl] The Jalopy Journal dates the Hawaiian Motor Timing Association to 1957.[^jj20] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did the Hono-Kai Timing Association race on a Navy road in 1949?** Everything we have comes from one 1952 annual, *Best Hot Rods*, as summarized by Kustomrama and shared by The Jalopy Journal; we have not read the original.[^kr][^jj09] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-6" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Roger or Rudy Ramos, '32 or '33 Plymouth?** His daughter calls him Roger and his car a '32 Plymouth; the editor's note says '33, and the two newspaper captions DragStripList reproduces (1965) call him Rudy Ramos.[^dsl] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-6" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
