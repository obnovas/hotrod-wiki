# Corn Through the Track: the Simons' M-N Raceway, Wichita (1963– )

*A farm family put Wichita's drag strip on its sandiest field, and never sold it*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (the founding comes from the family, through a 2024 *Wichita Eagle* interview with the founders' granddaughter, Helen Simon's 2022 obituary and the track's own history; the opening year and the club's dates disagree) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> local news (*Wichita Eagle*, 2013, 2016 and 2024); obituary (Baker Funeral Home, Valley Center, 2022); a club member's local-history column (*Ark Valley News*, 2024); a county racing-venue list (*Kansas Racing History*); the track's own page; *Drag Illustrated* (2024) and *RacingJunk News* (2026) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-13 (Round 40) · **Open issues:** ⚠ first races in 1963 or 1964; ⚠ a club of 1954–61 helping at a strip of 1963; ◐ McConnell AFB in 1958; ? what "M-N" stood for · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "Even having it right there, we didn't spend a lot of time down there. We were out in the field."

    — Gwen Kelley, granddaughter of the founders, *The Wichita Eagle*, April 1, 2024.[^ke24]

## Neighbors at the door

Wichita's drag strip is a farm field. A farm family let the racers have it in 1963, kept farming around it, and still owns it more than sixty years later. That is not the usual way a hometown strip ends, and so far it has not ended.

Before it, Wichita's hot rodders raced where they could. A county list of racing venues has a quarter-mile runway strip at McConnell Air Force Base in 1958.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^krh] North of town in Valley Center, the Mill'ers Hot Rod and Custom Club ran from 1954 to 1961. Bill Arick, one of its members, wrote in 2024 that it belonged to the NHRA, the AHRA and the Southern Kansas Timing Association, handed out courtesy cards, sponsored car shows in Wichita and had a club car for the drags. All the members worked on the car, cleaned up the shop and helped in the pits. He wrote that the club helped at the M-N Raceway.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^avn24]

The Simons farmed northwest of Wichita, near 61st Street North and Ridge Road. In 1963, Gwen Kelley told *The Wichita Eagle* in 2024, "a couple of neighbors" came to her great-grandparents about putting a track on their land. That land was going to be divided among the family, and the track could not cross tracts owned by different people. So it went entirely on the property of her grandparents, Marcellus "Sally" Simon and his wife, Helen. It was sandy ground where not much grew anyway. "Still, corn sometimes popped up through the track."[^ke24] *RacingJunk News* adds that the field ran along the Arkansas River and stretched more than half a mile from the road to the river.[^rj26]

## Buckets and sand hills

The track's own history gives 1963 as the founding, and Helen Simon's obituary says she and Sally "opened the doors to M&N Raceway" that year.[^kid][^obit] Kelley remembered the first races as 1964.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^ke24] Her grandmother told her that people sat on five-gallon buckets to watch the cars, and on some sand hills.[^ke24] The family were not racing people. They had crops and cattle, and the strip was a way to diversify.[^ke24]

The name changed more than the place. A Sedgwick County list has M & N Raceway from 1963 to 1965<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Open question: see Open questions">?</a></sup>, Wichita International Raceway Park from 1966 to 1968, Wichita International Raceway from 1969 to 2008, and Kansas International Dragway since. All of them are at 61st Street North and Ridge Road.[^krh] For years the family leased the track to others to run. In some years, the general manager said in 2024, the cars ran all night, until 6 a.m.[^ke24][^di24]

In September 2013, more than two hundred people came to a 50th reunion of M-N Raceway's old racers, held in a borrowed car barn in northwest Wichita. Barbara Patterson and Jon Lemon put it together. The fuel-injected '57 Chevys of Del Buller and Bob Cassil were parked side by side at the gate. Chuck Grimsley, in his eighties, posed with the *Rebel Rouser* funny car, and "Kansas John Wiebe" was there. Lyle Smith brought his chopped A/Altered American Austin, still being restored. "This body survived being crashed once and burned once," he said. Dave Stuckey, best known as a customizer, made the rounds as an old drag racer. When someone asked when they'd do it again, Patterson said, "Probably in about 50 years."[^we13] They did it again three years later, with racing on the strip itself.[^we16]

## Hell on wheels

Helen Simon was born in 1921 to a Maize family and married Sally in 1946. Her obituary says she was the hardest worker on the farm, "helping in the chutes, running grain to the elevator, swathing hay, or helping build fence," and in her last years she rode a John Deere Gator around, "causing havoc to her neighbors."[^obit] Her granddaughter says she was known as "Hell on wheels because she liked to drive fast."[^ke24] The track's general manager credits her with keeping what her husband started going for as long as she did. She died in June 2022, at 100.[^obit][^ke24]

The track sat out the 2023 season. Then the grandchildren took it back from the leasing arrangement and reopened it in 2024, only the second time the family had run it itself. The Simons' house sits about an eighth of a mile from the strip, and Kelley's son lives there now. Wheat grows around the track's 90 acres.[^ke24][^di24] It began in 1963 on a sandy field where not much grew and corn sometimes popped up through the track. More than sixty years later, the same family owns it.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **First races in 1963 or 1964?** The track, Helen Simon's obituary and *Drag Illustrated* say the strip was founded or opened in 1963, and the county list starts M & N Raceway in 1963.[^kid][^obit][^di24][^krh] *RacingJunk News*, in a 2026 anniversary piece, says it drew twenty thousand spectators to its opening event in April 1963.[^rj26] Gwen Kelley told the *Eagle* that the idea started in 1963 and the first races were held in 1964.[^ke24] The 1963 crowd figure is in the anniversary piece only. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How did a club of 1954–61 help at a strip of 1963?** Arick dates the Mill'ers to 1954–1961 and says the club "helped at the former M-N Raceway."[^avn24] Every other source starts M-N in 1963.[^krh][^kid] Either the club outlasted 1961, or members helped as individuals, or the column means an earlier strip. It does not say which. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Drags on McConnell's runway in 1958?** Only the *Kansas Racing History* venue list, which calls itself "almost certainly an incomplete listing."[^krh] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **What did "M-N" stand for?** The sources write it M-N, M & N and M&N, and none of them says what the letters meant.[^krh][^obit][^kid] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
