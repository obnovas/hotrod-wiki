# A Rainout, a Blown Transmission and NHRA's First Champion: Perryville Airstrip (1954–1958)

*Rain moved the 1955 Nationals final here, and the clutch went in on the tip of a toe*

Rain washed out the 1955 Nationals at Great Bend, and the final moved to a Luke auxiliary field at Perryville. Calvin Rice won it, NHRA's first champion, after three foul starts. The base commander later ended regular racing there.

## Monthly on a Luke field

The Southwest Timing Association began monthly drag races at the Perryville Airstrip, a Luke Air Force Base auxiliary landing field, also called Marsh, at least as early as late 1954. News reports directed drivers nine miles west of the Goodyear Naval Air Facility. In April 1955 the program was NHRA-sanctioned.[^dslaz]

The strip's ordinary Sundays were already fast. On November 3–4, 1956, it hosted a two-day Arizona state championship: Red Greth and Lyle Fisher got the Lords Club's dragster to a 142 mph track record; Carl Grimes set a national A/Altered mark at 120.83 mph and raised it to 122.45 on December 2. Emory Cook ran 163.63 mph on April 14, 1957; Bud Sampson set a national single-engine A gas dragster mark at 150.86 mph the same day, with Lefty Mudersbach's tuning. On December 29, 1957, the Lords' Modified took top eliminator and top speed at 152 mph.[^dslaz]

## From Great Bend to Perryville

The first NHRA National Championship Drags opened at Great Bend Municipal Airport on September 29, 1955. By the third day six dragsters were still in contention; then the worst rainstorm in thirty years washed out Sunday's finals. NHRA moved the finish to the Southwest Regional Championship at Perryville on November 19–20—a "neutral site" just outside Phoenix. Full story of the Kansas meet: [Great Bend](great-bend-srca.md).[^nhra21][^nhra16]

On Perryville's 4,000-foot asphalt runway the drama matched the wait. Rice opened with a 10.90 at 141.28 to eliminate the Bean Bandits; Jack Moss beat Lloyd Scott's Bustlebomb; Fritz Voight beat "Jazzy Jim" Nelson's coupe; Rice then beat Mulkey and Moss to reach the final against Voight. Rice and Voight lined up three times with three foul starts. Accounts disagree on who jumped when, Voight then Rice, or Voight twice, but each race was waved off as a "no-go."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> Who fouled, and in what order, is still open. On the third try both jumped and Rice's transmission blew. Starter Paul Wallace had already waved it off, so Voight could not take the trophy by default. The Riley crew, led by Chuck Webster and Mel Dodd, got thirty minutes: a new transmission did not fit; a reworked Ford went in with no time to adjust the clutch linkage. Rice won in 10.30 seconds at 141.95 mph, NHRA's first national champion, a 25-year-old from Santa Ana.[^nhra21][^nhra16]

Six thousand spectators attended the final day. Ed Losinski clocked the meet's fastest speed and an Arizona single-engine record at 151.77 mph. A West Valley weekly watched a local teen club, the KARBS, place third after a head gasket and a wiring fire—told on the [Phoenix West Side page](phoenix-west-side-karbs-litchfield-road.md).[^dslaz]

## The commander's order

NHRA liked the weather but thought Perryville too remote for future nationals. The strip's own end came from Luke. The association hoped to race March 30, 1958; the base commander cancelled it and barred further drag events, saying the club had not cleaned up or repaired damage after races—an order that surprised the association, which thought it had. That ended regular racing at Perryville. Loren Barnes later posted memories of racing there again in 1961, so the ban was apparently lifted for a time.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>[^dslaz] Whether they did race again in 1961 is still open. The final had been moved here in the rain. Rice won it. Then the commander barred further drag events.
