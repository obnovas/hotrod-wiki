# Everywhere but Here: North Dakota's Borrowed Strips, a Car Against a Cessna and Minot's Fastest Man (1953–2026)

*A coupe that went to Oklahoma City, a Minot racer who raced an airplane, and a Top Fuel family that never raced at home*

## A coupe from Beach

Bruce Olson wanted a roadster. From 1951 to 1953 he looked for one around Beach, a North Dakota town on the Montana line, and came to the conclusion, his son's account says, "that Ford didn't sell many roadsters in North Dakota. The old timers told him it was too cold." So in 1954, at 18, he paid $40 for a 1932 Ford five-window coupe, without an engine, from its original owner. He scrapped the fenders, hood and bumpers, channeled the body, bought a 1953 Chrysler 331 Hemi for $150 and had it back on the road in 1955: flat black, no exhaust, no interior, no license plate. "The cops loved it," he later wrote to *Rod & Custom*.[^olson] There was no club to join. "There were no hot rod and custom car clubs in that part of North Dakota at the time, so Bruce didn't belong to any clubs."[^olson]

To race it he had to leave the state. His son, posting on the H.A.M.B. as HEMI32, the coupe's name, wrote that his father "ran the Coupe at both the Lewistown & Bozeman (Belgrade) Air/Drag Strips (circa 1958)," across the line in [Montana](montana-hoodoos-road-runners-royal-coachmen.md).[^hemi32] And in late August 1958 Olson flat-towed the coupe from Beach to Oklahoma City for the fourth annual NHRA Nationals at the [fairgrounds strip](oklahoma-city-nationals-rush-springs.md), where it ran 13.40 at 107 mph in B Altered Coupe.[^olson] He married in 1960, moved his young family to Fremont, California, in December 1962, and drove the coupe to shows and drags until cancer took him in October 1990. In August 1991 his ashes were scattered over the Bonneville salt flats during Speed Week.[^olson] One more Fremont detail ties the Plains to the Northwest: the coupe's 1975 engine swap was done at Don Stortroen's "DONZ" race-car shop in Fremont, and a Don Stortroen had driven [Spokane's Thrifty Auto dragster](deer-park-spokane-nitrous.md) before he moved to California.[^olson][^hrnos]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>

## A car against a Cessna

In Minot the organized racing was on dirt ovals. The Nodak Race Club began in 1953 with 11 charter members, racing its first season on North Hill land owned by a local businessman, C. Morris Anderson, and moving to the state fairgrounds in 1954, where the old horse track became an auto track.[^nodak] The club's own hall of fame calls charter member Andy "Choo-Choo" Nikitenko, a track champion in 1956 and 1957, "arguably the most popular driver in the history of the race club." But "He will probably always be best known for his race against Cessna Airplane at the Minot International Airport in 1964." It ended badly: he "lost control at the end of the run way at 150 miles an hour, causing the car to flip 9 times before coming to rest."[^nodak]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> He raced on until 1971, and when a fellow charter member lost his right arm in a 1969 sprint-car crash, the same entries credit Nikitenko with inventing the arm straps that keep a driver's arms inside the car.[^nodak]

Those who wanted a drag strip kept driving west or east. Hank Berry, a later Nodak hall of famer, raced a Kawasaki and then big-block Chevelles and Camaros at a drag strip in Glendive, Montana, 55 miles from his home; when it closed in the late 1970s, he went dirt-track racing instead.[^nodak] Across the Red River from Fargo, the strip south of the Moorhead airport, in rural Glyndon, Minnesota, "has been there since 1959," a Fargo paper's columnist found in 2018.[^forum]

## North Dakota's fastest man

Bob Struksnes was born in Minneapolis in 1940, moved to Minot with his family in 1947 and graduated from Minot High School in 1959. In 1964 he became the owner of Truck & Equipment Sales and Service in Minot, and he ran it for more than 61 years with his wife, Joy, beside him.[^obit] "Out of high school I had a 1940 Chevrolet with a hemi-Chrysler and six carburetors on it. I drove that on the street," he told the *Minot Daily News* in 2018.[^mdn18]

His license to drive a dragster came at a Minneapolis strip, where he had to run within 10 percent of the track record. He could not get the car to shift into high gear and still clocked 212 mph. "They signed off on me," he laughed. "When I hit the throttle it was like an explosion."[^mdn18] At Joy's urging he moved up to Top Fuel, and from 1974 to 1980 the couple raced from the Carolinas to Saskatoon. Joy hauled the car to the next race and did mechanical work, and Bob often flew home to the business; the obituary adds the kids and the Yorkies to her load.[^mdn18][^obit] NHRA counts six divisional wins, fourth place in the 1976 world championship and third in 1977.[^nhramem] The paper credits him with the top speed of the 1976 Gatornationals, 248.61 mph, and puts that race in Louisiana.[^mdn18]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> In 1975 his ninth-seeded car came within a whisker of beating the top qualifier, Don Garlits, in the first round of the World Finals. "He asked what my name was and said he would never forget it."[^mdn18]

Racers called him "Backdoor Bob," because he kept his foot down past the finish line, through the speed trap 66 feet beyond it.[^mdn18] Yet his fame in Minot "remained limited," the paper wrote, because there was no strip in the city. "We didn't have any here because we never raced here," he said; the fans who came to help were at Brainerd, from Minneapolis, and in South Dakota.[^mdn18] A crash at the 1979 Winternationals knocked him unconscious at 200 mph. He had a new car built with a bigger roll cage, drove it seven times, and then, with nitromethane gone "from $300 a barrel to $1,600 for a barrel of 50 gallons," pushed it into the hauler in Minot and left it there. "I wanted to be around my son and be a father." He sold it late in 1979, and Jeb Allen drove it to the 1980 IHRA and 1981 NHRA championships.[^mdn18][^cp24] He was inducted into the NHRA Division 5 Hall of Fame and, in 2024, the North Dakota Motor Sports Hall of Fame, and he died at home in Minot on January 2, 2026.[^obit][^nhramem]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>

## A strip at the fairgrounds, and the runways

Minot finally got its strip on the east side of the State Fairgrounds: Magic City International Dragway, an eighth-mile track that a Minot Air Force Base public-affairs story called in 2010 an IHRA-sanctioned strip, "the only one of its kind in the state."[^af10] One of its owners, retired Air Force master sergeant Jim Grote, was a state fair volunteer when the strip was built, and one of the builders made him the starter: "When the cars come up, just flip the switch." Around 2007 or 2008 he joined an ownership group that bought the strip from the people who built it.[^mdn20] He told the base's public-affairs writer that his passion for giving amateurs a place to race came from a tragedy he lived through as a teenager, street racing with friends: "We killed somebody," he said. "He was a father of five on Christmas Eve."[^af10] In 2020 he said the owners "would love to get more street racers down here rather than them being out on the streets."[^mdn20] Cory Evenson, who says he "Never missed a day at the strip since it opened," volunteered for its first nine years and by 2024 had run it for 17; he also owns two of Struksnes's Top Fuel cars.[^cp24]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>

The rest of the state kept borrowing runways. The Dakota Drag Racing Association raced on runways at Dickinson's Theodore Roosevelt Regional Airport from the summer of 1999 until the airport authority ended the arrangement on Dec. 12, 2007. The races often coincided with Roughrider Days. Its president, Dennis Streitz, then planned "North Dakota's first legitimate quarter-mile drag strip" on a little more than 100 acres five miles east of Richardton, farmland that Craig Fisher, "a long-time drag racing fan," was arranging to sell the club. Grand Forks, Harvey, Jamestown and Minot had racing facilities, the paper said, "but none are quarter-mile drag strips." The nearest quarter-miles, the paper noted, were Interstate Dragway at Sabin, Minnesota, east of Fargo, and Yellowstone Drag Strip, 15 miles north of Billings. "It makes for a long trip either way," Streitz said.[^dick] At Jamestown's airport the Jamestown Drag Racing Association, formed in 1999, has run 660-foot drags since 2001. In 2013 its co-founder said they were the only airport drags in the state, and that "Every year the FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) has some new requirements."[^jsun]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Andy Nikitenko really race a Cessna at Minot's airport in 1964?** Only the Nodak Race Club's hall of fame entry tells it. It gives the year but no day or month, and it does not name the plane's pilot, say who arranged it or say what Nikitenko drove.[^nodak] Lead: the *Minot Daily News*, summer 1964. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **When did Magic City International Dragway open?** Only Evenson's 2024 count (nine years volunteering, then 17 running it) dates it, to about the late 1990s.[^cp24] Grote's 2020 interview does not give an opening year: his "around 2007 or 2008" is when the present owners bought the strip from the people who built it.[^mdn20] Lead: the *Minot Daily News* and the State Fair board's minutes. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When was Struksnes inducted into NHRA's Division 5 Hall of Fame: 2018 or 2019?** The *Minot Daily News* of Dec. 9, 2018 says he was "slated to receive the honor Jan. 26" at a Kansas City banquet; his obituary says "In 2018, he was inducted into the NHRA Hall of Fame."[^mdn18][^obit] The ceremony date suggests the class was announced in 2018 and inducted in January 2019. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Where did Struksnes run 248.61 mph?** The 2018 story says "at the 1976 Gatornationals in Louisiana." The Gatornationals are Gainesville, Florida's race (see [Lake Butler](lake-butler-mid-state-timing-association.md)), so either the state or the event name is wrong.[^mdn18] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Was Fremont's Don Stortroen the Spokane Thrifty driver?** The Olson account names Don Stortroen's "DONZ" shop in Fremont in 1975; *Hot Rod* says the Thrifty dragster's driver, Don Stortroen, moved to California to work for Vic Hubbard after a 1961 crash.[^olson][^hrnos] The name is rare, but no source we have read says they are the same man. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
