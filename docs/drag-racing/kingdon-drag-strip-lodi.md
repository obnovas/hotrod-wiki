# "Move Everything Off the Raceway": Kingdon Drag Strip, the Airfield That Stayed an Airfield (Lodi, Calif., 1950s–1978, or the mid-1970s)

*A policeman's idea, a speedway promoter's money, Tuesday nights on a wartime runway, and small planes that still had the right of way*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (the founding comes from three local newspaper stories written 50 years later, which agree on a Stockton policeman and disagree on the year, the spelling of his name and who bought the field; the racers' and fans' memories are their own, one each) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> *Lodi News-Sentinel* (2003, 2006, 2011), *The Record* (Stockton, 2004, reposted by Stockton 99 Speedway with a note on its founder), a Northern California drag-racing history site reprinting Kustomrama · **Last verified:** 2026-11-27 (Round 52) · **Open issues:** ⚠ Cress or Kress; ⚠ who bought the field, and when; ⚠ the 201.34-mph record; ⚠ 1978 or the mid-1970s; ◐ Garlits's first blown Swamp Rat · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "We had to move everything off the raceway. It added another show that you don't normally see at any other drag."

    — Gary Cox of Stockton, manager of Kingdon in its last years as a drag strip, on small planes landing during the races, *The Record*, 2004.[^rec04]

## A policeman's runway

Plenty of drag strips were old airfields. Kingdon was an airfield that never stopped being one. For about two decades the races shared the runway with the planes, and when a plane came in, the racing waited.[^ncdr][^rec04][^lns06]

The field west of Lodi was built for the military in the early 1940s, for flight training.[^lns11][^rec04] Student pilots took off from Stockton Field and flew north to land at Kingdon, where the crosswinds made it a challenge, and then went on to practice somewhere else.[^lns03]

The drag strip was a policeman's idea. The *Lodi News-Sentinel* wrote in 2006 that in 1953 a Stockton officer it called Bob Kress was tired of writing speeding tickets for young people he caught racing in the back alleys, and began eyeing the old training strip.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^lns06] *The Record* named him Robert Lee Cress, an auto-racing aficionado, and said he helped make Kingdon a place to race legally in the early 1950s. Now and then, it said, he told the people he stopped for speeding to check out the drag strip.[^rec04] The money, by the *News-Sentinel*'s account, came from Billy Hunefeld, who saw the crowds and gate receipts in the idea, bought the surplus airport from the federal government and put the officer in as general manager.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^lns06] Stockton 99 Speedway, which says the Hunefeld family still owns it, calls him Kingdon's original owner and first promoter.[^rec04]

## Tuesday nights at Kingdon

"Kingdon was designed to get kids off the street for organized racing and enhance the camaraderie for all these racers," said Frank Mauro, owner of Stockton Wheel Service, who first went as a spectator in 1959 at 14. "In the '50s and '60s, it was very grass-roots."[^rec04] Valley Timing Association members from the Modesto Century Toppers, Gene Winfield and Big Jon Enberg among them, ran there in the 1950s.[^ncdr] So, the same history says, did Don Garlits, who brought his Swamp Rat to Kingdon on March 13–14, 1959 for its first race with a blower, put on at Iskenderian's shop.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>[^ncdr] Mauro remembered Garlits, Tommy Ivo, Don Prudhomme and Connie Kalitta racing there once the strip joined the NHRA circuit.[^rec04] The *News-Sentinel* wrote that Garlits set the Kingdon record at 201.34 mph in 1964.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^lns06]

Everybody else came too. Arlene Gifford rode up from Livermore as a teenager most summer weekends in the 1950s with her boyfriend and another couple. "We stopped at a deli, got drinks and drove up here," she said in 2004. "We would sit along the strip on top of the hood of our car and watch the races all day. Sometimes, the dragsters would get out of control and head for us. We'd jump off and run."[^rec04] Larry Gaines of Stockton raced there in the late 1960s and early 1970s in his mom's Chrysler Imperial. "I raced here with my dog in my car," he said.[^rec04] Joe Nemie, who grew up on Armstrong Road, snuck through the fence to watch as a kid, and later ran Road Runners and Cyclones there from 1968 to 1971 with his buddies on Tuesday nights.[^lns06]

The planes never left. Gary Cox, who managed the strip in its last years, remembered small planes having to land while some 2,000 people watched.[^rec04] The *News-Sentinel* told it the same way: a pilot would come in during the Tuesday night races, everyone would scatter off the runway, and once the plane had taxied through, they set the equipment back up and went on racing.[^lns06]

## Back to the planes

In 1978, by *The Record*'s account, Kingdon went back to being a full-time airport.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^rec04] The maps took longer. The *News-Sentinel*'s 2003 history of Lodi aviation said charts as recent as 1987 still labeled the field Kingdon Dragstrip.[^lns03] In 2004 crop-dusters and private planes used it, and the manager and co-owner, Albert Troglin, had drag raced a dune buggy he built there in 1972 and 1973. Gaines came back as a grown man and learned to fly there.[^rec04]

The racing came back too. Members of the CVC, a group of Lodi car people who park their cars at Pete Knight's A&W every Thursday night, joined the airpark staff for "Return to Kingdon: A Day at the Races" on October 7, 2006, the first reunion there since 1994–95, with the proceeds going to the Lodi Junior Flames football program.[^lns06] By 2011, the *News-Sentinel* reported, David Arnaiz owned the airport with a partner, Rick Grewel, after his wife had bought him flying lessons, and his family had been running the drag day for six years.[^lns11] It is still an airport that lets the hot rods borrow the runway.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Cress or Kress?** *The Record* (2004) and the Northern California history site write Robert Lee Cress and Bob Cress; the *News-Sentinel* (2006) writes Bob Kress.[^rec04][^ncdr][^lns06] The story uses Cress in the telling, the spelling two of the three use and the only one with a full name, and keeps Kress beside it. A Stockton Police Department roster or his obituary would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Who bought the field, and when?** The *News-Sentinel* in 2006 says Billy Hunefeld bought the surplus airport from the federal government and installed the officer as general manager, after an idea the officer had in 1953; Stockton 99 Speedway's note calls Hunefeld the original owner.[^lns06][^rec04] The *News-Sentinel* in 2011 says that "in the mid-1950s, the base was purchased by a Stockton police officer," and the history site also says the mid-1950s.[^lns11][^ncdr] *The Record* says only the early 1950s.[^rec04] A deed, a War Assets Administration sale record or a 1950s *Lodi News-Sentinel* story would answer both questions. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Was 201.34 mph a Kingdon record?** The 2006 *News-Sentinel* says Garlits "set the Kingdon record in his 1964 Swamp Rat IV in winning the Top Eliminator title with a world speed record of 201.34 mph."[^lns06] That is the same speed this archive's [Island Dragway](island-dragway-great-meadows.md) sources give for Garlits's run in *Swamp Rat VI* at Great Meadows, N.J., on August 2, 1964. The two may have been run together in the telling. Island's page flags the figure and points here. A Kingdon time slip or a *Drag News* result is needed before the record stays in the story without this flag. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did the racing stop?** *The Record* says Kingdon became a full-time airport in 1978, and the 2006 *News-Sentinel* says the strip "shut down in 1978"; the 2011 *News-Sentinel* says "the mid-1970s," and the history site says "the 1970s."[^rec04][^lns06][^lns11][^ncdr] Troglin's own races in 1972 and 1973 fit either.[^rec04] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Garlits's first blown run?** The March 13–14, 1959 date, the blower from Iskenderian's shop, the win over Tony Waters and Dave LeRoy as starter come from one history site, which reprints Kustomrama, and we have not read its source.[^ncdr] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
