# Sundays at Airport No. 2: Utah's Runway Drags, a Clubhouse Paid for by a Street Race and the Salt Lake Strip War of 1965 (1950–1975)

*Fifteen cars at Provo, a closed street in Ogden, and the Sunday the crowd walked two miles east*

## Fifteen cars and a runway

On Sept. 30, 1950, the Provo Clutchers car club got permission to race on the east-west runway of the Provo airport, a mile and a quarter long. Fifteen cars entered, spectators had to stay outside the perimeter fence, Ken Dove won, and as far as anyone can tell it never happened again.[^dslutah] Two years later, Salt Lake City's commissioners approved a drag race for July 20, 1952 at the city's Airport No. 2, west of Midvale, supervised by the city police, with Norman Hayes of Murray in charge and cars and motorcycles electrically timed.[^dslutah]

Airport No. 2 became Salt Lake's strip. The Salt Lake Timing Association ran quarter-mile drags there from 1955, usually for about 100 competitors and once, in June 1956, for 180. In 1957 it was one of 90 NHRA-sanctioned strips in the country. At that year's Intermountain Championship Drag Races, on Sept. 7–8, Dan Noorda broke the track record at 134.54 mph on nitro in the Wignall-Noorda Special. For 1958 the city commission granted thirteen dates at $100 a day, and 1,500 spectators watched 122 entries on opening day.[^dslutah]

## A street race for a clubhouse

Ogden's clubs had no airport, so they borrowed a street. On May 1, 1955, the Salt Kings Rod & Custom Club got approval to close Wall Avenue between 33rd Street and Riverdale Road, beside Affleck Park, with the support of the city recreation department, Ogden police and the Weber County sheriff. NHRA sanctioned it, and the proceeds paid to move a surplus Army building to the Ogden airport to be the Salt Kings' clubhouse.[^dslutah] In 1959 the Ogden Timing Association charged 50 cents at the gate and 50 cents for a pit pass at Little Mountain, west of the city, to pave a strip on land that Perry Land and Livestock Company had donated.[^dslutah] In the south, Cedar City's Knights of the Road car club raced St. George's mesa-top airport runway in 1955 and 1956, for a quarter admission.[^dslutah]

## The Sunday the crowd moved

In early 1960 a young drag racer, Gary Nielsen, and some businessmen built Salt Lake Raceway on the north side of 2100 South at 4900 West, with bleachers for 3,000.[^dslutah] His obituary puts it more simply: "In 1960, Gary and Bonnie opened Salt Lake Raceway, the first drag strip in Salt Lake City."[^nielsen] Walt Arfons brought his "Green Monster" jet dragster that first season, and Saturday night racing began under lights in June 1962. On May 17, 1964, Bill Leon was killed in qualifying, and the track held a benefit for his family.[^dslutah]

The racers were not happy. In March 1964 the Salt Lake Valley Quarter Mile Association voted to petition the county for a strip built to NHRA specifications, and a group including Dick Godfrey built Bonneville Drag Strip, NHRA-sanctioned, on the old Utah Central Airport at 3400 West, two miles east of Salt Lake Raceway.[^dslutah] It opened on May 2, 1965. "All the spectators and most of the racers transferred their interest and attendance to the new strip," and the old track, unable to face it on Sundays, tried Friday nights for one summer and closed.[^dslutah] The same spring, West Jordan's Copper Raceway broke ground on March 8, 1965 and opened under the AHRA; it lasted about two years. Its co-owner Ron Winegar said they built it themselves and raced on Saturday nights, never on Sundays.[^dslut] In 1972, Ted McKenna remembered, street racers took over the abandoned Copper strip, with some West Jordan police officers among them, until trenches were cut across it in the spring of 1973.[^dslut]

## Bonneville Raceway and the rest of the state

The Bonneville Drag Strip's founders wanted more. In 1966 Godfrey, Bob Gee, Jim Campbell and the Howard Haynes family bought 60 acres at 6555 West 2100 South and spent $250,000 on a half-mile oval, a road course and a nearly 3,000-foot drag strip; Bonneville Raceway held its first drags on July 23–24, 1968.[^wvc] Larry Rees, 30, was killed there in his AA/FD that Aug. 31.[^dslutah] Bob Ipson leased it in 1973, and as Rocky Mountain Raceway it ran until 2018.[^wvc] One of its 1970s Top Fuel racers, Rex Pearmain, recalled buying cheap Kennecott diesel 6-71 blower rotors, and said Mark Danakis and a partner named Capps "started a blower company called DanCapp."[^dslut]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> When Don Garlits came, the papers counted the crowd: 4,200 people saw him beat Noorda at the Bonneville Drag Strip on May 7, 1967.[^dslutah]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>

Outside the Salt Lake Valley the old runways kept serving. Moab's abandoned airport held its first NHRA-sanctioned race on Oct. 10, 1965, with 42 entries from Utah, Colorado and New Mexico and 500 spectators.[^dslutah] At Pleasant Grove in 1972, police officer John Frampton organized races on a closed road by the county dump "to give youth a place to race under controlled, supervised conditions"; they were nicknamed the "Dump Yard Nationals."[^dslutah] And at St. George, Dan Johnson remembered running 221.25 mph and being paid $200 for every track record he broke.[^dslut] In Hanksville, on Labor Day 1975, a church youth group ran drags on an abandoned road between two river bridges for the town's bicentennial.[^dslutah]
