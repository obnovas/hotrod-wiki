# Six Miles of Runway: Casper's Old Airport, Sheridan's Hot Rod Club and Wyoming's Drag Strips (1947–2023)

*A fairgrounds club in 1947, 6 miles of runway bought for $20,500, the only active dragstrip in the state when Douglas closed in 2015, and a legal street race in Bar Nunn*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Low (the Sheridan club's history is its own; the Bar Nunn story comes from a local-history compiler and a newspaper feature quoting the *Casper Star-Tribune* of 1982, but neither dates a drag race; Douglas rests on a trade news story and a local radio piece; no contemporary Wyoming newspaper has been read, because the state's digital newspaper archive is blocked to our tools) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> the Sheridan Speedway club's own history; *Abandoned & Little-Known Airfields* (Paul Freeman), with residents' recollections; Cowboy State Daily (2023); My Country 95.5, Douglas (2020); Dragzine (2015); *Road & Track* (2023) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-01 (Round 29) · **Open issues:** when were the Bar Nunn runway drags ?; Interstate Speedway's first race ?; Wyoming's first drag strip ? · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "I read this ad in the Tribune that they wanted to put it up for auction. I thought by golly, why don't I put in a bid?"

    — Romie Nunn on buying Casper's old Wardwell Field, as the *Casper Star-Tribune* quoted him in February 1982 and Cowboy State Daily retold it in 2023.[^csd]

Wyoming's hot rodders began on a Sheridan fairgrounds track in 1947. Romie Nunn bought Casper's old airport in 1954, with 6 miles of paved runways, for $20,500. When Douglas closed in 2015 it was the only active dragstrip in the state, an NHRA-sanctioned one at that. Bar Nunn, the town on those runways, now has a legal street race.

## Sheridan: ether in the tank, 1947

Wyoming's oldest hot rod club on our map is in Sheridan. Its history, kept by today's Sheridan Speedway, begins: "The original club was formed in 1947 and was then called Sheridan Hot Rod Club, Inc.," with Vern L. Morris as president, Cecil Wentz as vice-president and Don Welton as secretary-treasurer. The cars were called "The Wyo Big Cars": stripped frames with a T bucket and a roll bar, Ford front axles and flathead Ford and Mercury engines. "The only place they had to run was on the fairgrounds track. They used ethyl gas with a quart or so of pure ether."[^sher]

After a trip north to see the Wonderland Speedway in Billings, Montana, the members built cars and a track of their own, the Mountain View Speedway, on a little piece of land south of the Girls' School, with the help of the Rasmussen Brothers. The club went dormant during the Korean War, came back in 1953 as the "Race Car Association," and raced stock cars on a quarter-mile track until 1960, under George Good from 1957 to 1960.[^sher] It was oval racing, not drag racing, but it was the same impulse, and the club came back again in 1970 with Interstate Speedway.[^sher] The club's history gives the first race as August 2, 1970, and, a few paragraphs later, August 7.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## Casper: the runway rancher, 1954

Casper's first airport, Wardwell Field, lost its airline service when the county moved to the old Army air base after the war. "Wardwell Field was sold in 1954 for the sum of $20,500 to Romie Nunn. The abandoned airport contained 6 miles of paved runways," writes Paul Freeman, who compiles the history of abandoned airfields.[^af] Nunn dreamed of horses. But "From 1954-72, Nunn sponsored a variety of activities on the land, including square dances, rodeos, a national road race, drag races, and even the filming of a movie." In 1956, the National Road Race was held on the old runways, "and later, other car races were run there."[^af] Cowboy State Daily adds that "There are also accounts of drag racing on the old runways before the area became more developed."[^csd] Neither gives a date, a club or a result.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

In 1958 Nunn subdivided the land as the Bar-Nunn Ranch Subdivision, laying out streets that "were in fact the old runways of Wardwell Field." A 1959 *Denver Post* Empire Magazine story called him "The Runway Rancher": "When you buy a ranch and build an airport on it, that's one thing. But when you buy an airport and make a ranch out of it, that's Wyoming!"[^af] Part of John Wayne's *Hellfighters* (1968) was filmed on the runways. Bar Nunn is now a city of 3,000, and its mayor says the wide streets need a lot of repairs because the old runways "did not have a thick base."[^csd]

## Douglas: the last strip standing, mid-1980s to 2015

When Douglas built a new airport about six miles north of town in the mid-1980s, the old one closed. A former local aviation businessman, Rod McKenzie, told Freeman: "I understand the airport was used as a drag strip after that. This is evident by the twin black streaks on the west end as seen in the aerial photos." A pilot, Rick Caldwell, reported in 2005 that the strip "is now used as the Douglas race track. They have drag races in the summer. If the need were to arrive I would think twice about landing there. You could get passed by some very fast automobiles though."[^af]

Dragzine later wrote that the old field "was converted to a drag strip in the mid-1980s after the nearby Converse County Airport was constructed."[^dz] By the time it closed, Douglas Motorsports Park was, in Dragzine's words in December 2015, "the only active dragstrip in the state (an NHRA-sanctioned one at that)," which had "served the racers of Wyoming for three decades." Its starting line sat less than 1,000 feet from a neighborhood, and when the city planned construction on the east end, the council declined to renew the lease into 2016.[^dz] The operators, Chris and Nicole Moore, signed off: "God bless the road, it's racers and keep all of us safe a 1/4 mile at a time!"[^dz] Douglas's radio station looked back in 2020 at the days "when Wyoming's only drag strip was right here in our backyard."[^mc] Neither says it was the only one for all three decades.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## Bar Nunn again: racing in the street

The runways' town came back to drag racing in a new form. *Road & Track* found Bar Nunn Cash Days, "a street-racing event held on a public roadway in Bar Nunn, a tiny suburb of Casper," a legal version of no-prep racing, run for an eighth of a mile on Westwinds Road, "just two lanes of concrete pavement running pin straight through the grasslands." Organizer Balor Zigmont explained the pairings: "It's chip-draw style," he said. "If we've got 20 racers, we'll have 40 chips, half of them red, half of them blue." The chips, by no-prep lore, come out of a Crown Royal whisky pouch.[^rt] The magazine also noted that the illegal kind still plays out after dark on Amoco Road outside Casper.[^rt]

(For the other northern plains states' strips, see [Sioux Falls, South Dakota](sioux-falls-south-dakota-clubs.md); for another state's runway strip, see [Palmer, Alaska](palmer-alaska-polar-dragway.md).)

Nunn bought the airport for $20,500 and made a ranch out of it. The legal race in that town is on Westwinds Road. When Douglas closed in 2015, it was the only active dragstrip in the state.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **When were the drag races on Wardwell Field's runways, and who ran them?** Freeman lists drag races among Nunn's events of 1954–72, and Cowboy State Daily mentions "accounts" of drag racing before the area was built up;[^af][^csd] neither gives a date, a club or a result. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Was Interstate Speedway's first race on Aug. 2 or Aug. 7, 1970?** The Sheridan club's history gives both: "the first race at the speedway was on August 2, 1970," and, a few paragraphs later, "August 7 was the first race at the Interstate Speedway."[^sher] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **What was Wyoming's first drag strip?** Douglas was converted to a drag strip in the mid-1980s and was, when it closed in 2015, "the only active dragstrip in the state";[^dz] Douglas's radio station later remembered it as "Wyoming's only drag strip."[^mc] Neither says it was the only one for all three decades, and we have found no Wyoming strip from the 1950s or 1960s beyond the undated Bar Nunn runway races. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
