# For Parents Only: Harmon Raceway (Monroe, La., 1964–67)

*A Sunday paper's ads and sports page follow a strip on Prairie Road from a page of answers for worried parents to the funny-car match races of 1967*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (one local daily, mostly its display ads and short sports items, read as full-issue OCR; Monday results pages are missing) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> city daily (*Monroe Morning World*, Monroe, La.): display ads, sports-page previews, a column, an obituary and a weather story, 1962–67, read on the Internet Archive · **Last verified:** 2026-10-27 (Round 24; quotes rechecked against the issue OCR) · **Open issues:** who Bob Harmon was ?; who owned the strip ?; the 1967 match results ?; when it closed ? · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "Q. Is drag racing dangerous for my boy or girl? A. No!"

    — "For Parents Only," Bob Harmon Memorial Raceway ad, *Monroe Morning World*, June 28, 1964.[^mmw640628]

Before it had run a single race, the new drag strip south of Monroe, La., bought a full page in the Sunday paper to talk to mothers and fathers. "Due to the numerous inquiries from the parents of prospective competitors at the strip," it began, "the management feels a sincere obligation to the public to answer these questions in print."[^mmw640628]

## "Coming soon to Monroe area" (1964)

The June 28, 1964, page was headed "BOB HARMON MEMORIAL RACEWAY COMING SOON TO MONROE AREA! LOCAL CARS BEING READIED FOR RACING." It pictured Jack Trost, and Jimmy McIntosh and Robert Lyons "with their Chevrolet Gasser." Then came the questions.[^mmw640628]

Was it dangerous? "No! The strip will be run and competition will be under the strict supervision of trained and experienced personnel." No more than two cars would be on the strip at once, and "special attention will be given to protect and supervise the novice driver." Would racing damage the family car? "Under the above mentioned supervision, No! However, some tire wear may be noticeable." Did a boy need to spend a lot to soup up his car? No; the owner of a stock car would have "as much chance at a trophy" as anyone in his class. And one more thing: "at no time will alcoholic beverages be allowed on the property." The management pledged "a clean, safe competition that will afford experience, good fellowship and competition to the youth of the Monroe area." The address was "Memorial Raceway Inc. on Prairie Road between Charmingdale and Fondale."[^mmw640628]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

On Thursday, Sept. 24, the raceway held a drag racing clinic at Ouachita Parish High School, with a sound-and-color film of the National Hot Rod Association's championship and a question period after each showing. Parents and public officials were specially invited.[^mmw640920]

## Opening day (1965)

The strip was tested in April 1965. B. E. Woosley, driver for the "California Transplants," "narrowly escaped a bad spill when the chute on his ... fuel dragster failed to open during a test run at the new Bob Harmon Raceway," after going through the traps "at a speed in excess of 195 mph." Woosley called it "a mere mishap" and went back out for his fourth run.[^mmw650425]

The first meet, on May 1–2, 1965, brought Eddie Hill's twin-engine fuel dragster and Roy Davis, the reigning NHRA world champion. The cars went on show the night before on the Globe Food Market parking lot, free. The ad's promise was three words: "Sanctioned—Insured—National Hot Rod Association."[^mmw650501] A week later the paper reported that the raceway "opened last week before a capacity crowd." Track director Whit Alger was reworking the staging area so races would run smoother than on opening day. The strip was "nine miles south of Monroe off of Highway 165."[^mmw650509]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> By late May the ads added a line no other ad on the page had: "We proudly display our insurance certificate."[^mmw650523]

On July 4 the paper reported that Amos Lindsey, 47, had died of an apparent heart attack. "Mr. Lindsey was manager of the Bob Harmon Raceway here."[^mmw650704] On July 17, after two weeks closed while the lights went in, the strip ran under its new "$25,000 beams" for the first time.[^mmw650717] A Saturday-night card in late July cut admission to $1.00, let children under 12 in free, and featured Bob Sullivan of Kansas City in "Pandemonium," a 1965 Plymouth Barracuda with a blown 392 Chrysler that had run 156 mph and 10.06 seconds.[^mmw650724] When a thunderstorm dumped more than an inch of rain on the Twin Cities that night, the races "at Bob Harmon Raceway" were reset for Sunday morning.[^mmw650725] In October the strip held a "Two Day Memorial Race" with a "Bob Harmon Sportsman Trophy Award," and told fans it was "Open Only 3 More Days This Year."[^mmw651016]

## The divisional and the Kingfish (1966)

In June 1966 Harmon was one of seven tracks in the NHRA's Southwest Region to host a divisional meet, with Oklahoma City, Victoria, Opelousas, Tulsa, Austin and Amarillo. Columnist Keith Prince explained the points system: the top five in each class from each region went to the world finals at Tulsa, where someone would pocket $100,000. A divisional at Opelousas the week before had drawn 13,000. "Harmon won't be able to match 13,000 in attendance," Prince wrote, "However, Harmon can find room for approximately 8,500." He admitted he didn't know "a carburetor from a radiator," but "I'll see you at Harmon."[^mmw660625] The Anderson Brothers' AA fuel dragster, driven by Waitus Simpson, came in with a 7.61-second, 207.16 mph time.[^mmw660625]

In July the ad said Harmon would be "back under the lights throughout the rest of the summer," and, "being the only dragstrip in this area to run at night, will draw a large array of competition throughout the Ark-La-Miss."[^mmw660709] In August it booked a match race between Leonard Hughes's "Big Bad Plymouth," "Jakes' Speed Equipment," which held "the best E.T. and M.P.H. record at the local strip," and "The Kingfish," a blown-Hemi 1966 Barracuda from Memphis. The strip was open every Friday night for time trials.[^mmw660820] (For the Memphis racers who also ran the Bootheel strip, see [Memphis Comes to the Bootheel](semo-malden-drag-strip.md).)

## Funny cars and the "New Look" (1967)

"After a year's absence, the big AA Fuel Dragsters are returning to Harmon Raceway," the paper said in May 1967. Six NHRA district contenders would run in the seven-second bracket, and the meet inaugurated a "New Look" at Harmon: a newly paved pit area and clubhouse concession.[^mmw670514] The ad for that card added a funny car match race: "Joe Lunati— 3 Time National Champ — VS — Lenard Hughes— Jakes Speed Shop."[^mmw670513] (See [Joe Lunati](../people/joe-lunati.md).)

On June 24–25 came "the Big One": six "Super 'Funny Cars,'" with three "Flip Top Camero" entries for Kelly Chadwick, Mike Schmidt and Joe Lunati, Gene Snow's Dodge "Rambunctous" (as printed; see [Gene Snow](../people/gene-snow.md)), and two Texas Chevys, "Texas Bandit" and David Robinson's "Texas Twister."[^mmw670624]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> For another Louisiana-border strip of the same years, see [Church First, Then the Drags (Hope, Ark.)](hope-airport-drag-strip.md).<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Who was Bob Harmon, and who owned the strip?** None of the ads or stories we read says who the raceway remembered or who owned Memorial Raceway Inc. The paper's 1961 sports roundup, printed in January 1962, recorded the death of "Bob Harmon of Monroe, a former pitcher for the St. Louis Cardinals and Pittsburgh Pirates."[^mmw620121] Nothing we read ties him to the track. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Who ran it?** The paper names Whit Alger as track director in 1965 and Amos Lindsey as manager at his death that July, but no owner, operator or club.[^mmw650509][^mmw650704] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Who won the 1967 match races?** The Internet Archive run of the *Morning World* for 1966–67 is mostly Saturday and Sunday issues, so the Monday results pages for the Lunati–Hughes match (May 14) and the June 24–25 funny car meet are missing. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **When did Harmon close?** The online run we read stops in August 1967, with the strip still racing. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
