# A Lease on the Beeline: The Salt River Strip and the AHRA Winternationals (Mesa, Ariz., 1963–1979)

*Two asphalt lanes on Community land, a winter national under the rival sanction, and a timing tower that stood for decades after the racing stopped*

## "The first drag strip to be built on an Indian Reservation"

At the end of August 1963 the *Navajo Times*, the tribal weekly at Window Rock, ran a short item for "Hot Rodders and Fans." A new firm, the Beeline Corporation, had broken ground on a dragway on the Salt River Indian Reservation, "east of the McDowell-Beeline Highway intersection." It would have dual-lane timers and push lanes for the dragsters, and it was due to open that fall. "The first drag strip to be built on an Indian Reservation," the paper called it. The president of the firm, which was building the $100,000 dragway and planned to run it, was "C. TIM ROGERS of North Woodmere in Scottsdale."[^nt63]

Later accounts remember him as Jim. Leatherman's 2012 *New Times* story and the Three Rivers Historical Society both say the strip was built in 1963 by "local entrepreneur Jim Rogers" on land leased from the Salt River Pima; Wikipedia, citing Drag Strip List, spells it Rodgers.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> A local guide dates the groundbreaking to July 20, 1963, and notes that the highway itself was new: a 1950s diagonal shortcut across Community land toward the Saguaro Lake turnoff, paved to Payson in 1958.[^pnt12][^trhs15][^tips] It was a busy summer for Valley racing: in July a Scottsdale promoter had announced Phoenix International Raceway, with its own drag strip, on the [West Side](phoenix-west-side-karbs-litchfield-road.md).

The strip sat about a mile north of Mesa, on the south side of State Highway 87 by the historical society's account. The EPA's 2015 factsheet, written with the Community, describes the property as native desert until it was developed in the late 1950s and early 1960s as a gravel pit and dragway: about 63 acres of dragway and a roughly 10-acre gravel pit to the north. In the summer heat the races were on Saturday nights, the historical society remembered; in the cooler months, Sundays. Over it all stood a three-story concrete-and-steel timing tower.[^trhs15][^epa15][^pnt12]

## AHRA's winter stop

For more than a decade Beeline hosted the American Hot Rod Association's yearly Winternationals, and the *New Times* says thousands came. The muscle cars of the day ran there (Chargers, Mustangs, GTOs), along with the long, skinny dragsters, and so did the stars: Don "Big Daddy" Garlits, "Dandy" Dick Landy, Tom McEwen and Shirley Muldowney; the historical society adds Don "The Snake" Prudhomme. The local guide credits Dave Strickler with Top Stock at the 1964 meet.[^pnt12][^trhs15][^tips]

In 1971 [Garlits](../people/don-garlits.md) brought his new rear-engine Swamp Rat XIV. A fan's uncle filmed that year's Winternationals, and when the reel turned up in 2011 Dragzine argued that the footage showed "the actual debut of the Swamp Rat XIV at Beeline," since the meet "took place in late January – prior to the NHRA Winternationals." NHRA's own history has the car's first public outing at Lions in early January. A race-results database dates the Beeline meet February 21, 1971, with Garlits out in the quarterfinals to Gary Cochran's slingshot, which went on to beat Chris Karamesines in the final.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^dz11][^nhra15][^urh71]

## When the clocks went quiet

The *New Times* says the place lost its luster in the mid-1970s to falling interest and television, and that the new Firebird International Raceway near Chandler filled its niche; the historical society names Firebird and Phoenix International Raceway. On the closing year the sources split. Leatherman says 1975. The Three Rivers quarterly says 1973. The EPA factsheet says operations "ceased in January 1979," that the strip was torn down in the 1980s, and that most of the demolition debris went into the gravel pit. At some point, the historical society says, Motorola used the timing tower for radio testing.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^pnt12][^trhs15][^epa15]

The tower stood for decades, graffiti on every face but the fading ad for a Mesa business, Saguaro Automotive. Then it was gone. "I am not sure when it was demolished," the historical society's writer admitted in late 2015, "but it was there last summer and is gone this summer." The EPA's assessment found no soil contaminants above levels of concern and called the debris nonhazardous, and the Community planned to return the land to its natural state for cultural ceremonies and open space.[^pnt12][^trhs15][^epa15]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did Beeline close: 1973, 1975 or January 1979?** Three Rivers Historical Society (2015): "It closed in 1973." *Phoenix New Times* (2012): "It closed in 1975." EPA Region 9 (March 2015): "Operations at the Beeline Dragway ceased in January 1979, and the dragstrip was demolished in the 1980s." The local guide also reports a historical-map date of 1980 and an older Dragzine account of a landowner–operator dispute in the early 1980s, neither of which we have read. A dated race result or the lease file would settle it.[^trhs15][^pnt12][^epa15][^tips] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Who built it: C. Tim Rogers, Jim Rogers or Jim Rodgers?** The only period source we have, the *Navajo Times* of Aug. 29, 1963, names the Beeline Corporation's president as "C. TIM ROGERS of North Woodmere in Scottsdale." The 2012 *New Times* and the 2015 historical-society piece say "Jim Rogers"; Wikipedia, citing Drag Strip List, says "Jim Rodgers." "Tim" and "Jim" could be a typesetter's slip either way.[^nt63][^pnt12][^trhs15] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When was the 1971 Winternationals at Beeline, and was it Swamp Rat XIV's debut?** Dragzine (2011): the meet "took place in late January" and the film shows "the actual debut." NHRA (2015): Garlits first unloaded the car at Lions for AHRA's Grand American, Jan. 8–10, 1971, then ran Orange County a week later. Ultimate Racing History lists the AHRA Winter Nationals at Beeline on Feb. 21, 1971, with Garlits eighth in Swamp Rat XIV; that database's notes also say Dave Russell "is reported to have replaced" Garlits for the third round after engine trouble, and that Paul Pritchett was killed in an accident after his round, neither of which we have confirmed.[^dz11][^nhra15][^urh71] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
