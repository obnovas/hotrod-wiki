# Christmas Lights and a Panel-Truck Tower: W. H. and Jayne David's Pel State Dragway (Opelousas, La., 1955–1970)

*A strip strung up every other Sunday on the airport, and a starting light made with 1950s Christmas bulbs*

    **Confidence:** Medium (for the strips and dates: a Division 4 hall of fame page, a grandson's account and racers' memories agree on Pel State at the Opelousas airport and the Davids' later Baton Rouge strip; the inventions are Low: family claims, repeated by a racer, which NHRA's editor says he cannot verify, and no patent in David's name was found) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> Rick David's account of his grandparents on DragCars.com (family account); racers' memories and a *Tire Marks* caption on *Drag Strip List*; the NHRA Division 4 Hall of Fame's "ghost tracks" page; Phil Burgess, NHRA (2013); Wikipedia · **Last verified:** 2026-11-10 (Round 37) · **Open issues:** ⚠ who invented the Christmas tree; ◐ David's patents; ⚠ the year of the fuel test at the Nationals; ◐ the 1968 closing · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

W. H. and Jayne David ran a strip you built every other Sunday, at Esterwood in 1955 and then on the Opelousas airport. The tower sat on a 1951 Chevy panel truck. The family says he invented the Christmas tree. NHRA's history calls that one of several claims.

## A strip you built every other Sunday

The man behind Louisiana's early strips said his name "dah-VEED." W. H. David of Lafayette founded the Pel State Timing Association, Pel State being short for the Pelican State, and ran it with his wife, Jayne.[^nhra13] His grandson Rick David wrote that he "opened his first drag strip in 1955 at Esterwood, LA," and moved to Opelousas in 1958.[^rdavid] The Division 4 Hall of Fame dates Pel State Dragway's opening to May 4, 1958, "with 427 race cars and 2,200 spectators."[^d4hof]

Pel State was on the Opelousas airport, so nothing could stay up. Before every meet, on the second and fourth Sundays, the crew strung the fencing, the public-address wire and the timing wire, and when a dragster left the pavement for "a trip through the grass," it could take out the timing or the PA lying on the ground. There were no guardrails; spectators sat in or on their cars behind the temporary wire fence. The tower was a platform and canopy on top of a 1951 Chevy one-ton panel truck, and from it Dave McClelland, a Shreveport television personality who made the trip down, called the races. Across from the front gate stood a place called The Gate, "although no racer would admit to ever frequenting the place."[^d4hof]

Every strip in Louisiana worked that way, one racer remembered. Max Williamson, who raced his C/A '32 Ford three-window for Top Eliminator at Pel State "many Sundays," wrote that "every track in that era was run on runways," most of them still active, and had to be set up and torn down each time. He also kept copies of Pel State's *Tire Marks*, "which was published after each race"; *Drag Strip List* shows the back page of the July 5, 1959, issue.[^dslla] Jayne David wrote, edited and printed it, her grandson says.[^rdavid]

## Four strips at once

By Rick David's count his grandfather at one point had four NHRA-sanctioned tracks running at once in Louisiana: Opelousas, Hammond, Lake Charles and Houma.[^rdavid] The racers place themselves on that map. Leonard G. Sitzman beat "Power Glide Pete" for the trophy at the Hammond airport in August 1957 in his '57 Chevy, on his way back to LSU. Williamson, between 1955 and 1962, raced Pel State, Hammond, Houma, Lake Charles, Alexandria and Mansfield, and his club, the Cenla Gents of Alexandria, put on its own races at the old Pollock airport and at Esler Field in Pineville.[^dslla] David Lambdin, who won the state C Sports title in a '63 Corvette convertible, raced Pel State in 1966 and 1967.[^dslla]

The Division 4 page closes Pel State in 1968.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> Only that page gives the year. David then helped build the strip at Baton Rouge, "State Capital Raceway" on that page and "State Capitol Dragway" to his grandson, which opened in 1970.[^d4hof][^rdavid]

## The fuel test and the Christmas tree

The family's story goes further. Rick David wrote that his grandfather invented the fuel test, "we think in 1958," and was hired to run it at the Nationals in Detroit, where his father, then 15, went along. Jayne is the woman taking the fuel test at the '59 Nationals in the film *Ingenuity in Action*.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> Whether that was 1958 or 1959 is still open. After that the Davids were hired to run fuel, displacement and cam checks at most national events for a year or so. In 1959 David was NHRA's regional technical advisor and one of six committee members who rewrote the rule book.[^rdavid]

"His biggest mark on drag racing," the grandson wrote, "was the invention of the christmas tree." His grandfather's concern was safety. In the late 1950s he started races with a traffic signal, the starter standing at the back tires (usually Rick's father), because in handicap starts at local meets the flagman had to stand down the strip, in front of the car with the head start. The tree was meant to get him off the track. Rick still keeps one of the earliest mock-ups, "about 18" tall and 14" wide," built with "real christmas tree lights from the '50s and '60s on it, hence the name 'christmas tree.'" His grandfather built them for a few years, the family says, and then sold the rights to Chrondek.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> A racer repeats the claim: Pel State's owners "created and patented the Christmas Tree starting light system in use today," Lambdin wrote.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^rdavid][^dslla]

NHRA's own history tells it another way and lists David's claim beside it. In 2013 NHRA's Phil Burgess found a La Verne newspaper story saying that NHRA's national field director, Ed Eaton, brought Chrondek's Oliver Riley the idea of a step-light countdown in 1962 and brought in Lew Bond of Dragtronics; *Hot Rod*, reporting on the tree's debut at the 1963 U.S. Nationals, credited "Drag-Tronics on the East Coast." McClelland, asked who was first, would not choose: "most likely they were all working on it at the same time, maybe with knowledge of the other, maybe not." Burgess's verdict: "as clear as bayou mud."[^nhra13][^hr63] Who was first is still open. The fence came down after every meet. On the starting light, Burgess's verdict was "as clear as bayou mud."

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Who invented the Christmas tree?** Rick David: his grandfather built it, after starting races with a traffic signal in the late 1950s, and "sold the rights to Chrondek." NHRA (Burgess, 2013), from a La Verne newspaper story: Ed Eaton took the idea to Oliver Riley in 1962 and brought in Lew Bond of Dragtronics, and "Bond and Riley then worked together to develop the first Christmas Tree." *Hot Rod*, November 1963, as quoted by a print seller: "It was developed by Drag-Tronics on the East Coast." Burgess: "I'm certainly not in a position to verify any of the claims." Wikipedia gives David's full name as Wilfred H. "W.H." David, Jr., citing a 2010 forum post we did not read.[^rdavid][^nhra13][^hr63][^wikixmas] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did David patent the tree, the fuel test or the engine checks?** Rick David says his grandfather "held patents for the fuel test, engine displacement and cam size check" and "sold the patent" for the fuel test; David Lambdin says the owners "created and patented" the tree. A Google Patents search on Nov. 10, 2026, for inventors named David, 1955–1970, with drag racing, starting-signal and fuel-test terms, found none of them; patents may have been filed under another name or a company.[^rdavid][^dslla] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
