# The Leftover Parcel: John Grivins and Martin Dragway (Martin, Mich., 1962– )

*A strip of land a new expressway left behind, a college student who saw a quarter mile in it, and a family that ran the gate and the hot dogs*

## A farmer's bad shape

What makes Martin Dragway's start unusual is the land. A new expressway left behind a parcel a farmer would not want, long and narrow, and a young drag racer saw that it was the right shape for a strip.[^mmshof]

John Grivins was born Ivars Grivins in Riga, Latvia, on November 13, 1938.[^obit22] In 1958, 1959 and 1960 he raced a series of Ford Stock Eliminator cars at Midwestern strips like Stanton, Michigan, and Osceola, Indiana.[^mmshof] Then the new U.S. 131 expressway went through between Grand Rapids and Kalamazoo, about a mile west of the old two-lane road. It cut off a 198-acre parcel with more than a mile of frontage on the new highway, near the Martin exit. "Although it was conveniently located near the Martin, Michigan exit, the large parcel was long and narrow by a farmer's standards," the Michigan Motor Sports Hall of Fame wrote. "To a young John Grivins however, the layout appeared to be perfect for a drag strip."[^mmshof]

He and his father started building in 1961. U.S. 131 Dragway opened in 1962, while he was still a student at Western Michigan University.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^mmshof][^ds12] The Hall of Fame calls him the youngest track operator in the country at the time.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^mmshof] It was a family affair from the start. His mother ran the concession stand, and his brother, Gunnar, helped out in a variety of roles.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^mmshof]

Louie Hall raced there the first season and went on to win two track championships. "John was one of the first track owners in the area who guaranteed a purse," he said. "If you went in there and paid your entry fee, you knew how much you could win." The grass was mowed, he said, and the traction was right.[^ml22] Ted Nohava remembered Grivins "taking me down the Strip in the AFX'r he bought off Ram Chargers."[^obit22]

## The August race

In 1969 Grivins hosted the Popular Hot Rodding Nationals. It became an annual August event and, MLive wrote, grew into the largest independent drag race in the nation in the 1970s and 1980s.[^ml22] "It turned out to be as big as any NHRA race outside of the U.S. Nationals or the Winter Nationals," said Gary Slabbekoorn, the track's publicist from 1969 through 1980. It came a couple of weeks before Indianapolis, and a lot of drivers used it as a warmup. Garlits, Prudhomme, Shirley Muldowney and Dick LaHaie all raced there.[^ml22]

The locals had their own ideas. Tom Carter of Grand Rapids was one of the big local stars of the 1970s, in a 1969 Cougar 428 Cobra Jet, and Grivins called him "Hollywood." "I don't know why he called me it," Carter said. "He always chuckled when he said it, but he never told me why." In 1972 Carter's group, the U.S. 131 drag team, held a seminar at Rogers High School about how much safer the strip was than the street, and asked Grivins if they could follow it with a high school drag race. "He said, 'Well, yeah,'" Carter said. "He sat back and let us run the event." The High School Drags became a spring event at Martin.[^ml22]

The biggest crowd was not for a race. In 1977 Grivins put on a concert with Bob Seger, Heart, the Little River Band and Wolfman Jack. The traffic was so heavy that people parked on U.S. 131 and walked in. "The state police were not happy," Slabbekoorn said.[^ml22] The IHRA came for a one-off national event in 1986 and put Martin on its schedule full time in 2002.[^ds12]

Grivins ran it for nearly 40 years and sold it in 2000 to Alec Pinsonneault, a Canadian businessman.[^ml22] Renamed U.S. 131 Motorsports Park, it got $14 million in renovations in 2002, the IHRA said, and it marked its 50th year in 2012.[^ds12] He was inducted into the Michigan Motor Sports Hall of Fame in 2004. He was living in Otsego when he died on January 28, 2022, at 83.[^ml22] His obituary says he was remembered "as being one of the nicest track owners that many drivers had ever raced for."[^obit22] A neighbor who had babysat for the family and worked at the dragway as a kid wrote that "He was a nice guy to the skinny kid next door."[^obit22] He had taken a parcel nobody could farm and made it a drag strip, with his mother at the concession stand.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did it open in the spring or the middle of 1962?** The IHRA's 50th-anniversary release says Martin Dragway "opened to the fanfare of the western Michigan motorsports community" in "the spring of 1962."[^ds12] The Hall of Fame says it "opened, mid-1962," and the Grand Rapids Chamber says "Opening in mid-1962."[^mmshof][^grc25] All agree on 1962. The *Kalamazoo Gazette* and *Grand Rapids Press* of 1962 would give the first race date. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Was he the youngest track operator in the country?** Only the Hall of Fame says "John was the youngest track operator in the country."[^mmshof] He was 23 in 1962, by his obituary's birth date.[^obit22] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How are his parents' names spelled, and what was the August race called?** The Hall of Fame writes "his father Voldamer" and "His mother, Martha."[^mmshof] The family obituary writes "the son of Voldemars and Marta (Mikelson) Grivins."[^obit22] The obituary calls the August race the "Popular Hot-Rodding Championships"; MLive calls it the "Popular Hot Rodding Nationals."[^obit22][^ml22] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
