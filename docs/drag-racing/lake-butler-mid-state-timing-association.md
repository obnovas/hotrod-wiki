# "Only Their Own Necks": Lake Butler, Williston and the Mid-State Timing Association (1959)

*A Gainesville club loses its runway to a crop duster, a small-county paper argues itself in a circle, and the county commissioners drive to Williston to ask the neighbors*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (one small-county weekly, read week by week as page OCR, gives the whole episode, with names and dates; what Williston said about the club comes to us secondhand, through Lake Butler's committee) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> small-county weekly (*Union County Times*, Lake Butler, Fla.): front-page news, the "Odds & Ends" column and county business, 1959–61, read as Library of Congress Chronicling America page OCR; Wikipedia and an EAA chapter page on the Williston airfield; an NHRA first-person piece by Don Garlits · **Last verified:** 2026-10-31 (Round 28) · **Open issues:** which Williston airfield ?; the "university men" and Williston's boys ◐; where Mid-State raced next ? · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "The Mid-State Timing Association is an organization which promotes drag racing. That is, when a bunch of youngsters spend a lot of time and money to build up a car where you get the maximum speed out of it, they naturally wish to find out who has the fastest, and the association allows them to do so under controlled conditions where only their own necks are risked, not the public's."

    — C. S. Lecky, "Odds & Ends," *Union County Times* (Lake Butler, Fla.), Sept. 11, 1959.[^uct0911]

In late August 1959 a teenage girl who lived out in the country told the *Union County Times* that she and her friends were no longer allowed to come into Lake Butler at night, and that they missed the roller rink. One parent explained why. "It's the drag racing," the parent said. "We both work. At night we're too tired to bring the youngsters in to the skating rink and wait until it's time to go home. And with all the drag racing on our road We don't feel that it's safe for them to come alone." Most of the racing, the paper said, now centered on Sanderson Road, State Road 238, "which is comparatively little traveled at night."[^uct0828]

Lake Butler, the paper insisted, had it easy next to Lake City, where the local paper had just charged that teenage boys were "openly defying" the law with "their public designation of First Street as a Drag Strip," having painted starting and finishing lines on the new highway. A state official who stopped by the *Times* office confirmed the painting story and said it cost $300 of public funds to repair.[^uct0828] The next week the paper's columnist, C. S. Lecky, reported that Highway Patrolman Paul W. Carter "is not a Times subscriber and says he has no intention of becoming one. But he evidently reads the paper": Carter had been upset by the story, apparently thinking it meant the painting had happened in Lake Butler. It hadn't, Lecky wrote, and suggested Carter check the young drivers' licenses instead.[^uct0904]

## A crop duster takes the runway

Eleven days after that first story, the drag racers came to Lake Butler with a proposal. On Tuesday, Sept. 8, John D. Surrency of Gainesville told the county commission that the Mid-State Timing Association, with its headquarters in that city, wanted to race on Satellite Field, "the old Navy air station south of Lake Butler," whose hard-surfaced runways were, some of them, a mile long. The association was several scores of drivers, "Primarily, they are hot rod and drag race enthusiasts," and its members said their object was "to take drag racing off the streets and highways and to promote safety." It held meets twice a month on Sunday afternoons, and Surrency said about 400 spectators usually came. For some time they had raced at the Williston airport, but that field had been leased to a crop-dusting firm, "and the entire area is needed for its helicopters."[^uct0911n]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

Satellite Field was the county's own. The commissioners had arranged in December 1958 to buy it from the federal government for $100,000, $30,000 down and $10,000 a year; in January 1959, after the government ended the leases under which local cattlemen grazed their herds on the old airport, they asked the sheriff to lock its gates.[^uct6101][^uct5901] That summer the county's Development Authority showed it to a man who wanted to open a slacks factory, but the city water mains did not reach that far, and he took a tract just east of town instead.[^uct0904b] The commissioners, all of them members of the Development Authority, sent the drag racers' request there.[^uct0911n]

In his column, Lecky passed along the case for the club. Sheriff John Whitehead had talked with Sheriff Frank Crevasse of Alachua County, Lecky wrote, and "Crevasse recommends the association highly," and would be glad to have the races in his own county if there were a site. The races might bring the county money, since they drew crowds of several hundred from many cities, and "The association claims that it keeps drag racing off the streets and highways. There is some corroboration of that." The only possible drawback, he thought, was that a strip might encourage local boys to drive more recklessly on the street. "I doubt if it would."[^uct0911]

## "A definite detriment"

The Development Authority went to Williston to ask. On Sept. 25 the *Times* reported the answer under the headline "Drag Racing Will Not Be Permitted At Old Airport." Williston officials told the Lake Butler committee that the races had brought them no benefits "and had found them to be a definite detriment." The association, they said, was "a group of university men," and Williston youths were not allowed to take part. Because of the racing, some Williston boys had built hot rods anyway; shut out of the airport, "they began to try them out on the highways. Williston previously had had no drag race problem but permitting the airport to be used created one." Three filling-station operators said they had had no business at all from the racing, and a restaurant man said the same, adding that the association brought its own concession stand.[^uct0925]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>

So the old Navy runways stayed locked. In January 1961 Union County filed its quitclaim deed for Satellite Field, after selling part of the airport's timber for $55,355.[^uct6101] Where the Mid-State Timing Association raced next, the Lake Butler paper did not say.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> Florida's racers of the 1950s, Don Garlits later wrote, "also ran on all the airports," Lake City's among them, where the NHRA's traveling Drag Safari had made its last stop in 1955 before the Nationals. And Gainesville got a track of its own: Garlits remembered racing there "for more than a year" before the NHRA "created the Gatornationals for 1970."[^garlits] (For a Florida town that said yes, and whose Jaycees turned an old air base into a Sunday strip in 1956, see [Brooksville](brooksville-jaycees-drag-strip.md).)

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Which "Williston airport" did the club use?** The *Times* calls it simply the Williston airport, leased in 1959 to a crop-dusting firm.[^uct0911n] The field now called Williston Municipal Airport was Montbrook Army Air Field in the war, appears to have closed by the end of 1944, and was deeded to the city of Williston only in 1974, so in 1959 it was not yet a city airport.[^wikiw][^eaa] Whether the drags ran on that wartime field or on another strip near town is unconfirmed. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Was Mid-State really "a group of university men" who kept Williston's boys out?** That is what Williston officials told the Lake Butler committee, as the *Times* reported it; the club's own side (Surrency's "several scores of drivers") does not say who could join.[^uct0925][^uct0911n] Gainesville's University of Florida papers or the *Gainesville Sun* could confirm who the members were. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Where did the Mid-State Timing Association race after the fall of 1959?** The *Union County Times* does not say,[^uct0925] and we have not found the club in any other source yet. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
