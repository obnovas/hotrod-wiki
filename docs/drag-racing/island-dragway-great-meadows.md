# A Golf Course or a Racetrack: Island Dragway, Great Meadows (1960–2026)

*He couldn't decide between a golf course and a racetrack, and the family strip is now the last one in New Jersey*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (the founding, the floods and the reopening come from the family itself, through a county magazine in 2021 and the track's own history, with the Warren County daily's reports of 2010 and 2013; the 1964 record is well documented but its "first" is argued; the racers' memories are one each) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> *Inside Warren* (2021); *The Express-Times* (2010, 2013); racers' memories on *Drag Strip List*; the track's own history; *Wander Jersey* (2026); *MotorTrend* (2013) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-11 (Round 38) · **Open issues:** ⚠ 1959 or 1960; ⚠ the first 200; ⚠ the year Edward Kowalick died; ◐ the Frantic Four's 7.92 · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "My life would be a lot different if this was a golf course!"

    — Melissa Milano, third-generation operator, *Inside Warren*, April 2021.[^iw21]

## The louder option

When California started building drag strips, Edward Kowalick was looking for something to do with his farm in Great Meadows. The family story, as his granddaughter Melissa tells it from her mother, is that he couldn't decide between a golf course and a racetrack, until he announced: "We're going to bring this drag racing stuff to the east coast." Construction began in the late '50s and the first races were held in 1960.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^iw21] Whether that was 1959 or 1960 is still open. He and his wife Catherine built it on part of their fields, just off Route 46 and Hope Road.[^lvl10][^lvl13] The strip uses 43 acres of the family's 500.[^iw21]

Nobody knows for sure where the name came from, and no one is left who could say. One theory: "Back in the 1800s or so, when this was all marshland, that was called Rose Island. This was always called Island Farm, so the drag strip just became Island Dragway." The other is that the land is ringed by water, "the ditches and the Pequest River on the far end – it's an island. It could be either one."[^iw21] Rhea Fountain was there "in its opening year" and kept a friend's middle eliminator trophy from March 25, 1961.[^dslnj] *Car Craft*'s 1961 calendar had it racing "Every Sunday, weather permitting" ([Pocono strips](pocono-strips.md)).

## Two hundred, and the chute

On August 2, 1964, Don Garlits brought *Swamp Rat VI* to Island for a match race against the Frantic Four and ran 7.78 at 201.34 mph, which *Wander Jersey* and the track itself call the first officially recognized 200-mph pass in drag racing.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^wj26][^idw] Jon Asher saw it. Gary Ternyila, there that Saturday, always thought the Frantic Four ran the first sub-eight that day, "at something like 7.92."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> John Vreeland still has "the 1964 Island Dragway pit pass that Don Garlits autographed for me the day he went off the end of the track because his chute didn't open."[^dslnj] In 2010 the NHRA honored Garlits at Island on the record's 46th anniversary, during the track's 50th-year celebration,[^lvl10] and *Wander Jersey* writes that he took *Swamp Rat 6B* down the same strip for a victory lap fifty years after the record.[^wj26]

The weekly racers were mostly local. Bill Diggles, "Deacon" to the other racers, ran an F/SA '59 Catalina tri-power, street-legal with "removable plates for open dumps," on Sundays through the 1960s. His son rode along "as often as I could."[^dslnj] Joe Lomastro, 19, came with one buddy and found "so many dealer-sponsored cars there," with four or five men working on each, and himself asking "What do you mean degree of camshaft?"[^dslnj] Ray DeCesare remembered an old green Hudson, "The Pickle," then the newer "Pickle Jar," and "the guy on the motorcycle with the 16 cylinder Allison aircraft engine." Don Hinkle's father ran a '58 Corvette, the "Red Baron," and on the way home they stopped "at a local hot dog stand for some awesome dogs."[^dslnj] In the 1980s Wednesday nights were for motorcycles, James Higgins wrote: "It cost us a whopping $3.00 to run," and "More than once, the fog came off the bogs and we had to quit."[^dslnj]

## Floods, and the third generation

After Edward Kowalick died<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>, Catherine kept managing the track until her own death in 2010.[^idw] By its 50th season that year it was run by their daughter Kathy DeMarcky and her family, and it had not missed a year of racing.[^lvl10] Hank Dinger of Belvidere has announced there since 1966, when he was a senior in high school.[^iw21] Melissa's first job, like every child in the family's, was "working the concession stand, filling soda cups with ice."[^iw21]

Hurricane Irene flooded the strip in 2011, and Sandy flooded it again in 2012. By the end of 2012 Kathy had had enough: "I just can't do it."[^iw21] The NHRA's northeast director confirmed the closing in January 2013 ("It has seen a lot of racing over the years. I could go on for hours"), and a "Help Keep Island Dragway" Facebook page had more than 1,500 likes by late that afternoon.[^lvl13] Melissa and her husband Carl reopened it on the Fourth of July weekend in 2013.[^iw21] Englishtown stopped drag racing after 2017 and Atco closed in July 2023, so Island Dragway, which a 2021 *Inside Warren* story had said opened "a few months" after Atco, became the last drag strip in New Jersey.[^wj26][^iw21]

He had announced a racetrack, on the family farm. Melissa and Carl reopened it. Since Atco closed in July 2023, it is the last drag strip in New Jersey.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **1959 or 1960?** The family and the track give 1960 for the first races, *MotorTrend* gives July 1960, and *Inside Warren* says Atco (Memorial Day Weekend 1960) opened "a few months before."[^iw21][^idw][^mt13] Jon Asher remembered seeing "my first-ever drag race at Island in 1959," with a "(I think!)" after one detail.[^dslnj] A 1959 test day or an informal meet would explain both. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Was Island the first 200?** The track, *Wander Jersey* and *MotorTrend* call the August 2, 1964 pass the first officially recognized 200-mph run, and the Express-Times in 2013 said simply that Garlits "was the first to clock more than 200 mph in a dragster" there.[^idw][^wj26][^mt13][^lvl13] But the *Inquirer*'s Atco history says Garlits "hit 200 mph elsewhere by August 1961," and Art Arfons's jet car ran 201.78 at Atco in May 1962 ([Atco](atco-strato-rods-south-jersey.md)). The word "officially" (an NHRA record) is what separates them. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did Edward Kowalick die, and who was Kathy?** The track's history says 1991; *The Express-Times* said 1992, in 2010 and again in 2013.[^idw][^lvl10][^lvl13] The 2010 piece calls Kathy DeMarcky the Kowalicks' daughter, as do *Wander Jersey* and *Inside Warren*; the 2013 story calls her "the granddaughter."[^lvl10][^wj26][^iw21][^lvl13] Catherine Kowalick's 2010 obituary, filed by the Hackettstown Historical Society, should settle both; its PDF came back empty for us. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did the Frantic Four run 7.92 that day?** Only Ternyila's memory.[^dslnj] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
