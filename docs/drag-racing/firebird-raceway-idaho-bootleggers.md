# A Hood in the Dump and a Strip in the Sagebrush: the Bootleggers, Gowen Field Road and Firebird Raceway (Idaho, 1953–2019)

*A club that started because another was full, a NOS hood the garbage truck took, and the first American dragstrip on the National Register*

## When the Gearjammers were full

In May 1953 Bill New and Ron "Sonny" Hon tried to join Boise's Gearjammers. The club was full—ten to fifteen members was enough for most clubs then—so on May 8, at 8:30 p.m. in Hon's home, they formed the Bootleggers. New was president, Hon vice president, Pete Wheadon treasurer, according to the club ledger Gale Sasser saved; charter membership ran to ten, with dues that started at $5 a month and fell to $2. They kept a clubhouse on Reserve Street, not far from the Gearjammers' own, and pulled in hot rodders from the air base at Mountain Home. The hangouts were Murray's Drive In, the Howdy Pardner Drive In and Kellison's Speed Shop on Broadway. New was nineteen, pumping gas at a service station by day and building a 1932 Ford three-window by night: a Bernie Green chop, a stroked 59AB Merc flathead with a Stromberg 97, primer at first and later Chrysler red. He ran it in the drags on Gowen Field Road and, early on, tried nitro in the tank.[^fb20]

Gowen Field had trained bomber crews in the war; afterward the city got the field and the Idaho Air National Guard leased it. From the mid-1950s into the early 1960s, city and police approval let temporary drag contests run on the county road beside the field so the racing would stay off the street. The National Register nomination dates those road races 1954–61; the club's own story has New racing there by 1953.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> NHRA noticed. Field Director Bud Coons came looking for a satisfactory strip.[^nrhp][^fb20]

## Pocatello, and the hood in the dump

In 1954 the NHRA announced a Safety Safari stop at an abandoned air base at Pocatello; the nomination counts two such meets, 1954 and 1955, and calls them Idaho's first major drag races at a facility set up for quarter-mile racing.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> *Hot Rod* covered the event; the strip's own story is on the [Pocatello page](pocatello-mcdougall-field-drag-strip.md). New and most of the Bootleggers drove across the state. A couple of days before the trip, while New worked after hours at Peter Wheadon's Shell on 11th and Front, he left his NOS hood, top panel and sides, "lying next to the wall outside overnight." In the morning it was gone: "the garbage truck took the hood early the next morning to the valley dump." He and his friends searched the landfill and never found it. At Pocatello, Bud Coons wouldn't pass the deuce at tech without a hood. So New's "newly purchased wheels and wide white wall tires" went onto a buddy's car instead, a point of pride when most guys could afford only imitation porta-walls. The back half of that car, and those wheels, made the *Hot Rod* feature. "His opportunity at fame," the track's family history calls it—rolling stock in the bible of early hot rodding.[^fb20][^nrhp]

Around 1956 New and Hon left for Southern California's strips—Lions, San Gabriel, Santa Ana, Pomona. Gale Sasser kept the Bootleggers ledger going with a second generation. New was drafted, served in Germany, and came home in the early 1960s to find the Gowen Field Road races no longer club-run. He started hunting land for a permanent strip.[^fb20][^nrhp]

## Sagebrush off Highway 16

Sites near Highway 55 toward Horseshoe Bend and near Eagle Road and Chinden were too close to houses. The land that worked was a vacant Dan and Beatrice Land dry farm off State Highway 16—old homestead, rolling sagebrush, central to Ada County's projected growth. Robert Blazier, Ron Ashley and Bill New formed a corporation; Wayne Wright Construction cut and paved the strip on a north-south line so racers kept the sun at their backs. The track sat in the natural valley at about 2,695 feet; hillsides on both sides became the viewing slopes. Firebird Raceway opened July 29, 1968. About 4,000 people came standing-room-only. Bob Riggle's Hemi Under Glass wheelstanding Barracuda was the feature. Evel Knievel jumped there in 1969. In 1972 Alice Cooper's School's Out tour drew an estimated 9,000—then the largest one-day concert crowd in the state, the nomination says.[^nrhp]

Pocatello's municipal strip on the old airfield ran until 1973, when the city declined to renew the lease ([Pocatello](pocatello-mcdougall-field-drag-strip.md)). Thunder Ridge at Star opened in 1967 as a 4,000-foot strip and, the nomination says, closed six years later and became a local airport. Firebird took NHRA sanction early, briefly hosted AHRA's Ignitor Nationals in 1981 (Gordie Bonin over Henry Harrison in a 16-car Funny Car field), then returned to NHRA after AHRA closed in 1984 and the American Drag Racing Association, formed by AHRA track operators, closed in 1986. Bill New sat on NHRA's Division 6 track operators' council. Garlits raced there in 1973, and the nomination lists Shirley Muldowney, Tom McEwen, Ed McCulloch, Dale Armstrong, Gary Beck and Tommy Ivo among Firebird's competitors. The Nightfire 500, first run in 1972 under the lights and now the Nightfire Nationals, counts John Force, Don Prudhomme and Raymond Beadle's "Blue Max" among its past winners. High-school racing began in 1976—second in the nation after Portland, the track says—and some local schools letter in drag racing.[^nrhp][^hemm19]

## First on the Register

On December 24, 2018, the National Park Service listed Firebird in the National Register of Historic Places—the first purpose-built U.S. dragstrip so honored. (The announcement waited until February 2019 because of a federal shutdown.) Period of significance: 1968–1978. The 1968 Christmas tree is still there, with upgraded electronics, and so are the telephone-pole track lights that made Firebird "the only lighted track in Idaho when constructed." Bill New had managed the place for 46 years when he died on February 11, 2014; Ellanor and sons Scott, John and Brad kept the family operation going. Scott New called the listing a fortification of "Firebird's footprint in western Idaho for many generations." Development has since crept up from the south and west—the very nuisance Bill New had tried to outrun.[^nrhp][^nhra19][^hemm19]

Twin Falls, on the other side of the state, had fought its Main Avenue "drag strip" nickname in the late 1950s and hunted a real strip of its own; that story is on the [Twin Falls page](../places/twin-falls-main-avenue.md). Utah's runway fights sit next door on the [Salt Lake strips page](utah-airport-no-2-salt-lake-strips.md).

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **When did organized drags on Gowen Field Road begin and end?** The National Register nomination, citing Drag Strip List, gives 1954–61. The track's Bootleggers history has New racing there by 1953, the year the club formed. Both can be true if 1953 was informal and 1954 the first season the nomination counts; a Boise or Mountain Home paper would settle it.[^nrhp][^fb20] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Was there one Pocatello Safety Safari or two?** The track's family history speaks of one, announced for 1954 and "featured in the pages of Hot Rod magazine." The nomination says New attended "the first two NHRA Safety Safari Drag Meets in eastern Idaho on an abandoned air base west of Pocatello in 1954 and 1955." The track's 2022 remembrance of Roger Worley, a Gear Jammers member, has his W.A.S. roadster at the Pocatello Safety Safari meets "circa 1954 and 1955." Which base, the old Pocatello Army Air Base or the city's McDougall Field, is a separate question on the [Pocatello page](pocatello-mcdougall-field-drag-strip.md#q-2).[^fb20][^nrhp][^worley] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
