# Soda-Pop Signs for Hoods: Linden 1954, the New Jersey Timing Association and the Short Strips (1952–1967)

*Thirty clubs met in a basement, drivers beat soda-pop signs into hoods at Linden, and Route 12 had a traffic light at the line*

In 1952 more than 30 New Jersey hot rod clubs founded the New Jersey Timing Association in the basement of Coby's Restaurant. In 1954 the Safety Safari came to Linden Airport. The main runway was too short for a quarter mile, so they ran a fifth of a mile. Drivers beat soda-pop and beer signs into hoods, and one welded his sign on. After Linden the state raced short tracks, 320 feet at Old Bridge and a traffic light at the line on Route 12, until the quarter-miles opened.

## Thirty clubs and a basement

New Jersey had more than 30 hot rod clubs by 1952, Frank Carey writes, and that year they came together, founded and incorporated as the **New Jersey Timing Association**. It was an association of clubs, each sending two voting representatives, and it met in the basement of **Coby's Restaurant** on Route 9 in the South Amboy area.[^carey1] Its first event, in 1953, was a free hot rod "jamboree" in **Johnson Park** in New Brunswick, written up in *Hot Rod* in March 1953. Then came a "Lime Bag Run," which Carey himself cannot explain, with 29 NJTA members taking part, and a Garden State Autorama at Julian's Garage on Route 9.[^carey1]

What the clubs did not have was a strip. A state sports history says the first paved ones were at **Woodbine** and the **Manville Airport** in the early 1950s<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>, and that a few years later a drag strip opened on a runway at **Linden Airport**, where stock cars had raced and where Al Keller won a NASCAR Grand National race in a Jaguar in the spring of 1954.[^njsh]

## The Safari comes to Linden

In 1954 the NHRA's traveling **Safety Safari** came to Linden with the NJTA. The main runway was too short for a quarter mile, so they ran a fifth of a mile; *Hot Rod* covered it in November 1954, and Carey's dated photographs show meets on July 25 and September 18.[^carey2] Bruce Larson was there on July 25. He was 16, and he drove his chopped blue '32 five-window to Linden and back home to Camp Hill, Pennsylvania, with his friend Bob Williams.[^dslnj]

The best account is the Safari's own. Richard Parks, telling it from Eric Rickman's journal, writes that the crew was up at 5 a.m. and was met at the airport by a group of men who had chartered a bus, the **Crabtown Dragsters** of Annapolis. So many cars came that the inspection line had to shut down. "They just about took us over," Rickman wrote. Chic Cannon turned cars away, and back they came "with their new roll bars still too hot to touch." Cannon rejected cars with no hoods, so the drivers fanned out, pulled down soda-pop and beer signs for miles around, beat them into hoods and came back to race. One welded his sign on, which meant no one could inspect the motor.[^dslnj]

Rickman was angry with the promoter, the airport manager, who said he had lost money: "The promoter sat there and lied in his teeth. We know from past experience that there were from eight to ten thousand people there. He charged a dollar a head and said he only made $2600.00."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> Whether that crowd was four thousand or eight to ten thousand is still open. Coons, of the Safari crew, told the manager he ought to fix the hole in his fence. Coons also talked New Jersey law with Officer Whycoff, the local Highway Patrol inspector, who liked how the Safari ran things. A 15-minute rain squall came through and nobody left. "The meet was a howling success in every respect," Rickman wrote.[^dslnj]

## Three hundred and twenty feet

After Linden, New Jersey raced on whatever pavement it was allowed. **Old Bridge Stadium**, a NASCAR stop from the mid-1950s to the late 1960s,[^njsh] ran drags on its straight. Jon Asher, later a drag racing editor, went around 1959–60: "Oh man, what a place! Drag races that went 320 feet!" He took his dad's car without his knowledge and called it C/GA ("yeah, I know: who has automatic gasser classes?") to get more runs. The Don's Speed Shop dragster "supposedly ran something like 178 MPH in the track's 320 feet, but who am I to question it?"<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> Ralph Bernstein drove a friend's new Mustang there in March 1964 because the owner had only a learner's permit: "The 3/8 circle track had 1/16 mile drags," and he ran 5.9 at 61 mph. A fan named Milt remembered his first trip there, around 1966, when he lettered a buddy's red Mustang "Perc's Pig" in white shoe polish, which stayed on the doors "in ghost letters for a long time!"[^dslnj]

**Joe Lomastro**, interviewed by phone in 2020, compared the tracks. At Harmony Speedway the eighth-mile ran right in front of the grandstands, on Saturday nights. At Old Bridge "you had to shut down on a curve, but not at Harmony." He had bought his '63 Max Wedge Dodge at 19 by paying a man twenty bucks to give his father a ride in one. Reedman Motors had already refused to warranty the car ("This car was made for accelerated time trials. You can't drive it on the streets of Pennsylvania"), and his father dragged him out of the showroom. After the ride his father said, "OK. You can buy the car. The guarantee? To hell with the guarantee."[^dslnj]

Down south, **Atlantic City Speedway** in Pleasantville ran an eighth-mile on its half-mile stock car oval on Friday nights. Ed Mathot worked the starting line, "holding onto door handles to keep the stick cars from rolling while the flag man (yes the flag man) jumped up in the air." Greg Zyla won his first drag race there in 1968 in a GTX; the trophy has a 1957 Plymouth on top.[^dslnj] **Vineland Speedway**, a paved oval and road course where Mark Donohue and Roger Penske raced,[^njsh] held its first drag race on May 10, 1958, Bill Hall remembered (he was 14), and ran until, Lou Casazza wrote, "The track closed at the end of '65." Hall, at 17, raced his mother's 1960 Comet there while she was on vacation ("She did not know"), and he painted the rims red. John Fiorani kept his time slip from September 5, 1959.[^dslnj]

## A traffic light on Route 12

The **Flemington Route 12 Dragway** sat near County Road 615 and Route 12, near Pittstown, northwest of Flemington. On the H.A.M.B., a poster called Bruce 23 said it was "abandoned in 1967," that a family who bought the house next door had "one helluva driveway," and that the state now owns it.[^hambpitt] Jon Asher raced there in high school in the late 1950s, which "meant taking my Dad's Dodge out there without his knowledge!"<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> Jim Colleran ran it in 1964: "I remember the traffic light at the line." Lomastro won his class there three or four times in 1964–65 in his white Dodge, "Rampage" on the fender, against Jerry Stein's red "Teacher's Pet" Plymouth and Johnny Abraitis's '62 Plymouth, "Both went on to be famous, big-time drag racers."[^dslnj]

In 1965 Raceway Park opened in Old Bridge off Englishtown Road and became a major NHRA track.[^njsh] Island Dragway at Great Meadows and Atco Dragway had both opened around 1960, and with three real quarter-miles the short tracks' day was over. It came from a restaurant basement, and at Linden the hoods were soda-pop signs.

## Open questions & disputed details
