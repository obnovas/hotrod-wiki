# "Them or Us": Lebanon Valley Dragway (West Lebanon, N.Y., 1963– )

*A dirt-track family, a rumor about the speed shop down the road, high school friends with axes, and a starting line made of two trees*

!!! abstract "Article record"

## The cornfield down the street

Most strips start with someone who wants a drag strip. Lebanon Valley, by its owner's account, started with a family that did not want someone else to have one first.[^dds08]

The family already had a race track. Lebanon Valley Speedway was laid out in 1953 in a cornfield and on the Lebanon Valley Airport, beside Route 20, then a main road between Albany and Massachusetts. Lou Spanier owned it from 1954.[^lvc] His nephew, Howard Commander, had started young. The Eastern Motorsports Press Association says an uncle once gave him a box of Sugar Daddy candies to sell to his elementary school classmates at a small profit, and he did. By 1962, at 18, he was working the speedway's concessions, and his Uncle Lou took him to Chicago for one of the first workshops for racing promoters.[^empa]

That was the year drag racing caught Commander's eye. As he told it in 2012 to Dino Lawrence of the Dover Drag Strip site, he saw how Dover, South Glens Falls and Fonda were packing in the crowds, and the speedway already had parking and room to grow. Then came a rumor. Down the street, the Red Barn Speed Shop had a big cornfield, and people said it might build a strip. Commander said it was them or us.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> He got a group of high school friends and they started knocking down trees. Charlie Putnam came in with about five pieces of land-clearing equipment, and the strip took shape.[^dds08]

## Two trees and a homemade clock

Lebanon Valley Dragway opened on August 18, 1963, NHRA-sanctioned from the first day, Commander said.[^lvd][^dds08] It started with a flag starter. The first starting lights were two "trees," one for each lane, until the NHRA had them switch to a single tree in the center.[^dds08] There was no ready-made timing equipment to buy, so the track built its own. The man who built the clocks and kept them running was Mike Nesland of Dover Plains. By Commander's account, Nesland had seen Dover's equipment and said, "I can do it better."[^dds08] The quarter mile today is 400 feet of concrete and asphalt the rest of the way.[^lvd]

The people who raced there remember small things. One forum poster, whose cousin Ted Ryan was the starter for years, called the track's red jacket, with a yellow lightning bolt and "Lebanon Valley" stitched on it, the holy grail of racing fashion. He also remembered watching cars make passes while men fly-fished in the stream beside the strip.[^dds08] Another remembered Bill Lawton's 1963 Tasca Ford lightweight running an 11.9, with the announcer shouting that it was the first Super Stock on the East Coast under 12 seconds.[^dds08] Commander said Don Prudhomme made his first five-second run there.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> He also said Shirley Muldowney got her license at Lebanon, not at Raceway Park as the film *Heart Like a Wheel* suggested.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>[^dds08]

## Where Dover went

When Dover rained out, Dover's racers called up Lebanon to see if it was running. If it was, Lawrence remembered, 30 or 40 cars towed north in a convoy through the little towns. After Dover and the Connecticut strip closed, he wrote, Lebanon Valley became their replacement.[^dds08] A Connecticut legislative analyst reported in 1998 that the state's racers had gone to Lebanon Valley, New England Dragway and Englishtown.[^ct-olr]

Commander kept both tracks. He became the speedway's general manager in the spring of 1970 and took over from his uncle, still prepping the dirt each week.[^empa][^lvc] He had a degree in business and education from what was then Albany State and had taught at Philip Schuyler High School in Albany.[^dg14] In 2014 he had been the speedway's promoter for 44 years.[^dg14] Van Abernethy counted 47 drag strips that had once run in New York and found only five still operating when he visited Lebanon Valley in September 2022.[^di23] He went for the Dover Drag Strip Nostalgia Drags, the reunion Lawrence holds there every September.[^di23] Steve Clukey ran the Plymouth Duster he had bought new in 1970.[^di23] The strip a family built so nobody else would build one is where Dover's racers still come back to race.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did a speed shop's plans push the family to build?** The story of the Red Barn Speed Shop's cornfield, the rumor and Commander's "Them Or Us" comes from one account: Howard Commander, as relayed by Dino Lawrence on the Dover Drag Strip forum on September 5, 2012, nearly 50 years later.[^dds08] We have not found where the Red Barn shop was or whether it ever planned a strip. The *Berkshire Eagle*, the *Chatham Courier* or the Albany papers of 1962–63 might say. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Shirley Muldowney get her license at Lebanon Valley?** As Lawrence relayed it, Commander said the film's hint that she got her license at Raceway Park "is Not true. She Did that at Lebanon."[^dds08] A forum member, maso, added that Clyde McCabe and Don Garlits co-signed her application there, "That's what Clyde told me anyway."[^dds08] The quote from Muldowney that Lawrence posted in reply says she got her "Top Fuel license at Cayuga in 1973," signed by Tommy Ivo, Connie Kalitta and Don Garlits.[^dds08] Lawrence suggested the two accounts might be two different licenses, an earlier competition license and the fuel license. Both sides reach us through the same forum thread, so the Lebanon Valley license stays a single account until an NHRA record or a dated report turns up. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Prudhomme run his first five-second pass at Lebanon Valley?** Only Commander's 2012 account says so.[^dds08] A date and a time slip, or a *National Dragster* report, would confirm it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
