# They Turned the Track Around: Westhampton Drag Strip, Long Island (1953 or 1952–2004)

*A Long Island strip that lasted half a century, and the racers who rebuilt its starting line to keep the neighbors happy*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (the strip's names and years come from an NHRA columnist's 2008 roundup and a 2012 local news story; the noise fight and the sale come from a 2017 *Southampton Press* story quoting racers; the early days are a blogger's photos and his readers' comments; the opening year is told two ways) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> local newspaper (*The Southampton Press*, 2017); local news site (Westhampton Patch, 2012); a Long Island car blog and its readers' comments (2009–2023); a strip-history forum (Dover Drag Strip); NHRA (2008); Hagerty (2021) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-17 (Round 42) · **Open issues:** ⚠ 1953 or 1952; ◐ who ran it first; ◐ the Thanksgiving turkeys · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "Back in the 1950s and 1960s, anyone with a hot rod or custom car on Long Island headed to a drag strip at Westhampton most Sundays."

    — Ken Bausert, who raced his '50 Mercury there, on his blog, May 20, 2009.[^kb09]

## Sundays out Sunrise Highway

Strips lost to houses and noise complaints are common enough that Hagerty calls their obituaries "commonplace."[^hag21] Westhampton's is different in one way. Told the noise was the problem, the racers rebuilt the starting line at the other end of the track with their own hands, and the strip was sold anyway. It had been running about fifty years by then.

The strip sat off Old Country Road on eastern Long Island. It opened as a dirt strip in 1953 and was paved two years later, according to NHRA's Phil Burgess, who also listed its names: Westhampton Raceway from 1953 to 1971, then Suffolk County Raceway, Hampton Raceway in the 1980s, Long Island Dragway from 1991 and Long Island Motorsports Park from 1996.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^nhra08] A 2012 Patch story calls it the "1953-built dirt drag strip."[^patch12]

Gary Goetz went as a kid in 1957 or 1958 with his brother. He remembered something close to a sandlot, with little grass, an open timing shack six feet off the ground and a flagman. He watched the Highwaymen's chopped '39 Chevy altered and the Forean Kustoms' chopped altered "Crosby," as the column spells it. He also remembered "Hiding in the trunk and sneaking in, being able to stand at the end of the quarter as close to the cars as your common sense allowed."[^nhra08]

Ken Bausert took photos there in the early and middle 1960s. He shot a '55 Ford belonging to one of the guys from the 116th Street garages "on Sunrise Highway along the way to the strip," a white '59 Chevy with a row of "kill" decals on the rear quarter glass, and his own '50 Merc at the entrance in 1961.[^kb09] His readers filled in the rest. One stopped for a cup of coffee at a donut shop on Sunrise Highway every Sunday on the way out. A Brooklyn man ran a 1963 Bonneville convertible he called "The Ox." Another raced a '55 Chevy named "Miscalculation" on Friday nights and Saturday mornings.[^kb09]

A commenter who signed himself Johnny C wrote in 2011 that his family owned the strip, and that on the Sunday before Thanksgiving his father would release live turkeys halfway down the track, then drop the flag "letting all the drivers at the line race for their Thanksgiving dinner."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^kb09] On a strip-history forum, another racer wrote that Ron Catronio's family operated it.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^dov]

## The starting line moves

Before it closed, *The Southampton Press* wrote, hundreds of people would show up on a Saturday night to watch under the lights, and many raced their own cars. Helen Lawrence of East Quogue raced there and watched her daughter win there, and she met her second husband, Jeff, at the track in 1998. Jeff Lawrence said that in the late 1960s and most of the 1970s, if you had a fast street car, the natural thing was to go to Westhampton on a Saturday night. He opened a racing-engine machine shop in 1988.[^27e17]

In 1998, he said, rumors started that the track would close over noise complaints, and his business began to taper off. Town officials asked the owner, Gary Chimeri, to turn the track around: the starting line had been near Old Country Road and the nearby homes, and now racers would start near Sunrise Highway and finish toward Old Country Road. Nick Montana, a fabricator who had raced there since the late 1970s, said the racers did most of the work. They moved and rewired the scoreboard and timing system and poured a new starting line, and he estimated that volunteers did about 90 percent of the renovations. Some drivers added mufflers.[^27e17]

Then, in 2004, Chimeri sold the track and its 66 acres to Engel-Burman of Lynbrook for an estimated $7 million, and a 189-unit senior housing development went up on the site.[^27e17] Hagerty later wrote that the land had been rezoned for residential use and that the town supervisor had told *The New York Times* a drag strip was "an idea whose time has come and gone."[^hag21] Ted Pappacena, a custodian from Smithtown who photographed the track for decades, told Patch in 2012, "I was disappointed it closed."[^patch12] Bausert went back on a rainy weekday and found a gated entrance where the old one had been, with homes about where the pits and stands stood.[^kb09] Burgess found the old strip on a satellite image in 2008, and a street in the development called Drag Street.[^nhra08]

## Back on a runway

Long Island racers towed off the island after that, to Englishtown, N.J., and farther.[^27e17] In 2016 John Cozzali of Mastic started a Facebook group called "Long Island Needs a Drag Strip."[^hag21] In 2017 the *Press* reported that a Suffolk County Legislature committee was looking for a site, and that Cozzali, who had raced an '88 Thunderbird called "The Bounty Hunter" at Westhampton, sat on it.[^27e17] In 2021 the Riverhead town board allowed eight events on the old runways at EPCAL in Calverton, about 15 minutes from the old strip, with NHRA sanction and a promoter from Florida, Pete Scalzo, who had offered compromises such as mufflers and a limit of about 115 mph to keep the sound down. The town marked August 21 as "Drag Racing Day."[^hag21]

The Westhampton racers did what towns usually ask of a strip. They moved the starting line away from the houses and poured a new one themselves. It closed anyway, and it took seventeen years and a town resolution to put Long Island's racers back on a runway.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did the strip open in 1953 or 1952?** NHRA's Phil Burgess (2008) says it "opened in 1953 as a dirt dragstrip" and was paved two years later, and gives "Westhampton Raceway (1953-1971)."[^nhra08] Westhampton Patch (2012) calls it the "1953-built dirt drag strip."[^patch12] Hagerty (2021) says "Westhampton Raceway opened in 1952. It was paved two years later."[^hag21] A forum post that copies Burgess's list of names adds question marks to 1971 and 1972.[^dov] A 1952–55 Long Island newspaper would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Who built and ran the strip in its first years?** One forum post says "Ron Catronio's family operated Westhampton Dragstrip."[^dov] A blog commenter, Johnny C, says "my family owned it," and that they drove out from Huntington every weekend.[^kb09] The two may be the same family; neither gives a name for the founder or a year. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did the owner really release turkeys on the track at Thanksgiving?** Only one blog comment, from a man who says his family owned the strip, tells it. It gives no year.[^kb09] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
