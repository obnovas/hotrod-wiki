# Sylvester and the Tweety Bird: Salina's Drag Strips and the Old Runway on Markley Road, Kansas (1950s–1970s)

*A town that forgot it had a drag strip, until a Facebook page went looking for the starting line*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Low (the strip's outline is one 2026 local radio story; the 1955 racing is a racer's own memory in the *Great Bend Tribune*; the Tweety Bird story is its present owner's account in a 2026 local news interview, built from library clippings and the first owner's memory; no 1950s–70s newspaper has been read directly) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> local radio news (KSAL, 2026); local news sites (Salina311 and the *Salina Post*, 2026); local newspapers (*Great Bend Tribune*, 2022; *Salina Journal*, 2022); the Salina Airport Authority's history page · **Last verified:** 2026-11-17 (Round 42) · **Open issues:** ◐ the air-base strip of the 1950s; ? !!! quote "In their own words"
    "Come, come watch Sylvester try to try to catch the Tweety bird."

    — Mike Blanton, Tweety Bird's owner, on how the match races were sold, to Salina311, April 26, 2026.[^s311a]

Salina's drag strip came back into view in 2026 through a Facebook page, a library's clipping file and a yellow Thunderbird. That May, 407 cars showed up at an old starting line on an empty runway. That is the story here: a strip kept by memory, until people went looking for where it had been.

Gary Wooster raced in the 1950s, when, as he told the *Great Bend Tribune* in 2022, most drag races happened on city or country roads. "Around Salina, stretches of country roads would be blocked off for informal drag races," the paper wrote. In 1955 he and his friend Jim Sampson towed a cherry red 1932 Ford five-window coupe with a four-cylinder engine to the strip at the Great Bend airport and won the top elimination and fastest time trophies.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Open question: see Open questions">?</a></sup> "There just wasn't any place around Salina that had a drag strip of this caliber," he said. Afterward he pulled the four-cylinder, put his flathead V-8 in, and drove the car to Kansas State in Manhattan as his daily driver.[^gbt22]

A 2026 KSAL story says Salina did have a strip in the 1950s, west of town at the Smoky Hill (later Schilling) Air Force Base.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^ksal26] The base closed on June 30, 1965, and the city turned it into the Salina Regional Airport.[^airport] By KSAL's account, the drag strip then moved east of town to the old runway, which had been abandoned, and in the late 1960s and early 1970s drag racing "thrived there."[^ksal26]

## Fiberglass from the ride factory

That runway is where Tweety Bird ran. Its story comes from Mike Blanton, who has owned the car for 28 years and told it to Salina311 in 2026 after the Salina library sent him newspaper articles from about 1968 to 1971 and he tracked down the first owner by calling Gary Wards until he found the right one. By Blanton's account, Gary Ward bought the 1955 Thunderbird off a Salina used car lot in 1966, when it was teal green or baby blue, and built it to race, with a Ford 427 side-oiler built by Ed Pink and an Art Carr C6 automatic. After the car rolled, Ward rebuilt much of it in fiberglass, using what he knew from Ward Manufacturing in Salina, which, Blanton said, made fiberglass amusement rides, small cars, boats and motorcycles. The front clip, roof, doors and trunk lid were fiberglass, and when it was done, Blanton said, "it only weighed 1,810 pounds."[^s311a]

The yellow paint and the name came from Dan Fritz's Precision Automotive in Salina. Fritz had a 1957 Chevy named Sylvester, and the pairing became a match-race theme. "It drew interest, it drew fans," Blanton said. It also wore license plates and was driven around town.[^s311a] One clipping promoted Tweety Bird against Ed Terry's factory-powered Mustang, and a later one said it won. Blanton said that in 1971 a Ford promotional team came through with Jack Roush and a Hemi-powered Pinto, and after Roush saw the Thunderbird run he suggested racing it. The Pinto was declared the winner, 10.80 to 10.81, "really nip and tuck."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^s311a] KSAL, separately, remembered Roush and the Ford team coming to the strip, big crowds, and once "even a Playboy Bunny" to wave the checkered flag and hand out trophies.[^ksal26] After its racing years, people told Blanton, the car sat on top of a pole at a used car lot and later hung in the rafters at Ward Manufacturing.[^s311a]

Shawn Jennings of Salina started a Salina Raceway Drag Racing page on Facebook during the pandemic to pass the time. In February 2026 he went out to the old runway and found the original starting line, still there; the finish line is under baseball stadium signage. He asked if anyone wanted to come take pictures.[^ksal26] He got a reservation from the city and called it "Back to the Track," a photo and memory day, not a race, on the old runway near Dean Evans Stadium off Markley Road, on Sunday, May 3, 2026.[^ksal26][^spost26] By 10:30 that morning, Jennings said, about 250 cars were there, before the food bank volunteers had even arrived. The final count was 407.[^s311b] Tweety Bird was expected there too.[^s311a]

The cruising never needed finding. The *Salina Journal* noted in 2022 that Salinans know the stoplight-to-stoplight races of Santa Fe Avenue, and the Leadsled Spectacular, the Kustom Kemps of America show that has called Salina home since 2005, opens with a sundown cruise down Santa Fe. That year its eighth-mile drags ran at the Berkley Family Recreational Area, the same corner of town as the old runway.[^sj22][^ksal26] The strip was the part Salina had lost. It came back the way it had been kept all along, in a car's paint and in the memories of people who knew where the starting line was.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Was there a 1950s strip at the air base, and when did the runway strip run?** KSAL (2026) says Salina "initially had a drag strip west of town at the Schilling / Smoky Hill Air Force Base in the 1950s," then moved to the abandoned runway east of town after the base closed, and thrived "for a few years, in the late 1960s and early 1970s."[^ksal26] No other source we read mentions the base strip. Wooster's 1955 memory, that there was no strip "of this caliber" near Salina, does not rule out a smaller one.[^gbt22] The base closed June 30, 1965.[^airport] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
