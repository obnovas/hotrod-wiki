# The Hospitality Drag Strip: KK Sports Arena's Big Years and Bankruptcy (Kaukauna, Wis., 1966–70)

*A farmer's ravine gets a mile-long plan, a borrowed clubhouse and A. J. Foyt, then an auctioneer*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> daily newspaper (*Appleton Post-Crescent*, Appleton, Wis.): a racer's letter to the editor, county-board reports, sports stories and photo captions, display ads, a 1969 feature on safety rules, bankruptcy reports and a trustee's auction ad, all read as scanned page OCR · **Last verified:** 2026-10-22 (Round 21) · **Open issues:** the 1966 plan to make racing parts at the site ◐; the letter's "Saturday, June 11" ◐; "opened in June, 1966" against the 1964 start ⚠; the "one of the longest in the world" claim ◐; whether the arena reopened ◐; the president's Union Grove link ◐ · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "Breaking the rear end was a 15 minute tie-up and in 20 minutes we blew the transmission and blessed their beautiful track with 7 qts. of 30 wt. oil."

    — Clarence J. Dombeck of the Fontanini–Nannini Racing Team, letter to the *Appleton Post-Crescent*, June 29, 1967.[^pc670629]

The team from Highland, Ill., had never been to Kaukauna. In June 1967 the Fontanini–Nannini car broke its rear end, then its transmission, and left seven quarts of oil on the strip. Then, as the team's Clarence J. Dombeck wrote to the Appleton paper, "someone mentioned that we could use the Ghost Rod Clubhouse to fix our car. One fellow even took Al all over Wisconsin looking for a part."[^pc670629][^pc680615]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

He said it was the first time he had written to praise or complain about any drag strip, but "to keep silent in view of the many kind gratuities extended to the team of Fontanini and Naninni at KK Sports Arena would be a defamation to our character." He signed for Al Fontanini, Reno Nannini and Bill Biaggi, and he had a slogan to offer: "Their by-line should read: The Hospitality Drag Strip!!"[^pc670629]

This is the second half of the story of the KK Sports Arena, the stock car oval in a farmer's ravine south of Kaukauna that added a drag strip after the Fox River valley's clubs spent four years asking for one. (For the asking and the building, 1960–68, see [A Strip in a Ravine](kk-sports-arena-kaukauna.md).) The same newspaper that printed the clubs' letters followed the place through its best years to the day it closed.

## A mile-long plan and a Dodge in the showroom (1966–67)

The drag strip's first summer drew a crowd and a show. On a Sunday in July 1966 a jet car called the Exodus Super Jet, running only as an exhibition, was "the main attraction of the 212 cars entered." Competition Eliminator went to a local man, Jack Brouillard of Appleton, in a Chevy-powered dragster.[^pc660727] That September the arena advertised four Chicago gassers, "Pictured in all sports magazines as the four fastest gassers in the Midwest," and added that the "Publisher of the All-American Hot Rod Association magazine will be at the KK Drag Strip Sunday."[^pc660916]

The owners were thinking bigger. In December 1966 the Outagamie County board rezoned 75 acres next to the arena to light industrial. The land "will be used for construction of a mile-long drag strip," according to Supv. Joseph DeBruin of Kaukauna, and "Other development by the firm may include manufacturing parts for racing machines," DeBruin said. The arena's holdings were now about 215 acres, north of County Trunk KK and east of State 55.[^pc661214]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

The car makers' world came to the strip through a showroom. In September 1967 Bill Maverick's "Little Red Wagon" sat on display at R & R Dodge, 1610 W. Wisconsin Ave. in Appleton, with Maverick "explaining details about the drag racing car" to "interested youngsters." It was booked into the drag program at the KK Sports Arena that Sunday.[^pc670928] The dealer got a crowd in the showroom, and the strip on the county road got its headliner. That was one way the national drag racing business reached the Fox valley.

## Big time (1968–69)

By 1968 KK was drawing the northern Illinois nitro cars. On opening day that April "an opening day crowd of 4,367 drag racing fans" saw Dick Witherby of Rockford, Ill., win the nitro class in his "Cheetah" at 7.87 seconds and 197 miles an hour. Jerry Weith of Des Plaines, Ill., went over 190, "only to have the parachute on the dragster fail at the end of his run." His car crashed over the end of the strip into a ditch, and he was taken to Kaukauna Community Hospital.[^pc680408]

That June the arena held summer nitro funny car championships, "sponsored by the United Drag Racers Association" and "sanctioned by the National Hot Rod Association." The strip record belonged to the same man whose crew had needed the clubhouse a year before: "Current Holder of the Drag Strip record at the KK Sports Arena is Al Fontanini, of Highland, Ill.," at 8.06 seconds and 176.47 miles an hour in "a 1968 nitro-fuel burning Hemi Dodge Charger with a fibreglass body."[^pc680615]

In March 1969 the paper's Tom Verhagen laid out the arena's safety rules. He opened with a crash at Covington, Ga.: "Eleven killed, 46 injured!" (That crash is told on [Yellow River Drag Strip](yellow-river-covington.md).) Then he described KK's answer. The strip had "three-quarters of a mile of blacktop track, (4,440 feet)," which he called "one of the longest in the world," with a half-mile of paved shutdown past the quarter-mile lights.[^pc690311]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> Spectators sat 60 feet from the racing. Police kept the return road to 15 miles an hour. "No alcoholic beverages are sold or allowed in the pit area and any contestant who has alcohol on his breath is immediately disqualified." The general manager, Clarence (Connie) DeLeeuw, said KK was one of very few strips with a "'safe start' area," where cars that would otherwise be pushed down the track were strapped onto platforms and "started with an automatic engine." He credited the Kaukauna police and Sheriff Cal Spice with "100 per cent cooperation."[^pc690311]

Then came the stock car big time. On June 1, 1969, the paper announced that KK would "hit the big time today" with its first 100-mile race for late model stock cars, led by A. J. Foyt and Roger McCluskey, who had finished "among the top 14 in Friday's 500-mile" race at Indianapolis. Dick Trickle of Wisconsin Rapids, KK's biggest winner on the half-mile, was in the field too.[^pc690601] "Approximately 12,000 racing enthusiasts" came. Foyt "held the lead for the next 48 laps" but was forced out, and "Butch" Hartman of Zanesville, Ohio, won. Miss Wisconsin handed him the trophy.[^pc690602]

## Ninety-six creditors (1970)

Thirteen months later it was over. On Friday, July 17, 1970, KK Sports Arena, Inc. filed a voluntary bankruptcy petition in federal court at Milwaukee, listing "$780,799 in debts and $14,081 in assets" and 96 creditors with unsecured claims of $141,675.[^pc700718] The next day the arena, "scene of several major midwest auto races since it opened in June, 1966," closed.[^pc700719]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> DeLeeuw, now vice president and secretary, said plans for "the racing complex, largest of its kind in Wisconsin," were indefinite, and he confirmed that "attempts had been made in the past to sell" it. The president and treasurer was "Joseph Van Daalwyk, route 3, owner of a large construction company." The paper summed up its life in a sentence: "Stock cars, dragsters and motorcycles have raced on KK tracks in the five years of operation."[^pc700719]

In November a trustee from Oshkosh put the arena's contents up for a "Bankrupt Auction" on the site: race equipment, restaurant and concession gear, office and ticket equipment, trucks and ambulances.[^pc701113a] The same day's paper caught Supervisor DeBruin, the man who had announced the mile-long drag strip, arguing for a rebuilt county road. He "predicted that KK Sports Arena, which was closed last summer under bankruptcy, would reopen next year."[^pc701113b]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

The clubs had asked the county for a strip, and the county had said no. A farmer's ravine gave them one anyway, and it lasted five summers. It drew Indy drivers and the Illinois nitro cars, had its own safety rules, and once lent its clubhouse to a team from Illinois. The ambulances were sold at auction.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-6"><a href="#q-6" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup><sup class="hr-q" id="qr-7"><a href="#q-7" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** *The letter dates the visit to "Saturday, June 11." In 1967 June 11 fell on a Sunday, KK's drag day, so either the day or the date is a slip. We have not found a race report for that weekend. The Ghost Rods are named only as a clubhouse; we know nothing else about the club yet.*[^pc670629][^pc680615] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** *Neither the mile-long strip nor the parts plant shows up again in what we have read. The 1969 feature describes a quarter-mile strip with long paved shutdown, not a mile-long course.*[^pc661214] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** *"One of the longest in the world" is the arena's own claim, passed on by the writer; it describes the whole paved length, not the racing distance.*[^pc690311] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** *The 1970 story dates the arena's opening to June 1966, the month the drag strip opened. The paper's own 1964 stories describe stock car racing at KK that year, and the 1968 feature says the building program "started back in 1964." See [A Strip in a Ravine](kk-sports-arena-kaukauna.md). "Five years of operation" fits 1966–70 only if counted by seasons.* <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
