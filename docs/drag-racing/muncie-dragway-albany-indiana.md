# The First Trophy: Muncie Dragway (Albany, Ind., 1959 or Easter 1960– )

*An Indiana strip three car clubs got opened, and a trophy that found its way home*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (the opening and the trophy come from a racing columnist's account of what the track's historian told and showed him; the owners' years come from an obituary and racing papers; the opening year is disputed) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> the track historian Yancey Johnson's memories and collection, as told to Van Abernethy (*Drag Illustrated*, 2025); Dave Himes's obituary in *The Herald Bulletin* (Anderson, Ind., 2011); Ball State University's archives blog (2025); *Drag Illustrated* (2018), BangShift (2011), the Don Garlits museum's Hall of Fame page for Ted Jones, a venue directory and Performance Racing Industry (2017) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-24 (Round 49) · **Open issues:** ⚠ 1959 or Easter 1960; ⚠ when the Daniels bought it · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

## Three clubs and a 1951 Mercury

What makes Muncie's strip worth a story is a trophy. The first one ever won there is dated June 6, 1959. The man who won it kept it for more than fifty years, and then he gave it away to the one person he was sure would treasure it more.[^di25]

The strip sits on State Road 67 at Albany, a few miles north of Muncie.[^bsu25][^xps] Three local car clubs, the Muncie Chariots, the Rod Benders and the Crusaders, were instrumental in getting it opened in 1959. That is how Yancey Johnson told it. Johnson worked at the track, and he is something of a historian of all things Muncie.[^di25] The local racer Carl Thomas drove his 1951 Mercury to the opening-day win and took home the first trophy, dated June 6, 1959.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^di25] Racing around Muncie was older than that. A group led by Tom Cherry had formed the Mutual Racing Association in 1938 and raced at the Muncie Steeplechase in 1939.[^bsu25]

The track was laid out with two lanes split by grass for the whole 1,320 feet.[^di25] It is still not a flat, matched pair. "Our lanes are uneven where one lane is a little higher than the other," a later owner, Scott Daniels, said. "It takes driving to the finish line. You have to be a driver, not a wheel racer."[^xps]

## Ted Jones and the Northern Nationals

For a while it was Mid-America Dragway.[^xps] Ted Jones bought his local strip at Kettlersville, Ohio, at 19 and then built a quarter-mile at Van Wert. He went on to buy Muncie, and Shelby Dragway near Sidney besides. He ran two IHRA national events at Muncie, and Larry Carrier was impressed enough to talk him into selling his strips and moving to Bristol, Tennessee, as an IHRA vice president.[^dgm] The two events were the Northern Nationals of 1973 and 1974. In the 1974 finals Dale Funk beat Gordon Riley in Top Fuel, Ron Colson beat Billy Meyer in Funny Car, and Wayne Gapp beat Bob Glidden in Pro Stock.[^xps] Barry Setzer of Hickory, North Carolina, went to the Funny Car final in 1973. Johnson kept a photo album from that weekend.[^di25] Ball State's archivists add that the track hosted NHRA events from 1967.[^bsu25]

The grass between the lanes was later paved over to the eighth mile. From the 660-foot mark to the shutdown, the lanes are still split.[^di25]

## The engine builder and the monkey

By 2007 the track had been, in BangShift's words, "largely neglected," and its future looked grim.[^bs11] In November 2007 Dave Himes, an engine builder from Westfield whose family business was called Automazing, took over its operation with his wife.[^hb11] The comeback party was a stop on *Hot Rod*'s Drag Week in 2009.[^bs11] In 2010 the national racer Kenny Underwood called it "one of the best prepared tracks in the country," his obituary says. Himes died on January 30, 2011, at 45, three months before the season.[^hb11][^di25] His widow opened the track that April anyway.[^di25] Carl Thomas, who still lived nearby, still had the 1951 Mercury from opening day, and he made a pass in it at the 2010 nostalgia race.[^di25]

Scott and Dawn Daniels bought the track in 2012.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^di18][^di25] They lived about 50 miles north in Huntington, where he ran an auto repair shop and tire store. Dawn handled the books, the gate and the concessions, and Scott handled the track.[^di18] A man who made their first T-shirts told them every successful business has a mascot, so Scott put on a monkey suit and became the "Muncie Monkey," throwing prizes into the stands. They put in a playground too, to give the kids something safer to do than ride scooters through the pits and down the return road.[^di18] When the columnist Van Abernethy met the new owner, Scott took off the monkey head just long enough to shake hands.[^di25]

Johnson showed Abernethy the crown jewel of his collection on that first visit in 2011. It was Carl Thomas's trophy from June 6, 1959. Thomas had given it to him because he was certain Johnson would treasure it more than anyone else he could think of.[^di25] Three clubs got the strip opened, and the first trophy it ever gave out ended up with the man who keeps its history.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did the strip open in June 1959, or on Easter Sunday 1960?** Yancey Johnson's first trophy is "dated June 6, 1959," and he told Van Abernethy the clubs got the track "opened way back in 1959."[^di25] *Drag Illustrated*'s 2018 profile says the track "was built in 1959."[^di18] Performance Racing Industry (2017) says the facility "opened on Easter Sunday in 1960," and so does a venue directory; Ball State's archives blog says it "opened in 1960," citing an Indiana drag strip list.[^pri17][^xps][^bsu25] A 1959 race on an unfinished strip, and a formal opening the next spring, would fit both, but no source says so. The front-matter `years` span is `[1959, 2025]`. That field is `[first, last]`, two integers, and 1959 is the earlier side of the dispute, the earlier year a source gives, so the span covers Easter 1960. It does not decide the question. The title says 1959 or Easter 1960. The 2025 end is the date of the latest column, not a close. The track still runs. The Muncie papers of June 1959 and April 1960 would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did the Daniels buy the track in 2012 or 2013?** *Drag Illustrated* says 2012, twice (2018 and 2025).[^di18][^di25] Performance Racing Industry (2017) says "Since taking over ownership of track in 2013."[^pri17] The obituary spells Himes's wife "Diane"; Abernethy's column spells her "Dianne."[^hb11][^di25] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
