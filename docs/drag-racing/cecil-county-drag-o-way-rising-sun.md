# The Traction Capital: Cecil County Drag-O-Way (Rising Sun, Md., 1963– )

*A Maryland strip whose fame was its grip, a starter who stayed for decades, and a local kid who beat the stars*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (the strip's early years come from a 2016 *Cecil Whig* history built on a former track worker's memories and the paper's own opening-day report; the traction story is also in a 1969 drag test told in *Hot Rod*) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> Paula Newton in the *Cecil Whig* (Elkton, Md., 2016), with Ron McNeal's memories; the track's own page; RacingJunk News (2015); *Hot Rod* (1999, first-person) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-23 (Round 48) · **Open issues:** ⚠ "of the Nation" or "of the East"; ◐ the first plastic-body car; ◐ who ran the strip between the mid-1970s and 1999 · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

## Opening day on Theodore Road

Cecil County's strip was famous for one thing above the rest: it stuck. The asphalt and the county's humid summers gave it a name for traction, and racers knew it as the place to set a best time. That is what drew the touring stars. The local drivers came anyway, and they filled the pits.

Cecil County Drag-O-Way opened on Sunday, April 14, 1963, and 3,000 spectators watched 135 cars race for trophies. It was sold as the finest and newest strip in Maryland: 4,000 by 60 feet of high-traction asphalt on Theodore Road, sanctioned by the National Hot Rod Association, with seats for 4,000, a Chrondek two-lane timer, an automatic wind judge and foul system, and a pit area for 800 cars that had running water. Pits opened at 9 a.m., inspection at 10, and eliminations started at 2.[^cw16]

The owners were Al Procipo and Sonny DeCesaris, contractors from Upper Marlboro who had an interest in Capital Raceway. They had bought the property from a Mr. Racine. Six county drivers took trophies that first day. Daniel Johnson of Chesapeake City ran 87.63 mph in a 1956 Chevrolet. Walter Jermyn of Elkton ran a 1963 Falcon at 72.46. Bob McMillan of North East ran 88.92 in a 1962 Ford, and Robert Trainer of North East ran 102.28 in a 1962 Corvette. Dan Boines of Elkton ran 101.23 in a 1948 Ford with a Pontiac engine, and James Given of Elkton ran 77.31 in a 1963 Corvette.[^cw16]

## Why it stuck

The strip came to be called the "Traction Capital of the Nation," the *Whig* wrote, because of its kind of asphalt and Cecil County's humid weather, and racers knew they could set their best record there.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^cw16] In 1969 a magazine writer and Ronnie Sox took a preproduction 440 Six-Barrel Road Runner there on street tires. Sox's best was 13.02 at 111.52. Thirty years later the writer still wondered why the car had run so well in Maryland: "Consider that Cecil County was billed as the traction capital of the East, and that a slight deviation from the level was part of its lore."[^hr99]

It was a Division 1 track, and in its early days it drew the big names, many from California. Drivers were paid for match races, which mattered when there were no sponsors yet. Coca-Cola later became one of the first.[^cw16]

## Ron McNeal's Fridays

Ron McNeal worked at the strip from 1968 to 1977, and he remembered how it ran. In summer there were match races on Friday and Saturday nights. Time trials ran from 3 to 7, and eliminations started under the lights at 8. The hot cars ran in lane 1 and the super stockers in lane 3. Bob Fry, who announced at Atco, came on Fridays. The timing used three lines: the speed was the average of the clocks at the first and third, and the second was the finish. The girls in the tower wrote each car's time card and dropped it down a downspout into a box, and McNeal handed it to the driver. McNeal, Willis May and Parker Keene inspected cars at the scales and put the number on the side window.[^cw16]

Tommy Grove, who traveled the circuit, once showed up with no name on his car. Procipo was not going to let him race. McNeal, who had a garage nearby, took the car there and had Larry Gilbert paint a name on the side for $35. The stars had to agree to three runs to get paid, since they were the ones who brought the crowds. The owners sold tickets themselves at the pit-side gate and paid the county an amusement tax on what they took in. During intermission they played the same Four Tops song.[^cw16] The *Whig*'s history also names Don Nicholson's "Eliminator," in 1966, as the first plastic-body car.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^cw16]

The circuit drivers were the draw, the *Whig* wrote, but the local drivers filled the pits, because everyone with a fast car wanted to see what it would do. When *CARS* magazine brought the top drivers in for a race weekend, a local, Jim Sellers, beat them all in a 1967 Chevelle he had bought from Rand Chevrolet in Elkton. McNeal hired Lance Keene of Aberdeen as the starter, and Keene kept the job until a few years before 2016.[^cw16]

## Atco's owners, and Halsey

In the mid-1970s the four owners of Atco bought the strip and renamed it Cecil County Dragway. Under them, the *Whig* wrote, it ran down.[^cw16] Jim Halsey bought it in 1999.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^rj15] By 2015 a street-car series sponsored by Strange Engineering ran there on the first Saturday of the month and drew 250 to 350 cars an event. "We still had 250+ entries," Halsey said of a July 4 race under a threat of storms; when the storms came in the second round, they split the purse and set off the fireworks.[^rj15] The track still runs a quarter mile with concrete for the first eighth, on Theodore Road outside Rising Sun.[^cctrack] Under the 2016 history a reader wrote, "My husband first started going in the summer of 1963. He is a spectator still."[^cw16] It was the grip that brought the big names to Cecil County. It was the locals, wanting to see what their fast cars would do, who filled the pits.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **"Traction Capital of the Nation" or "of the East"?** The *Cecil Whig*'s 2016 history says the strip "became known as the 'Traction Capital of the Nation.'"[^cw16] The 1969 drag test, told in *Hot Rod* in 1999, says it "was billed as the traction capital of the East," and adds that "a slight deviation from the level was part of its lore."[^hr99] Both agree on the reputation. Which billing the track used, and when, would take a period ad or program. Whether the strip has a grade is the writer's mention of lore; we have not found a survey. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Was Nicholson's "Eliminator" the first plastic-body car there, or anywhere?** The *Whig* history says "'Eliminator' driven by Dino Don Nicholson was the first plastic body car in 1966," in a paragraph of Ron McNeal's memories, and does not say whether it means first at Cecil County or first in the sport.[^cw16] We have no second source. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Who ran the strip between the Atco owners and Halsey?** The *Whig* says Atco's four owners bought it in the mid-1970s and calls Jimmy Halsey "the current owner" in 2016; RacingJunk News (2015) says Halsey bought it in 1999.[^cw16][^rj15] Whether the Atco group held it for the whole stretch from the mid-1970s to 1999 is not in either source. The *Whig* spells the Atco announcer "Bob Fry"; our [Atco page](atco-strato-rods-south-jersey.md) has "Bob Frey." <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
