# East to the End of Rittiman Road: San Antonio's Double Eagle and Alamo Dragway (1957–77)

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> first-person racer stories (a Draglist "Story of the Day," 2001, and a H.A.M.B. forum thread, 2009–14); the *San Antonio Express-News*, 1975–77, read as OCR on the Internet Archive; a 2024 funeral-home obituary · **Last verified:** 2026-10-27 (Round 24; forum, Draglist, obituary and newspaper quotes rechecked) · **Open issues:** Double Eagle's opening year ◐; Martindale as San Antonio's first strip ◐ · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "Many a son returned his father's car to him on a Sunday afternoon with a new clank and idling suspiciously high after he tried to put it through the paces at the Double Eagle."

    — A *San Antonio Express-News* writer remembering the 1950s, Aug. 31, 1975.[^sa750831]

San Antonio's drag racing history is mostly told by the people who were there, and it comes down to two strips and a back road. The Double Eagle lived on the northeast side, out past the end of Rittiman Road. Alamo Dragway sat south of town on the Poteet highway. In between, the kids who could not get to either raced on a dark stretch they called "Little Green Valley."

## The Double Eagle (1950s–60s)

A 1975 columnist looking back at the 1950s put it plainly: "The real hot rods were to be found, anyway, at the old Double Eagle Drag Strip, an attempt to legitimitize the sport which our parents never did trust."[^sa750831]

Racers who posted on the H.A.M.B. forum put the strip "off Gibbs-Sprawl between Rittiman & Walzem"; people who grew up in nearby Windcrest and Converse remember seeing its stands and starting line through the brush.[^hamb1] One poster thought "it was opened in '57 or so."[^hamb2]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> A radio ad still rang in one Austin-area listener's ears decades later: KTSA's Ricky Ware sending fans to the drags "east to the end of Rittiman Road!!"[^hamb2] The same poster thought there was an older strip too: Martindale, "a dragstrip in the 50's. Probably the first in SA."[^hamb2]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

Travis Perrenot's first run came at the Double Eagle in the summer of 1957. His family ran an A/Gas Dragster with a Cadillac engine and six Stromberg "leakers" on a homemade log manifold, with his father as "driver, owner, crewchief." Travis had turned 15 that February and held a Texas "hardship" license, which was enough for the track. Then his father handed him the car. Travis expected 140 mph, maybe 160. "First time slip (which I have plans to gold plate) says only 77 MPH!!!" And there was no escape: "They wouldn't let me hide under the pickup or in the weeds."[^perrenot]

The forum memories fill in the rest. One racer's father wrecked their front-blown Hemi gas dragster there after the crew swept the track but not the shutdown area. The car "skidded up and over the really high embankement turn and flipped," and he spent months in the hospital. A year later the son made his own first dragster run at the same strip: "14 years old and going 140."[^hamb2] The Thomas brothers' go-kart was there too, a blown four-cylinder of 71 cubic inches on 85 percent nitro that Roy Thomas said ran 10.90 at 135 mph in 1965 on Montgomery Ward boat-trailer tires.[^hamb2] The best-remembered star was August "Augie" Hartkopf, whose nickname was "Hands." The stories about him, including a "cheater" engine bored bigger on one side than the other, are told on the forum as "Rumor, or maybe legend."[^hamb2]

The Double Eagle closed, but the land stayed empty for years. "They then cut trenches accross it to keep people from racing on it, now houses sit on the land where double eagle once was," one local wrote in 2009.[^hamb1]

## Little Green Valley

Not every race was on a strip. A local who used to ride his bike to the Double Eagle from the Austin Highway before he could drive later flagged street races between Biff Jones's altered-wheelbase '36 Chevy pickup and Edgar Beck's '57, "The Fugitive," at a spot "we all called 'Little Green Valley' - Now Thousand Oaks Blvd. at Jones-Maltsburger Rd." The name borrowed from the big AHRA track near Fort Worth (see [Green Valley Raceway](green-valley-raceway-smithfield.md)). "You always knew there was a street race going down because of the twenty to thirty car parade heading north from the Austin Highway late at night."[^hamb2] Jones's pickup, later lettered "Texas Trick Truck," was a San Antonio Gear Grinders club truck, and in 2011 a club member said he still had it.[^hamb2]

## Alamo Dragway and the Martinez family (1970s)

By the mid-1970s the action had moved south. Alamo Dragway was "located five miles south of Loop 410 on Hwy. 16 going towards Poteet," and in October 1975 it booked a three-round match race between Don Garlits and John Wiebe.[^sa751004] A March 1976 *Express-News* magazine cover story by Jack Handey said drag racing would "draw some 200,000 fans locally this year," and a photo caption at the strip said "More than 200,000 drag racing fans went to the races here last year."[^sa760328] Joe Martinez, "one of the owners of Alamo Dragway," told Handey that about 1,500 children under 12 got in free each week, and summed up the draw: "Everybody's a car bug in one way or another."[^sa760328] Racers like Patsy Pettit of San Antonio waited with the rest for their turn at the starting line.[^sa760328]

In April 1977 the strip matched Shirley Muldowney against Raymond Beadle's Blue Max funny car in a best-of-three grudge race.[^sa770402] A week later construction started on Highway 16 Raceway, a half-mile oval right next door.[^sa770409]

## The nationals come to San Antonio (1977)

Then came the big one. "After 13 years at Green Valley Race City in Fort Worth, the American Hot Rod Association (AHRA) decided on San Antonio's Alamo Dragway as the site of its national event," Mike Bruton wrote in June 1977.[^sa770619] The track was run by "The Martinez brothers, John, Dee and Joe, who operate Alamo Dragway Inc.," and they expected about 40,000 fans over four days. "There's been 10,000 seats added," Dee Martinez said. "The strip has been lengthened 750 feet as a safety factor."[^sa770619] The local racer Buddy Cortinas thought the meet would stay: "if the crowds are good here, it'll be automatic."[^sa770619] The Panhandle funny car racer Randy Lackey had just moved to San Antonio from Muleshoe.[^sa770619] Why the AHRA moved is a story told on the [Green Valley page](green-valley-raceway-smithfield.md#the-ahra-leaves-1977).

The nationals got off to a rough start. The *Express-News* counted "the 12,360 on hand" when Frank Bradley's dragster "catapulted its blower at least 30 feet into the air." Gary Beck won Top Fuel by default when Jim Edwards of Angleton broke a blower belt on the line.[^sa770704] That fall the strip went back to local fare: a Wheel Stand Nationals for cars running "up to 120 m.p.h. on their back wheels."[^sa771001]

John Ray Martinez, born in San Antonio in 1951, died in 2024. His obituary says he "owned and operated the esteemed Alamo Dragway in San Antonio" and that "He was born with speed in his blood." He was laid to rest at the Mendolia-Martinez Cemetery.[^martinez]
