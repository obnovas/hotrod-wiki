# The Bookkeeper Who Owned the Drag Strip: NEITA Raceway (Cedar Falls, Iowa, 1964– )

*A timing association's strip in John Deere country, a woman who kept the books and the track, and a reunion that talks for hours*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Low (the strip's early years are known only in pieces: an obituary, a museum exhibit's blurb, a memorial site's summary of the 1967 *Courier*, and racers' posts; the track's later years come from a 2020 trade visit) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> DeAlda Ackerman's obituary (*Waterloo Courier*, 2001, kept by the University of Northern Iowa's archives) and Red Droste's (*Waterloo Courier*, 2011); the Grout Museum's 2018 exhibit page; Midwest Racing Archives (2011); Motorsport Memorial, citing the *Courier* of July 5, 1967; H.A.M.B. posts by Iowa and Nebraska racers (2010–12); a track directory; *Drag Illustrated* (2020) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-21 (Round 46) · **Open issues:** ⚠ whether NEITA began as an AHRA track; ◐ Red Droste's part in the strip; ◐ when DeAlda Steward owned it · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "Every year at our Mopar race, we also hold a NEITA reunion to celebrate the track's rich history, and those guys who raced here back in the 1960s and 1970s will sit and talk for hours about the old days."

    — Justin Kruse, track manager, to Van Abernethy of *Drag Illustrated*, 2020.[^di]

## A timing association's strip

Most small-town strips came and went. The Northeast Iowa Timing Association's strip outside Cedar Falls opened in 1965 and never stopped, and the people who kept it going were from right there: a Waterloo stock car star's name is tied to it, and a woman who worked in a college business office owned and ran it. The track still holds a reunion for the racers who knew it as NEITA.

Ground was broken in 1964, and the strip opened the next year. It was built as a quarter mile, with what today's manager calls "a quarter-mile racing surface and a half-mile of shutdown!"[^di] The name was the club's: Northeast Iowa Timing Association, NEITA for short. *Drag Illustrated* says NEITA was also its first sanction; a track directory says it was first sanctioned by the AHRA.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^di][^xtra]

Waterloo's Grout Museum, in a 2018 exhibit on racing in the Cedar Valley, called it "Ray Steward's and Red Droste's NEITA Raceway."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^grout] Red Droste was the big name in Waterloo racing. He had started Red's Auto Service in Waterloo in 1947, and race drivers came from all over to have him build their engines. "It was the city's first real speed shop," his son Rich recalled. From the early 1950s to the mid-1970s he was the stock car driver everybody in Eastern Iowa had to beat, starting at his hometown Tunis Speedway.[^mra] His obituary lists Red's Auto, a mobile home park, and Red's CB and The Sound Room among his businesses over 40 years. It does not mention the drag strip.[^drob]

## The early years

The racers came from around the region. Terry Doukas of Omaha and his partner, Gary Malmberg, ran a white '61 Corvette with a gold metalflake top. "Mostly ran NHRA but we took it to Eddieville and NEITA every year for the big races they had in the early 70's," Doukas wrote in 2012. They were runner-up to Ray Murphy's A/MP "Pulm Nuts" Cuda.[^hamb6] A pit crewman for Denny Prochaska's Top Fuel car from Iowa Falls remembered that they "tested at Cedar Falls before going to Brainard."[^hamb1]

It had its worst day on the Fourth of July, 1967. Clint Hagedorn, 25, an Air Force veteran and a supervisor at the John Deere tractor plant in Waterloo, was killed when his B/Altered, the *Corruptor*, went sideways and came apart. The *Waterloo Courier*'s headline the next day was "Clint Hagedorn 1st Fatality At NEITA."[^mm]

Raymond Steward married DeAlda, whose first husband had died in 1960, on February 23, 1966. She worked in the business office at Iowa State Teachers College, now the University of Northern Iowa, for many years, and kept the books for the Cliff and Holly Shell Station. Her obituary adds that she "owned and operated NEITA Raceway (Cedar Falls Raceway)."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^uni] Ray Steward died on June 30, 1984.[^uni] Sometime in the 1980s NEITA became Cedar Falls Raceway, and over the years it ran under the IHRA and the NHRA.[^xtra][^di] DeAlda, of Cedar Falls, died in 2001, at 83, as DeAlda Ackerman.[^uni]

## Fifty years, and a reunion

By 2010 a racer on the H.A.M.B. was asking, "wasn't NEITA an AHRA track?", and another was hunting photos of a green '37 Chevy truck called "Gang Green" that raced there in the late 1960s and early 1970s.[^hamb3] In 2012 Jim Volgarino of Waterloo asked for pictures of "early days at NEITA Raceway" for a possible book about racing in "the tractor capitol of the world."[^hamb6]

In 2014 Joe and Deb Kruse bought the track from a group led by Scott Gardner, and their son Justin manages it. He had made his own first pass there in 2004, in a 1983 Olds Cutlass. Bracket racing had moved to the eighth mile, and the quarter mile was saved for a few events. Not long before 2020, the family found some of the track's earliest timing equipment in one of the buildings, from the days of hand-written tickets.[^di] One Waterloo racer, Tom Roschen, had met his wife, Vicky, racing at Cedar Falls.[^di] Every year, at the Mopar race, the old NEITA racers come back and talk for hours.[^di] The strip a timing association built in 1964 is still running, still remembered by its first name.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did NEITA begin as an AHRA track, or under its own sanction?** *Drag Illustrated* (2020) says the track "was first called Northeast Iowa Timing Association, better known as NEITA, which also served as its original sanction before switching over to the nationally known IHRA and most recently NHRA."[^di] The XTRA Action Sports track directory says it was "Originally sanction with the AHRA (now defunct)," changed its name in the 1980s, and later ran under the IHRA and NHRA.[^xtra] On July 7, 2010, Corn Fed asked from memory, "If I remember correctly, wasn't NEITA an AHRA track?", and out plowing the same day asked for photos of the "Gang Green" truck. No reply in those posts pastes the directory.[^hamb3] The two can both be true (a local association running the strip, with AHRA sanction), but neither says so. The *Courier*'s 1965 opening coverage would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Was Red Droste a partner in the strip?** Only the Grout Museum's 2018 exhibit page puts his name on it, "Ray Steward's and Red Droste's NEITA Raceway."[^grout] His obituary and the Midwest Racing Archives' long tribute talk about his shop and his stock cars and do not mention NEITA.[^drob][^mra] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **When did DeAlda Steward own and run the track?** Her obituary says she "owned and operated NEITA Raceway (Cedar Falls Raceway)" but gives no years.[^uni] The parenthesis suggests she still had it after the name changed in the 1980s, perhaps after Ray Steward's death in 1984, but no source says when she took over or sold it. Who owned it between her and Scott Gardner's group is also open. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
