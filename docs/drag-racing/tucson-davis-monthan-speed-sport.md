# Three-Eighths of a Mile Between Cattle Guards: Tucson's Cutoff Races, the Davis-Monthan Strip and the Speed Sport Roadster (1951–1985)

*Midnight races in the desert, a runway on an Air Force base, and a roadster with a nose made of curtains*

## Cattle guard to cattle guard

Alex Jacome remembered the car culture of late-1950s Tucson as a loop of Speedway, drive-ins and the desert. "You had to go out Speedway and do the street races where you can roll up your sleeves and hang your arm out the door," he told Archive Tucson, the University of Arizona Libraries' oral history project. The hot rodders hung out at the Polar Bar Drive-In, where airmen from the base and high school boys traded punches over girlfriends. A motorcycle officer named John Dudak enforced the noise law outside Tucson High, his foot on the bumper, writing tickets, so "the guys would go home and fill the exhaust pipe full of steel wool and then the car would pass the quiet test."[^at]

The real racing was northeast of town on the Mount Lemmon cutoff, three-eighths of a mile from one cattle guard to the next: "whichever one got to the other end first won." Spectators lined the road with their headlights on the pavement. When the sheriff's department raided it from both ends, "you'd see all these sedans and coupes and roadsters going—and pickups—going in all directions through the desert."[^at] Red Greth, who came to Tucson at 14, raced there too; the *Arizona Daily Star*, citing the Pima County Sports Hall of Fame, says the start and finish lines "consisted of two cattle guards." At 16 he was driving to Phoenix for organized drags, "since there was no track in Tucson."[^ads21]

## Marana, and the clubs

The first organized drags near Tucson were on the old Marana Army Air Field, northwest of town. Thomas Funk says his father, Duane Funk, was an early member of Tucson's Outcasts club, which was "racing as early as 1951 at Marana"; he has a photo of his dad's street coupe with an ad for the Marana races painted on it and a 1951 plate tag. Dave Smith, whose father was a Highway Patrol officer, thought he was there for the first race, in 1952: "The motorcycles were the fastest."[^dslaz] Ted Sitterley drove in his first drag race at Marana early in 1953 on a learner's permit, "but no one cared," and remembered "Lyle Fisher spinning donuts in the dirt, Billy Herbert on his Velocette motorcycle, the Lords, and all the other young racers."[^dslaz] The Lords were the "storied 'Lords of Tucson' car club" that Greth joined while still in high school.[^ads21] A Palominas man whose father, Larry L. Price, raced in Tucson in the 1950s keeps a newspaper clipping on the garage wall about the formation of one of the city's first hot rod clubs, which he calls the Low Riders, from Tucson High.[^hamb12]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-6"><a href="#q-6" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

Southern Arizona's other towns ran their own. Jim Baker won his first trophy, inscribed "CGTA Class Winner," at an airport strip just north of Casa Grande on the Tucson–Phoenix highway that ran on the third Sunday of each month in the late 1950s; in Yuma a racer's son still has his father's Yuma Timing Association shirt, trophies and the Tucson Timing Association's rule book.[^dslaz]

## The runway at Davis-Monthan

"Got such an uproar about street racing that they opened up the runways at Davis-Monthan for organized racing on Saturdays, Sundays," Jacome said.[^at] A racer who went as a junior high student placed the strip on "an unused runway" at the north end of the Air Force base.[^hamb08] The *Star*'s columnist Greg Hansen dates its opening to 1957 and calls it "one of the Southwest's featured racing sites," with a crowd estimated at 5,000 on June 17, 1957; the photos of Greth's roadster on the Cacklefest site put its first outings there in early 1956, at a base "that allowed drag races once a month," and Cyril Leon says he raced the second event there in 1956.[^ads17][^cf][^dslaz]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> Leon raced it "until all racing was cancelled"; Drag Strip List's editor found him in the results winning B Open Gas at 106 mph on Oct. 19, 1958, and Middle Eliminator in his "Taco Wagon No. 3" on Sept. 25, 1960.[^dslaz]

The car the crowds came to see was the Speed Sport roadster, built in 1955 by Greth, Lyle Fisher and Don Maynard around a 331-cubic-inch Chrysler Hemi with six carburetors, on 100 percent nitro, under a '23 T body.[^cf] Its nose was fiberglass, which the Cacklefest site thinks made it "the first drag car to ever use fiberglass in the body configuration." Sitterley, a partner in the Speed Sport roadsters, explained where the fiberglass came from. Driving around, the builders "found a trash can in an alley full of fiberglass drapery that was being thrown away," the kind decorated "with palm trees, palm fronds, and Hawaiian stuff. That's what the nose was made out of. Fiberglass drapes. I don't think fiberglass drapes really caught on."[^dslaz][^cf] The steering wheel came from a B-29 trainer.[^ads21] Its eight exhaust pipes, all under the rear of the car, gave it the nickname "Ol' Noisy," and its best was 9.10 seconds at 169.11 mph. It took top honors at the AHRA Nationals one year in [Great Bend, Kansas](great-bend-srca.md), and in three years "it was never beaten in its class."[^cf] Hansen has Greth driving it 154.3 mph before the 5,000 in 1957; the *Star*'s obituary story has him running 154.25 in 1958, in a match race against Bob Huebner, whose jalopy coupe got a flying start at 90 mph and won by a car length.[^ads17][^ads21]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>

The team retired the roadster in 1958 for a blown Speed Sport II.[^cf] A Speed Sport III ran 174.03 mph, by the 2021 story a Davis-Monthan track record set on Nov. 19, 1961, by Hansen's column a world record in 1963, and the following year, by the 2021 story, a turbine-powered Speed Sport IV set the base strip's e.t. record of 8.75 seconds.[^ads21][^ads17]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> The junior high student remembered an Art Arfons jet dragster so new "they had not even made the body panels yet."[^hamb08] Then the Air Force took its runway back: Hansen writes that the base "closed the dragstrip in order to began housing out-of-service B-47 bombers."[^ads17]

## Houghton Road

In 1964 the original Tucson Dragway opened at the northeast corner of Valencia and Houghton roads, with Fisher among its owners and Greth helping to run it, and more than 6,000 people came to the first race.[^ads21] It was a hard place to grow up fast. Phil Minor, one of the young racers Greth mentored, was killed in 1965 at 17 when he lost control of his dragster at 160 mph. "That wounded dad for a long time," Greth's son Kenton told the *Star*.[^ads21] In November 1966 Greth and Fisher booked Evel Knievel, in Tucson waiting for permission to jump the Grand Canyon, to jump ten cars loaned by Bill Breck Dodge at their car show.[^ads21]

The books were kept by a racer. Dorothy Fisher, Lyle Fisher's wife, started drag racing in 1959 and raced on and off into the mid-1960s, in a Chrysler 300, a '29 roadster, a '59 Corvette and an A/S Dodge Charger. She traveled with the Speed Sport roadster's barnstorming tour and, as secretary for Tucson Dragway, took care of "the books, personnel, entries, trophies, pit passes, and concessions." That account comes from a compiled biography site, not yet from a Tucson paper.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-8"><a href="#q-8" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>[^wdr]

Greth sold display ads for Tucson Newspapers Inc. for 47 years, and his house was a flop house for racers headed west. "I would walk in the living room, stepping over sleeping bodies," his son said.[^ads21] Neighborhood kids learned to weld in his shop. "Every waking moment I had that I was not in school or doing homework, I was at that shop," said Dan Owens, who moved in next door at 6 in 1965.[^ads21] At the strip, a fan swept the stands as a kid to get into the races, "home of the fisher & greath speed sport special."[^hamb08]

By the 1970s the strip drew national meets. Doug Waggoner remembered the AHRA Winternationals there every January, with "about 35,000 spectators every year"; in 1979, 114 Top Comp dragsters tried to qualify for 32 spots, so a second 32-car bracket was added, and he won it.[^dslaz] An airman stationed at Davis-Monthan in 1976 raced a Camaro the Arizona Air National Guard had sponsored in red, white and blue "almost every Saturday night at the old Houghton Road drag strip."[^dslaz]

The old strip closed in the mid-1980s: in 1984, by the 2021 story, or after the AHRA Winternationals of Feb. 17, 1985, by a 2017 blog and a fan's page.[^ads21][^dsb][^grc]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> Why it closed is told as a story. A forum poster, asking whether the strip had been called "Corona," recalled that if so, its owner in the late 1970s or early 1980s was "busted flying his little plane full of drugs from Mexico and landing on the drag strip," and another answered, "I remember when they busted the strip."[^hamb08]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> The *Star* dates the present Tucson Dragway, near the Pima County Fairgrounds, to 1997, and Dan Owens told the paper that Greth "was instrumental" in getting it opened.[^ads21][^dsb][^grc]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-7"><a href="#q-7" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> Greth had already brought the old roadster back: he restored it in 1997 for a display spot in the then-new NHRA Motorsports Museum, and the Cacklefest site, in an undated note, says that when it is not on the road it sits in the museum at Pomona.[^cf] He died on April 13, 2021, at 86, and that spring, by the *Star*'s account, the bright red Speed Sport was traveling with the track's manager to promote the strip.[^ads21]

Jacome had one more Tucson story, a reminder that in the desert a runway was never just a drag strip: "I drag-started a race car against an airplane." The dragster, he said, beat the plane to the end of the strip.[^at] (Minot, North Dakota, had [its own car-against-a-Cessna race](north-dakota-minot-borrowed-strips.md) in 1964.)

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did the Davis-Monthan drag strip open: 1956 or 1957?** Greg Hansen in the *Arizona Daily Star* says it "opened in 1957."[^ads17] The Cacklefest site dates photos of the Speed Sport roadster at Davis-Monthan to "early 1956" and 1956, and Cyril Leon says he raced "the second race event held at DM air base in 1956."[^cf][^dslaz] The *Star*'s 2021 story says only that it opened "a few years prior" to 1958.[^ads21] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did Greth run 154 mph at Davis-Monthan, and in what race?** Hansen: on June 17, 1957, before a crowd of about 5,000, "154.3 mph."[^ads17] Schmidt, citing Drag Strip List: "In 1958, Greth hit 154.25 mph" in the Fisher-Greth Tucson Service Station Supply roadster, in a special match race that Bob Huebner's flying-start jalopy won by a car length.[^ads21] They may be two runs or one run with two dates. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When was the 174.03-mph run: 1961 or 1963, and was it a track record or a world record?** Schmidt: "On November 19, 1961, Greth set a track record in his Speed Sport III roadster, hitting 174.03 mph." Hansen: "in 1963 he set a world record by reaching 174.03 mph."[^ads21][^ads17] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did the old Tucson Dragway close: 1984 or 1985?** The 2021 *Star* story says the original site "closed in 1984."[^ads21] A 2017 blog and a fan's page both say it closed after the AHRA Winternationals of Feb. 17, 1985 (the fan wrote "It seems the track was closed after the AHRA Winternationals in February 1985"); the blog may draw on the fan's page. A third account points to a 1985 meet at the old strip: Cyril Leon, under "Tucson Dragway, 1984-85," wrote that his "biggest wins were in 1984 and 1985 at the Winter National s alcohal dragster class."[^dsb][^grc][^dslaz] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Was the strip really closed over drug flights?** The story comes from forum memories, and the two written versions we have hedge it: the 2017 blog ("The story goes ...") and the fan's page ("As I understand it, the track was being used as an airstrip for the delivery of illegal drugs").[^hamb08][^dsb][^grc] We have read no court record or news report of a bust. Lead: the *Arizona Daily Star* and *Tucson Citizen*, 1983–85. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-6" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **What was the Tucson High club in the Price clipping called, and when was it formed?** Price's son first calls it "the Low Riders (formed in 1943-1954 at Tucson HS)," then calls a club plaque from about 1952 "the Low Rollers of Tucson" and says it was probably the city's first hot rod club.[^hamb12] The clipping itself has not been published. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-6" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-7" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did Tucson's new strip open: 1985 or 1997?** The 2021 *Star* says Greth helped get "the current Tucson Dragway opened in 1997."[^ads21] The 2017 blog says that after the old track closed in February 1985, "a new track, Southwestern International Raceway, a 1/4 mile dragstrip, opened shortly after the closing," later renamed Tucson Dragway; the fan's page of about 2007 already calls it Southwestern International Raceway "at the Pima County Fairgrounds."[^dsb][^grc] The same blog says the original strip "was built in 1962, and opened in 1964"; the *Star* gives only the 1964 opening.[^dsb][^ads21] Lead: the *Arizona Daily Star*, 1985–97, and Pima County Fairgrounds records. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-7" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-8" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **What did Dorothy Fisher do at Tucson Dragway, and when?** Only the *Speed Queens of the Drag Strip* biography so far says she raced from 1959 and served as the strip's secretary; it gives no dates for that job. A Tucson Dragway program or an *Arizona Daily Star* entry list would confirm it.[^wdr] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-8" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
