# Light Bulbs on the Runway: Western Washington's Airport Strips, from Shelton to Bellingham (1952–1977)

*Navy fields, county airports, a refinery's donation and a quarter-mile of 100-watt bulbs*

## Fields the war left behind

Washington was once home to "nearly two-dozen drag strips, many housed on former military airfields."[^exch] West of the Cascades, the first organized drags we have found were at the Shelton-Mason County airport on May 11, 1952, put on by the Lewis County Quarter Milers, a Chehalis safe-driving club founded in 1950. The Tacoma Timing Association, which had no strip of its own in Tacoma, raced at Shelton, Bremerton or Gray Field instead; one note puts its membership at 125 in 1953, a photo caption elsewhere at 135.[^dslwa]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> Ken Simons remembered Shelton's "Gopher Strip" from his high-school years, with an instructor, Bill Hartline, and a promoter, Eddie Kneeland.[^dslwa] Of the Labor Day 1957 Cascade Drag Races at Shelton, Spokane's drag racing historian Gary Hordemann, citing the *Spokesman-Review* of Sept. 1, 1957, wrote: "we never had another such dominant performance."[^dslwa]

## Arlington: the Navy's field

Arlington's airport had been the Navy's auxiliary air station in the war, and the federal government did not quitclaim it to the city until Feb. 25, 1959. By then the hot rods had been there for years. "Drag racing began on the airport in 1953," the city's history says. Cars ran on the north-south and northeast-southwest runways and the taxiways until 1964, when the FAA barred racing on the north-south runway, and on the other until 1969.[^arl] HistoryLink calls it "a popular site for drag racers from miles around."[^hl] A 46-second color film from about 1955, likely of a Western Washington Timing Association meet there, shows local police on hand, "in these early days of hot rodding, when the goal was getting racers off the street and on to the track."[^jj] Racers from British Columbia came south too: a Vancouver club, the BC Custom Car Association, said it raced at Arlington the same year it formed, 1952.[^dslwa]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>

## Bremerton: the club that took the lights home

In Bremerton the street racing was on Pacific Avenue, from First to 10th streets, until the early 1950s, when the county airport became a quarter-mile strip "With guidance from the Crankers Car Club." The Handlers Car Club, formed in 1956 by John Flesher, Leon Fox and Dean Benson, leased the strip from the county airport board for the 1960 season, and it has promoted racing there ever since.[^cp09] A Drag Strip List note says the club began at West High School and, with a county youth auto council, leased ten dates for 1960; its first night race, on Oct. 15, 1960, drew up to 4,000 people.[^dslwa]

Night racing meant a temporary strip. "Sam Fitz and I were the ones winding up all of the lights when racing was over because we couldn't leave anything behind," Flesher said. "Nothing over a foot high was allowed near the air strip because it was still in use as an emergency landing area for the airport."[^cp09] Fitz, a Bremerton businessman, became promoter and booked Jerry Ruth, Don Garlits, Tommy Ivo and Art Arfons's jets. The strip has always been NHRA-sanctioned and held Division 6 events through 1975.[^cp09] A young Gary Beck, later a two-time Top Fuel world champion, and Gaines Markley shared a DeSoto-powered altered: "We raced at Bremerton, Arlington, Bay View and Shelton, as best I can recall."[^cp09] It was a club, not a business, said a staff member in 2013: "It was a car club. This was a social thing." Of about 100 original Handlers, only Gordon and Barbara Howell were still active at the track.[^kdn]

## Bellingham, Bay View and the north

At Bellingham, cars raced on the southwest quarter of the airport by 1953. In 1954, a newspaper note says, General Petroleum (Mobil) gave money from its Ferndale refinery "to provide the youth of Whatcom County a place to race under the supervisor of the Sheriff's department"; one of the clubs was the Road Angels of Sumas.[^dslwa]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> To the south, the Skagit County Airport at Bay View had drags in 1958–60, and racers met at the Toledo-Winlock airport in 1954.[^dslwa]

## Puyallup: Thun Field

The strip at Thun Field, near Puyallup, was the one Spokane's Eagle team ran on Saturday nights before towing to Woodburn, Ore., for Sunday.[^sr05] "The only name I ever heard was Thun Field," Fred Austin said of 1957–59, and another racer, Chuck Tamburello, remembered it as "notorious as an outlaw track" before it closed in 1977.[^dslwa] Pierce County's own plan for the area later recorded that "Puyallup Raceway Park near Thun Field was closed down partly due to community opposition protesting the noise and other impacts from the auto race track."[^pierce]
