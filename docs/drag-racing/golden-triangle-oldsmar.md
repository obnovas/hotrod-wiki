# A Cow Pasture in the Swamp: Golden Triangle Drag Strip and the Blockbusters (Oldsmar, Fla., 1950s–1970s)

*A skinny flagger, a pair of backyard racers, and a pasture between Tampa, Clearwater and St. Petersburg where the touring names came to choose off Garlits*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Low (two flaggers' memoirs and later forum memories agree on the Blockbusters, the Oldsmar pasture and Garlits as a regular; they split on how he got the name Swamp Rat, and no period newspaper page was read this round) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> firsthand memoir (Bob Nugent, HotrodsOnline), H.A.M.B. forum recollections (2011–2021), the revived Strokers Car Club's own history page · **Last verified:** 2026-11-08 (Round 35) · **Open issues:** ⚠ whether the swamp, or a California insult, named him Swamp Rat; ◐ opening and closing years; ◐ Art Malone's 1959 record · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "I began to wave the flags. A roar went up. They had to run it over. That's it! no question, no flincing, call'em like I see'um."

## Sunday morning to the pasture

Bob Nugent remembered the drive out: a crisp Florida morning, a black 1956 Pontiac hardtop against a Sierra Gold Ford Tudor, a horse lifting its head as they flew past a horse track, several miles of deserted road to the cutoff. "We went to the Golden Triangle Drag Strip in Oldsmar, Florida and made drag racing history." It was, he wrote, "a cow pasture, out in the boondocks, in between nowhere and noplace." He and his buddy Larry Davis were the flaggers. "Standing in front of a pair of backyard engineered racers and flagging them off was a cool but dangerous business."[^nugent]

On the H.A.M.B. forum in 2011, a poster writing as hotrod1940 said he too had been a flagger there "in the late fifties and early sixties," that the club running it was the Blockbusters, and that the strip sat in Oldsmar "between Clearwater, Tampa and St. Petersburg and literally out in the swamp." Nugent's memoir puts the same club in charge: young guys who put on car shows, ran the drag strip and bought their own building. The revived Tampa [Strokers](../people/don-garlits.md) club, on its own history page, lists Golden Triangle among the early central-Florida strips its predecessors ran, along with Henderson Field, Brooksville Airport and Zephyrhills Airport.[^hamb11][^nugent][^strokers]

Nugent wrote that the strip's advantage "was that it was virtually in the backyard of Don Garlits." Garlits would come "to test his car out and collect $100 because he usually took top eliminator." "He was not particularly fond of the track and made that fact known, but being a racer, he went where he had to." The touring names came "to choose off the king." Nugent remembered flagging Garlits, Chris Karamesines, Connie Kalitta, Art Malone, Connie Swingle and "T.V." Tommy Ivo, in both the twin-engine and four-engine Buick dragsters. He also remembered [Don Prudhomme](../people/don-prudhomme.md), "but he wasn't driving then, he was the crew driver for Ivo."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^nugent]

His favorite local was Ed Pantley, who ran a small Chevy-powered dragster with barbell weights bolted to the front to keep it from doing wheelies. Every so often Pantley would say it was time to please the crowd, take the weights off and carry the front wheels halfway down the track. If the show slowed down, the flaggers would ask him to do a wheelie and, Nugent wrote, "the crowd would go nuts."[^nugent]

## "Call'em like I see'um"

Nugent's sharpest memory is a winter Top Eliminator between Karamesines and Garlits, on a day he was the only flagger, "one skinny and nervous kid," with "literally thousands of people lining the edges of the quarter mile track." The strip was narrow, with banks close on each side. There was no Christmas tree; the flagger started the cars. Karamesines came to the line. Garlits inched up "when he was good and ready and not a second before." Nugent pointed the red flag at each man, put it beside the green, both down. "The exact second before I decided to raise the flag, Garlits was gone. So was the 'Chizler.'" Every eye was on the flagger. Garlits had jumped. Nugent waved them back. Pat Garlits drove out to tow her husband in, "eyes were straight ahead, no reaction." Guys at the far end later told Nugent that "the 'Swamp Rat' was not a happy camper." As the car was towed past, "I had saw two eyes stare a hole right through me." Who won the rerun? "I forgot."[^nugent]

How Garlits got that name is told, as a California insult in a 1959 *Drag News* ad, on the [Setto Postoian page](../people/setto-postoian.md). Nugent himself wrote that before he was "Big Daddy," Garlits "was known as the 'Swamp Rat', a name given him by the dudes in California." The H.A.M.B. flagger remembered it differently: the Oldsmar strip "is where Garlits got the name 'Swamp Rat.'"<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^nugent][^hamb11][^postoian]

The strip was not NHRA-sanctioned, Nugent wrote, "therefore any runs made there were not official." He remembered Garlits setting a world's record one day that was not recognized; a few months later Garlits broke the record at a West Coast track and it counted. Nugent could not remember whether the figure was 160 mph or 180 mph. Years later he visited the abandoned site and "stood in the same spot that I flagged years ago": junk parked over what was left, "little of the track remains." Not all the memories were fond. "Two people lost their lives at that track. I remember a lot of fist fights. I am haunted by the memory of a separate section for black people." At Garlits's museum he saw a poster advertising Golden Triangle, and in another room a photograph of a skinny kid beside Garlits at [Brooksville](brooksville-jaycees-drag-strip.md). The skinny kid was him.[^nugent]

## Twin City nights

On the same H.A.M.B. thread, the editor of *Drag Strip List*, writing as Beavertail, said he was trying to date the closing of "Twin City Dragway (aka Golden Triangle Drag Strip)." He had "found newspaper articles about it through 1975" and thought it might have run a year or two longer; he also wrote that he had uploaded a newspaper photograph of Art Malone setting the 1/8th-mile world record while driving for Garlits in 1959 at Golden Triangle. Those newspapers were not opened this round.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^hamb11]

A later poster, writing as ancientdad, said he had submitted his own history of the track to *Drag Strip List* and had Super 8 film his father took while he ran at Twin City in 1973–74. "Very few bikes ran there back then and bikes ran against cars in bracket racing, first in their Top Dog/Little Dog series every Friday and Saturday nights and later in their points series throughout the summer." He won the 1974 points series on a Honda 450, "because I was there practically every Friday and Saturday."[^hamb11]

The years the story covers, 1959 and 1975, are the earliest and latest dates named in those later notes—the Malone photograph and the last newspaper the list's editor said he had found—not opening and closing dates read on a page.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did Garlits get the name "Swamp Rat" from the Oldsmar strip?** The H.A.M.B. flagger wrote that Golden Triangle, "literally out in the swamp," "is where Garlits got the name 'Swamp Rat.'" Nugent, the other flagger, wrote that the name was "given him by the dudes in California." The site's canonical account is on the [Postoian page](../people/setto-postoian.md): Detroit's Setto Postoian called Garlits "a swamp rat" in a 1959 *Drag News* ad, and Garlits named the cars after the insult.[^hamb11][^nugent][^postoian] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **When did Golden Triangle open and close, and did Art Malone set an eighth-mile record there in 1959?** No period newspaper page was read this round. A *Drag Strip List* editor wrote on the H.A.M.B. that he had newspaper articles through 1975 and a newspaper photograph of Malone setting the 1/8th-mile world record while driving for Garlits in 1959. A later racer dated his own running there to 1973–74. The two flaggers describe the late 1950s and early 1960s without an opening year.[^hamb11] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Prudhomme crew for Ivo at Golden Triangle, and did Garlits run an unrecognized record there?** Both details come from Nugent's memoir alone: Prudhomme "wasn't driving then, he was the crew driver for Ivo," and Garlits set a world's record that the unsanctioned strip could not make official, either 160 or 180 mph, Nugent was not sure which. The Ivo four-engine Buick and the other touring names in that paragraph are from the same memoir.[^nugent] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
