# Headlights, Scoreboards and Rollers: Jim Hullinger's Bunker Hill Dragstrip (1956– )

*Indiana's first strip was a stock car man's workshop, and the push-start problem was one more thing he fixed*

## A stock car man's strip

Bunker Hill was Indiana's first drag strip, and it was run by a man who fixed what bothered him. He lit the track with car headlights. He built his own scoreboards. And when push-starting dragsters slowed his show, he built a machine to start them, and other tracks bought it.

Jim Hullinger was born in Howard County on July 30, 1925, graduated from Clay Township High School in 1943 and served in the Army from 1944 to 1946. He married Mary Matt Crenshaw in October 1945. He worked at Continental Steel and Syndicate Sales. In the 1950s he was a leading stock car and modified racer at the Kokomo and Warsaw speedways.[^obit]

He opened Bunker Hill on August 25, 1956.[^kt21] By a 2015 summary of a *Kokomo Tribune* story, the original owners built the track "atop the gravel base of an old interurban railroad line."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^iib15] The racers came from the towns around it. A 2003 reunion notice, read only in a search excerpt, honored four early members of Central Indiana Timing. One was John Eads, whose home-built, Cushman-powered scooter beat stock and modified cars at better than 80 mph, with a camshaft ground in his own garage. Another was Fred Robinson, a charter member of the Quarter-Milers Car Club of Tipton, who had first raced at the Converse airport in 1955.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^drc03]

## Headlights and scoreboards

Hullinger did his own engineering. NHRA's Phil Burgess, writing in 2020, called him "an industrious guy, having previously developed his own track lighting (using car headlights) and had built his own scoreboards."[^nhra20] Bunker Hill had no hot-car staging, and Hullinger "figured there had to be a better way of getting the cars lit and staged." He is generally credited with the first roller starters, built for his own track in 1964 and set in the staging lanes, so a driver could light the engine there and drive straight into the burnout box.[^nhra20]

He kept tinkering and sold them as the Safe-Start. A one-car unit cost $3,377 and a two-car unit $5,495, each driven by a 283-cubic-inch Chevy engine and transmission. Wider ones were made for sprint cars, and he staged a demonstration for USAC. NHRA counts as many as three dozen tracks that used them, mostly in the Midwest, Indianapolis and Bowling Green among them.[^nhra20] His obituary puts it at almost 40 units at major tracks and says parts of the design were patented.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Open question: see Open questions">?</a></sup>[^obit] The first ones had a hydraulic ramp that came up behind the car to hold it, and it did not last long. His son Jay remembered the trouble drivers had. A driver who ran the tires faster than the rollers could shoot off the platform, and when he grabbed the brake the rollers "would slam him back against the gate. It was just amazing that there we so many racers who couldn't figure out the basis of it."[^nhra20]

Roller starters lasted only from 1964 through 1974. NHRA required remote starters for Top Fuel cars in 1975.[^nhra20] Roland Leong said rollers in the pits "made it a lot safer" and called them "definitely a blessing."[^nhra20] By 2020 the collector Mike Roth had dug Bunker Hill's original single-roller bed out of "its long resting place at the Hullinger home," its chains rusted and broken, to restore it.[^nhra20]

## The kid on the bicycle

The strip stayed a hometown place. Jerry Engel of Bunker Hill raced a Dodge there in the 1960s. His son Brett rode his bike to the track as a boy: "I used to ride my bicycle there and Jim Hullinger (the track owner) would chase me off." From home, a few miles away, he could hear the cars on Saturday nights. Brett started racing motorcycles at Bunker Hill in 1986, won a track championship in his first full season in 1987, and by 2022 his father was his pit crew.[^bh22]

Hullinger and his wife ran the track until they sold it to Steve and Darlene Daniels in the 1990s.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^obit][^drc03][^iib15] It was repaved in 2010. In 2015 the Daniels put it on the market, 23 acres in Miami County with a house, a restaurant and a barn, for $650,000.[^iib15] In 2021 it marked 65 years with bench racing and free cake and ice cream.[^kt21] Today it is an eighth-mile strip run by the former Top Fuel racer Terry McMillen and his wife, Cori.[^bhabout] Hullinger died in Kokomo on January 23, 2012, at 86. In lieu of flowers, the family asked for gifts to Racers for Christ or the Drag Racing Association of Women.[^obit] He had lit his strip with headlights, and he started its dragsters with a machine he built himself. The rollers spread to other tracks from Bunker Hill.

## Open questions & disputed details

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Sold in 1996 or 1997?** The *Kokomo Tribune* obituary says Hullinger "founded and operated the Bunker Hill Drag Strip from 1956 to 1997."[^obit] The 2003 reunion notice, read in a search excerpt, says he ran it with Mary "until selling it to the Daniels' in 1996."[^drc03] A 2015 summary says only that the Daniels bought it "in the 1990s."[^iib15] The paragraph of the 2021 *Tribune* notice that gives the sale year was not readable to us. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Was the strip laid on an interurban bed?** Only *Inside Indiana Business*, summarizing a 2015 *Kokomo Tribune* story, says the track was built "atop the gravel base of an old interurban railroad line."[^iib15] Which line, it does not say. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Eads's scooter and Robinson's Converse airport races?** Both come from a 2003 *Drag Race Central* notice of the first Bunker Hill Hot Rod Reunion. Its page returned a bot block (Incapsula) to every request, and we read it only as a search-engine excerpt, so it is paraphrased here and nothing from it is quoted. The same excerpt says Eads's scooter was in *Hot Rod* in 1958, which we have not seen.[^drc03] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
