# Garlits Drive and a Hundred Acres of Pine Trees: Blaney Drag Strip and the Midlands (1957–1985)

*A road builder's hobby became Blaney Drag Strip, the strip moved on the same land in 1967, and the mobile-home park named its streets for drag racers*

Smith Jr.'s obituary (2018); David R. Burlington's memoir on *GeorgiaDragRacing.com*; racers' memories on *Drag Strip List* · **Last verified:** 2026-11-12 (Round 39 citation review) · **Open issues:** ⚠ 1962 or earlier; ⚠ why Blaney moved; ⚠ leased or bought; ◐ Bennettsville · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "My husband did it as a hobby . . . We owned the road construction equipment and the real estate, and drag racing was a growing sport, so we saw it as a business opportunity and a change [chance] to have some fun."

    — Sandra Smith, to Jim McLaurin of *The Columbia Record*, November 1985 (as printed; the bracket is ours).[^mcl85]

Ed and Sandra Smith turned a road builder's hobby into Blaney Drag Strip, on land they owned off U.S. 1 near Elgin. In 1967 they built a new strip on the same land. The last drags were on Saturday, November 16, 1985. The streets of the mobile-home park were "Liberman Lane, Platt Drive, Ivo Circle, and Garlits Drive."

## A road builder's hobby

Ed L. Smith Jr. was born in Fayetteville, Tennessee. He finished Dentsville High School in 1953 and went into road construction and real estate. His obituary says he "turned his hobby, drag racing, into a business and built Blaney Drag Strip in 1962, bringing NHRA championship racing to South Carolina."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^obit18] Sutton was pretty sure it started before 1960. Whether that was 1962 or earlier is still open. He and his wife Sandra owned 325 acres off U.S. 1 near Elgin, and for the strip's first 13 years they ran it themselves.[^mcl85] Elgin was Blaney then. Tom Sutton, who raced there from 1961, wrote that the town changed its name "when the watch company moved there."[^dslsc]

There were two Blaney strips on the same land. The first ran down the hill, perpendicular to the later one. In 1967, McLaurin wrote, rather than lengthen its shutdown area to keep the NHRA sanction, the Smiths built a new strip elsewhere on the property, "with a three-quarter mile stopping stretch."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^mcl85][^dslsc] Sutton remembered that the track moved up the hill because of noise. The announcers made the most of it. David R. Burlington, who first came as a teenager in the late summer of 1969, remembered the chatter about a long shutdown, then "tons of soft sand" in the sand trap, and after that "a hundred acres of 'soft pine trees'" for any car still going.[^gdr]

## Radio spots and a Gremlin at 200

Blaney was the only quarter-mile NHRA strip in South Carolina when Burlington started going. There was a points race each year, and the year-end Dixie Finals.[^gdr] The radio spots crackled with "Saturday-night-at-the-Blaney-Drag-Strip" and a roll call of Sox and Martin, Dyno Don Nicholson, Garlits and Ivo. They always ended "Beeee There!"[^mcl85] "I guess about everybody who was anybody in drag racing has raced there at one time or another," Sandra Smith said in 1985. She named Shirley Muldowney, "back before anybody even knew who she was."[^mcl85]

Burlington's memories are small and exact. Pete Robinson pitted his 427 cammer Ford rail under a big tree one night around 1969, struggling to reassemble a C-6 transmission, and still found a friendly hello for "a somewhat over-awed 15 year old." There was rosin sprinkled on the strip for Ronnie Sox. Front-engined nitro cars were push-started downstrip. The Powell and Burnett top fueler from Charleston was a red metalflake car that stayed with a Chevy "rat motor" into the mid-1970s. When Lou Azar's Gremlin-bodied funny car went through the traps at about 200 mph and its parachutes came out late, officials said afterward they had "to practically pry Azar's hands off the wheel."[^gdr]

The local racers paid their own way. Wilson Davis McDuffie ran a '40 Ford with a '56 Chevy engine in D/Gas and a '52 Ford in N/Stock in 1963, and kept the receipts: $15 and a trophy for a D/G class win, $10 for N/S. "My dad was not a mechanic, but could build anything, built a pretty mean flathead using the old 'chain in the tree.'"[^dslsc] Johnny Dowey first raced there in a '55 Chevy for "25 bucks some weeks, and a little trophy."[^mcl85]

## Leased, then lots

When the strip began cutting too deeply into their other businesses, the Smiths leased it to Dowey, a Columbia racer, who ran it for ten years.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^mcl85] Burlington remembered that Dowey bought the strip and managed it. By 1985 the land was worth more as lots than he could pay for the lease. "There's no hard feelings, or anything," Dowey said. "I was thinking about buying the land an [a] couple of years ago, but I just waited too long."[^mcl85] The last drags at Blaney were on Saturday, November 16, 1985: a $33,500 purse, a three-engine jet truck, Pro Stockers and jet funny cars, Ronnie Sox and Bunnie Burkett on the bill, general admission $12, pit pass $3, and "$2.00 Discount Tickets at Po-Boy Speed Shop."[^ad85]

The old strip became the main street of the Tall Pines subdivision. The new one became part of Blaney Hills.[^mcl85] When Burlington went back years later, the gate to the pits was the mobile-home park's main gate, the bleacher berms were still there, and the streets had names: "Liberman Lane, Platt Drive, Ivo Circle, and Garlits Drive."[^gdr] Ed Smith died in 2018, survived by Sandra, his wife of 54 years.[^obit18]

## Around the Midlands

Blaney was not the only strip within a tank of gas. At Palmetto Raceway in Pelion, Henry Doriot came from Tennessee for a big NHRA meet in the early '60s. The announcer asked the crowd to stand for the national anthem, "then played 'Dixie.' Nothing else," and NHRA tech made a red '55 Chevy gasser from Atlanta "put his generator back on before he could race." McDuffie won a trophy at Palmetto in 1961 in his "fearsome K Stock 1952 Ford" and remembered, "We used to sneak guys in Pelion in the trunk." Dewey Brock went in 1971 or 1972: "Big Daddy Don was there, TV Tommy Ivo, the Blue Max, and I think Sox and Martin was there."[^dslsc]

Camden's strip, out in the Shepard area, was owned by Junior Rodgers, wrote Robert Anderson, whose father raced there. Lonnie Campbell, a Great Falls high school student in 1966, described "basically a dirt dragstrip in the sand" with a "cement 'launch pad,'" and still had the trophy. Kent Lamb raced there several times as a teen in the 1970s and remembered the "Yellowbug," a '67 Austin Healey Sprite that "was a multiple winner on a regular basis in the early '70s."[^dslsc]

At Bennettsville in 1957, Allen Edwards, then 12, helped his father sell spectator tickets at a strip owned by Senator Paul Wallace, on the site of an old drive-in theater where the Evans Correctional Institution stands now. "It was a short-lived venture," he wrote. Some said it was too short for the faster dragsters to stop. He was not sure whether it was called the Bennettsville Drag Strip or Wallace Drag Strip.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^dslsc]

And at Walterboro in 1968, Jack Hutchens, a flight-simulator tech rep sent to the Marine air station at Beaufort, watched his new wife Donna, "just a farm girl from Indiana," enter a powder puff race in their automatic '67 Barracuda. She won her first round and then drew a 440 GTX. His advice was to go when the flagman "even looks like he's going to raise his flag," and she beat it without a red light. The trophy sat in his attic for fifty years. "I got skunked and she won a trophy."[^dslsc]

It had been a road builder's hobby, then a strip that moved on the same land in 1967, then a park. The streets were Liberman Lane, Platt Drive, Ivo Circle, and Garlits Drive.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did Blaney open?** Smith's obituary says he built it in 1962, and McLaurin's "after 23 years" in November 1985 points the same way.[^obit18][^mcl85] But Tom Sutton wrote that he first ran there in 1961 with a '60 Tempest and was "pretty sure it started before 1960."[^dslsc] An earlier informal strip on the Smiths' land would explain both; the *Camden Chronicle* and *The State* for 1960–62 should tell. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Why did Blaney move up the hill?** McLaurin's 1985 story says the Smiths built the new strip in 1967 to get a longer stopping area rather than lose the NHRA sanction.[^mcl85] Sutton remembered that "the track moved up the hill because of noise."[^dslsc] Both may be true. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did Johnny Dowey lease Blaney or buy it?** In 1985 the *Columbia Record* and Dowey himself said he leased it, on a ten-year lease, and had thought about buying the land; Burlington remembered that "Johnny Dowey of the Columbia area bought the strip and managed it."[^mcl85][^gdr] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **What was the Bennettsville strip called?** Only Allen Edwards's memory, and he was not sure whether it was called the Bennettsville Drag Strip or Wallace Drag Strip.[^dslsc] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
