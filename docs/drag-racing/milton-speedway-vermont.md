# A Safer Place Than Route 7: Milton Speedway, Vermont's Only Drag Strip (1963–71)

*Vermont's only legal strip, opened beside Route 7 on June 23, 1963, as a safer place than the public roads*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (a 2019 state historic marker and the dedication-day story give the opening date, the owners and the closing; the rest rests on racers' and spectators' memories posted 50 years later, and on two BangShift features that guessed at dates before the marker went up) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> Vermont Division for Historic Preservation marker text (2019), checked against the marker photograph in the Historical Marker Database; WCAX-TV's report of the Milton Historical Society's dedication (Aug. 17, 2019); the Vermont Historical Society's "This Place in History" note; firsthand comments on BangShift.com (2013–14); BangShift features (2013, 2014); a *Burlington Free Press* history column (2017), behind a paywall and read only as a search excerpt · **Last verified:** 2026-11-01 (Round 29) · **Open issues:** opening year ⚠; closing year ?; why it closed ⚠; length and site ?; what Shirley Muldowney raced ◐ · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "I can still hear the thunder on this track. And every time I go by, I just — I don't know. We lost magic here."

    — a speaker at the dedication of the Milton Speedway marker, Aug. 17, 2019, as WCAX reported it.[^wcax]

On Sunday, June 23, 1963, Vermont got its first drag strip, beside U.S. Route 7 in Milton, north of Burlington. The state's historic marker, put up in 2019 at Route 7 and Racine Road, credits three men: Maurice Bousquet and Hubert "Bud" McCormick of Milton's B&M Motors, "with top Vermont driver Jack DuBrul of Burlington," who opened it "before a capacity crowd." Locals called it Milton Drag Strip. It was NASCAR-sanctioned, and the marker says it gave the town "a new source of entertainment and, to the delight of police, a safer place to race than Milton's public roads."[^marker]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> It was the only legal one in the state. The opening year, the closing, and the reason it closed are told differently.

It was, the Vermont Historical Society says, "the only legal dragstrip in Vermont," and people "from all walks of life, and from all over the country" came to race "everything from the family car to modified dragsters."[^vhs] McCormick told the crowd at the 2019 dedication that it had cost him $150,000 to get the track up and running.[^wcax] His son Mike was 12 when it opened.[^wcax]

## Sunday regulars and visiting stars

The marker describes a weekly program of local and regional drivers, with match races and special shows, among them "Walt Arfons' 'Bonneville Avenger' jet dragster in June 1966," and it names Jack and Shirley "Cha Cha" Muldowney and "Big Daddy" Don Garlits among the national stars who raced there.[^marker] The track, WCAX reported, "used to host 20 races a year."[^wcax]

The spectators remember it in detail. "I remember hitchhiking up to the Milton strip when I was 14 or 15 in the mid sixties," Tim Clark wrote in 2013. "I recall seeing Shirley in a Corvette one season and she was back the following season in an injected rail." He also remembered a '55 or '56 Chevy gasser sponsored by Santa Chevrolet across the lake in Plattsburgh, New York, and the "Mr. Blister" '66 SS Chevelle.[^bs13c]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> A man who spent the summer of 1966 in Vermont before joining the Navy went "a half dozen times," and recalled that "the dealerships brought out the 396 chevells and 390 fairlanes every time I was there," and one A/FX Falcon that ended up in the field beside the track and came back to race again.[^bs13c] A racer named Johnny wrote simply: "Ran my 58 Chevy 348 g stock auto 1964 Best time 16.2."[^bs13c]

The bigger names came on tour. BangShift's Brian Lohnes found a scanned page of *Eastern Drag News* telling how the Royal Bobcat Pontiac crew visited Milton and took on all local Pontiac comers, and an ad for the strip's weekly purses.[^bs13] A year later a reader's photographs of the strip in action reached him, among them a young Bobby Lagana "racing his wheelstanding, altered wheelbase A/FX Mercury."[^bs14] And the hometown cars had their fans: in February 2013 a man wrote that he had just had breakfast at the Milton Diner, beside the old strip, with Woodard and Keefer, who "raced the A/FX , Vermonster at Milton back in the 60's," and that the three of them had stumbled on "the old dragster in a barn about 10 miles from the old strip" and meant to restore it.[^bs13c]

## The end of the strip

The strip was a success for eight years before it became too expensive, WCAX reported, and McCormick explained why in 2019: "It takes quite a bit of money to finance the cars to get them in top shape. And it takes a lot of money to maintain the track and have the equipment. It's just not a profitable business."[^wcax] The station dated the closing to 1971,[^wcax] and the marker agrees: "Milton Speedway's final competitions were held in 1971."[^marker]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> The Vermont Historical Society gives another reason: the track "eventually shut down as speeds got too fast for the track location."[^vhs]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>

Before the marker, the place was nearly a ghost. When Lohnes went looking in 2013 he could not say who had owned or sanctioned it; "From best we can tell," he wrote, the facility ran half a mile from the starting line to where the asphalt "seems to terminate in the woods," and found only "Drag Strip Bingo" on the old grounds.[^bs13]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> The memories outlived the pavement. Tom Bergeron, from the next town over, wrote that he and his friends did burnouts on the old strip "back in the nineties.(until the Milton PD showed up and kicked us out)," and that the local car club now holds a show "on the end of the old track every Summer."[^bs13c] At the 2019 dedication Mike McCormick said the strip could still be seen in the distance, "although it's overgrown at this point," and added: "Milton was a cool place to go in the 60s and early 70s."[^wcax] It had been the safer place beside Route 7. The accounts still do not tell the closing the same way.

(For New England's older airport strips, at Sanford, Maine, and Charlestown, Rhode Island, see [Barricades at the End of the Runway](sanford-cain-bannister.md) and [Racing Toward Route 1](charlestown-dragway-rhode-island.md).)

*See also:* [Winterport, Maine](winterport-fernald-field-maine.md), another northern New England strip of the same years, opened in 1967 on a town airfield.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did Milton open in 1963, 1964 or 1965?** The state marker gives June 23, 1963.[^marker] Before the marker, Lohnes wrote that 1965 "seems like a year that has come up" and, a year later, that it opened "in the middle 1960s";[^bs13][^bs14] one commenter, "Del," thought "the strip opened in '64,"[^bs13c] and a reader the next year summed up that thread as saying "it opened by 1964."[^bs14] Del also believed "Jack Dudrul" (so spelled), "a local businessman," built it, where the marker names DuBrul as the partner of the two B&M Motors owners.[^bs13c][^marker] The marker's exact date is the best source we have; a 1963 *Burlington Free Press* or *St. Albans Messenger* notice would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Did it close in 1971?** Probably. The marker says "Milton Speedway's final competitions were held in 1971,"[^marker] WCAX gives 1971 and "eight years,"[^wcax] and a commenter from the next town said "1970 or 71," which a later reader repeated as "70 or 71" in summing up the thread.[^bs13c][^bs14] The only dissent is Lohnes's guess, before the marker, of the 1980s or earlier, then "sometime in the early 1980s (as best we can tell)."[^bs13][^bs14] A 1971 *Burlington Free Press* or *St. Albans Messenger* report of the last meet would close the question. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Why did it close: money or speed?** McCormick said the track was "just not a profitable business";[^wcax] the Vermont Historical Society says it shut down "as speeds got too fast for the track location."[^vhs] Both may be true; neither source gives the other's reason. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **How long was it, and was it an old airport?** The marker calls it a "quarter-mile track."[^marker] Lohnes estimated from what he could see, "From best we can tell," that the asphalt ran half a mile from the starting line into the woods, which can include the shutdown area, and wrote that "This was not an old airport or an old military facility."[^bs13] A 2017 *Burlington Free Press* history column, which we could read only as a search-engine excerpt (paywall), says the owners turned "the North/South runway" of the old Schiff Airport into the strip and calls it a "short, eighth mile strip."[^bfp] If the full column says so, the eighth mile would conflict with the marker's quarter mile, at least for some years; until it is read in full, we treat it as a lead, not a dispute. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **What did Shirley Muldowney race at Milton?** The marker says only that she and Jack raced there.[^marker] Lohnes had read that she ran a super stock car "a couple of times" but was not sure she ran her gas dragster there;[^bs13] one spectator remembered a Corvette one season and "an injected rail" the next.[^bs13c] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
