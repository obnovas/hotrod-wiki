# Oranges for the Record: The Jaycees' Drag Strip at Brooksville (Fla., 1956–62)

*Two county-seat weeklies follow a Junior Chamber of Commerce project from an abandoned runway to the world record books, and back to a car club that wanted the strip for itself*

!!! abstract "Article record"

In December 1958 a Tampa driver ran a quarter mile of old Army runway at Brooksville, Fla., at 180 miles an hour. Before the run the president of the town's Junior Chamber of Commerce handed him a box of Florida oranges.[^bj581218] The oranges came from the Brooksville Citrus Growers Association. The strip came from the Jaycees, who had found it lying unused south of town two years earlier.

## The old air base (1956)

In August 1956 the Brooksville Jaycees and a St. Petersburg group called Pastime Dragsters, Inc., agreed to hold races on alternate Sundays at the "Brooksville Drag Strip at the old air base south of here." The St. Petersburg men brought their "know-how," and the Jaycees promised "a safe and sane program without the dangers that accompany unauthorized and unsponsored racing."[^bs560803]

The same front page announced a club. Under Jaycee sponsorship a local "hotrod club" had formed, and it chose "the descriptive name 'The Spooks.'" Clyde Batten was president, Eddie Jouvenas vice president, Glen Glass secretary and Ralph Bell treasurer. By giving the county's young racers a place to compete safely, the Jaycees hoped "to lessen the danger of unsafe 'dragracing' on public roads." The Spooks would have "full run" of the strip and met in the Jaycee Room, in the basement of the Veterans Hall.[^bs560803]

The *Sun* didn't pretend the runway was new to racing. "Unauthorized races have been held on the strip before," it said. The Jaycees wanted to "build a name for the 'Brooksville Drag Strip' that will attract drivers time after time." Admission was 50 cents. Fans were asked to use the far entrance, nearest Masaryktown, because the main airport gate was guarded for those with business at the state prison camp or the Russel Flying Service hangar.[^bs560810]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

"Nearly a thousand" fans lined the main west strip for the first meet on Aug. 12.[^bs560817] By the second, on Aug. 26, the crowd was 1,400 and there were 109 entries, from Brooksville, Tampa, Clearwater, St. Petersburg, Dunedin, Zephyrhills, Orlando and a long list of other towns. The *Journal* named the top eliminator as "Dan Garlits, of Tampa, driving a Chrysler dragster," with 11.8 seconds for the quarter mile.[^bj560830] By early September the Jaycees were advertising "FLORIDA'S FINEST FASTEST CONCRETE DRAG STRIP."[^bs560907]

At the fourth meet, in late September, the Spooks got their first trophy. "Clifford Batten, president of the Spooks Drag Race Club, took the Class SS (strictly stock) with his 1950 Ford, the first time a local driver has placed first."[^bs560928]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> The money went to Jaycee civic projects; that fall a $1,000 check went toward a new scoreboard at Emerson Field.[^bs560928]

## A world record by telephone (1957)

On Sunday, Nov. 10, 1957, Garlits, "veteran drag strip driver, who has won scores of trophies at the local races during the past year or more," covered the quarter mile in 8.79 seconds. "His lapsed time was clocked at 176 miles per hour." The dragster had a Chrysler engine and burned a mix of 97 percent nitro, and the clocks were run by Johnny Hicks of Tampa, the official timer for the area's races.[^bj571128]

The Jaycees thought they had a world record, and the confirmation came by phone. The editor of the *Drag Race News* of Long Beach, Calif., called John M. Weeks Jr., the Jaycee who was business manager of the races, to say the Tampa driver "now holds the new world's record for the quarter mile driven on the Brooksville drag strip." The story began: "Brooksville broke into the world news this week."[^bj571128]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> In January the *Sun* ran a photo of Garlits accepting a trophy from Jaycee president Bob Spencer, which it called the "finest trophy of his collection."[^bs580117]

## Brooksville colors (1958)

By the fall of 1958 Garlits was a Brooksville Jaycee himself. In October the club voted to have its races sanctioned by the NHRA, after hearing from the association's regional director, Ernie Schorb of Miami. Under sanction, the *Sun* said, drivers and spectators would be "protected in case of an accident," and Brooksville's races would be reported nationally.[^bs581017] The *Journal* put it more simply: the races would put Brooksville "on the map." That same week Garlits took his dragster to New York to "run under the Brooksville colors."[^bj581016]

On Dec. 14, 1958, a crowd of "over 3000" came out to see him race Bob Langley of Texas. Langley's car broke after its first run, so the match never happened. Garlits ran alone and crossed the line "at 180.00 m.p.h. to break his own previous world's record of 176.40 m.p.h." Before their runs, Jaycee president Tom Varn gave both drivers boxes of Florida oranges, donated by Travis L. Slayden of the Brooksville Citrus Growers Association, and a photo of Garlits went out on the Associated Press wire.[^bj581218]

A month later the *Journal* reprinted the *Drag News* story of the run. "People immediately began to ask who was Don Garlits and where was Brooksville?"[^bj590115]

## Visitors from the north (1959)

In January 1959 the Jaycees booked Serop Postoian of Detroit, the Midwest's big winner (see [Setto Postoian](../people/setto-postoian.md) and [Detroit Dragway & Cordova](detroit-cordova.md)). The *Journal*'s preview, which spelled him "Postorian," said the two men "have been at several previous meets but have not had the opportunity to run against each other before."[^bj590122]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> Rain spoiled it. On a wet track Garlits topped out at 166.666 mph in 9.42 seconds, and Postoian ran 163.636 in 9.24.[^bj590129]

In February Art Arfons of Akron, Ohio, brought his gasoline-burning dragster and ran 180.906 mph before a crowd of "some 600 or 700."[^bj590226] The March issue of *Hot Rod*, the *Journal* reported, gave a full page to pictures of Brooksville's strip, its pits and its concrete.[^bj590226b] And on a Sunday in late April Garlits came back and set "a new world's record of 185.566 miles per hour for the quarter mile" there.[^bsj590430]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-6"><a href="#q-6" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

Not every Florida county wanted what Brooksville had. That fall, Union County turned down a Gainesville timing club that asked for its old Navy airfield at Lake Butler; see ["Only Their Own Necks"](lake-butler-mid-state-timing-association.md).

## "We did not get the drag strip" (1961–62)

In 1961 the Jaycees advertised an AHRA-sanctioned "Southeastern Championship Drag Race" for May 12–14, with a $1,875 purse and "Don and Ed Garlets" (as printed), Art Malone and Ed Pantley of Tampa. The strip was "where Don Garlets began his drag racing career and excelled in reaching the top speed of 186.7 m.p.h."[^bsj610420]

The last trace is a club column. In March 1962 the Road Angels, writing in the *Sun-Journal*, talked about "when we were going to open our Drag Strip in which we will have races on Sundays," and invited the sheriff's and police departments to a film on how a car club starts.[^bsj620322] Six weeks later the columnist, Arthur Borde, had the answer: "we did not get the drag strip from the group of officials assigned such duties in Hernando County. We would like to openly state that there are no hard feelings." They had been "led to believe we would receive said strip," he added, and "it was quite a let down to the club and its members."[^bsj620503]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

For the Georgia strips where Garlits ran a few years later, see [Yellow River](yellow-river-covington.md). For another Jaycee strip on an old air base, see [SEMO at Malden, Mo.](semo-malden-drag-strip.md)<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-7"><a href="#q-7" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Clyde or Clifford Batten?** The *Sun*'s first club story, on Aug. 3, 1956, names the president "Clyde Batten." Every later item we read says Clifford: the *Sun* on Aug. 10 ("with Clifford Batten as president") and Sept. 28, and the *Journal* on Sept. 6 and Sept. 27.[^bs560803][^bs560810][^bj560906][^bj560927][^bs560928] The club's name changes too: "Spooks Sport Car Club," "Spooks Racing Car Club," "Spooks Drag Race Club." Four against one points to a slip in the first story, but a second Batten is not ruled out. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Which 176 mph record, and when?** The Nov. 28, 1957, *Journal* gives "176 miles per hour" for the Nov. 10, 1957, run.[^bj571128] The *Drag News* story the *Journal* reprinted in January 1959 also dates the run to Nov. 10 (its OCR reads "1967") and gives 176.40.[^bj590115] In October 1958 the *Journal* gave his record as 176.06 mph; in December it said the 180 mph run broke "his own previous world's record of 176.40," and the reprinted *Drag News* story also gives 176.40.[^bj581016][^bj581218][^bj590115] A June 1958 Illinois story said Garlits had been clocked at 176 "that spring" (see [Setto Postoian](../people/setto-postoian.md)). The local papers we read date the first 176 to November 1957; whether there was a second run at 176-plus in spring 1958 is not settled. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Where exactly was the strip?** The papers put it on the airport "four miles south of the city on U.S. 41" (Aug. 1956), "six miles south" (Aug. 1956) and, for a crash at the turnoff, "eight miles south" (Jan. 1958), and fans in 1956 were sent to the entrance nearest Masaryktown.[^bs560810][^bj560830][^bj580116] The story map pins it at Brooksville. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Had Garlits and Postoian raced each other before 1959?** The Jan. 22, 1959, *Journal* preview says they had "not had the opportunity to run against each other before."[^bj590122] The wiki's canonical account, from *Car Craft* and an Illinois paper, has Postoian beating Garlits in the 1957 and 1958 finals of the Midwest series (see [Detroit Dragway & Cordova](detroit-cordova.md#1957-the-garlits-upset)). The preview may simply be wrong, or the two may not have met head to head. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **How did the Jaycee strip end?** The last Jaycee meet we found is the May 1961 championship. The Road Angels' May 1962 column says the county did not give them the strip, but not why, or who did get it.[^bsj610420][^bsj620503] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-6" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **185.566 mph?** The April 30, 1959, *Sun-Journal* is our only source for the 185.566 record at Brooksville, and the 1961 ad's "186.7" is not tied to a date or place.[^bsj590430][^bsj610420] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-6" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
