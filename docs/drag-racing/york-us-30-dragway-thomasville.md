# Four Wide on the Runway: York U.S. 30 Drag-O-Way (Thomasville, Pa., 1955–1979)

*A potato farmer's paved runway, a cigar-chewing tech man, an outlaw Super Stock meet, and planes that still had the right of way*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (the airport's own history and the York papers' memory pieces agree on the run from the 1950s to 1979; who operated the strip in which years, and how long the runway was, differ between sources) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> the York Airport's own history; Jim McClure's *York Town Square* posts for the *York Daily Record* (2008, 2011), quoting the paper's 2006 story and sports editor Jim Hubley's 2008 column, and readers' memories; *Speed Sport* (2023), quoting Lancaster and York racers; Drag Racing List (2019, and a racer's 2001 notes); *Hot Rod* (2015) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-22 (Round 47) · **Open issues:** ⚠ who ran the strip, and when; ⚠ how long the runway was; ◐ the 1965 Nationals as the biggest one-day race; ◐ every national event in 1966 · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "I used to go up to U.S. 30 every Saturday when I was younger. I used to race. I had a '69 Chevelle. I had a lot of good memories, especially when Supernationals were up there. It kept me out of trouble."

## A runway the farmer paved himself

York's drag strip was on a working airport. On race weekends the field closed to planes and the dragsters took over,[^yts1] and sometimes the racing still had to stop while a Cessna or a Piper came in on final, with the drag cars lined up four wide waiting for the pattern to clear.[^ss] It ran that way for about twenty years and became one of the best-known strips in the East, and it ended for an airport's reason, not a racer's.

The airport began as one man's fight. In the early 1950s a Joint York Airport Authority planned a big municipal field at Admire, a few miles north of Oscar Hostetter's grass strip. Hostetter was not pleased. He paved his runway "at no expense to the taxpayers" and sued the Authority. The big airport faded, the Authority disbanded in 1960, and his 4,200-foot paved runway became the York Airport.[^yav] The strip ran perpendicular to the Lincoln Highway, U.S. 30, about six miles west of York.[^ss][^hub]

The South Penn Hot Rod Council ran drags there on a limited schedule from 1955.[^dl] When the Garden Spot strip outside Lancaster was shut down by a court over a neighbor's chinchillas, its promoter, Bill Holz, came to York. In 1960 he took over the York site as the York U.S. 30 Drag-O-Way, by one history; a Lancaster club man remembered the move as 1959.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^dl][^ss] The Lancaster story is told in [The Chinchillas Next Door](lancaster-drag-o-way-kohr-v-weber-chinchillas.md).

## The man with the cigar

Dick McLaughlin, who had founded the Vagabonds Car Club in Lancaster, got a job at York. Hostetter's assistant, Chuck Lorah, "directed me to a stubby little guy with a cigar and said, 'Go work with him.'" That was a young Bill Jenkins, already called "Grumpy," who helped classify and stage the cars coming in. Jenkins was dyno-tuning drag cars all over eastern Pennsylvania, among them the Chevrolets that York dealer Ammon R. Smith fielded with Dave Strickler driving. The track's first tough guy, by McLaughlin's memory, was Jack Kulp, in a Chevrolet panel truck with a supercharged Oldsmobile engine.[^ss] The racers came from everywhere between Harrisburg and Baltimore, and from clubs like the Gents of Hanover, the Motor Menders of York and the Sportsters of York.[^ss]

Don Garlits liked the place. "The average track is 60 feet wide and 4,000 feet long," he told York fans in 2006. "In York, the tracks were 100 feet wide and 5,000 feet long. And you could race four at a time."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^yts1] He remembered a Saturday race early in the 1960s when his team had wrecked its car the week before, drove home to Florida, built a new one from scratch, and got back to York just in time. "As our truck pulled onto the track, we could here the fans go nuts," he said. They lost the first round to Dick Belfatti of Philadelphia and won the next two, for $750.[^yts1] By the memory of Darwin Doll, a York man who was later the NHRA's first Northeast Division Director, the strip had only one death in its whole run, and it was a mini-bike that collided with a plane.[^yts1][^dl]

## The Woodstock of Drag Racing

On August 7, 1965, York ran the first Super Stock Nationals, put on by the magazine *Super Stock & Drag Illustrated*. It was an independent "outlaw" meet, the first big one just for the super stocks, factory experimentals and altered-wheelbase cars that the NHRA was trying to ignore, and it became known as "The Woodstock of Drag Racing."[^nap] The crowd was so big and unruly that the promoters gave up trying to charge admission, and people stood on the apron almost the whole length of the track. The final rounds ran after midnight, and Bobby Harrop won in his injected, nitro-burning *Flying Carpet* Dodge.[^hr15] One history calls it still the largest one-day drag race ever held in the country.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^dl] The Nationals came back to York in 1969 through 1972,[^nap] and in 1969 Bruce Larson of Dauphin won it in his Logghe-chassis *U.S.A.-1*, a Camaro that still had working doors. "That win has kind of lived with my career for years thereafter," he said.[^ss]

The strip grew a racing business around it. Jere Stahl came to York from the Midwest, and Stahl-Moroso and Stahl and Associates set up there; Stiles Performance of York became a well-known shop.[^dl] Bill Stiles and Jere Stahl ran a 1966 Plymouth Belvedere in NHRA competition that won every national event that year, by a photo caption on the *York Daily Record*'s history blog.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^yts2] Hostetter sold the facility to the York U.S. 30 Drag-O-Way company in the late 1960s, the airport says; a racing history says he ran it himself from 1965 until the end.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1b"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^yav][^dl]

## Saturday night, then October 1979

By 1972 the dragway was struggling, the *York Daily Record*'s blogger later wrote.[^yts2] That May the promoters sent the paper a release for the Funny Car Nationals, promising "One dozen bare-breasted beauties" working the gates and the press lounge, a purse of more than $75,000 and a 64-car field, and adding, "The traction at York U.S. 30 has never been better." The paid ad was tamer: "Girls with California-style exposures join the working staff for this event only." Saturday was rained out. On Sunday the track's publicists announced a crowd of 11,477, plus 11,000 more outside, and the program ran without the promised women.[^hub]

The memories York people kept are plainer. Knaub raced his '69 Chevelle on Saturdays, and afterward "we used to, after races . . . come in through York with the white numbers on our cars and go through the circuit."[^ydr11] Crowds of 10,000 or more were common.[^yts1][^hub] The property was sold to York Building Products in 1975, and in October 1979 the drag races ended and the field went back to being a full-time airport.[^yav] "We've really missed York, ever since it closed in 1979," Larson said.[^ss] York fans have held Musclecar Madness at the York Expo Center in its memory since the early 2000s, and in 2019 a tribute weekend went back to the airport itself.[^yts1][^dl] It had always been the airport's ground, and in the end the planes got it back.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Who ran the strip, and when?** Bill Pratt's 2019 history: the South Penn Hot Rod Council ran limited races "from 1955 until 1959," Holz took over "In 1960," and the strip operated "from 1965 until 1979 with the business prowess of Oscar Hostetter."[^dl] McLaughlin, in *Speed Sport*: after Lancaster, Holz went "to speak with its operator, Oscar Hostetter. It was 1959."[^ss] The airport's own history: "Mr. Hostetter sold the facility to York US 30 Drag O Way in the late 1960s."[^yav] The York papers say only that the strip ran from the late 1950s to 1979.[^yts1] Deeds and the York papers of 1959–60 and the late 1960s would settle who held the strip and the land. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How long was the runway?** Garlits, in 2006, said York's strips were "100 feet wide and 5,000 feet long," and the *York Town Square* blog says "The airport afforded a 5,000-foot strip."[^yts1] The airport's history calls it a "4,200-foot paved runway" that has since been "extended 1,000 feet."[^yav] The racers may have counted the paved runway plus overrun, or the extension may date from the drag years; no source says. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **The biggest one-day drag race?** Only Pratt's history says the 1965 meet "was and still is the largest one day drag race to be presented in this country!"[^dl] *Hot Rod* (2015) says the crowd was so big the promoters stopped charging admission, which is why nobody has a gate count.[^hr15] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Every national event in 1966?** Only the blog's photo caption says it: Stiles and Stahl "ran their 1966 Plymouth Belvedere in National Hot Rod Association competition and won every national event in ’66."[^yts2] NHRA's 1966 national event results would check it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
