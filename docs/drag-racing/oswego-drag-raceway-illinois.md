# "Sunday! Sunday! Sunday!": Oswego Drag Raceway, the Farm Strip the Map Called a Landing Strip (Oswego, Ill., 1954 or 1955–1979)

*Two farm brothers, a car club from Aurora, a village with one policeman, a Boy Scout troop under the bleachers, and a federal map that got it backwards*

## Poor ground on Route 34

Most of the early strips in the country were old runways. Oswego was a farm field that two brothers graded on purpose. It ran for 25 years at the edge of a village of about 1,200 people, and for part of that time a federal map called it a landing strip.[^hotf][^ldb]

Dan and Wally Smith were sons of William Smith, who farmed the north side of U.S. Route 34, about a mile and a half west of the Oswego bridge over the Fox River. The strip of land there did not farm as well as they liked, and they had heard about sanctioned drag racing as a safer place for street racers to go.[^hotf] Roger Matile, the village's longtime newspaper editor and director of the Little White School Museum, said the brothers enjoyed racing and figured they might as well build a dirt strip, since the soil was not the best for farming.[^cm14] Tommy Lee Byrd's *Lost Drag Strips* adds that two friends, Dale and Darold Cutsinger, encouraged them.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>[^ldb]

The Smiths had seen a crowd before. In October 1949 India's prime minister, Jawaharlal Nehru, made the W.A. Smith farm a stop on an agricultural tour of the United States, and he signed autographs for Oswego High School students there.[^hotf]

As near as Matile could work out from the local papers, the brothers graded a quarter-mile dirt strip running north and south, to keep the rising and setting sun out of the racers' eyes, in 1954.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> Crowds showed up. The brothers decided they did not have the people to run it, so they called the Aurora Autocrats Car Club, which agreed to operate it.[^hotf] The *Beacon-News* reported in 1956 that the Autocrats had sponsored racing for five weeks the year before on a "crude" oil strip, and that in that short 1955 season the Oswego Drag Strip "attracted up to 122 entries in one day."[^hotf] Other car clubs, the Traction Masters and the Bearing Busters among them, pitched in on upkeep.[^ldb] In April 1956 the *Beacon* said the club meant to blacktop the runway and make an investment of approximately $40,000.[^hotf] It was paved in time for the 1956 season, and that September Oswego held what Shaw Local calls the Chicago area's first championship drag race event.[^sl22]

## A village with one policeman

The planned layout, as the *Beacon* described it, had bleachers for 1,000 fans and a parking lot for 5,000 cars, with NHRA safety rules and every car "weighed, checked, and approved."[^hotf] The strip itself was the highest ground on the property, so the crowd looked across at the cars, not down on them.[^ldb]

Then the people came. The *Oswego Ledger* of April 18, 1957 counted a record 4,352 people the Sunday before, with 364 cars making 1,276 runs.[^hotf] Matile said the strip drew more than 3,000 spectators each Sunday, three times the village's population.[^cm14] Oswego then had 1,220 people, no traffic signals, a couple of stop signs, and one police officer, Paul Dwyre, helped by a night watchman.[^hotf]

Time trials started at 8 a.m., and the noise carried up the valley. On May 9, 1957 the *Ledger* reported that state police had come in after complaints to the county courthouse and to Springfield, and had written more than 100 tickets, most of them for noisy and improper mufflers. The village noticed the fines were going to Springfield. By the next week the troopers made sure Dwyre was on hand for the arrests, and a justice of the peace court was set up at the village hall.[^hotf] After 30 arrests on May 12, Justice of the Peace Art Tramblie told the *Ledger* that most of the young people in his court "were honorable, respectable young citizens who merely needed a small fine as a reminder."[^hotf]

The town's boys found their own way in. Matile remembered walking along Route 34 from a friend's house to watch for free. His Boy Scout troop cleaned up the trash on Mondays after the races. The plum job was under the bleachers, where customers dropped money, and the policy was "strictly finders, keepers."[^hotf]

## Run what you brung

The starter for years was Jim "Woody" Woodrow, who leaped into the air with a flag in each hand.[^hotf] Byrd says folks remember his white pants, white shoes and blue jacket.[^ldb] His red and green flags are now in the Little White School Museum.[^hotf] Electronic starting lights replaced him as the sport turned more professional.[^hotf]

Matile called the mid-1960s the golden years, when locals still filled the bill. Bob Mead of Oswego, Don Hausler and his crew from Plano, and Al Thompson's team from Al's Speed Shop in Aurora built and raced their own dragsters.[^hotf] Al's, off North Lake Street, sold the parts so many of the racers needed, and built dragsters for other drivers.[^hotf] The Cottingim Brothers Shell station in Plano sponsored a 1932 Ford three-window coupe that Gar Davies, Carl Tripp and Hausler built in 1958, with Hausler driving.[^hotf] Mead and Jerry Swanson raced a dragster with a 392 Chrysler Hemi there from 1968 to 1971.[^hotf]

When Matile went off to Northern Illinois University in the fall of 1964, the Chicago students on his floor knew Oswego right away. They yelled "SUNDAY!" at him, after Jan Gabriel's radio ads for the strip on WLS.[^hotf] The big names came: Don Garlits, Shirley Muldowney, Don Prudhomme, Tommy Ivo, Connie Kalitta, Arnie Beswick, and an Arfons jet called the Green Monster.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^hotf][^cm14] In high school Matile saw a dragster's chute fail to open. "The driver drove right through the cornfield and ended up almost in Bristol," he said.[^cm14]

The money changed. Former racer Donny Carlton told Matile that by 1967 and 1968 things were shifting: "Before that time, a guy working out of his own basement or garage could show up and be competitive. By 1970, it was very difficult for the weekend guys to compete with the full-time teams."[^hotf] Promoter Ben Christ ran Oswego for several years, and in the 1970s Byrd calls it one of the nation's premier "doorslammer" tracks.[^ldb] "Animal Jim" Feurer's orange 1957 Mercury, with a 427 Tunnel Port and a four-speed, was track champion in 1973 and 1974.[^ldb] At some point the track swapped ends, which put the starting line close to the nearby streets.[^ldb]

The traffic still went right past one farm half a mile east. Matile wrote that veterinarian Howard Koch could hardly get in and out of his own driveway on race days, and that in 1979 Koch bought the land. The operators announced there would be no 1980 season.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^hotf] A tree nursery leased the ground, and the pavement was left to crack.[^hotf][^ldb]

The last word went to a mapmaker. The U.S. Geological Survey's 1973 Yorkville Quadrangle map showed the raceway as a "Landing Strip." In the 1990s, Matile wrote, a USGS cartographer stopped at the *Ledger-Sentinel*, where Matile was editor, and asked if any errors needed fixing. Matile told him the landing strip was the old Oswego Drag Raceway.[^hotf] Harry Warner of Harry's Auto Repair was holding the ninth Drag Raceway Reunion in 2014, and the 19th was held beside the village's amphitheater in September 2025.[^cm14][^sl26] Across the country, racers had taken over old airfields. In Oswego they built a drag strip on a farm, and the government mistook it for an airfield.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **1954 or 1955?** Matile, piecing together what he called an "admittedly confusing chronology" from local newspapers, dates the grading of the dirt strip to 1954, and the museum's 2014 exhibit was titled "The Oswego Drag Raceway, 1954-1979."[^hotf][^cm14] Shaw Local (2022 and 2026) and Byrd give 1955 as the first season.[^sl22][^sl26][^ldb] The *Beacon-News* line Matile quotes, five weeks of racing in "the abbreviated racing season in 1955," fits a strip graded late in 1954 and raced in 1955, but no source we read says so. Matile also calls 1955 the strip's "second year" in a photo caption. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Why and when did it close?** Matile's 2024 account: Koch, tired of the traffic past his farm, bought the land in 1979, and a nursery (he names the Hinsdale Nursery) leased it.[^hotf] Matile in 2014, as *Chronicle Media* reported him: the brothers sold the farm to Koch, "who later closed the track due to liability concerns."[^cm14] Byrd: Koch closed it in 1979 "for unknown reasons"; some say he bought it meaning to close it, others blame the economy and attendance; and he names the King Nursery.[^ldb] A T-shirt company's blog (Psyne Co., 2021) says Koch bought the raceway in late 1976 and that the land was bought by a nursing home; we have not used it in the story.[^psy] A Kendall County deed record or the *Ledger-Sentinel* of 1979–80 would settle the date and the buyer. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Which Arfons?** Matile's 2024 list names "Walt Arfons and his Green Monster jet dragster"; *Chronicle Media*'s 2014 story, quoting him, names "Art Arfons and his famed Arfons' jet-powered 'Green Monster' dragster."[^hotf][^cm14] Both brothers ran cars by that name, so the story does not pick one. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did the Cutsingers talk the Smiths into it?** Only Byrd's book names Dale and Darold Cutsinger as the friends who encouraged the brothers.[^ldb] Matile's account has the Smiths acting on their own idea and then calling in the Autocrats.[^hotf] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

A small count to check: the *Chronicle Media* story calls the September 2014 reunion the ninth, and Shaw Local calls the September 2025 one the 19th. A skipped year would explain it; we have not found one.[^cm14][^sl26]
