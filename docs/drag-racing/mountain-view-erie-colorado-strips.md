# An Uphill Shutdown and a Pistol at City Council: Erie's Mountain View Dragway and Colorado Beyond Bandimere (1959–1976)

*A strip run "with an iron fist," and a shutdown that climbed "just like a kids' carnival slide," with a rancher's Cessna on the way to a 1976 Pueblo strip*

*A strip run "with an iron fist," Anglias rolling back to the line, a burnout that began with your bumper on the fence, a 10-year-old in a too-big helmet, and a rancher's Cessna that stopped a drag race for an hour*

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "That wooden fence had the county road right on the other side and when we came around the staging lane, we had to actually back up to the fence to then do the burn out and pull to the line."

    — Jimm Murray, on Mountain View Dragway, 1971, *Drag Strip List*, undated.[^dslco]

Mountain View Dragway sat about 10 miles north of Denver, just west of I-25, with a view of the Front Range. Its shutdown climbed past the finish line "just like a kids' carnival slide." No source we have read gives a year for when it opened or closed. Around it were Thunder Road at 96th and Tower Road, Century 21, and a Pueblo meet where a local rancher landed his Cessna.

## A carnival slide at Erie

It was sanctioned by both the NHRA and the AHRA for a time, and "A gentleman named Albert Broncucie operated the strip with an iron fist." The starting line was so close to a state road that there was barely room to turn a dragster around. It was also the first strip in the Denver area with covered pit spaces wired for electricity.[^dz11]

Robert Leach went as a boy, "right after it was opened."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> His father was a friend of Al Broncucie. The Broncucie family's dump-truck business worked with his father's road builder, Northwestern Engineering of Commerce City, "which was how the paved track was built," and the Leaches got in free. "The actual pavement is still there with the up-hill shutdown."[^dslco] Colin McDowell remembered Anglias on a starting line that seemed slightly uphill: one would roll back and pop the clutch to jump back to the line, and the third time he did it he blew the clutch. He remembered TV ads for the races, too, with ELO's "Fire on High" behind them. When he wrote, in 2016, the property was up for auction.[^dslco] Jimm Murray and his wife raced there in 1971, each making one time trial in their street cars, with the quicker one going on to eliminations.[^dslco]

The racers were local, and so was the trouble. Stan Kopczenski remembered Judy Lilly out one Sunday in a new Mopar, and her boyfriend, Ron Mowers, "got caught cheating with his Chev and got kicked off for a year." For John Young, after a week of street racing out by Tower Road in Aurora, "going to Erie was really a Sunday treat." His friend's family owned Denver Buick and sponsored "Stevie's Wonder," a black '67 Gran Sport, for the owner's son.[^dslco] Del Wiesner, who raced out of Loveland, drove a 150-mph Olds-powered '23 T AA/Street Roadster: "Car almost killed me at Mountain View Dragway in Erie Colorado, Sept 1966."[^wiesner] Bill Canty raced a pickup there and at Rocky Mountain before Vietnam. In 1971 his mother-in-law bet him $5.00 he would never build a race car, and three weeks later he was racing stock cars in one.[^canty] A *Dragzine* caption credits Alan Bockla as "the first racer in Colorado to break 200 MPH, at the Mountain View Dragway."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^dz11]

## Tower Road and Century 21

East of Denver, Thunder Road Dragway sat at 96th and Tower Road, "where the airport is now," Matthew McClure wrote; he thought it started in 1965. He went at 12 to watch Bockla, who match-raced the "Surfers" top fueler there in '66, along with local cars like "Tak's Toy," an Austin from Tak's Auto in Lakewood.[^dslco] Tim Pimentel saw a racer named Mike break an axle on the launch in 1967 and push the car the rest of the strip, "the slowest ET I have ever witnessed. He won the round."[^dslco] Rob Moffitt's uncle was part owner around 1971 and let him work concessions at about ten. He got a ride in an AMC factory AMX, hanging on to the roll bar "in a helmet that was way too big."[^dslco]

Just south of today's Denver International Airport, Century 21 International Speedway in Aurora had a quarter-mile drag strip, a paved oval, a figure-eight and a 10,000-seat grandstand. It hosted the AHRA Spring Nationals and lasted from 1971 to 1974,<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> undone, the Aurora History Museum says, by "bad luck, financial challenges and geopolitics."[^aur25] Jimm Murray remembered its "cement stairs with individual seats. So advanced in customer amenities for its time."[^dslco]

## Out on the edges

Out west, Grand Junction's Midway Drag Strip used an old runway at the south end of the airport, next to I-70. Admission was free and pit passes were $1.00, and Chuck Lerable saw Tommy Ivo's twin-engine dragster there.[^dslco] Whitey Shoffner raced at Midway from its first days, "with his ever-present cigar dangling from his mouth," in a photo the *Colorado West* ran on April 16, 1972.[^dslco] Tom Brown of Wray called Julesburg, up in the northeast corner, his home track in 1962–64. At Trinidad, Rick Milinazzo's dad raced at the airport in the early to mid-'60s, "even before the Jaycees had events there."[^dslco]

Pueblo built its strip the long way. In 1974, Tom McKenna wrote, the non-profit Pueblo Motor Sports, Inc. held a meet on a private airstrip "with a wind sock" in Colorado City, 25 miles south, to work up enough "racing fever" to raise money for a real strip. The announcer was a state legislator, the minority whip, who was also a Pueblo radio personality, Bob "Leon" Kirsht. McKenna's Honda failed inspection. At one meet they had to stop for an hour "while a local rancher landed his Cessna on the drag/air strip," and "in '76 PMI was opened."[^dslco] The Greater Pueblo Sports Association's hall of fame tells a wilder story about the money. Its biography of Tom Gilmore, owner of Tom's Auto Beauty Shop and a city council member, says he "Won numerous trophies for car, midget and motorcycle racing" and rode a unicycle in the Cheyenne Frontier Days parade, and that he was "Accused of holding a pistol on the rest of the council members to get approval for funding from the City and County to establish the Pueblo Motor Sport Park."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> Gilmore died on October 25, 1982, and was inducted after his death.[^gpsa] The shutdown at Erie climbed like a carnival slide. Down at Pueblo, a rancher's Cessna stopped a meet for an hour, and a hall of fame says a councilman was accused of holding a pistol for the funding.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **When did Mountain View open and close, and was it renamed?** No source we have read gives a year for either. The memories run from July 1966 (Timothy Petras's F/HR win) to 1971 (Murray: "I have no recollection of the strip being open after that"). McDowell believed it became "Mile High Raceway Park in the late '60s," and Steve Schultz filed his memory of "Erie Drag Strip in either 1971 or 1972" under the name Mile High Raceway.[^dslco] The *Longmont Times-Call* and *Boulder Daily Camera* are the next places to look; Boulder's library local history photos of the strip were behind a bot check we did not bypass. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Where did Bockla break 200?** The *Dragzine* caption: at Mountain View.[^dz11] The Colorado Motorsports Hall of Fame: "In 1964, set a speed record at Rocky Mountain Drag way of 200.44 miles per hour," the first over 200 in the state.[^bockla] Whether "Rocky Mountain Dragway" was another name for one of the Denver strips is itself unknown. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How long did Century 21 last?** The Aurora History Museum: 1971 to 1974.[^aur25] A H.A.M.B. poster in 2010: it "went under after about 2 years."[^hamb11] The drag strip may have closed before the oval did. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did a councilman really pull a pistol for the Pueblo strip?** Only the hall-of-fame biography says so, and it says "Accused of," which reads like a teasing local legend. We repeat it as that.[^gpsa] Pueblo council minutes for 1974–75 would show how the city and county money was approved. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
