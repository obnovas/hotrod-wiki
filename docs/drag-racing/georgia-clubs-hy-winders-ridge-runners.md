# Who Builds the Strip? Hot Rod Clubs and an Editor in Three Georgia Towns (1957–64)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "You write an editorial against a drag strip and I'll write you a letter tearing down everything you say."

    — A young Perry man to the editor of the *Houston Home Journal*, as the paper told it, Oct. 6, 1960.[^hhj601006]

"When are you going to build us a drag strip?" a young man asked the editor of the *Houston Home Journal* in Perry, Ga., in the fall of 1960. He meant the grown-ups of Perry: "it would give us a place to race our cars; it would take a lot of this speeding off the highways," he said. "The cops are always following us around trying to catch us racing." The editor told him that "if he is old enough to race cars he ought to be old enough to build his own drag strip." The young man promised a letter "tearing down everything you say." The editor promised to print it if he signed it, and ran his editorial under the headline "Put Us Down for Contribution On a 'Drag Strip' for Our Youngsters."[^hhj601006]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

A week later the paper said the piece had "caused a lot of comment" and restated it: "We just object to a public fund to build a drag strip. We do not believe a drag strip would cut down on the speeding, but would actually be a strong stimulant to speeding."[^hhj601013] Two other Georgia towns show what happened when the young men took the editor's advice.

## Forest Park's Hy-Winders (1957–58)

In August 1957, in the fast-growing town south of Atlanta, the *Forest Park Free Press* announced that the Hy-Winders Auto Club would be formed at the Youth Center. Its purpose was "to keep racing off the highways, to eliminate 'scratch-off' activities and to teach boys organization of clubs and social activities." Thirty-five boys had come to the last meeting. The club was "joining the National Hot Rod Association of Los Angeles"<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> and, until it had its own strip, would "attend drags at Macon and Dallas."[^fpfp570807]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> Assistant Police Chief Jack Grant, the club's advisor and later its "honorary sponsor,"[^fpn570821][^fpfp571204] said there was "a wonderful change in regard to 'scratch-off' driving and use of illegal mufflers since plans for a club have been made." The paper's columnist Jack Troy agreed: "Hotroding on the highways is not only an annoyance but extremely dangerous."[^fpfp570807]

The boys raced. Within weeks Wayne Oliver won his class at the Dallas strip in a '34 Ford with a 1942 flathead, a car he owned with Jack Briscoe and Frank Baker, with a "stroked (4"), bored (30 over 3 and 5/16), ported and relieved engine" built in Tennessee and a cam "patterned after those used in the big cars at Indianapolis." The engine cost them $300. The president was Wayne Farrell, 21, who worked for Retail Credit Corporation.[^fpn570821] The next Sunday Baker set a track record there of 17.3 seconds for the quarter mile, with a broken gear shift lever.[^fpn570828]

That November Grant told a visitor that none of the club's members had been stopped or fined for a traffic violation in its four months. The Hy-Winders were planning to buy "several hundred acres" in Clayton County for a strip, "where is belongs."[^fpfp571204] A member, Herman Brown, went to the county commissioners, who favored the idea "provided the club can get the necessary funds."[^fpn571218] In January 1958 the club took its cars and trophies to the South Expressway Drive-In for two nights, at the concession stand during intermission of "Teen Age Thunder" and "Carnival Rock," with membership applications in hand.[^fpn580122]

By June the plan had a backer. The Atlanta-Metropolitan Automobile Safety Association, "working in conjunction with the Hy-Winders," offered to finance a "$100,000 straightaway track" on 128 wooded acres between the Expressway and Panhandle Road, to be built "immediately upon the grant of a use permit," with a pledge: "NO BEER, LIQUOR or Gambling will be permitted."[^fpfp580625]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> The club, meanwhile, was meeting at a member's house to plan a "Week-end of Free Road Service" for the road safety program.[^fpfp580611]

## Summerville's hot rod club (1964)

Seven years later, in Chattooga County, the same thing started again. In May 1964 Summerville's mayor, J. R. Dowdy, called a public meeting after a call from John Groover, "a young man who is interested in the establishment of such a strip."[^sn640528] A week later Summerville's hot rodders, who shared a name with [a Pennsylvania club](stroudsburg-ridge-runners.md), were formally organized as the Ridge Runners, with Groover as president. Some 50 people had turned out, including the mayors of Summerville and Trion. "Our organization is for the purpose of safety on the highways," the club said. "We also will formally suggest stiffer penalties for violators of our traffic laws."[^sn640604] The paper's editorial called it a step "in the right direction about the drag racing menace in Chattooga County."[^sn640604ed]

The club set a $5 entrance fee and $1 monthly dues, washed cars at Fowler's Standard Service for $1.50 (and raised about $62), and invited Otto Timms, "owner of the Calhoun drag strip," to speak.[^sn640611][^sn640618] By late July it had about $400. "Investors for the drag strip are needed and the cost is estimated at $15,000," the paper reported, and "Many members are going to the Paradise Drag Strip in Calhoun."[^sn640730] In August Groover announced a site on the Jerry Hamby property on Dry Valley Road, about three miles west of town, which the club planned "to lease," and the mayor urged the public to help, saying "The land for the drag strip has already been donated."[^sn640806][^sn640813]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> The club named a "Safe Driver of the Month" from its own members. The prize was "a wax job done by the other members."[^sn640820]

The Summerville paper did not follow the strip to an ending. In 1988 it profiled a former member, John "Monk" Groover, 48, then racing stock cars at Woodstock and Rome. In the 1960s, it said, the "Ridge Runners Club" had been "a group of friends in Chattooga County who, according to other former members, raced on county roads. They decided the illegal midnight road racing was too dangerous for themselves as well as others, so they formed the club and leased a drag track in Calhoun."[^sn880428]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-6"><a href="#q-6" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup><sup class="hr-q" id="qr-7"><a href="#q-7" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Which Dallas strip?** The Forest Park papers say "Dallas" without a state. Dallas, Ga., in Paulding County, is the likely reading, and Baker's remark that the track had been running "for the past four years" would date it to about 1953. We have not found the strip's name.[^fpfp570807] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did the Hy-Winders join NHRA?** The club said it was joining NHRA. We have not found it in an NHRA club list.[^fpfp570807] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Was the Clayton County strip ever permitted?** We have not found whether Clayton County granted the use permit, or anything more about the Atlanta-Metropolitan Automobile Safety Association. In April 1958 the club had voted to hold no more meetings "until further notice" and postponed its free road service weekend, then started meeting again in May.[^fpfp580625] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did the young man's letter run?** The exchange is the editor's own account. The follow-up of Oct. 13, 1960, mentions comment but prints no letter from the young man, and we have not checked later issues.[^hhj601006] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Leased or donated, and was Dry Valley built?** On Aug. 6, 1964, the *Summerville News* said the club planned to lease the Hamby land, and its Aug. 13 editorial said members "are leasing a site in Dry Valley." The mayor's statement in the same Aug. 13 issue says the land "has already been donated." The 1988 profile says the club "leased a drag track in Calhoun," which suggests the Dry Valley strip was never built. We have not found a 1964–65 story that settles it.[^sn640806][^sn640813] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-6" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Is "Monk" Groover the 1964 president?** The 1988 profile does not say that John "Monk" Groover was the club's president, only a member; the 1964 president is "John Groover." The ages fit (48 in 1988), but we have not confirmed they are the same man.[^sn880428] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-6" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
