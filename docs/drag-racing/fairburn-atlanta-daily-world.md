# Saturday Nights at Fairburn: Drag Racing in the Atlanta Daily World (Fairburn and Covington, Ga., 1959–63)

*Atlanta's Black daily paper sends its readers to a skyport strip on Highway 29, and starts keeping score*

!!! abstract "Article record"

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "There is racing year round at Houston Bros. Drag Strip ... See you Saturday night."

    — Drag racing report, *Atlanta Daily World*, Sept. 14, 1963.[^adw630914]

The Labor Day card at Ted Edwards Skyport in 1960 ran all day. A motorcycle drag race sanctioned by the American Motorcycle Association started at noon, and a parachute jump came at 3 p.m., along with "a drag race for white drivers." Then, the *Atlanta Daily World* told its readers, "Colored racers will swing into action at 7:00 p.m." The night race paid double elimination money, and a $50 war bond waited for anyone who broke Charlie Scott's record of 125 miles an hour.[^adw600903]

For the next three years the *Daily World*'s sports pages followed the night races south of Fairburn the way other papers followed baseball.

## "A legal place to speed" (1959–60)

In July 1959 a *Daily World* sports column introduced Ted Edwards, "who operates the Ted Edwards Sky port and Drag Races, on U.S. High way 29." His ads placed it "SOUTH OF FAIRBURN, GA." Edwards said his races drew hundreds each week, with "22 classes of races based on weight, horsepower and other factors," and he described the strip as the "only legal place to speed in Fulton County." His ads promised "22 Classes 22 Trophies" and "A LEGAL PLACE TO SPEED."[^adw590715][^adw590717] By 1960 it was "Every Sat. Night 7 P.M.," a dollar at the gate.[^adw600701]

That summer the paper had a champion to promote. Dewitt Prickett, it reported, had "recently chalk ed up a speed of 110.2 at the Ted Edwards Drag Strip," with an elapsed time of 12.60, and "has been acclaimed southern champ of the sport." Edwards billed a "SOUTHERN NEGRO CHAMPIONSHIP RACE" for July 16, with a savings bond for anyone who broke Prickett's record.[^adw600716] Two weeks later Prickett was matched against Charlie Scott, "Well Known Stock Car Driver," whom the ad called the "Only Negro to Qualify for NASCAR."[^adw600730]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> By mid-August the ad featured Scott alone: "New Track Record Set August 6 Of 125 Miles Per Hour."[^adw600813] A columnist passed along Edwards' report that the sport's popularity was "growing week by week," and his own advice: "Addicts who crave this type of racing should make a bee line for Ted Ed wards' place which is only 17 miles from Atlanta."[^adw600816]

## Fox, Jo-Jo and Sonny Boy (1962)

In April 1962 Marion E. Jackson devoted his column to the sport. "Drag racing strips lure many youth with racing blood to Fair burn and Covington," he wrote, and he quoted the NHRA president Wally Parks on a new rule requiring seat belts in all cars on NHRA strips from June 1.[^adw620421] Edwards' ad now called the strip "Georgia's First, Atlanta's Nearest."[^adw620420]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

The weekly reports read like a box score. Joe "Jo-Jo" Johnson won the super stock money in the season's second Saturday night run in a '62 Ford, at 106 miles an hour, and won top eliminator again the next week at 13.62 and 107. That week also brought the season's first appearance of Willie "Shank" Stinchcomb in his C altered.[^adw620424][^adw620425] In June it was Norman "Sonny Boy" Smith's turn, in his "62" Ford.[^adw620620][^adw620626] Early in July the paper said Smith had set track records "at every track he ran": at the Newton County Drag Strip on a Tuesday night, at 13.25 and about 109, at the Fairburn Fourth of July meet, and at Fairburn again that Saturday, at 13.52 and 108.06. The fans "jumped up and down on their feet" when Jo-Jo Johnson pulled to the line to run him in Pete "Fireball" Lester's 409 Chevy.[^adw620710]

At the end of August Wallace "Fox" Jones came back. Driving a "Nichol son-Nalley Dyno-tuned" '62 409 Chevrolet owned by Billy Scheetz, then his own car, he beat Jo-Jo Johnson with a 12.90 at 114.60 and held the Fairburn record for low elapsed time. Then, the paper said, Fox "took his fast Chevrolet, which he had painted bright yel low, to the National Drag Race, being held in Indianapolis, Indiana."[^adw620901]

## Houston Bros. and Yellow River (1963)

In 1963 the Saturday night ads were for Houston Bros. Drag Strip, Fairburn, at the phone number Edwards' ads had carried the year before.[^adw630419][^adw620407]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> The reports, now signed by Emel Scott Jr., followed the same drivers and a growing super stock purse: $100, $75, $50 and $25 in July, and "$1000.00 given away Labor Day in Fairburn."[^adw630717][^adw630830]

They followed the drivers to Covington too. In April Thomas "Tebe" Holcombe (spelled Holcomb that week) won the first-place money in super stock "at Yellow River drag strip Saturday night," beating Jo-Jo Johnson and Pete Lester in "the 409 Chevy from Scheetz Motor Co."[^adw630430] In July the paper reported $20 given away for super stock at Yellow River and promised Sunday racing there, with time trials at 1 p.m., and in August Yellow River ran its own ad: "2nd & 4th SUNDAYS, P.M. COLORED DRIVERS ONLY 50% Of Gate To Drivers." It added that "the DUKE MOTOR CLUB From Macon" was coming up "To Challenge Some Of The Atlanta Drivers!"[^adw630717][^adw630824]

The circuits overlapped. Fox Jones turned up in "his 427 Foid, the original 'Georgia Shaker No. 2"," in a report that also names Hubert Platt, and the same report sent readers to Yellow River for Sunday racing.[^adw630810] On Labor Day at Fairburn, Joe Johnson won the first $200 "over Wallace 'Fox' Jones driving Houston Platt's Chevy, the Dixie Twister."[^adw630906] In September a report closed with a promise: "There is racing year round at Houston Bros. Drag Strip." Then it signed off: "See you Saturday night."[^adw630914]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

For the Covington strip's own story, see [Yellow River Drag Strip](yellow-river-covington.md); for the Newton County strip that became the [Atlanta Speed Shop Dragway](atlanta-speed-shop-dragway.md), see that page.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup><sup class="hr-q" id="qr-6"><a href="#q-6" title="Next leads: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Was Houston Bros. the old Ted Edwards strip?** Both are "Fairburn, Ga.," and the 1963 Houston Bros. ad gives 463-9891, the number in Edwards' 1962 ads.[^adw630419][^adw620407] The paper never says the strip changed hands. Fulton County property records would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Georgia's first strip?** "Georgia's First" is the strip's own advertising claim, and we have not tested it against other Georgia strips' opening dates.[^adw620420] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **The "Only Negro to Qualify for NASCAR"?** The phrase is the strip's ad copy for Charlie Scott, and we have not checked it against NASCAR's records.[^adw600730] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Who raced on Saturday nights?** The 1960 Labor Day schedule separates a race "for white drivers" in the afternoon from the evening race, and the 1963 Yellow River ad says "COLORED DRIVERS ONLY."[^adw600903][^adw630824] The weekly Fairburn ads do not say whether the regular Saturday night races were for Black drivers only, and the reports do not say. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **When do the reports stop?** The last Fairburn report we have read is from Oct. 16, 1963, when Joe Johnson, pleased with "his 409 Chevy (The Bounty Hunter)," challenged Wallace Jones to a race at Houston Bros.[^adw631016] We searched the paper only through the Georgia Historic Newspapers search service, which may miss pages whose OCR is poor. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
