# "A Practice Murder Place": North Carolina Argues About Drag Strips (1956–60)

*From Chapel Hill to Manteo, small-town weeklies printed both sides of the question that every state was asking, and a state column that answered it with a flat no*

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "That's just like providing a practice murder place that would cut down on murders by letting them get it out of their systems."

    — Superior Court Judge J. Frank Huskins, on legal drag strips, as quoted by the *Asheville Citizen* and reprinted in the *Chapel Hill Weekly*, Nov. 16, 1956.[^chw561116]

It began, in Chapel Hill at least, with a newspaper clipping mailed to Texas. In July 1956 four men were arrested in what the *Durham Morning Herald* called "the first local instance of 'drag racing,'" at 1:30 on a Sunday morning on Highway 54 west of town. A friend sent the clipping to Giles F. Horney Jr., a Chapel Hill man serving at Kelly Air Force Base in San Antonio, and he sent it to the *Chapel Hill Weekly* with a letter. He was "disgusted," he wrote, because he believed this sort of thing could be almost entirely wiped out if the area had a drag strip where drivers could take their cars "under supervision, and drag for a small fee." In all the places where it had been given a chance, he claimed, "this plan has cut the racing on the highway about 75 per cent."[^chw560710]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> "Everyone at some time or another has the urge to 'step on it' and see what these new high powered cars will do," he went on. "This isn't for the highway and they need a place where it can be done."[^chw560710]

## The stock car man and the judge (1956)

Three weeks later the *Weekly*'s Charlie Dobson put the question to a local man who drove fast for a living on weekends. Doug Yates owned the Yates Motor Company on West Franklin Street and had been racing stock cars for four years at Raleigh, Charlotte, Richmond and other tracks. "It would be all right," he said of drag racing, "if it were properly supervised on drag strips specially set aside for this." He agreed with the *Weekly*'s recent letter writer, Dobson wrote, "that there ought to be a drag strip in every town."[^chw560731]

The other side had a louder voice. That November the *Weekly* reprinted an *Asheville Citizen* editorial praising Superior Court Judge J. Frank Huskins, who had sentenced a fifth offender to six months on the roads for speeding at more than 100 miles an hour. "Jail or the roads" was the judge's motto, and he "belittled the idea of setting up legalized drag strips." The only cure, he said, was "for juries to have the intestinal fortitude to convict them (the drivers) and the courts to put them on the roads."[^chw561116]

## The state says no (1956–57)

The official position reached small towns through an unsigned traffic column, full of Highway Patrol notes, that ran nearly word for word in papers as far apart as Manteo and Tabor City. "Official approval of drag racing strips isn't likely to be forthcoming," it said in August 1956, "despite a number of letters to the editor appearing in newspapers recently declaring ominously that 'unless we get drag strips there will be racing on the highways.'" Enforcement officials "have turned thumbs down on all forms of drag racing in North Carolina. Supervised or otherwise." The column quoted Motor Vehicles Commissioner Ed Scheidt: one of the best ways to stop drag racing was "a cooperative public ready to report individuals participating in the races and willing to take the stand to testify against them." Then it asked the reader: "Want to get in line?"[^ct560824][^tct560808]

In April 1957, after two highway races killed three people in Forsyth and Franklin counties, the *Tabor City Tribune*'s columnist Bill Crowell came back to the subject under the headline "Drag Racing, Again." He gave the draggers their due: "what the more thoughtful of them say often sounds reasonable." But the International Association of Chiefs of Police had turned the idea down, so had the National Safety Council, and so had the Motor Vehicles Department. He also passed along a detail worth chasing: "In the Elizabeth City area, site of the state's first supervised drag strip, the Daily Advance has complained editorially of semi-organized racing on US 17."[^tct570417]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>

Private money did not wait for permission. In July 1957 the *Coastland Times* news roundup reported that two Asheville businessmen planned to operate a private drag strip at Black Mountain, with "supervised" drag racing on a small private air strip, a week after a judge there handed down stiff punishment to 13 teenagers accused of drag racing.[^ct570705]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## Sunday at Easy Street (1960)

By the end of the decade the argument had been settled on the ground, if not in Raleigh. In December 1960 the Chapel Hill high school's news column in the *Weekly* reported that "High school boys—and I hear a few girls—made a mass migration to the Easy Street Drag Strip last Sunday afternoon to watch the races." For the kids who stayed home, the student columnist added, it was boredom: the usual Sunday afternoon riding had been cut short "by loss of the boys with cars."[^chw601208] Easy Street, at Newton Grove, is where Malcolm Durham began racing in 1957.[^durham]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did supervised strips cut highway racing "about 75 per cent"?** That figure is Giles Horney's claim in a reader's letter, with no source given.[^chw560710] It matches the kind of claim made in other states' strip debates, but we have not found a study behind it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Was Elizabeth City the site of North Carolina's first supervised drag strip?** Only Bill Crowell's 1957 column says so, and it gives no name or date.[^tct570417] The Elizabeth City *Daily Advance* for 1955–57 should name the strip. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Did the Black Mountain air strip drags ever run?** The July 1957 roundup reports only the plan.[^ct570705] The *Asheville Citizen* and *Black Mountain News* for 1957 are the next places to look. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
