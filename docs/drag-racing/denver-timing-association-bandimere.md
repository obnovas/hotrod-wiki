# A Quarter in the Bucket: The Denver Timing Association, the Lowry Drags and John Bandimere's Proving Ground (1948–1986)

*A gas-station crowd that filled a school, an interstate before the traffic, and a parts dealer who built his own hill*

    **Confidence:** Medium (the club's founding is its own 2003 newsletter history; the Bandimere story is told by the family, the track, the *Denver Post* and NHRA, which disagree on several dates; the racers' memories are single voices) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> Denver Timing Association newsletter history (2003), Colorado Motorsports Hall of Fame biographies, a club member's recollection (*Drag Racing Story of the Day*, 2001), *Drag Strip List* memories, Denver Public Library blog, the track's own history page, *The Denver Post* (2023), *Hot Rod* (2015), NHRA (2025) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-02 (Round 30) · **Open issues:** ⚠ when Bandimere bought the land (1957 vs 1958); ⚠ the first NHRA national event at Bandimere (1977 vs 1978); ⚠ Golightly's "Kansas City in 1954"; ◐ the unopened I-25 drags · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

## Two hundred at Wyman school

"Back in 1948 there was considerable street racing in Denver," the Denver Timing Association's own history begins. A group of enthusiasts who hung out at a gas station at 17th and High Street wanted to race legally, so they put an ad in the *Rocky Mountain News* for a meeting at Wyman Elementary School. "The attendance expectation was 35-40. A tad bit off.... since over 220 people showed up for this meeting!" The hallway was full too. The DTA was incorporated on April 9, 1949.[^dta]

At first the club chased top speed, on Arsenal Road between Smith Road and the Rocky Mountain Arsenal, "with both State and Colorado State Patrol approvals." When drag racing came in, "State sanctioned DTA drags were held on I-25 between Evans and Yale!" on a stretch of the new interstate that was finished but not yet open to traffic.[^dta]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> Later there were drags on a dirt strip in Colorado Springs, at Centennial Race Track at Castle Rock and at Lowry Air Force Base. In the late 1950s the club went quiet, and it stayed dormant until 1994, when a few old members began meeting for coffee on Thursdays; it was incorporated again in September 1998.[^dta]

Bob Olds, a charter member, had raced a $120 Model A roadster "all over town, including timing and speed trials at Lowry Field and old Tower Road." At Bonneville in the early 1950s, Denver's Bill Kenz loaned him wheels and tires, and Tom Beatty let him drive his belly tanker one way at 186 mph.[^olds] (See [Tom Beatty](../people/tom-beatty.md).)

## Lowry, the Safari and the Strippers

By 1953, when Roy Golightly came home from military service, "organized racing was a regular occasion at Lowry Air Force Base."[^golightly] The Strippers, a Denver hot rod club formed in 1954, helped the DTA spearhead the Drag Safari's Denver stop in 1955, according to a member's later recollection.[^strip] *Hot Rod*'s Drew Hardin tells what happened at Lowry when the Safari came: the hometown team of Kenz & Leslie, already famous for their Bonneville streamliner, won A Hot Roadster at 109.63 mph with a Lincoln-powered Deuce, while their stripped rail with a 296-inch Mercury flathead, driven by Joe Kelly, ran the day's fastest pass, 122.28 mph.[^kenz] The same summer, John Bandimere Sr. drove what the Denver Public Library calls "the first 18 wheeler ever used to haul a race vehicle to a national event" to the first Nationals at [Great Bend, Kansas](great-bend-srca.md).[^dpl]

Golightly, a charter Stripper, built his first dragster in 1955 after seeing the Nationals, which his Hall of Fame biography places "in Kansas City in 1954."[^golightly]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> In 1958 he became the first in Colorado to reach 160 mph, and he was the first Colorado racer to run 100 percent nitro, "which others had been afraid to try."[^golightly] The clubs went on to help build Continental Divide Raceway at Castle Rock, which opened in 1959 (that story, and its doubts, are on [the March 1957 survey](march-1957-drag-strip-survey.md#the-denver-puzzle) page).[^strip] There, at a 1961 national meet, NHRA's Division 5 tech director worked tech while the mothers kept the records and made lunch, and "all of us kids were running wild," his son Craig Pindell remembered.[^dslco]

## The proving ground on the hogback

John Bandimere Sr. had built his first race car in 1937, a flathead Ford he took up Pikes Peak to ninth place, and in 1957 he entered the first fuel-injected car in the Pikes Peak Hill Climb.[^cmhof] He was in the high-performance parts business in Denver, and his son, John Jr., was a street racer. *The Denver Post* says he wanted a place to test his wares and a safe place for the area's youth to race. His first choice of site, near the Jolly Rancher factory in Wheat Ridge, met stiff neighborhood resistance. In the fall of 1957 he paid $11,000 for about 180 acres against the hogback west of Denver, bought a scraper, a grader and two pushers at auction, and in 1958 moved several thousand tons of dirt himself.[^post]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> The track's own history adds that Jefferson County's commissioners helped him find the land.[^bhist]

He called it the Safety Proving Grounds of America: an oval, a drag strip, garages "to teach youth about automotive repair and performance," and a high-altitude test site for Detroit, with $75 lifetime memberships for testing.[^bhist] It was a parts dealer's strip in every sense. "Texas Don" Zielinski, who started racing there in 1960 with a '50 Ford carrying a 1957 383 Mercury Turnpike Cruiser engine, found that "no speed equipment was available for that particular engine." So "John Bandimere, Sr. custom ground a camshaft for me and hand-built a 8 Stromberg 97 log manifold for it."[^dslco]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>

The strip joined NHRA in 1968, and its High School Drags began in 1973.[^bhist] Rising taxes in the 1970s pushed the family toward bigger events, and NHRA came to the mountain in the late 1970s.[^post]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> In 1981 the track says the Compulink timing system was first used there, the work of John Jr., local racer Bob Brockmeyer and manager Larry Crispe. John Sr. died in 1986, at 78.[^bhist]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did Bandimere buy the land in 1957 or 1958?** *The Denver Post* says he paid for it "In the fall of 1957," and the track's timeline says that fall he located the parcel and got a building permit; the track's own introduction and his Hall of Fame biography say he "purchased a parcel of land" in 1958. NHRA says he "found property" in 1957.[^post][^bhist][^cmhof][^nhra25] A Strippers member's 2001 recollection, that Bandimere "opened in the late '60s," conflicts with all of them; NHRA membership came in 1968.[^strip][^bhist] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **What was the first NHRA national event at Bandimere, and when?** The track and *The Denver Post* say 1977 (the track names it the NHRA Sports Nationals), with the Mile-High Nationals in 1978; NHRA says the Mile-High SPORTSnationals came in 1978 and the Mile-High Nationals "a year later."[^bhist][^post][^nhra25] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Which Nationals did Golightly see?** His Hall of Fame biography says "The NHRA nationals, held in Kansas City in 1954."[^golightly] NHRA's first Nationals were at Great Bend in 1955 and the second at Kansas City in 1956 (see [Great Bend](great-bend-srca.md) and [Kansas City](kansas-city-timing-association.md)); either the city or the year is off. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **When were the drags on the unopened I-25?** Only the club's 2003 history mentions them, without a date.[^dta] The stretch between Evans and Yale would date them to the late 1950s. Lead: the *Rocky Mountain News* and *Denver Post*, 1956–59, and Colorado highway department records. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Bandimere make speed parts?** Zielinski's memory of the cam and the eight-carburetor manifold is the only account we have of Bandimere building parts by hand; the *Post* and his family describe him as a high-performance parts dealer who wanted manufacturers to test there.[^dslco][^post] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
