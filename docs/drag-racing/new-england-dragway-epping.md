# Built by Racers: The New England Hot Rod Council, Ma Sidebotham's Orientals and the Strip at Epping (N.H., 1953–2026)

*A mile of woods in two towns, bleachers carried in by the shareholders, and a vote not to sell*

## A decade on borrowed runways

New England's drag racing began on airports. Wikipedia's account, drawn from two regional histories, has a few Boston-area enthusiasts forming the New England Timing Association in 1950 and racing at airports in Newington, N.H., Beverly, Mass., and Sanford, Maine; three years later five regional clubs formed the New England Hot Rod Council, which held its first official drags in 1953 at the Newington Airport.[^wp] The track's own history agrees on Newington in 1953 and on July 1955 for the council's first races at the Sanford airport.[^ned] (The full Sanford story, with its barricades for the pilots, is [Fran Bannister and Fred Cain's](sanford-cain-bannister.md).) The New England Auto Racers Hall of Fame credits a racer and car builder from the 1950s, Jack Doyle, with forming the council "in the mid-1950's," with a long-term goal of a permanent strip of its own.[^nea]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>

The council was a federation of clubs, and the clubs did the work. In September 1957 Sy Sidebotham, his high school friend Harvey Richardson and a few other hot rodders formed the Orientals Hot Rod Club, Inc., "with the help and encouragement of Ma Sidebotham." By 1960 it had 24 active members, and *Hot Rod* saluted it that year for regular safety inspections of members' cars. As council members, the Orientals took jobs at the Sanford strip every other Sunday, helped needy families at the holidays, and spent their spare time, some 75 percent of them, building drag machinery. At the first race of the 1961 season, the club says, Sidebotham's dragster hit 170.45 mph.[^orient] Robert Corriveau, who ran A/SR at Sanford with the Tappets Auto Club of Manchester, N.H., went looking years later for a picture of Eleanor "Ma" Sidebotham: "She was a major influence and assistance to a group of young hot rodders."[^dslme] Steve George raced there in 1963 or 1964 with "some other guys from the New England Hot Rod Council Clubs from the MA, NH, ME area."[^dslme]

But an airport was a lease, not a home. Wikipedia's account says pilots complained, the clubs could not control the pavement, and races were canceled at the last minute.[^wp]

## A mile of woods

In 1963 council members began asking real estate agents in New Hampshire's seacoast region for a piece of land about a mile long. They were laughed at, Bernier remembered, except by Ben D'Agostino, who called back within weeks. His tract was two parcels, in two towns, "One piece was owned by the Trefetherens and the other by a guy who lived on an island off the coast of South Carolina. We took a look at the land and told him to go for it." Epping and Brentwood were both receptive, and Route 27 out front was an easy haul off Route 95 from Boston.[^ned] Ground was broken in 1965.[^nea]

The shareholders built it. Crews moved thousands of yards of dirt, and stockholders "logged countless man-hours dismantling used bleachers, hauling them to Epping and reassembling them"; the bleachers had come used from a Massachusetts university. They built a tower, put up fencing and painted, and on Sept. 11, 1966, New England Dragway opened its gates.[^ned] Doyle, who bought stock to help pay for it, managed the track from opening day until 1982 and kept his original shares for the rest of his life.[^nea]

Not everyone loved it at first. Paul Maynard raced his '57 Ford C/Gas, straight axle and all, "at Epping Dragway the first year it opened and rolled the car over coming home when the tow bar broke."[^dslnh] Adam Neithercut's father, who raced a Chevelle called the "CHEV-HELL" out of Saco, Maine, much preferred Sanford; the new strip's early surface was considered "greasy."[^dslme] Sanford did not last long after that. "1967 was Sanford's final year," Bruce Wheeler wrote. "I know. I raced my fueler there that summer."[^dslme]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>

## Hall of fame at the pit gate

The strip outlived its sanctioning bodies. Since its first year, *BangShift* wrote in 2015, it had run under the AHRA, the IHRA and the NHRA, and its first true NHRA national event, the New England Nationals of June 2013, drew close to 50,000 people over four days, 47 years after the gates first opened.[^bs15] Wikipedia dates the IHRA years, with its North American Nationals, to 1992–2009 and the NHRA to 2010.[^wp] In 2010 the concrete launch pad was extended from 745 feet to the full 1,320, and the track calls itself the only quarter-mile drag strip in New England.[^ned]

The Orientals kept the memory. On June 12, 1999, the club hosted its first New England Hot Rod Reunion at the dragway; in 2003 it placed "The Rock" there; and each year it inducts up to four people or groups into its New England Hot Rod Hall of Fame.[^orient] Wikipedia puts the memorial at the entrance to the pits and counts 106 inductees as of 2021, among them a disc jockey, [Arnie "Woo Woo" Ginsburg](../places/route-1-saugus-adventure-car-hop.md), for the music of the cruising years.[^wp]

## "We'd rather stay racing"

On the evening of Dec. 2, 2021, New England Dragway's shareholders met to consider selling the track and its land. By the report that ran in *Competition Plus* and *The MotorSportsNews.Net*, they voted it down 428 to 78, with seven abstaining.[^cp21][^msn21]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> Doyle, by then a stockholder and former manager, put it plainly: "they stayed true to the original idea that New England Dragway is a race track for the racers, by the racers, and they refused to take a $12.5 million deal. They said, 'No thanks, we'd rather stay racing.'"[^cp21]

Doyle, whom the New England Auto Racers Hall of Fame calls "The Godfather of New England Drag Racing," received an NHRA Lifetime Achievement Award in 2011 and went into the International Drag Racing Hall of Fame in 2016.[^nea] His wife, Mabel, a board member of the New England Racing Museum, pushed it to show more drag racing cars, and in her last weeks in 2025 was phoning the "NED Allstars" from hospice to promote their bench-racing event.[^nerm] Jack Doyle died on June 1, 2026.[^nea]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When was the New England Hot Rod Council formed, and by whom?** Wikipedia's account, which rests on two regional histories we have not seen, has five regional clubs forming it three years after the New England Timing Association's 1950 start, and holding its first official races at Newington in 1953.[^wp] The New England Auto Racers Hall of Fame says "Doyle formed the New England Hot Rod Council in the mid-1950's."[^nea] The track's history says the council's members began racing at Sanford in July 1955 without giving a founding year.[^ned] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How long did the Sanford drags last after Epping opened?** The track's history gives Sanford "ten years" from July 1955; Bruce Wheeler says "1967 was Sanford's final year," and another racer's son says his father raced there "from 1964 until it closed."[^ned][^dslme] (The Wilmington paper's "over 15 years" is weighed on [the Sanford page](sanford-cain-bannister.md#q-2).) <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Was the 2021 vote 428 to 78?** The two reports we read are the same text, which says "Sources indicate the shareholders voted the proposal down by a 428-78 margin" and that seven stockholders "reportedly" abstained; neither says whether the count was of shareholders or shares.[^cp21][^msn21] Because *The MotorSportsNews.Net* reprints *Competition Plus*, the $12.5 million figure also rests on one witness, Jack Doyle; neither report names a buyer.
