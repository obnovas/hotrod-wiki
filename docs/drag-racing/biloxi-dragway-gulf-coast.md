# A Parade of Boats Down the Strip: Biloxi Dragway (1957–67)

*A Gulf Coast strip that opened with 307 entries, sent boats and a high school down the asphalt, and closed when the landowner said no*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (the dates and records come from a Gulf Coast historian's own site, built from the strip's programs and the local papers, and from a 2011 trade-press summary of it; the two disagree on some years; the racers' memories are one each) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> *Biloxi Dragway* (local historian Michael "Cory" Evans's site, timeline and a reprinted 1976 profile of Paul VanderLey); racers' memories on *Drag Strip List*; *Dragzine* (2011); Wikipedia (Nolan's price and the 1960 crash, both flagged; not a source for Ivo's or Arfons's year) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-12 (Round 39 citation review) · **Open issues:** ⚠ 1960 or 1961 for Ivo and Arfons; ⚠ who ran it, and the 1962 gap; ◐ the builder and the $30,000; ◐ VanderLey's injectors; ⚠ a 1968 memory · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

## Trial races in Woolmarket

In the spring of 1959, a drag strip north of Biloxi, Mississippi, did something that does not sound like a race track. It held a boat show. On April 26, at its First Annual Boat Show, the program called for a "parade of boats down the strip" at three o'clock.

A drag strip is a straight course laid out for one kind of race: a standing start, two vehicles at a time, and a clock. Biloxi is a city on the Mississippi Gulf Coast. This strip sat just north of town, just outside Woolmarket, on ground that now lies between Hudson Krohn Road and Old Highway 67.[^dz11] The strip was a town amenity as much as a race track, which is how boats ended up on the asphalt. That was not how the place began.

The racing had started two years earlier. Trial races, a test before the official opening, were held on August 18, 1957. The strip opened officially on September 1 and 2 with 307 entries.[^bdtl] An entry is one competitor signed up to run, and not every one of them was a car. That December the Mississippi championship, the state's title, went to Albert Waits of New Orleans, Louisiana, and he won it on a motorcycle.[^bdtl] For its first years the place ran as the Gulf Coast Drag Strip. Wikipedia, citing the Biloxi *Daily Herald* of September 7, 1957, says Earl C. Nolan built it for $30,000.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^wp] That builder and that price rest on the one account. In 1961 about twenty members of the Pascagoula Timing Association, a club from Pascagoula that timed and organized races, drove over for a Gulf Coast Drag meet, entered ten classes and won ten trophies ([Ten for Ten at Biloxi](pascagoula-timing-association.md)).

The boat show was the odd day, not the only town day. In 1959 the featured attraction was "Bucking Bessie from Handsboro." The sources give the name and do not say what Bessie was. On the boat-show day the racing still produced a winner. Top eliminator is the last competitor left after the elimination rounds, winners advancing and losers going home, and that day the top eliminator still ran 13.88 at 98.90 mph.[^bdtl] Those two figures are how a drag race is written down. The 13.88 is the time in seconds from the start to the finish. The 98.90 is the speed, in miles an hour, at the far end. On May 3 the guests were "All Students and Teachers" from Biloxi High School, with the junior high invited on a following Sunday.[^bdtl] The invitation was to the town's students, not only to its racers. That spring Danny Taylor broke his own B dragster record twice in eight days, at 126.20 and then 132.50 mph.[^bdtl] A dragster is a car built to make that straight run and not to do much else, and B dragster was the class he ran. The two speeds are the record, in miles an hour, the second higher than the first.

## Who came to Biloxi

Once the strip was a local habit, the national names followed. These were the racers who towed from track to track and were known well past the Gulf Coast.

The timeline kept by Michael "Cory" Evans, a local historian whose website has gathered the strip's history since 2006, has Don Garlits's *Swamp Rat* team there on January 31, 1960. The *Swamp Rat* was Garlits's dragster. A pass is one run down the strip. The team made three passes, with Art Malone driving, and the second was clocked at 195 mph.[^bdtl] Tommy Ivo came with "Crewman Don Prudhomme," the timeline's word for the man working on Ivo's car, and won the final, the last pairing of the meet. Art Arfons brought the *Green Monster* jet car, a car pushed by a jet engine rather than by the usual pistons, and his fastest of four passes was 164.37 mph.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^bdtl][^dz11] Whether Ivo and Arfons came in 1960 or 1961 is the dispute. Evans's own pages do not agree, and a later summary picks one of the years. The two dates are at the bottom of this page.

Chris Karamesines took Garlits's track record with an 8.84 in 1961. A track record, here, is the quickest time the strip had on its books, and an 8.84 written that way is 8.84 seconds from start to finish, not miles an hour. By 1966 the record was a 7.75 at more than 200 mph by Malone, the same Art Malone who had driven Garlits's car on the January visit: 7.75 seconds, at more than 200 miles an hour.[^dz11]

*Dragzine*, a drag-racing news site summarizing Evans's work in 2011, wrote that Biloxi was "widely considered as up-to-date as a strip could be at that time," with electronic timing in the early 1960s and lights for night racing, and that its gasser meets could draw as many as 200 entrants.[^dz11] Electronic timing means the clocks were tripped by the cars, not kept by a person with a watch. The lights meant the program could run after dark. A gasser is a full-bodied car, the body still around it, raced on gasoline rather than on the racing fuel the sport simply calls fuel, and rather than in a dragster.

Shirley Shahan, the "Drag-On-Lady," beat John Donilson's Flying Ford there on April 24, 1966, in the fourth round, after he red-lighted.[^bdtl] The Flying Ford was Donilson's car. Elimination racing pairs the field and sends the winners on, so the fourth round is the fourth of those pairings. A red light means the driver left the starting line before the green, and the run is lost before the finish. On June 25 that year Bill Golden "wheel stands the entire length of the track" in the *Little Red Wagon*.[^bdtl] A wheelstand is the front wheels coming off the ground. Golden held that pose for the whole run, and the *Little Red Wagon* was the car he did it in.

The weekly racers were local. Theodore G. Bilbo II ran a '66 Chevelle, a 1966 Chevrolet Chevelle, in J/Stock and wrote, "I won the class each time I raced"; he remembered Bubba Cummings' "Wompin' Wagon" and S. T. Cooper's "Jiffy" '57 Chevy, a 1957 Chevrolet.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^dslms] J/Stock is a stock class, for cars nearer the shape the factory built than a gasser or a dragster. He put those class wins in 1966 through 1968. The strip's last event was July 1, 1967, so the 1968 is where the sources part company, and that argument is at the bottom of the page. Tom Murphree's father, B. E. Murphree, raced with a Mr. Knight of Paul's Automotive in Biloxi, a local shop, and together they campaigned a '57 Nomad called the "Nut Wagon."[^dslms] A Nomad is a Chevrolet station wagon. To campaign a car is to race it as your regular entry, week to week. Who ran the strip after its first years is less clear.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> The accounts do not settle the operator, or whether 1962 was a blank year. That stays at the bottom of the page.

## A family pit crew on Callavet Street

Biloxi's own star was Paul VanderLey, and his story starts before this strip did. A 1976 profile, reprinted on Evans's site, says he raced in organized quarter-mile competition from 1949. A quarter mile is the distance those sanctioned races were timed over. He began once a month at Hammond, Louisiana, with flathead cars. A flathead is an older engine with the valves set in the block. Then he went to Southern California, on his sister's advice, to finish school. At Santa Barbara in the early 1950s he raced with Bob Joehnck, including a '23 Model T, a Ford Model T of nineteen twenty-three, that the profile says had "the first set of commercial injectors produced by Hiborn [Hilborn]" and ran 137 mph at Pomona in 1954.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^bdvl] Injectors are fuel injection, a way of metering fuel into the engine without a carburetor. The name in brackets is the correction: the profile's Hiborn is Hilborn, as in Stu Hilborn, whose injectors these were. The claim that this set was the first commercial one is the profile's alone. Pomona is the California place where that 137-mph run was timed.

He moved home to Biloxi in 1956, was in the eight-second zone at 178 mph on gasoline by 1959, and in 1961 bought what the profile calls "the third 'Swamp Rat III' of Don Garlits" and switched to fuel, later handing the driving to J. L. Payne and then Connie Swingle while he built engines.[^bdvl] The eight-second zone means the car was covering the course in eight-point-something seconds, here at 178 miles an hour, and it was still on gasoline rather than on the racing blend. Fuel, in this sport, is the word for that racing blend. He stopped driving the car himself and built the engines, with Payne and then Swingle taking the wheel. A shop fire in 1966 "destroyed his cars and stock."[^bdvl] The profile does not itemize what the stock was.

In 1976 he won the NHRA South Central Division IV Modified Eliminator title in a '61 Corvette, a 1961 Chevrolet Corvette, clinching it at the season's last race in Jackson. The NHRA is the National Hot Rod Association, the body that sanctioned the racing and kept a championship by region. South Central was its Division IV. Modified Eliminator was the title for modified cars in that division, as against the stock classes. Clinching means the championship was decided at that last race in Jackson. By then he ran an automotive shop on Callavet Street, a street in Biloxi, and the profile is plain about how the work got done: his wife Kay and their children, Dan, 19, Cathy, 16, and Linda, 13, were his assistants and his pit crew, Dan on the engine with his father.[^bdvl] A pit crew is the group that works on the car between rounds. His was his family.

The strip did not last as long as he did. Its last big meet was a two-day gas coupe championship in April 1967, with more than 33 gassers "from Florida to Texas," the second day run at Mobile Dragway. A gas coupe is the gasser idea written as a class: a coupe body, on gasoline. Mobile Dragway is in Mobile, Alabama, across the state line. Then the landowner refused to renew the lease. The strip stood on someone else's land, and when that owner would not sign again, the dates ran out. Biloxi Dragway ran its final event on Saturday night, July 1, 1967, with $1,250 in cash and "three foot trophies for all class winners."[^bdtl][^dz11] Murphree was eleven.[^dslms]

He was the boy whose father had campaigned the "Nut Wagon." Looking back, he said they had won a television in a ticket drawing, that his picture ran in the track's own paper, the Biloxi Dragway News, and that he was heartbroken the strip was gone. The sentence is his, in the quotation at the top of this page. The jet car, the *Swamp Rat*, and the *Little Red Wagon* had all been through. What the record leaves at the end is a lease the landowner would not renew, a shop on Callavet Street where a wife and three children were the pit crew, and an eleven-year-old who had won a TV in a drawing.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **1960 or 1961 for Ivo and Arfons?** Evans's timeline puts Ivo and Prudhomme's visit on "Aug 7th" and Arfons on "Oct 23rd," both under 1961, and lists Ivo's return with his four-engine car as another 1961 entry.[^bdtl] The same site's home page lists "August 7, 1960" and, on the next line, "Ivo vs DiBenedetto."[^bdhome] *Dragzine*, summarizing Evans's site, says Ivo and Prudhomme raced for top eliminator in 1960 at 160 mph and that Arfons came "in October of that year."[^dz11] The two dates are not independent reports: *Dragzine* is retelling Evans, and Evans's own pages disagree. Wikipedia names Ivo and Arfons among the racers and gives neither visit a year.[^wp] The *Daily Herald* for those months would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Who ran it, and was there a gap in 1962?** Wikipedia says a dragster crashed into the fence toward Highway 67 on May 8, 1960, that this changed the ownership, that events were canceled in 1962, and that Harvey and Bill Walther ran the strip from 1962 until it closed.[^wp] But Evans's timeline lists Garlits running *Swamp Rat III* there in 1962, and the track's record list has Arfons at 204 mph on December 2, 1962.[^bdtl][^bdhome] We have not read a source of our own for the crash or the Walthers. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Earl C. Nolan build it for $30,000?** Only Wikipedia, citing the *Daily Herald* of September 7, 1957, page 12, which we could not read; the same article says airmen from Keesler Air Force Base raced a dragster as the "Keesler Coasters."[^wp] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Were the Joehnck–VanderLey T's injectors Hilborn's first commercial set?** Only the 1976 profile says so, and it adds that the car was the subject of a three-page *Hot Rod* feature in 1954, which we have not seen.[^bdvl] See [Stu Hilborn](../people/stu-hilborn.md) for the injector's own story. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did anyone race at Biloxi in 1968?** Bilbo dated his class wins "1966-1968," but the strip's last event was July 1, 1967.[^dslms][^bdtl][^dz11] He may be counting a later track on the coast; Evans's site links to the Gulfport Dragway.[^bdhome] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
