# A Parade of Boats Down the Strip: Biloxi Dragway (1957–67)

*A strip that opened with 307 entries, held a boat show and a school day, and ended when the landowner said no; and the Biloxi racer whose wife and children were his pit crew*

## Trial races in Woolmarket

The Gulf Coast's drag strip sat north of Biloxi, between what are now Hudson Krohn Road and Old Highway 67, just outside Woolmarket.[^dz11] Trial races were held on August 18, 1957, and it opened officially on September 1 and 2 with 307 entries. That December the Mississippi championship went to Albert Waits of New Orleans, on a motorcycle.[^bdtl] For its first years it ran as the Gulf Coast Drag Strip, and Wikipedia, citing the Biloxi *Daily Herald* of September 7, 1957, says Earl C. Nolan built it for $30,000.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^wp] In 1961 about twenty members of the Pascagoula Timing Association drove over for a Gulf Coast Drag meet, entered ten classes and won ten trophies ([Ten for Ten at Biloxi](pascagoula-timing-association.md)).

The strip was a town amenity as much as a race track. In 1959 the featured attraction was "Bucking Bessie from Handsboro." On April 26 it held its First Annual Boat Show, with a "parade of boats down the strip" at three o'clock, and the top eliminator that day still ran 13.88 at 98.90 mph. On May 3 the guests were "All Students and Teachers" from Biloxi High School, with the junior high invited on a following Sunday.[^bdtl] That spring Danny Taylor broke his own B dragster record twice in eight days, at 126.20 and then 132.50 mph.[^bdtl]

## Who came to Biloxi

The national names followed. The timeline kept by Michael "Cory" Evans, the local historian whose site has gathered the strip's history since 2006, has Don Garlits's *Swamp Rat* team there on January 31, 1960, making three passes with Art Malone driving and clocked at 195 mph on the second.[^bdtl] Tommy Ivo came with "Crewman Don Prudhomme" and won the final, and Art Arfons brought the *Green Monster* jet car, whose fastest of four passes was 164.37 mph.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^bdtl][^dz11] Chris Karamesines took Garlits's track record with an 8.84 in 1961, and by 1966 the record was a 7.75 at more than 200 mph by Malone.[^dz11]

*Dragzine*, summarizing Evans's work in 2011, wrote that Biloxi was "widely considered as up-to-date as a strip could be at that time," with electronic timing in the early 1960s and lights for night racing, and that its gasser meets could draw as many as 200 entrants.[^dz11] Shirley Shahan, the "Drag-On-Lady," beat John Donilson's Flying Ford there on April 24, 1966, in the fourth round, after he red-lighted, and on June 25 that year Bill Golden "wheel stands the entire length of the track" in the *Little Red Wagon*.[^bdtl]

The weekly racers were local. Theodore G. Bilbo II ran a '66 Chevelle in J/Stock and wrote, "I won the class each time I raced"; he remembered Bubba Cummings' "Wompin' Wagon" and S. T. Cooper's "Jiffy" '57 Chevy.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^dslms] Tom Murphree's father, B. E. Murphree, raced with a Mr. Knight of Paul's Automotive in Biloxi, and together they campaigned a '57 Nomad called the "Nut Wagon."[^dslms] Who ran the strip after its first years is less clear.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>

## A family pit crew on Callavet Street

Biloxi's own star was Paul VanderLey. A 1976 profile, reprinted on Evans's site, says he raced in organized quarter-mile competition from 1949, first once a month at Hammond, Louisiana, with flathead cars, then in Southern California, where he went on his sister's advice to finish school. At Santa Barbara in the early 1950s he raced with Bob Joehnck, including a '23 Model T that the profile says had "the first set of commercial injectors produced by Hiborn [Hilborn]" and ran 137 mph at Pomona in 1954.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^bdvl] He moved home to Biloxi in 1956, was in the eight-second zone at 178 mph on gasoline by 1959, and in 1961 bought what the profile calls "the third 'Swamp Rat III' of Don Garlits" and switched to fuel, later handing the driving to J. L. Payne and then Connie Swingle while he built engines.[^bdvl] A shop fire in 1966 "destroyed his cars and stock."[^bdvl]

In 1976 he won the NHRA South Central Division IV Modified Eliminator title in a '61 Corvette, clinching it at the season's last race in Jackson. By then he ran an automotive shop on Callavet Street, and the profile is plain about how: his wife Kay and their children, Dan, 19, Cathy, 16, and Linda, 13, were his assistants and his pit crew, Dan on the engine with his father.[^bdvl]

The strip did not last as long as he did. Its last big meet was a two-day gas coupe championship in April 1967, with more than 33 gassers "from Florida to Texas," the second day run at Mobile Dragway. Then the landowner refused to renew the lease, and Biloxi Dragway ran its final event on Saturday night, July 1, 1967, with $1,250 in cash and "three foot trophies for all class winners."[^bdtl][^dz11] Murphree was eleven.[^dslms]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **1960 or 1961 for Ivo and Arfons?** Evans's timeline puts Ivo and Prudhomme's visit on "Aug 7th" and Arfons on "Oct 23rd," both under 1961, and lists Ivo's return with his four-engine car as another 1961 entry.[^bdtl] The same site's home page lists "August 7, 1960" and, on the next line, "Ivo vs DiBenedetto."[^bdhome] *Dragzine*, summarizing Evans's site, says Ivo and Prudhomme raced for top eliminator in 1960 at 160 mph and that Arfons came "in October of that year."[^dz11] The two dates are not independent reports: *Dragzine* is retelling Evans, and Evans's own pages disagree. Wikipedia names Ivo and Arfons among the racers and gives neither visit a year.[^wp] The *Daily Herald* for those months would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Who ran it, and was there a gap in 1962?** Wikipedia says a dragster crashed into the fence toward Highway 67 on May 8, 1960, that this changed the ownership, that events were canceled in 1962, and that Harvey and Bill Walther ran the strip from 1962 until it closed.[^wp] But Evans's timeline lists Garlits running *Swamp Rat III* there in 1962, and the track's record list has Arfons at 204 mph on December 2, 1962.[^bdtl][^bdhome] We have not read a source of our own for the crash or the Walthers. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Earl C. Nolan build it for $30,000?** Only Wikipedia, citing the *Daily Herald* of September 7, 1957, page 12, which we could not read; the same article says airmen from Keesler Air Force Base raced a dragster as the "Keesler Coasters."[^wp] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Were the Joehnck–VanderLey T's injectors Hilborn's first commercial set?** Only the 1976 profile says so, and it adds that the car was the subject of a three-page *Hot Rod* feature in 1954, which we have not seen.[^bdvl] See [Stu Hilborn](../people/stu-hilborn.md) for the injector's own story. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did anyone race at Biloxi in 1968?** Bilbo dated his class wins "1966-1968," but the strip's last event was July 1, 1967.[^dslms][^bdtl][^dz11] He may be counting a later track on the coast; Evans's site links to the Gulfport Dragway.[^bdhome] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
