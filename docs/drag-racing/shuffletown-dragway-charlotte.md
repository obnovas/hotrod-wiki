# Engines After Church: Shuffletown Dragway, the Strip Its Neighbors Built (Charlotte, N.C., 1950s–1990s)

*Tractors and creek dirt, Fords against '55 Chevys, gates at 11 and engines at noon by Queen City News's account, a motorcycle racer's thousand passes, and a park that kept a piece of the strip*

## Tractors and creek dirt

Shuffletown Dragway was built by its neighbors. Queen City News reported in 2022 that on Sundays the gates opened at 11 and the engines stayed quiet until noon, out of respect for the church services nearby.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-6"><a href="#q-6" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>[^qcn22] It ran for more than 30 years, until the neighborhood around it changed and the city that came out to meet it closed it.[^c5]

Shuffletown was a hamlet of farmers beside the Catawba River, with a ferry since at least 1764.[^uncc] By the 1950s people were drag racing on Rozzelles Ferry Road, from the service station to the bridge. It was dangerous and illegal, and it was not going to stop. So local residents built a drag strip with their own tractors, out of dirt from the banks of Long Creek.[^c5] Norman "Puddin'" Thompson helped make it.[^c5] The county and the university library date it to 1958.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^meck][^uncc] Frank Spittle, who grew up about five miles away and later collected the story of [Chester's strip](chester-drag-strip-south-carolina.md), remembered it being built beside the creek at the request of the Charlotte police to get hot rodders off the street.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>[^hamb] Joel Collins, whose family raced there, put it as "a combination of the police and the kids on the streets."[^qcn22]

It started as dirt, and short. *CharlotteFive* calls it an eighth of a mile that took 10 to 15 seconds to run; Spittle remembered a fifth of a mile.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^c5][^hamb] Spittle was 15. He rode his bicycle there before he had a license.[^hamb]

## Fords against '55 Chevys

The Shuffletown Grill on Rozzelles Ferry Road opened around the same time, in 1957, and it is still the place to hear about the track.[^qcn22][^c5] The races everyone came for were Ford against Chevrolet. Thompson raced his new '55 Crown Victoria and then a '57 Fairlane against the new V8 Chevrolets. Rivalry races brought 2,000 to 3,000 people. Cotton Coltharp and Van Hatley both ran '55 Chevrolets, and Coltharp says of those races, "I never beat Van Hatley, the Big Kahuna." Hatley, by local legend, was the first to reach 100 mph there.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> Coltharp explained the '55's place in it: "They were used on the track because they were a popular car, easy to convert for a drag race and there were lots of them around."[^c5]

It was paved in 1964, and the speeds went past 105 mph.[^c5] Spittle remembered it shortened to an eighth-mile when it was paved.[^hamb] He bought his first motorcycle, a '57 Triumph Bonneville, in 1963, and over the next 19 years made at least 1,000 passes there, on everything from street bikes to twin-engine Top Fuel Harleys. It was a very narrow track, he wrote, but in more than 30 years of racing there was not a single fatal crash. It ran year-round, weather permitting.[^hamb]

The racers' memories run small and specific. Jerry Blanton, racing there in the mid to late 1960s, remembered Liston Kendall's "Blue Angel" Anglia, with two top-loader four-speeds split into one transmission with two first gears and two seconds. "The car would hop down the track." The inspector, a man called Red, classified the cars under a tree. Ralph McConnell's uncle Roger still had the family's Anglia, *Mighty Mouse II*, and still raced it in 2017.[^bs17] Ronnie Matthews remembered watching out for chickens running across the track.[^bs17] David Wright's father, Larry, called the races from the tower.[^bs17]

## The city comes out

In 2018 Spittle read in the *Observer* that Tom Ferrell had died at 80. Ferrell, he wrote, bought Shuffletown from its original builder about 50 years before and owned it until Charlotte closed it, and also owned Greer Dragway in South Carolina until 2015.[^hamb] Tom Barto credited W.S. "Scratch" Whisnant with starting and running it in its heyday.[^bs17] It never stopped being a local track. When [Becky White](farmington-dragway-becky-white.md) left Farmington one weekend in July 1981, Shuffletown is where she went.[^bw10] In the early 1990s, one racer remembered, the door-slammer shows were standing room only, with names like Ronny Sox, Scotty Cannon and Tommy Mauney.[^hamb] BangShift's Brian Lohnes called it one of the South's great top sportsman quick-eight shootout tracks, and a place where pro modified was coming up.[^bs17]

Charlotte grew toward it. Houses went up nearby, and enough complaints followed.[^c5] Shuffletown was annexed into the City of Charlotte, the city's noise ordinance applied, and the racing stopped in the early 1990s.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^qcn22][^c5]

The land became a park in 2010.[^c5] Shuffletown Park is 54 acres now, with ball fields and a dog park, and a skate park planned in 2026 that the county says was inspired by the dragway.[^meck] Behind the volleyball courts there is an oddly paved walking trail. It is the drag strip, running from near Bellhaven Boulevard to I-485.[^qcn22] On July 30, 2026, by the playground maker's announcement, volunteers, county staff, Charlotte Motor Speedway people and Bobby Labonte were to build a new playground there, with signs telling the dragway's story.[^gt26] The neighbors built the strip with their own tractors, and, as Queen City News reported, held the engines for church. Now anyone can walk it.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **1957, 1958 or 1959?** Mecklenburg County's park page and UNC Charlotte's library guide say the dragway was built in 1958.[^meck][^uncc] *CharlotteFive* says only the 1950s, and gives 1957 for the Shuffletown Grill; Queen City News says the grill opened "around the same time as the drag way back in 1957."[^c5][^qcn22] Spittle remembered 1959.[^hamb] The *Charlotte Observer* or *Charlotte News* of 1957–59 should have the opening. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How long was the strip?** *CharlotteFive* says the dirt strip was an eighth of a mile.[^c5] Spittle remembered a fifth-mile of dirt, shortened to an eighth-mile when it was paved in 1964; another racer remembered winning a trophy there in 1963 when "it was a 1/5 mile dirt track."[^hamb][^bs17] Queen City News says it was a quarter mile when it first opened and was shortened twice as the cars got faster.[^qcn22] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **1991, 1992 or 1996?** *CharlotteFive* says it closed in 1991 under the city noise ordinance; Queen City News says the early 1990s.[^c5][^qcn22] UNC Charlotte's guide, citing a 2008 *Observer* story, and BangShift say 1996.[^uncc][^bs17] Two H.A.M.B. posters remembered 1992.[^hamb] City annexation records and the ordinance's date would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did the police ask for it?** Spittle remembered it built "at the request of the Charlotte police department"; *CharlotteFive* says local residents built it, and Collins said "I think" it was a combination of the police and the kids.[^hamb][^c5][^qcn22] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Was Van Hatley first to 100?** *CharlotteFive* says "supposedly," and no time slip or contemporary report we read says so.[^c5] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-6" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Gates at 11, engines at noon?** Queen City News reported in 2022 that on Sundays the gates opened at 11 and no engine could fire before noon, out of respect for church services nearby.[^qcn22] No other source we read gives the hours. A *Charlotte Observer* story or a church notice from the strip's years would confirm them. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-6" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
