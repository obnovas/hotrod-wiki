# "No Roll Bars Needed": The Drag Strip off Turn Four at Georgetown Speedway, Delaware (1956–63)

*A downstate Delaware drag strip that began as a straightaway bolted onto a stock car track, open on Sunday mornings to anyone with a license and the family car*

    "Bring your pleasure car—no roll bars needed."

    — advertisement for the official opening of the Georgetown Speedway drag strip, *Smyrna Times*, Aug. 16, 1956.[^st560816]

In the summer of 1954 Col. Shew of the Delaware State Police reluctantly asked permission to use unmarked patrol cars at night, "for the purpose of breaking up these teen-age 'hot rodders' who race along roads at night," warned of uniformed troopers by their own spotters. Two of the worst spots, he said, were the McConnell Bridge district and Milford Cross Roads, both near DuPont Company buildings. "Apparently, there are other 'drag strips' in southern Delaware," the *Milford Chronicle* added, and explained the term for its readers: a stretch of highway used "for all out racing and even for playing what is known as 'bumper tag'." The State Highway Department's chief engineer, Col. William A. McWilliams, reported that bumper tag was "becoming more and more prevalent."[^mc540716]

## A strip off turn four (1956)

Downstate, the answer came from a stock car promoter. Georgetown Speedway was a NASCAR-sanctioned half-mile oval with racing every Saturday night, and in July 1955 its ad offered "A Special Drag Race on July 23 Only."[^mc550722] The next June the promoter, Charles Herbert, announced that a quarter-mile drag strip was nearly finished and would be ready for a formal opening in about two weeks. It had been built at the south end of the speedway, "for a distance of 1440 feet off the fourth turn," so the strip connected with the oval's home stretch and gave the dragsters room to reach top speed and slow down after the traps.[^mc560615]

The rules were as open as a strip could be. "Anyone holding a valid driver's license and at least 16 years of age is eligible for competition on the quarter mile strip," the speedway's notice ran in July. "The strip will be for open competition and safety belts and helmets are not necessary. Any type vehicle may participate." Entries were first come, first served.[^st560712]

The official opening was Sunday, August 19, 1956, from 10 a.m. until dark: all classes, electronic timing, "Open to the Public," and the line in the ad that sums up 1956 better than any rule book, "Bring your pleasure car—no roll bars needed."[^st560816] The runs were sponsored by Georgetown Dragsters, Inc.[^mc560817] A month later the *Smyrna Times* reported that the first two days of drag racing had been "well received" and that Herbert had opened the strip for the next three Sundays, run by Craig Mellinger.[^st560920] By October the Sunday program put drag racing at 10:30 in the morning and the first stock car race at 2:30, and when the stock cars took a week off for the national championship at Langhorne, the strip opened anyway.[^st561004][^mc561012]

## Smyrna's turn (1959–60)

Upstate drivers had no strip of their own. In October 1959 Joseph Passwaters of the Smyrna Lions Club "suggested the need for a drag strip in this area," and in December he outlined a project "for providing a drag strip near Smyrna" for the club's board.[^st591008][^st591217]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> The next Labor Day weekend, among the state's highway dead, the *Chronicle* listed a 22-year-old Smyrna business student who died "after a reported 'drag race'" with his cousin.[^mc600909]

Delaware's racers traveled. Neighborhood columns in the *Chronicle* noted local young men who "enjoyed the drag races at Atco, N.J., on Sunday" in May 1961 and again in 1963.[^mc610512][^mc630531]

## Every Sunday, all year long (1962–63)

In May 1962 Georgetown was back in the drag business, on a shorter strip. Track owner Charlie Herbert would have "the 1-8th mile drag strip ready for the Drag Racing debut" on Sunday afternoon, May 27, the paper said, and "The Georgetown Speedway strip will be the only sanctioned drag racing operation in the state of Delaware." Racers from all over the Delmarva Peninsula had agreed to run. "Salisbury, Md. has furnished the largest group, with nearly every town on the Shore sending representatives."[^mc620518]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

All that summer the speedway's weekly ad closed with the same line: "DRAG RACING EVERY SUNDAY AFTERNOON."[^mc620601] In September the Delmarva Timing Association announced it would "continue to present Drag racing every Sunday weather permitting, all year long."[^mc620907] In April 1963 the association took over the speedway again for drags "on the 1/8th-mile Drag Strip," and by June it was running every Sunday afternoon on "the one-eighth mile asphalt strip," registration at 11 and eliminations at 2.[^mc630426][^mc630607]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **What happened to the Georgetown strip between 1957 and 1961, and to the Smyrna plan?** The papers we searched show the quarter-mile strip in the fall of 1956 and the eighth-mile "debut" in May 1962, but not the years between; nor did we find the Smyrna Lions' project after December 1959.[^st561004][^mc620518][^st591217] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Why did the strip change from a quarter mile to an eighth?** The 1956 notices describe a quarter-mile strip of 1,440 feet off turn four; the 1962–63 notices describe an eighth-mile strip.[^mc560615][^mc620518][^mc630607] Neither paper says why, or whether the 1962 strip was the old one shortened or a new one. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
