# A Mobil Flag on Eubank: the New Mexico Timing Association's Eleven Years to Albuquerque Dragway (1952–1974)

*A poster at a ballfield, a bypass the police closed on Sundays, and a strip paid for by its own fans*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (the story rests on two founders' written memories and one founder's son, which agree on the outline but not on every date) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> Roy "Moon" Mullin's history of the NMTA (written 2013, handed out at the 2014 reunion), Dale Schafer's memories, racers' memories on Drag Strip List, Lyle Greenburg in the *New Mexico Motorsports Report* (2016), NHRA Division 4 Hall of Fame, Schafer's obituary (2025) and the dragway's and NHRA's memorial notices · **Last verified:** 2026-11-04 (Round 32) · **Open issues:** ⚠ when the New Mexico Timing Association was formed; ? the "first Winternationals"; ⚠ who was the NMTA's president after 1961 · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "To get the drag strip in Albuquerque it took many members to finally get the final from the City and get the grading and pavement on the drag strip done [1952 to 1963] – eleven long years of hard work on everyone's part of the NMTA and the hot rod clubs."

    — Roy "Moon" Mullin, a charter member, writing in 2013.[^mullin]

## A poster at the ballfield

In 1952 Roy Mullin, 19, was playing baseball at Albuquerque's Height Community Center when he saw a poster calling car enthusiasts to a Wednesday night meeting. The group was ACME, the Albuquerque Competition Motor Enthusiasts, soon renamed the New Mexico Timing Association, and it was made of clubs: the Rebels, the Road Queens, the Swanks, the Lead Sleds, the Valve Lifters, the Drag Lynx, the Satans, the Turtles, the Chariots and Mullin's own Second Gear Club, among many more.[^mullin]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>

After many meetings, the city gave the NMTA a permit to race every other Sunday on the Menaul Truck Bypass, from Central Avenue to Eubank Boulevard. A police officer blocked both ends and supervised; the city required an inspection of every car; the entry fee was a dollar, for trophies. Money was so short that winners were asked to bring their trophies back for the next race, and Mullin gave back some of his own.[^mullin] Dale Schafer, who later ran the strip, began his racing career at the same 1952 races, his hall of fame biography says, calling them the police department's "sanctioned 'Street racing'."[^d4hof]

## The oil trucks and Eubank

In 1954 the city moved the drags to Eubank Boulevard south of Central, by the east gate of Sandia Base. The reason was trucks: big rigs were hauling oil equipment from the Hobbs fields to new wells around Farmington, and the city "wanted to get the big oil trucks back out of the downtown area on the Sunday when there were drag races."[^mullin] The NMTA thanked Police Chief Paul Shaver for two seasons of help, and on Eubank the assigned officer soon came only now and then, "as the City Police department was happy about what was going on." Racers drove a dirt rut beside the road back to the pits.[^mullin]

It was a homemade operation. George Buffet ran a food trailer and gave the club a share of his sales. Judd Jewelers, whose owners raced, sold the club trophies at about half price. Bob Pegue built the timing gear, because he wanted to know his own ET and speed on his motorcycle. And in 1956 Mullin, who owned a Mobil station on Eubank north of Central, got a Mobil checker flag from the company and let the NMTA start races with it. "I still have this Mobil Checker flag in one of my trophies cases."[^mullin] The NMTA adopted NHRA rules, which pleased the city, and the NHRA sent Tex Smith to explain drag racing's safety rules to city officials at a meeting at the University of New Mexico.[^mullin]

## Four men and a Sunday in November

By Schafer's account, organized racing on Eubank stopped in 1958, and "Illegal drags went on days and nights after that." In November 1961 four young men, Schafer, Dick Pool, Steve Smucher and Tom Volle, who thought the NMTA "was not pushing hard enough," formed the Albuquerque Drag Association. Pool, an associate of the sheriff's department, got city permission for a legal Sunday race on Eubank: about 60 cars, no clocks, a flagman and a finish-line judge, 50 cents admission. They cleared $800, then joined the NMTA and put the money in its nearly empty treasury. "Dave LaNear was voted president and I was vice president," Schafer wrote. "That was when the Timing Association took off."[^schafer]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>

## Bonds, a mesa and a mile-high strip

The city park board finally leased the NMTA a sandy, nearly flat tract of state university land south of the airport for a dollar a year. Members surveyed it themselves, and the association sold bonds to grade and pave it.[^mullin] Albuquerque Dragway opened in the spring of 1964, and Schafer managed it until 1974, when the bonds were paid off.[^d4hof][^schafer] That same year he was introduced to NHRA president Wally Parks and invited to work the Winternationals, checking fuel, and some 40 years later he was still certifying fuel at national events.[^d4hof]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> Lyle Greenburg, whose father was among the planners, remembered meetings on the family's back porch, riding his tricycle down the new strip at three or four, and bondholders getting in free.[^greenburg] It was a mile up, and Rex Porter of Las Cruces, who match-raced his fuel Hemi funny car there in 1966–67, said times ran a half second to a second slower than at Phoenix's Bee Line.[^dslnm] Five people who were at the 1964 grand opening came back for the season opener in 2021.[^dslnm] Schafer's 133-page history of the strip, with 275 pictures, started as a run of ten copies and had sold more than 500.[^dslnm]

Schafer died in Albuquerque on March 14, 2025, at 89. His family's obituary remembered him as "a central figure in the auto parts industry in New Mexico," from Teepee Parts Warehouse to co-owning Don's Auto Parts, and as a drag racing pioneer "having built the Albuquerque Dragway in 1963," then working NHRA technical inspection "well into his seventies."[^obit25] The dragway itself said he was "one of the founding members" and "a member of the NM Timing Association," and NHRA's year-end memorial retold his hall of fame biography almost word for word.[^cyd25][^nhra25]

## The rest of the state

In southern New Mexico the drags were on runways and unfinished roads. Around 1963–65, Andrew "JR" Gomolak recalled, "Many drag races were run on the newly-built, but not 'open' IH25, north of the Radium Springs exit," until the state police discouraged them; Bob Patterson then organized NHRA-rules drags at the New Mexico State University airstrip, and Las Cruces also had Porter & Sims Drag-A-Way on the old airport.[^dslnm] (Denver's racers had used an unopened stretch of the same interstate: see [Denver](denver-timing-association-bandimere.md).) At Walker Air Force Base near Roswell, airmen formed Duster's Hot Rod Club, whose president, Larry Sanchez, raced "at all the local strips and Oklahoma City Nationals"; by 1964 the base had stopped drag racing.[^dslnm] Hobbs had the Charioteers' strip on its old air base, where, Marv Burrows said, Eddie Hill broke "the 200 mph barrier on gas in his twin-engined rail" in 1962.[^dslnm]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> Carlsbad's Pecos Valley Dragway, run with help from the Dirt Dobbers auto club, started in 1963, and in 1968–72 a Cannon Air Force Base pilot, Bill Bradshaw, raced his Mustang on Portales's eighth-mile on West Cherry Street, once a month from April to October.[^dslnm] Sunland Park's strip, at El Paso's Westside Airport, was just inside New Mexico.[^dslnm]

*See also:* [Albuquerque's Central Avenue](../places/central-avenue-albuquerque.md), the cruising strip, for the 2005–18 cruising ban; [Phoenix's west-side clubs](phoenix-west-side-karbs-litchfield-road.md), next door in Arizona; and [the Oklahoma City Nationals](oklahoma-city-nationals-rush-springs.md), where a Walker AFB club president raced.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When was the New Mexico Timing Association formed: 1952 or 1961?** Mullin, a member from 1952, says the 1952 ACME group was renamed the NMTA and ran the Menaul drags in 1952–53; Schafer's own account has the NMTA already in existence when his group joined it in 1961; but his NHRA Division 4 Hall of Fame biography says "In 1961 Dale and some associates formed the New Mexico timing association."[^mullin][^schafer][^d4hof] The biography most likely compresses the 1961 merger into a founding. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Which Winternationals did Schafer first work?** His hall of fame biography says that in 1964 he "was invited to work the first Winternationals"; the NHRA Winternationals began in 1961, so either the year or "first" is off.[^d4hof] NHRA's 2025 memorial repeats the same words.[^nhra25] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Eddie Hill run 200 mph on gasoline at Hobbs in 1962?** One racer's memory says so; we have not found the run in a 1962 paper or magazine.[^dslnm] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Who was the NMTA's president after the 1961 merger?** Schafer's own account says "Dave LaNear was voted president and I was vice president"; his NHRA Division 4 Hall of Fame biography says "Dale was elected President of the NMTA," without a year.[^schafer][^d4hof] Both could be true if Schafer held the office later, but neither source says so. Round 32 checked three accounts published after his death in March 2025: NHRA's "In Memoriam" repeats the biography's sentence ("Schafer was elected President of the NMTA"), so it is not an independent witness; the dragway's notice calls him a founding member and "a member of the NM Timing Association"; and the family obituary does not mention the association at all.[^nhra25][^cyd25][^obit25] Lead: the *Albuquerque Journal*'s reports of NMTA elections, 1961–64. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
