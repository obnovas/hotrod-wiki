# Not the Idaho One: Firebird International Raceway on Gila River Land (Chandler, Ariz., 1983– )

*A desert complex Charlie Allen built after Orange County closed, a lease fight with the Gila River Indian Community, and a name that is easy to confuse with a sagebrush strip in Idaho*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (a 2007 local drivers' magazine, NHRA histories, an East Valley Tribune lease story and 2013–2023 local business coverage agree on Allen, the Gila River land and the later rename; they split on whether the track opened in 1983 or early 1984) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> local magazine (*Arizona Driver*, Nov.–Dec. 2007), *East Valley Tribune* (July 27, 2013), KTAR (Jan. 3, 2013), AZ Big Media (Dec. 8, 2023), NHRA (*National Dragster* / Phil Burgess, 2022; Alex Waldron, 2016; junior-dragster announcement, Dec. 2023), Hemmings (Jan. 4, 2013), Dragzine (Dec. 11, 2023) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-08 (Round 35) · **Open issues:** ⚠ opened in 1983 or early 1984 · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "It's not an exaggeration to say that Firebird International Raceway changed the direction of my life."

## After Orange County, a second Firebird

This is not [Firebird Raceway in Eagle, Idaho](firebird-raceway-idaho-bootleggers.md), the Bootleggers' sagebrush strip that became the first U.S. dragstrip on the National Register. The Phoenix-area Firebird sits on Gila River Indian Community land along Interstate 10, south of Chandler—about ten miles south of downtown Phoenix, a 2007 *Arizona Driver* feature said—and it began as a way for Charlie Allen to keep promoting races after [Orange County International Raceway](orange-county-international-raceway.md) closed under him.[^azd07][^nhra22]

Allen had begun construction on Firebird International Raceway outside Phoenix in 1983, he later told NHRA's Phil Burgess. The Irvine Company had let him haul away whatever he could salvage from Orange County; he got bids on moving that track's timing tower to Phoenix and decided they were "way ridiculous." In what Burgess called a "weird coincidence," the lake at the new track, Firebird Lake, was 113 acres, "exactly the size of the former OCIR parcel." *Arizona Driver*, writing for the complex's approaching 25th anniversary in 2008, said Allen, Dick Scott and Bill Guptill "unveiled" the facility in 1983. Burgess, writing in 2022, said the place now called Wild Horse Pass Motorsports Park "opened in early 1984 as Firebird Int'l Raceway."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>[^ocir][^azd07][^nhra22]

Allen had booked John Force into match-race shows while he still ran Orange County. Force came to the new desert track in its opening year to support him, NHRA wrote, and was in the first national-event Funny Car field the following year. He is, that 2022 preview said, the only driver entered that season who had also competed at the inaugural national event, a field that included [Don Prudhomme](../people/don-prudhomme.md), [Tom McEwen](../people/tom-mcewen.md) and Ed McCulloch. Force was runner-up to John Collins—his fifth NHRA runner-up without a win—after smoking the tires in a Coca-Cola Corvette, in his first year with crew chief Austin Coil.[^nhra22]

## The first Fallnationals

The track's first NHRA national event was the 1985 Fallnationals. *National Dragster*'s 2016 look back called it the first national event held at the facility, by then renamed Wild Horse Pass Motorsports Park, "formerly known as Firebird Int'l Raceway Park." [Gene Snow](../people/gene-snow.md) won Top Fuel, his first title in the class, after barely making the field: a 5.628 at 254.95 mph over Dick LaHaie's 5.635 at 241.93. Bob Glidden won Pro Stock over Joe Lepone Jr. and, the same article says, clinched the 1985 NHRA world championship; the win was Glidden's 46th, "12 more than anyone else in the history of the sport at the time." Collins collected his first NHRA national-event win in Funny Car after 22 years of racing, when Force spun the tires off the line. McEwen lost in the first round to McCulloch after a magneto was installed incorrectly.[^nhra85]

*Arizona Driver* billed the place as the only track in the country that hosted racing on both "the asphalt quarter mile and the liquid quarter mile," with an NHRA drag strip, an IHBA- and APBA-sanctioned lake (1.1 miles long, a 2.4-mile oval in that telling), road courses, the Bob Bondurant School of High Performance Driving and weekly programs for amateur racers. The magazine put the complex at 440 acres and said more than 600,000 people a year came through the gates; it also claimed more than 10,000,000 visitors since 1983. Force later won at the track eight times (1994, 1995, 1996, 1997, 1999, 2000, 2001 and 2005) and was runner-up six times.[^azd07][^nhra22]

The Valley had already had [Beeline Dragway](beeline-dragway-mesa.md), the Salt River strip that hosted AHRA's Winternationals; that earlier lease, and the argument over when it closed, stay on that page.

## The lease runs out, twice

In January 2013 KTAR reported that Allen, in a written statement, said the Gila River Indian Community would not extend or renew the track's lease, and that the facility—on Interstate 10 south of Chandler—would close in April. NHRA vice president of racing operations Graham Light told the station the Phoenix market mattered: "For the last 29 years, it's been one of our favorite spots on our 24-race NHRA series." Hemmings, a day later, reported that Friday editions of the *Arizona Republic* had quoted NHRA president Tom Compton as "very optimistic that the venue will be on the NHRA Mello Yello Drag Racing Series schedule in 2014," and that Forrest Lucas, who had built the off-road track, said the Community would allow him to operate that course and the lake. The Bondurant school was also based there.[^ktar13][^hemm13]

By July the *East Valley Tribune* was writing about new management. Longtime manager Allen had announced that the Community would not sign another lease; Sun Valley Marina Corporation, which managed the land, was asked to find a new tenant. Dick Hahne, recruited by Coppertrain Development Partners, took it over. Hahne had spent more than 25 years as vice president of operations at Daytona International Speedway. He told the *Tribune* he planned to resurface the drag strip, redo the suites and control tower, add starting-line seats and dress up the east road course along Interstate 10. The first event under the new name, Wild Horse Pass Motor Sports Park, was to be a Lucas Oil drag-boat race on September 13–15, 2013.[^evt13]

In March 2022, Dragzine later wrote, the Wild Horse Pass Development Authority announced that a new highway overpass would cut through the dragstrip, road courses and boat-racing lakes. What was supposed to be the end of a 40-year run turned. In December 2023, with support from the Authority and the Gila River Indian Community, the facility was renamed Firebird Motorsports Park, "an ode to its former glory days as Firebird International Raceway." Casey Buckman became track manager and Connie Bopp business administrator. Buckman, who had grown up going to events "when it was called Firebird," said he was "honoring the history of the past 40 years." The Authority, AZ Big Media reported, "worked to reroute a freeway overpass that was supposed to go through the middle of the facility in order to keep it open." NHRA announced that the 39th annual Arizona Nationals would run there April 5–7, 2024.[^dz23][^azbig23][^jr23]

Alan Reinhart remembered one Saturday night in the early 1980s when the regular announcer did not show up for a sportsman bracket race. Someone handed him a microphone and told him to get talking. "It's not an exaggeration to say that Firebird International Raceway changed the direction of my life," he told Dragzine when the name came back.[^dz23]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did Firebird International Raceway open in 1983 or early 1984?** *Arizona Driver* (Nov.–Dec. 2007) says Charlie Allen, Dick Scott and Bill Guptill "unveiled" the facility in 1983 and treats 2008 as its 25th anniversary. Allen told NHRA he "had begun construction on Firebird Int'l Raceway outside of Phoenix in 1983." Phil Burgess, in a 2022 NHRA preview, wrote that the facility "opened in early 1984 as Firebird Int'l Raceway." KTAR, in January 2013, quoted NHRA's Graham Light calling Phoenix a favorite stop "for the last 29 years," which counts back to 1984. AZ Big Media's December 2023 rename story speaks of "the past 40 years," which counts back to 1983. Construction in 1983 and an opening early in 1984 would reconcile some of this; the sources do not all say so in those words.[^azd07][^ocir][^nhra22][^ktar13][^azbig23] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
