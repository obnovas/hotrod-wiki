# "Anyone With $3000": Jackson, Pearl and Mississippi's Hometown Strips (1959–2024)

*Hometown memories, from a 1959 finish line that went to gravel to a 500-foot strip in Byram with the Jackson Dragway name*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Low (almost all of this is racers' and families' memories, one each, on a racer-built archive and a forum; Riverside's history comes from a local racer's site quoting an old-timer and the state's records; the strips' own dates are mostly unconfirmed) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> racers' memories on *Drag Strip List* and *Racingjunk Forums*; a Pearl racer's hobby site; WJTV (Jackson) "Your Hometown" (2024) · **Last verified:** 2026-11-12 (Round 39 citation review) · **Open issues:** ? which 1959 Jackson track; ◐ who built and owned Riverside; ◐ Riverside into the 1990s; ◐ Grenada in the 1950s · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "Anyone with $3000 and a little mechanical know how could go racing. Try that today."

    — Jeffery Slade, on Riverside Raceway in the late 1970s, *Drag Strip List*.[^dslms]

Mississippi's drag racers mostly raced at home. What the record kept is what they remembered. A 1959 track north of Jackson went to gravel past the finish line. Riverside, in Pearl, opened beside Interstate 20 in 1972 and later lost the land to a high school. The Jackson Dragway name ended up on a 500-foot strip in Byram.

## Gravel past the finish line

Asked on a racers' forum in 2009 where he made his first pass, a racer posting as JimmySmith answered: "Jackson Ms, 1959 Track was just north of Jackson." It was a "circle track and drag strip," and "after finish line it went to gravel."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>[^rj09]

In the 1960s the place to race in Jackson was the Jackson Sports Arena. David Church ran a '55 Ford in junior stock there and sent the *Drag Strip List* a photo, taken by a friend in the mid-1960s, of Claude Young's '55 T-Bird in a match race against Albert Ray Dulaney's '55 Chevy. "We own the T-Bird now and still race it (Y-Block power now)."[^dslms] Sam Williford, born in 1973, never saw the strip, but he owns a car that raced there: Barney Lewis Sharp's red '62 Pontiac Catalina, the "Big Chief," photographed at the Arena around 1963–64 before it was lettered.[^dslms]

## Riverside, beside the interstate

The strip most Jackson racers remember is Riverside, in Pearl, on the east side of Jackson and right on the north side of Interstate 20. A Pearl racer's hobby site, quoting an old-timer who raced there for its whole life, says James Alford built it and opened it in 1972.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> Its staging lanes ran along the I-20 right of way, the track ran north, and the pits and return road on the right were "very hilly." "It was a common sight for Mississippi Highway Patrol cars to be parked along I-20 when racing was going on."[^23t]

Jeffery Slade raced there in the late '70s. "James Alford Jr. fixed up the old Dodge trash truck into a nice hot rod. I went to school with James. Racing is what we did." He also remembered the *Little Red Wagon* wheelstanding down the track, and the invitation that came in the mail to tell him when the season started.[^dslms] Don Hurst and his future wife watched a Division 4 points race at "the track we called Jackson" in 1975 and saw one of his Pontiac heroes, Jack Mullins.[^dslms] In 1976 Biloxi's Paul VanderLey clinched his Division IV title at the season's last race "at Jackson," though the profile that says so does not name the strip ([Biloxi Dragway](biloxi-dragway-gulf-coast.md)).[^bdvl]

The site traces the owners from there. Cary Busby sold the track to Bob Heard around 1976. In June 1980 the state suspended its operation for failure to pay franchise taxes; it was still closed through 1981 and reopened in 1982 as Jackson Dragway, under Charles Rogers, with the Winston Rebel Funny Car Nationals on October 23–24, 1982. The land belonged to the Pearl Public School District, which the strip paid a yearly lease, and in 1987 the district sued to end the lease so that it could build a high school there. Pearl High School "obliterated much of the old drag strip," though remnants survive on its western end.[^23t] The old-timer remembered it running "up into the 90's" before the lease ended.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^23t]

The name moved again. WJTV's 2024 "Your Hometown" segment on Byram describes the Byram Dragway as the former Jackson Dragway, a 500-foot strip built in 2002 that races on Tuesday nights from 7 to midnight, from around Mardi Gras until around Thanksgiving. One driver, Melvin Smith, told the station he had raced since he was 17 and that it runs in his family.[^wjtv]

## The Delta and the hill country

Up in the Delta, a Jimmy Smith (we can't tell if he is the forum poster) managed the Greenville Dragway for seven years, "from '75 till '82," and wrote that he had raced at 15 of the 29 Mississippi tracks on the *Drag Strip List*. Don Hurst first raced at Greenville in 1973, probably at the two-day Mississippi State Championships, in a '69 GTO, and won his class. His future wife, T. J. Colvin, ran a '68 Firebird named "Easy Lovin.'" On her first pass she got "lost in space" after turning left past the finish line, looking for a return road that wasn't there, until she saw other cars driving back down the left side of the track. She won her bracket that night; the second day was rained out. They married on September 9, 1978.[^dslms]

David Church raced once at the Columbus strip in 1972, while a student at Mississippi State, against a "home-made looking 'Christmas Tree.'" He remembered the "Oldtimer," a 1950 Olds from Greenwood that "always ran good," and lost the Super Stock final to an AMX.[^dslms] At Nettleton, Carolyn Cooley's cousin Michael drove the children around Bud's Race Track in an old coupe; her grandmother lived across the highway. David and Dexter Orear raced a Mr. Riley there one day, "We had beat him at Fulton Dragstrip," and Michael M. Mann remembered "top notch guys" coming in to race.[^dslms]

Some of it is now only a trophy. Liz Durham's grandfather, Charles Preston Durham, "known locally as Brother Durham," came from Durant in Holmes County and raced around the state, she believes in the 1950s. Most of his trophies are lost, but she found one that says Grenada Dragways. "Does that ring any bells?"<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^dslms] They raced at home. The memories are what the record kept.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Which Jackson track was it in 1959?** The forum poster's 1959 track north of Jackson had both a circle track and a drag strip.[^rj09] We have not found whether it was the same place racers later called the Jackson Sports Arena, or when the Arena opened and closed.[^dslms] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Who built Riverside, and who owned it before 1976?** The hobby site says James Alford built it and opened it in 1972, and that Cary Busby sold it to Bob Heard around 1976, without saying when Busby bought it. The old-timer quoted on the same page also says it opened in 1972.[^23t] Jeffery Slade remembers a schoolmate, James Alford Jr., who "fixed up the old Dodge trash truck into a nice hot rod." He does not say that Alford Jr. built the strip, and neither source says how the two James Alfords are related.[^dslms] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Riverside race into the 1990s?** Only the old-timer quoted on the hobby site says so; the same site dates the school district's lawsuit to 1987, and it also names a later operator, Norman "Moose" Pearah, without dates.[^23t] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **When and where was Grenada Dragways?** Only the trophy and Liz Durham's belief that her grandfather raced in the 1950s.[^dslms] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
