# Cut Through the Mountain: The Black Widows, Lassiter Mountain, Helena and Alabama's Hometown Strips (1955–1966)

*A car club with a bulldozer's ambition, a strip behind a church, and a weekend circuit from Montgomery to the Chattahoochee*

## The club that cut a mountain

North of Birmingham, off Coalburg Road, the Black Widows car club built its own strip in 1958. Bhamwiki describes how: "They cut through the ridge of Lassiter Mountain, leaving deep embankments on either side of the dragway in lieu of guardrails."[^bham] A photo of about 1960 in the Encyclopedia of Alabama shows cars waiting to race at the strip outside Fultondale and calls it "an 1⁄8-mile strip that was founded in 1958 by the Black Widows Car Club."[^eoa] A 2008 newspaper story about its reopening, though, remembered "a quarter-mile strip of asphalt."[^cull]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>

The members were working men with their own garages. Gilbert Hurst of Fultondale, his son told *Junkyard Life*, was "a founding member of the Black Widows (a local car club that opened legendary local drag strip Lassiter Mountain)" and ran his own auto repair business in the 1960s; the family's 1958 Chevrolet Brookwood wagon went to the track.[^junk] The track later had an owner, Tony Lee, and in the mid-1960s, while Chrysler was boycotting NASCAR, Richard Petty "was a frequent competitor at Lassiter Mountain, driving his Barracuda."[^bham]

Promoter Randall Shew, who went there as a young man in the 1970s, remembered the early clubs and cars, the Black Widow Car Club, "Bad Habit" and "Wee Willie," and admitted the place had a rough edge: "The roughneck element of the dragstrip has gone away," he promised in 2008. The embankment still separated the 60-foot-wide strip from the bleachers. Rick Pennington, a Fultondale machine shop owner whose uncles took him there as a boy, called it "your typical southern-style dragstrip."[^cull]

## Helena, behind the church

South of Birmingham, in Shelby County, Helena had a quarter-mile strip from the mid-1950s to the mid-1960s, behind what is now Gospel Light Baptist Church on Roy Drive, off County Road 58; houses cover the site now.[^scr16] In February 2016 the Kenneth R. Penhale City of Helena Museum opened an exhibit about it, and the *Alabama Yesterdays* blog found the museum "crowded with people who had come to reminisce and learn about the quarter-mile Helena Drag Strip."[^alyest] Racers lent trophies, a brochure and photographs, and most of them, the *Shelby County Reporter* noted, were not from Helena at all.[^scr16]

At a 2018 reunion, one old racer told the *Reporter* that the strip ran every weekend and that Don Garlits had made a 200-mph run there.[^scr18]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> The same racer said a newer strip north of Birmingham drew the crowds away, presumably Lassiter Mountain.[^scr18] The two were closer than rivals: Mike Richards, who worked at Lassiter Mountain at 13, says he "also worked for the same track owners that also ran the Helena, Alabama track."[^dslal]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>

## A weekend circuit, 1958–66

Alabama's racers didn't stay home. Norman Browning, in a '54 Ford two-door with a '57 Ford Y-block, won the Semi-Modified class at the 1959 NHRA Southern Regional at Napier Field in Dothan, and in '58 and '59 won "the same class at Odena, Tuskegee, Coalburg, Mtg and Helena, Alabama. I still have the trophies."[^dslal] In east Alabama, Bill Capps's father "could race Friday nights at Montgomery Drag Strip, then either Valley [Shawmut] or Tuskegee on Saturday afternoons and finally Phenix City Drag Strip on Sundays." In May 1962 he beat Steve Honnell's '55 Thunderbird in Top Eliminator at Valley Drag Strip in Shawmut, for a trophy and $15. His mentor was J. C. Sizemore, whose '55 Chevy gasser was the "Golden Rod."[^dslal]

At Shawmut, Capps's father saw a dragster from Birmingham fail to shut down, overrun the strip and flip upside down in the Chattahoochee River, the driver pinned inside; everyone jumped into the shallows and lifted the car, "but they all arrived minutes too late."[^dslal]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>

In Montgomery, John B. Hart raced a '53 Corvette across from Gunter Air Force Base, at a strip also called Capital City Dragway. The car was sponsored by his own speed shop on Chandler Street and powered by an engine built by Elliot Sadler of Bessemer.[^dslal] On the coast, Ron Shaw's Counts Auto Club ran the Mobile Drag Strip near Dauphin Island "from about 1959 thru the mid 60's" (see [the Pascagoula Timing Association](pascagoula-timing-association.md), which raced there).[^dslal] Near Gadsden, the Green Valley Drag Strip, started in the late 1950s by local businessmen, ran into the 1970s; regulars such as Horace Johnson, in a '67 Fairlane 427, raced there for years.[^dslal][^gt16]

And at Miller's Ferry near Camden, in 1966, Ann Culpepper Barnett beat her father's time in the family's yellow 1964 Mercury Comet Caliente, and they let her race. "Some guys left when I beat them, but most were nice--bringing me water, etc (in the Southern heat)."[^dslal]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Was Lassiter Mountain an eighth-mile or a quarter-mile?** The Encyclopedia of Alabama and Bhamwiki say an eighth-mile; the 2008 *North Jefferson News* story says regional hot-rodders raced it on "a quarter-mile strip of asphalt" for five decades.[^eoa][^bham][^cull] It may have been both at different times; we have not found a dated measurement. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did Garlits run 200 mph at Helena?** One racer's memory, at a 2018 reunion, read only in a search excerpt of the *Shelby County Reporter*.[^scr18] Promoters were already billing Garlits's "200 mph SWAMP RAT" in 1961 (see [Chardon](chardon-road-ravens-thompson-drag-strip.md)), so a billed speed may have become a remembered one. We have found no timed 200-mph pass at Helena. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did the same people run Helena and Lassiter Mountain?** Only Mike Richards's recollection says so.[^dslal] Lead: the *Birmingham News* and *Birmingham Post-Herald*, 1958–66. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Who died in the Chattahoochee at Valley, and when?** A son's retelling of his father's memory gives no name or date.[^dslal] Lead: the *Valley Times-News* and the *Columbus Ledger*, about 1958–62. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
