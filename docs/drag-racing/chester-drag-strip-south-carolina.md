# Twice as Wide as zMAX: Chester Drag Strip and the International Timing Association (S.C., 1956–60)

*A wartime emergency airfield, a promoter with his own sanctioning body and timing clocks, and a Greenville club that knew how to run a legal drag race*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (racers' and a local historian's posts, many with names, photographs of programs, trophies and clippings, and interviews with the airport manager, a club president and Don Garlits, all reported secondhand on one forum thread; no 1950s newspaper read yet) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> forum recollections (the H.A.M.B. thread "Chester Drag Strip," 2012–25, started by Frank Spittle of Indian Land, S.C.); a racer-historian's drag strip story (Bill Pratt, Draglist, 2002); a drag racing magazine's track history (Drag Illustrated, 2025) · **Last verified:** 2026-10-30 (Round 27; quotes checked against the thread pages) · **Open issues:** opening year ⚠; closing year ⚠; the date of Garlits's fire ⚠; the I.T.A.'s founding ◐; Garlits at the 1957 International ? · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "I got in a little trouble when someone ask my mother if her son won a trophy in her new car after reading the Chester racing results in the paper the next morning."

    — Gene Boulware, who raced his mother's new '56 Pontiac at Chester at 14, as Frank Spittle quoted him (2013).[^hambch]

Chester's airport was built for a war that never needed it. Butch Church, its manager, told the local historian Frank Spittle that it was one of three identical World War II emergency fields near Charleston, with Darlington and Barnwell, each with three one-mile paved runways and a hangar. "Starting in 1956, however, the runways at Chester were closed on Saturdays. Bob Osiecki had the rights to the runways."[^hambch]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup>

What Osiecki got was enormous. Spittle, walking the old runway in 2013, compared it with zMAX Dragway, a modern strip that he said had been "the talk in drag racing" for being wide enough to race four cars at once: "CHESTER WAS TWICE AS WIDE AS Zmax." "The strip was 150 feet wide and over one mile long when most drag strips back then were 30 feet wide and a half to 3/4 mile long," Spittle wrote. The upper runway, also 150 feet wide and 5,000 feet long, made paved pits, a rarity in the 1950s, and a third runway served as the return road. Church remembered the portable grandstands riding on four balloon tires; an Allis-Chalmers tractor towed them out to the starting line and jacks were lowered at each corner to steady them.[^hambch]

## A club that already knew how

Osiecki did not have to teach anyone to run a drag race. In Greenville, Cecil Curry had come home from Korea in 1953, finished his roadster in '54 and been elected president of a new club, the Road Rebels. While other hot rodders raced on the main drag at night, Spittle wrote, Curry's club "worked with the police to organize legal drag races, first on a blocked highway and later at the Greenville military air base," furnishing the people and the timing equipment. When Osiecki put Chester together in 1956 he came to Curry, and the Road Rebels got the timing duties.[^hambch]

The sanctioning body was Osiecki's own. The timing cards read I.T.A., the International Timing Association; Garlits told Spittle that Osiecki not only sanctioned Chester through it but founded it.[^chdg]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup> Spittle later posted its top-16 points standings from 1957 and noted, "There were over 1600 drivers in the I.T.A." Osiecki also built his own timing equipment and offered to rent or sell it to the new strips springing up across the country; two former Chester employees said his man Jerry Hall installed it.[^hambch]

## Saturday nights

The weekly racers were local and the stories are personal. Gene Boulware was 14, old enough for a driver's license then, when he raced his mother's new '56 Pontiac: "I removed the fender skirts and wheelcovers," he said, and he won his class, and the paper printed the results the next morning. Robert Freeland had lost the use of his legs when he was accidentally shot in the spine at 23; after two years of recovery he put hand controls on his '54 Olds, and "In 1957 Robert went on a winning spree and won D-Stock 7 weeks in a row," plus the season's points trophy. A grandchild posted a photograph of Jimmy Thompson, labeled on the back as receiving "the D/S Trophy for First Place running 75 MPH August 3, 1957."[^hambch]

## The International, November 1957

Late in November 1957 Osiecki ran the 1st International Drag Races over four days. From the souvenir program's entry list Spittle picked out the names he knew: Walt and Art Arfons from Akron with their aircraft-engined dragsters, "Serop Postoian from Detroit" with a Chrysler Hemi dragster, and Joe Bush, who towed his Chrysler-powered "Speed Sport Roadster" from Tucson. Garlits is not on Spittle's list. He was racing at Chester that year, by Spittle's caption on a 1957 photograph of his dragster there, but no source we have read puts him at the International itself.[^hambch]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> A forum member wrote in 2021 that Harvey Galloway drove his '55 Pontiac from western Kentucky to the meet and won his class. Postoian came back: a photograph posted to the thread, its file labeled 1958, shows him at Chester.[^hambch] In December 2013 a reader found a Bakelite trophy at a flea market; its brass plate reads "Best Engineered I.T.A. Chester, SC Nov. 29 1957."[^hambch] (For Postoian's own story, see [Setto Postoian](../people/setto-postoian.md); for the Arfons brothers' home runway, [Akron's Fulton Airport](akron-fulton-airport.md).)

Chester's worst wreck, as the thread remembers it, was an Arfons dragster destroyed there, "the only serious crash in the 4 years the track was open"; Arfons was not hurt.[^hambch]

## The fire, and the end

In June 1959 a supercharger explosion at Chester nearly killed Don Garlits. Spittle later learned from Curry's scrapbook who won that day: Curry himself, beating Bondy Long in the Top Eliminator final.[^chcurry]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> The strip outlived Garlits's season there. Spittle had believed it closed in 1959, but "Chester did not close in 1959 as I had come to believe," he wrote after reading the scrapbook, and he posted a 1960 ad, "the last year the track was open," with Art Malone driving Garlits's car.[^chcurry]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> Why it ended is a story with no paper behind it yet: "The explanation I have heard the last 50 years is the "officials" would not renew his lease."[^hambch]

## The strips around it

Chester was not alone in the upstate. Bill Pratt wrote that "Ware Shoals Dragway, originally built as a dirt track in 1956, was one of the first drag strips built in the Southeast," built by the Gambrell family, who ran a drive-in on Route 25, "to get the street racers off Highway 25."[^ws02] Greer Dragway opened in October 1958; the drag strip historian Bret Kepner told Spittle that the I.T.A. sanctioned its opening and that the Greer Jaycees, who first managed it, brought Osiecki in.[^hambch] Kepner later dug up the name of the man who won the very first race at Greer in 1958, Norman Peace; the track tracked him down and made him guest of honor at one of its gasser races, where Kepner interviewed him on the starting line.[^di25] Greer still runs, under Mike Greer, who began racing there in the 1970s.[^di25]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did Chester open in 1956 or 1957?** The airport manager dates the Saturday closings from 1956, and Gene Boulware remembers racing "when it opened in 1956"; Cecil Curry places Osiecki's visit in 1956.[^hambch][^chcurry] But Garlits's recollection, as Spittle reported it, is that he raced there "from '57-'59,"[^chdg] and a later caption by Spittle himself says "When the track opened in 1957."[^chcurry] A dated 1956 program or newspaper result would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Bob Osiecki found the International Timing Association?** Only Garlits's word, relayed by Spittle, says so.[^hambch] We have not found the I.T.A.'s charter or a 1950s account of its start. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Did Garlits run the 1st International in November 1957?** Spittle's list of the entrants he recognized does not include him; Spittle captions a 1957 photograph of Garlits's dragster at Chester, and Garlits told him he raced there every year the track was open, but neither places him at the International.[^hambch][^chdg] The program's full entry list, which the forum shows only to signed-in members, would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Was Garlits burned on June 10 or June 20, 1959?** Garlits gives June 20 in a documentary posted online, and Spittle's caption for Curry's final against Bondy Long uses the same date; the narrator of another video, Steve Evans, says June 10.[^chdg][^chcurry] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Did Chester close in 1959 or 1960?** Garlits's recollection: "The track closed in '59, the year of his fire."[^chdg] Curry's scrapbook has 1960 coverage, and Spittle's 1960 ad calls 1960 the last year.[^chcurry] The thread's "4 years the track was open" fits 1956–59 or 1957–60 equally well. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
