# Church First, Then the Drags: The Hope Drag Strip at the Municipal Airport (Hope, Ark., 1962–72)

*A town paper's chatty column follows a drag strip from a garage meeting to an airport runway and back to a letter to the editor*

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "It's Our Suggestion That You Take Your Family and Go To the Church of Your Choice, Come Back and See Us On The 24th."

    — Notice from the Hope Drag Strip, *Hope Star*, Apr. 8, 1966.[^hs660408]

In April 1966 the Sunday races at Hope, Ark., fell on Easter. The strip ran a notice in the *Hope Star*: "Due To Our Drag Race Falling On Easter Sunday The Races Will Not Be Held At Hope Drag Strip This Sunday." Instead it suggested church, and promised racing again on the 24th.[^hs660408]

The paper's trail from the 1962 meetings to that notice has a four-year gap.

## A meeting at Collier's Garage (1962–64)

In March 1962 the *Star*'s "All Around Town" columnist went to "an organizational meeting of a proposed Drag Strip group, hoping to establish a local drag strip where the local fellows can work out their cars under proper supervision." It was "a very orderly meeting," the column said. "It seems these so called 'delinquents' realize the danger of dragging on public roads even more than the parents do . . . They want to do something about it too." The question for the town was where it wanted "the kids and their cars . . . on the public highways or on a policed drag strip where damage can be held to a minimum." The boys met at Collier's Garage, and "all interested persons, pro and con" were invited.[^hs620329]

The second meeting outgrew the garage. "There was between 50 and 75 present, including a few interested fathers," the column reported, and it wondered aloud whether the other parents "just don't care or maybe it was their night to play poker." The Arkansas State Police would try to send a safety man with films to the next meeting. "Incidentally has anyone got a larger place available for meetings for awhile?"[^hs620404]

Two years later the *Star* had a different drag strip to report. On a Saturday night in September 1964, Hempstead and Howard county officers "raided a drag strip on Highway 24, east of Nashville," and arrested eight drivers, "all youths." As the *Nashville News* put it, "they even had an audience." Two who pleaded guilty had their fines fixed at $71.15 and their licenses suspended for 30 days.[^hs640916]

## On the airport runways (1966–68)

By 1966 the town had its strip, on the municipal airport.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup> That June the *Star* reported "a new state record" set at the Hope Drag Strip by Richard Thorpe of Dallas, "whose Bush-Payne dragster was clocked at 209.78 miles per hour."[^hs660616]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> The 1967 ads, headed "HOPE DRAG STRIP, Hope, Ark, Airport," promised time trials at 9:30 in the morning and eliminations in the afternoon. One card featured "The Paddy Wagon, a Chevy Van powered by a fuel injected 427 Cu. Inch Chevy V8 Engine, doing High-Flying Wheelies";[^hs670721] another, "Coker Machine's Unblown Chrysler & Charles Smith's Unblown Chrysler turning 200 MPH on fuel."[^hs670812] That August Jim Cooper announced that the proceeds of the week's race would help pay for an operation to save the eyesight of a Hope policeman's five-year-old son.[^hs670812b]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

The runway that made it possible also ended it. In April 1968 the column relayed a plea from airport officials for local drivers to keep off the runway: "there is no drag strip anymore but some youths are coming in anyway."[^hs680419] In May the city board read a Federal Aviation Agency letter on the restrictions that came with an instrument approach to the airport from the Texarkana radio station, and "decided against reopening the Airport for drag racing." The restrictions, the minutes said, "would shorten the length of runway available."[^hs680529]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## Asking again (1972)

Four years later a letter came in. "Several years ago Hope had drag races at the Hope Municipal Airport and they seemed to draw many people from around this area," wrote David Baker of Route 4. "I can not understand why these races were stopped since it stopped racing on highways and public streets." The other runways, he said, were "grown up in weeds or are cracked and warped." Racing there "would give young people a place to run their cars legally and safely."[^hs720510]

A few days later the *Star* ran a feature on Hope's first two male telephone operators, David Chance and David Baker. Both had fairly new racing cars, and the night shift left their days free to work on them. "The nearest is at Haynesville, La., and that's a long way to go for a race," Baker said. Chance had already raced at Haynesville and brought back two trophies; Baker had yet to enter his first race. The chief operator, Mrs. Sarah Adams, said the young men's interest in racing cars "has brought a new subject to discussions during coffee breaks."[^hs720515]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **When did the airport strip open?** The Easter notice of April 1966 is the earliest Hope Drag Strip item we have found, and it reads like a strip already running.[^hs660408] We searched the Thursday, Friday and Saturday issues of 1965 and 1966 on the Internet Archive and found nothing earlier, but OCR misses words, and other weekday issues are unread. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **A state record at 209.78?** Thorpe's speed and the "new state record" come from one short item, which also mentions the world record in a phrase the scan leaves partly unreadable.[^hs660616] We have not found the run in a drag racing paper. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Who ran the strip?** Jim Cooper announced the 1967 benefit race, but the paper does not give his role, and the ads name no operator or club. Whether the 1962 Drag Strip Club ran the airport races is not stated.[^hs670812b][^hs620404] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **When was the last race?** The last ad we have read is from August 1967, and by April 1968 "there is no drag strip anymore."[^hs670812][^hs680419] Fall 1967 issues are unread. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
