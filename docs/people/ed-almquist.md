# Ed Almquist (1921–2015): Speed by Mail from Milford, Pennsylvania

*A rare non-California founder.*

- **Born** Nov 24, 1921, in Zim, Minnesota; **died** July 22, 2015, at home in Milford, PA.[^obit]
- Before the war he farmed celery and worked as a part-time railroad brakeman, raced "jalopies" on weekends, and took University of Minnesota extension courses in diesel engineering.[^hem]
- **U.S. Maritime Service** 1943–47 per his obituary.[^obit] He became a licensed marine engineering officer and wrote down his Minnesota racing hop-up knowledge while aboard ship.[^hem]
- **The manual:** he printed two small books on a borrowed mimeograph and advertised the *Speed and Mileage Manual* with a **$12 ad in Popular Mechanics**. He estimated more than 10,000 copies sold.[^hem] He is credited with the first widely circulated performance how-to, copyrighted **1946**.[^hem][^hof]
- **⚠ Conflict, founding year:** Hemmings and the Hall of Fame say Almquist Engineering started in 1946;[^hem][^hof] his obituary says he moved to Milford and created it in 1947.[^obit]
- **Early products** (Port Jervis *Times Herald-Record*, via search excerpt ◐): a water/alcohol vapor injector made from a **Mason jar** that cost 50¢ to make and sold for $6, and a two-carb "log manifold" for $12.95. He started in a $35 rented room, then a partitioned gymnasium.[^thr] The paper said that while California makers had the West Coast, Almquist equipment was the choice in the Midwest and East.[^thr]
- **Decline:** Hemmings says the mail-order business declined after the 1955 small-block Chevy, which "Ed wasn't ready for."[^hem]
- **Sparkomatic (1954),** with partner Jonas Anchel. A 1959 partnership with **George Hurst and Bill Campbell** on floor shifters dissolved, and the Times Herald-Record calls him co-designer of the Hurst shifter.[^thr] ◐ *Single local-press source; Hurst histories should be checked.*
- The obituary lists Almquist Engineering, Sparkomatic and **Offy Products** "through 1965," more than 100 products, and 7 U.S. patents. He later wrote *Hot Rod Pioneers*.[^obit]
- Received the International Drag Racing Hall of Fame Founder's Award in 2011.[^hof]

Ed Almquist also appears in period custom literature: a pamphlet from "Pennsylvania speed merchant Ed Almquist" taught restyling techniques alongside Dan Post's *Blue Book*.[^revs]
