# George Riley and the Two-Port / Four-Port Heads

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** secondary, forum/scrapbook · **Last verified:** 2026-10-12 (Round 1) · **Open issues:** WWII drone engine unverified · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

George Riley was an inventor who raced cars, planes and boats; Hot Rod credits him with the inboard/outboard boat engine configuration and says he opened his shop in **1919** to make Model T speed equipment.[^hr] ◐

## The design
Riley's conversions were **F-heads**: intake valves moved to the head, exhaust valves left in the block, unlike Miller heads with both valves overhead.[^hr] The **Two-Port** ("Valve-In-Head") was marketed from **1930**, advertised at 80 hp; foundry problems in the Depression left about half the castings worthless.[^hemm] The **Racing Four Port** followed in 1933,[^hemm] first seen in competition in **August 1932** per Hot Rod.[^hr] Riley claimed up to 160 hp at 4,500 rpm.[^moas] The heads suited short oval-track races more than long ones.[^cm]

## Forum knowledge (Ford Barn, first-hand owners) ◐
Owners on the Ford Barn forum report that:[^fb]

- Riley made "B," "C" and "D" two-ports (D low-compression for trucks); early two-ports had plugs on the driver's side and cracked.
- After 4-port production began, few if any 2-ports were made.
- Riley also built a radial outboard, a Model A-based two-cylinder, a racing side-draft carb, and a WWII drone engine that was "his monetary downfall."
- Aluminum 4-ports were made by **John Vesco** (San Diego) and later **George Butler** (San Bernardino).

These are unverified recollections and are marked for confirmation.

## Revival
Charlie Yapp's Secrets of Speed Society reproduced the Two-Port in cast iron from an original bought in 1998, with a redesigned water jacket.[^hemm]
