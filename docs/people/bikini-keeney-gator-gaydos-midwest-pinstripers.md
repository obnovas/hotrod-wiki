# If It Doesn't Move, I'll Pinstripe It: Bikini Keeney and Gator Gaydos, Two Midwest Brushes (Indianapolis and Taylor, Michigan, 1946–2016)

*A mother's Jeepster, a signature on a van, a club that made trophies out of cams, and a flamed urn*

## A Jeepster at 13

Most of the pinstripers in this archive so far are Californians. In the Midwest, two men's stories show the other side of the trade. They put a brush on whatever came in: race cars, vans by the hundred, pedal cars, mailboxes. What they made was their signature, sometimes literally.

Bob Keeney started on his mother's 1948 Jeepster, at 13. At Southport High School in Indianapolis he striped his schoolmates' cars.[^ssv] He graduated in 1957 and went to work hand-painting commercial advertising displays for Hamilton Displays until 1961, and then designing display racks at Indiana Wire for companies like John Deere, Coca-Cola, STP, Kodak and Procter & Gamble.[^ssv] He got his nickname by putting his first initial in front of his last name: B. Keeney, Bikini.[^ssv]

In the 1970s and '80s, *The Southsider Voice* wrote, he was well known at the Indianapolis Motor Speedway for the paint on cars for drivers such as Lloyd Ruby, Mark Donohue and Johnny Parsons Jr., and for team owner Roger Penske.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^ssv] At one point he was painting as many as 200 murals a year on custom vans and cars for a conversion company.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^ssv]

## The Wizard, Bikini Studios

One of those vans turns up in a club newsletter. In 2009 David Sanders wrote in the *Indy Mopar Club News* about growing up beside his father, who took him to the local custom van shows in a 1966 Dodge van. His father later bought a 1974 Tradesman 100 with an airbrushed paint job and custom carpet, and after a couple of years had it repainted at Bikini Studios in Acton, Indiana.[^imc] Sanders tracked down Keeney and talked with him about the work he and Harry Sering, the Wizard, had done in the '70s. What Sanders remembered was the signature on the van: "The Wizard Bikini Studios." His father died not long after.[^imc]

In 2016 Keeney was still working 40 hours a week out of Bikini Studios, restoring porcelain and metal antique signs and doing custom paint, including the designs on the trucks of Laura Kopetsky Tri-Ax. He often saw cars he had painted 30 years before, the *Voice* wrote, "with their artwork holding up nicely."[^ssv]

## Gator in Taylor

Mike Gaydos was born in Detroit on June 19, 1946, and started pinstriping bicycles as a teenager.[^nh] He worked in body and prototype shops when he was young, then opened his own automotive accessory shop in Taylor, the Kustom Shop. It fell on hard times and closed, and he went to work at Hollywood Auto in Dearborn until he retired. He built custom cars and motorcycles and drag raced at Detroit Dragway, until his children came along.[^nh]

He was one of the founders of the Taylor Area Rods and Customs, a club that put on benefit car shows for the Penrickton Center for Blind Children for 20 years. Its trophies were not the usual wood and chrome. They were often made from leftover parts, cams, cranks or whatever a member had lying around the garage.[^nh]

He flamed and pinstriped hundreds of pedal cars and mailboxes, and toilet seats, and once a pair of high heels. *CBS This Morning* featured him for his work on more than 200 pedal cars.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^nh] On the program he said a pedal car reminded him of a time "where things were a lot different," and of "riding down the hill at Underwoods and going as fast as I could in this little pedal car."[^nh] When his illness kept him from larger work, he kept striping smaller things in his home studio.[^nh]

He died on February 2, 2013. His ashes, the obituary said, were to go in an urn customized with flames and pinstriping by his family and friends.[^nh] He had said he would pinstripe anything that didn't move, and in the end his family and friends striped one more thing for him.
