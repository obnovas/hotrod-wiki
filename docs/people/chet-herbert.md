# Chet Herbert (1928–2009): Roller Cams from a Sears Lathe

Born March 1928 in Glenbar, Arizona, and raised in Los Angeles. His parents bought him a trumpet to curb his interest in speed; he traded it for a Cushman scooter.[^h]

- **Polio, 1948:** six months in an iron lung left him paralyzed from the chest down. He used the time to design parts and raced through hired drivers.[^h]
- **The Beast (1950):** a stripped Harley ran 121, then 129 mph at Santa Ana, beating every bike and four-wheeler there.[^h]
- **Roller cams:** Harley engines had used roller lifters since 1929. No SoCal grinder knew how to grind a roller cam, so Herbert bought a lathe at Sears and converted it. The Chevy six he built reached **275 hp**, more than the Offys it raced. That started Chet Herbert Cams.[^h]
- **Beast III:** fiberglass Bonneville streamliner designed with Caltech wind-tunnel consultant Rod Schapel. **Art Chrisman** drove it to 235.991 mph in 1952. See [Beast III](../cars/beast-iii.md).[^kr]
- **Drag racing:** pioneered twin inline V8 dragsters (1962) and is credited with the "zoomie" header.[^h]
- **Drag News:** in 1959 he funded his sister **Doris Herbert's** purchase of *Drag News*, which she built into "the drag racer's bible." Both were inducted into the International Drag Racing Hall of Fame in 1993.[^h]
- Died April 2009 at 81.[^h]
