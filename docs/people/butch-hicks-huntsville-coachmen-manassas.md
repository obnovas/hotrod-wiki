# The Mayor Said Yes: Butch Hicks, the Coachmen and Manassas Dragway (Huntsville and Harvest, Ala., 1956–2025)

*A Huntsville kid takes the city's first drag race to the mayor, then helps build the strip that outlasted him*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (Hicks's own milestones come from his family's obituary, the only source for most of them; his patents are on the federal record; the strip's later history is from racing papers) · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> Butch Hicks's family obituary in *Quad Cities Daily* (2025); two U.S. patents (1987, 1988); *Drag Illustrated* (2023) and *Sick the Magazine* on the strip; *Hot Rod* (1975) and NHRA (2014) on Shirl Greer's championship · **Last verified:** 2026-11-24 (Round 49) · **Open issues:** ◐ the street race and the mayor; ◐ who built Shirl Greer's 1974 championship Mustang · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

## A street, a club and a strip

Butch Hicks's story is short to tell and unusual in one way. By his family's account, Huntsville's first drag race was held in 1956 on a city street, Martin Road, with the mayor's approval, and the young man who asked for it was Hicks. Five years later he helped build the drag strip that is still racing outside town.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^qcd25]

Cecil Thomas Hicks was born in Huntsville on May 19, 1938, and finished Huntsville High School in 1955. He started early. At six he was "driving" from the floorboard of his grandfather's Plymouth. At ten his parents would not buy him a new bicycle for his paper route, so he built a car instead.[^qcd25]

After the Martin Road race, he co-founded the Coachmen Car Club in 1958 to bring the local car people together. He opened Moon & Hicks Performance, one of the area's first performance shops. In 1961 he played a pivotal role, his family wrote, in securing the land for Manassas Dragway in Harvest and in building it.[^qcd25]

## Manassas, Satellite, Madison County, Huntsville

The strip opened in 1961 at Turner Farms.[^di23] It started as Manassas Drag Strip. It was Satellite Drag Strip next, then Madison County Dragway, and then Huntsville Dragway, a name it kept for more than 40 years.[^sick24] It took an IHRA sanction in 1975. Scotty Cannon won Pro Mod at the IHRA's Bama Nitrous Nationals there in 1993. The strip held the first million-dollar race in 1996, the first $101,000-to-win Top Fuel purse in 2005, and the first Radial Fest in 2014.[^di23]

Hicks went on building. He set up a professional chassis shop in Huntsville that his family called nationally recognized, and held SEMA License #81. His family wrote that he built the 1974 NHRA Funny Car world championship Mustang that Shirl Greer drove, and many other cars, the Alabamian among them.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>[^qcd25] Greer clinched that 1974 title at the World Finals at Ontario after a fire in qualifying. He needed one more round win than Don Prudhomme, and he got it.[^nhra14][^hr75]

He was an inventor too. On August 11, 1987, Cecil T. Hicks of Huntsville was granted a design patent for a diaphragm pump. A second patent, issued October 18, 1988, covers a double-acting diaphragm pump: two pumping units, worked back and forth by a hydraulic cylinder set between them.[^pat87][^pat88] His family remembered them as his two hydraulic pumps.[^qcd25] He also took his daughters into quarter midget racing, and they went on to race Legends cars for Clabber Girl Racing and in NASCAR's Busch Series for Toyota.[^qcd25]

## The last car

At 73 Hicks had two strokes. His family wrote that they should have ended his time in the shop. Instead he got up before dawn each day and built what he called his dream car, a hot rod from a 1936 Chevy truck body, a Model A grille and a 1940 Dodge bed. Of the hundreds of cars he built, it meant the most.[^qcd25] He died on November 5, 2025, at 87, survived by Margaret Ann, his wife of 65 years.[^qcd25]

The strip he helped start had fallen on hard times before he died. David Sharp took over as its director in October 2023, signed it with the IHRA and renamed it Rocket City Dragway. It reopened on a Sunday, April 7, with an eighth-mile of concrete and a shutdown being stretched another 400 feet.[^di23][^sick24] "My mother brought me here when I was six weeks old to watch my dad race," Sharp said. "This is hallowed ground for me."[^di23] It started with a teenager asking permission to race on a city street, and the strip he helped build after that is still running.

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did the mayor approve a drag race on Martin Road in 1956?** Hicks's family obituary says that in 1956 "he hosted the very first Huntsville Drag Race on Martin Road, earning the mayor's approval to bring the event to city streets," and that he co-founded the Coachmen in 1958 and helped secure the land for Manassas Dragway in 1961.[^qcd25] None of the racing papers we read names who built the strip. The *Huntsville Times* of 1956 and 1961 would confirm the race, the mayor and the founders. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **Did Hicks build Shirl Greer's 1974 championship Mustang?** The obituary says he "built the 1974 NHRA Funny Car Chain Lightening World Championship Mustang driven by Shirl Greer."[^qcd25] We found no second source that names the 1974 car's builder. *Hot Rod* (July 1975) says Greer's new 1975 car, "Unchained Lightning," had a chassis from Jaime Sarte's Southern California shop, and that friends remade the old Mustang's body in aluminum at Ontario.[^hr75] NHRA's 2014 account says racers built a new aluminum rear section at Don Long's shop after the fire.[^nhra14] Neither says who built the original. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
