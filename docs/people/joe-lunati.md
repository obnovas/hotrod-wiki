# Joe Lunati: Eighth-Grade Math

*From the Memphis Rodders to Lunati Cams*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> family obituary (Memphis Funeral Home, 2023); small-town newspaper race ads and reports (*Sikeston Daily Standard*, 1966; *Monroe Morning World*, 1967); wire report of the 1966 Nationals; company obituary (Edelbrock Group, 2023); NHRA In Memoriam (2023) · **Last verified:** 2026-10-22 (Round 21) · **Open issues:** birthplace and year: Memphis, 1935 (family, company) vs. New Orleans, 1939 (NHRA) ⚠; cam shop founded 1968 (company, NHRA) vs. 1969 (family) ⚠; NHRA national titles: five (family, company) vs. three (NHRA) ⚠ · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "In a room full of engineers, he could have them all scratching their heads with, as he would put it, his '8th grade math'."

    — Joe Lunati's family obituary, Memphis Funeral Home, September 2023.[^obit]

Joe Lunati rode the city bus downtown as a kid to work at his Uncle Pete's grocery, sweated in the Kroger bakery, and raced with a Memphis car club. He ended up with his name on a line of camshafts. His family called him "the ultimate prankster": "when you saw that grin you knew something was up."[^obit]

## A Memphis kid

His family's obituary says Joe was born in Memphis on August 7, 1935, and attended Catholic High School, and that he "grew up from very humble beginnings."[^obit] The company that now owns his brand agrees on Memphis and 1935, and says he "began drag racing with the Memphis Rodders in the early '60s."[^edel] (For the club, which dates to 1947, see [Memphis](../places/memphis.md).)<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>

## Trouble Maker, Dixie Devil and a Corvair

The racing made his name. According to his family, his "home built and nationally known race cars, Trouble Maker and Dixie Devil," won him "5 NHRA national Champion titles, 9 class speed records, and 2 U.S. National Wins."[^obit] The company says he was a "five-time NHRA national champion title holder in AM/SP" who put himself on the map by winning Indy in 1964 in "his unique Devin drag car."[^edel]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>

The local papers show him on the road. On Easter Sunday 1966, a Sikeston, Mo., paper advertised "JOE LUNATI'S 1966 427 CORVAIR" against the Kingfish, a blown Barracuda, at the old air base strip in [Malden, Missouri](../drag-racing/semo-malden-drag-strip.md). Hundreds came. The Kingfish won the first run, and "Joe Lunati tore up his differential while warming up for the second race."[^sk660408][^sk660412]

Five months later he won on the biggest stage in the sport. The wire report from the NHRA's national championships at Raceway Park, Indianapolis, over Labor Day weekend 1966, printed that far away in a Marin County, Calif., daily, said: "Joe Lunati of Memphis, Tenn., captured the Street Eliminator in a dash against Richard Moroso, Old Greenwich, Conn. Lunati covered the distance in 10.244 and 133.53 to Moroso's 13.61 and 77.38."[^ij660906] The man in the other lane would start his own parts company two years later; see [Moroso](../companies/moroso.md). The final at Indy matched two future speed-equipment makers before either had a company.

By the next spring Lunati was a touring attraction in the Deep South. A May 1967 ad for Harmon Raceway at Monroe, La., billed a funny car match race: "Joe Lunati— 3 Time National Champ — VS — Lenard Hughes— Jakes Speed Shop."[^mm670513]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-6"><a href="#q-6" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> In June the same track's "Big One" put six "Super 'Funny Cars'" on the card, with Lunati in a "FLIP TOP CAMERO" alongside Gene Snow's Dodge "Rambunctous" and the Texas cars of Sam Harris and David Robinson.[^mm670624] (For Snow, see [Gene Snow](gene-snow.md).)

## The cam shop

Then came cams. By day he rebuilt production engines. In the evenings, the company says, he experimented "with different cam grinds using the shop's cam grinder and found his calling." "In 1968, Lunati made the leap to open his own cam shop," and by 1970 it was serving "weekend sportsman drag racers and local Memphis area circle track racers." Crankshafts, rods and pistons followed.[^edel]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> By the mid-1990s he sold the company to Holley. It went to COMP Performance Group in 2007, and in 2023 it became Edelbrock Group's sixth "power brand."[^edel] COMP, another Memphis cam maker, has its own Memphis roots; see [COMP Cams and RHS](../companies/comp-cams-rhs.md).

He never left the sport. He kept racing go-karts with his children and won a World Karting Association Mechanic of the Year award. He also kept going to the Memphis Rodders reunions "until his health declined."[^obit][^edel] He died in Germantown, Tenn., on September 13, 2023, at 88. His family's last word on him was local: "He was a cornerstone in the Memphis drag racing history."[^obit]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup><sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Memphis or New Orleans?** His family's obituary and Edelbrock say Memphis, 1935.[^obit][^edel] NHRA's 2023 In Memoriam says instead that he was "Born in New Orleans, Louisiana, in 1939" and moved to Memphis in the early 1960s.[^nhra] We go with the family's account, which the company's matches. A birth record or the 1940 census would settle it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Two, three or five national titles?** Edelbrock says five.[^edel] NHRA says "three-time." Its national records would show which count is right. A May 1967 Louisiana race ad also says "3 Time National Champ" (see below). The family's "2 U.S. National Wins" fits the company's 1964 Indy win plus the 1966 Street Eliminator win in the wire report.[^nhra][^mm670513][^ij660906] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did Lunati Cams start?** Edelbrock's account has the company serving racers by 1970;[^edel] his family dates it to 1969: "Joe founded Lunati Cams in 1969, later Lunati Crankshaft and Lunati Taylor Pistons."[^obit] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-6" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **How many titles by May 1967?** A promoter's ad, not a record book. Its "3 Time" matches NHRA's count as of May 1967; the family's five could include later titles. A 1968 book on funny cars, seen only as a search excerpt, adds that "Lunati continued at the top, capturing the Nationals Street Eliminator crown," which fits the 1966 wire report.[^cbfc] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-6" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Do the obituaries agree on the basics?** No: as the ⚠ marks above show, even the 2023 obituaries differ on his birthplace, his title count and the company's founding year.[^obit][^nhra][^edel] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
