# Howard Johansen: Lead on the Pistons, Twin Bears on the Strip

*Howard's Cams, c. 1910–1988*

Howard Johansen's first hop-up was raising the compression on his father's Model T "by adding lead to the top of the pistons. 'It worked... but the lead sure painted the inside of that T's tailpipe.'"[^hr89]

## From a Nebraska Model T to Los Angeles

Johansen was born in Nebraska. He moved to Los Angeles in 1941, and after the war he briefly raced roadsters with the California Roadster Association.[^wiki]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

## The business

The company's own history says he and his wife founded **Howards Power & Racing Equipment** in 1945 to make racing cams. They retired in 1965 and handed it to their oldest son Jerry, who ran it until 1985. Jerry and Don Madden ran the "Howards Cams Rattler" and "Howards Cams Special" Top Fuel cars "as test beds for new products." The company is now Howards Cams & Racing Components in Oshkosh, Wisconsin; it moved there in 1998.[^co]

Johansen did more than grind cams. He also built injectors, aluminum heads, forged aluminum rods and flywheels (Hot Rod, 1989).[^hr89]

The Inliners International "12 Port Story" says that by the end of 1952, Howard-equipped cars held 12 of 13 records at the Fontana, Santa Ana, Saugus and San Diego strips. In 1952 he also cast an aluminum wedge-chamber 12-port head for the GMC six, patterned on the 1953 Cadillac chamber.[^ilp] The same source says NHRA turned down his aircraft-style cable steering.[^ilp]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

## The lakes and the salt

He brought a **twin-tank streamliner** (engine in one tank, driver in the other) to the SCTA's first Bonneville meet in 1949. Hot Rod (Oct 1949) reported 147.54 mph on its first run, but "the car, which was rigidly sprung, failed to handle correctly on later runs and was withdrawn."[^tt] He raced it until 1953 and built a second twin-tank car in 1954.[^tt]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>

Hot Rod's obituary also lists a chopped '36 five-window streamliner with a Marmon V16 and an early '55 Chevy that took on the Oldsmobiles of Gene Adams and others.[^hr89]

## The Twin Bears

The obituary calls the **Twin-Bear** dragster, driven by Jack Chrisman, his "crowning achievement." It ran two blown small-blocks side by side with their flywheel ring gears meshed, one engine running backward, so a single driveshaft could be used. It "won Top Eliminator at the first NHRA Winternationals."[^hr89]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

## Dates

Hot Rod's "Finish Line" (Jan 1989) says he died aged 78.[^hr89] Earlier rounds cited IDRHOF's "1910–1988." The two are consistent but we have no exact dates yet. For his role in the Gasser Wars and the Doug Cook ad, see [Camgrinder Wars](../eras/1950s-camgrinder-wars.md).
