# Red Greth: Fiberglass Drapes, a B-29 Steering Wheel and Tucson's Speed Sport (1934–2021)

*A farm kid who moved west at 14, a roadster with a nose made of curtains, and a living room that was a flop house for racers headed to California*

## From Ohio to the cattle guards

Gary Lee "Red" Greth was born on July 3, 1934. The Pima County Sports Hall of Fame says on a farm in Centerville, Ohio; the *Arizona Daily Star* says Dayton. At 14 he moved to Tucson with his family.[^pcshf][^ads21]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> After school he worked at Miller Rasor Radio and Speedometer, where circle-track racers Hank Arnold and Roger McCluskey introduced him to high-performance cars. He crewed for McCluskey and for Bill Cheesbourg; McCluskey later raced the Indianapolis 500 seventeen times, Cheesbourg seven.[^pcshf]

At 16, with no strip in Tucson, he drove to Phoenix for organized drags and raced at night on the Mount Lemmon cutoff, three-eighths of a mile from one cattle guard to the next. He joined the Lords of Tucson while still in high school and raced dragsters, roadsters and funny cars.[^pcshf][^ads21] The full story of the cutoff races, Davis-Monthan and the Houghton Road strip is on the [Tucson page](../drag-racing/tucson-davis-monthan-speed-sport.md).

## Ol' Noisy

With childhood friend Don Maynard and Lyle Fisher he built the Speed Sport roadster in 1955: a 331-cubic-inch Chrysler Hemi with six carburetors on 100 percent nitro, under a '23 T body, on a chassis the trio built. Its nose was fiberglass; Ted Sitterley, a later partner, said the builders found "a trash can in an alley full of fiberglass drapery," palm trees and all, and that is what the nose was made of. The steering wheel came from a B-29 trainer. Eight exhaust pipes under the rear of the car earned it the nickname "Ol' Noisy"; its best was 9.10 seconds at 169.11 mph, a speed the hall of fame and the *Star* both treat as unequalled by any drag machine in 1957. It took top honors at the AHRA Nationals in [Great Bend, Kansas](../drag-racing/great-bend-srca.md), and in three years "it was never beaten in its class."[^ads21][^cf][^dslaz] Joe Bush's Tucson Speed Sport Automotive backed a long series of Speed Sport cars; Maynard later built motors for Chris Karamesines, including the Hemi that broke 200 mph at Alton, Illinois, in 1960.[^hof]

At Davis-Monthan he raced Emory Cook in 1956, ran 154.25 mph in 1958 in a match that Bob Huebner's flying-start jalopy won by a car length, and in the Speed Sport III set a 174.03 mph mark the *Star* dates to Nov. 19, 1961 (Hansen's column calls the same number a 1963 world record). The turbine-powered Speed Sport IV set the base strip's e.t. record of 8.75 seconds at 161.98 mph in 1962.[^ads21][^ads17][^dslaz]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions on the Tucson page">⚠</a></sup>

## A strip, a shop and a flop house

When Tucson Dragway opened at Valencia and Houghton in 1964, Fisher was among the owners and Greth helped run it. More than 6,000 came to the inaugural race; Jimmy Nix set a world record in a Dodge Charger, and a week later Fisher and Greth hit 193.66 mph in fuel eliminator. In November 1966 they booked Evel Knievel, then waiting in Tucson for a Grand Canyon jump permit, to jump ten cars loaned by Bill Breck Dodge at their car show.[^ads21]

Greth sold display ads for Tucson Newspapers Inc. for 47 years. His house was a flop house for racers headed west—"I would walk in the living room, stepping over sleeping bodies," his son Kenton said—and neighborhood kids learned to weld in his shop. "Every waking moment I had that I was not in school or doing homework, I was at that shop," said Dan Owens, who moved in next door at 6 in 1965. One of the young racers Greth mentored, Phil Minor, was killed in 1965 at 17 when he lost control at 160 mph. "That wounded dad for a long time," Kenton told the *Star*.[^ads21]

The Speed Sport team and the car went into the International Drag Racing Hall of Fame in 1994; Greth received an NHRA Lifetime Achievement Award in 2007. He restored the roadster in 1997 for the then-new NHRA Motorsports Museum at Pomona, and late in life lent it to Tucson Dragway's manager for track promotions. Owens told the paper Greth "was instrumental" in getting the present strip near the fairgrounds opened in 1997. Greth died of bladder cancer on April 13, 2021, at 86.[^ads21][^hof][^pcshf]
