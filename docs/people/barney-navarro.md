*1919–2007, Glendale's blower pioneer*

*Principal source: Tony Thacker's American Hot Rod Foundation biography, built on Navarro's recollections.*[^ahrf]

His '39 Tudor ran **the first Weiand intake Phil Weiand ever made**, and Navarro paid for it by machining Weiand's first batch of 10 castings.[^ahrf]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

He got his start at 10, as a gofer during the overhaul of his father's Willys-Knight. He attended Eagle Rock High and night machine-shop classes at Glendale High, and he once cut a straight-eight Locomobile in half for the Weaver brothers' midget.[^ahrf]

## Strokers and a last Muroc

In 1940 came the **Glendale Strokers** (<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> Kustomrama spells it "Stokers"; see [Tom Beatty](tom-beatty.md)): Navarro with Tom Beatty, George Hill and Doane Spencer.[^ahrf] At the last prewar Muroc meet, in November 1941, he ran 107 mph.[^ahrf]

## Business, on credit

In 1947 he went into business, with patterns by Hermann Husbey and castings on credit from Victor Caliva's Aircraft Foundry ("pay for them as he sold them").[^ahrf]

His **blown flathead** was advertised in the first issue of HOT ROD (1948). It was a de-stroked 176 ci '41 block with Winfield cam, Kurten ignition and a 3-71 GMC blower driven by four V-belts, with four Strombergs mounted back-to-back. It ran 139.75 mph at El Mirage, Sept 25–26, 1948.[^ahrf]

## The belly tank

With Tom Beatty, he built a 4-71-blown 295 ci Merc belly tank that set a Class D Lakester record of 185.80 mph at the 1951 Bonneville Nationals<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>, and 197.17 mph two-way in 1952.[^ahrf]

## Beyond the lakes

Navarro Engineering started at 718 Verdugo Road, then moved to 5142 San Fernando Road, Glendale. The shop built a Hemi boat for Henry Kaiser, a heart-lung machine used for 13 years at Kaiser Hospital in Hollywood, and concrete-cutting tools.[^ahrf]

He died **August 20, 2007**, his 88th birthday. His patterns went to Mike Herman of H&H Flatheads. His favorite saying: "There's nothing more fun than learning."[^ahrf]
