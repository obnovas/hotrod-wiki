# Barney Navarro (1919–2007): Glendale's Blower Pioneer

*Principal source: Tony Thacker's American Hot Rod Foundation biography, built on Navarro's recollections.*[^ahrf]

- **Origins:** at 10 he was a gofer during the overhaul of his father's Willys-Knight; attended Eagle Rock High and night machine-shop classes at Glendale High. He cut a straight-eight Locomobile in half for the Weaver brothers' midget.[^ahrf]
- **Glendale Strokers (1940)** (⚠ Kustomrama spells it "Stokers"; see [Tom Beatty](tom-beatty.md)): with Tom Beatty, George Hill and Doane Spencer. His '39 Tudor ran **the first Weiand intake Phil Weiand ever made**, paid for by Navarro machining Weiand's first batch of 10 castings.[^ahrf] ◐
- **Last prewar Muroc:** 107 mph, November 1941.[^ahrf]
- **Business (1947):** patterns by Hermann Husbey, castings on credit from Victor Caliva's Aircraft Foundry ("pay for them as he sold them").[^ahrf]
- **Blown flathead:** advertised in the first issue of HOT ROD (1948); a de-stroked 176 ci '41 block with Winfield cam, Kurten ignition and a 3-71 GMC blower driven by four V-belts, with four Strombergs mounted back-to-back. 139.75 mph at El Mirage, Sept 25–26, 1948.[^ahrf]
- **With Tom Beatty:** a 4-71-blown 295 ci Merc belly tank set a Class D Lakester record of 185.80 mph (⚠ RM Auctions text gives a 188.284 run) at the 1951 Bonneville Nationals, and 197.17 mph two-way in 1952.[^ahrf]
- **Later:** Navarro Engineering at 718 Verdugo Road, then 5142 San Fernando Road, Glendale; a Hemi boat for Henry Kaiser; a heart-lung machine used for 13 years at Kaiser Hospital in Hollywood; concrete-cutting tools.[^ahrf]
- Died **August 20, 2007**, his 88th birthday. Patterns went to Mike Herman of H&H Flatheads. His favorite saying: "There's nothing more fun than learning."[^ahrf]

[^ahrf]: Tony Thacker, "Barney Navarro," American Hot Rod Foundation. https://ahrf.com/pioneer/barney-navarro/
