# Arnie "Woo Woo" Ginsburg: the Night Train, the Ginsburger and a Quarter Mile on Route 1 (1926–2020)

*A Brookline kid with bells and a train whistle, a burger served on a 45, and the radio that filled a Saugus parking lot*

## Night Train

Arnie Ginsburg graduated from Brookline High School in 1944 and began professional radio in 1956 as a disc jockey at WBOS 1600 AM, building a Top 40 show that made him a force in Boston's pop and rock scene. In 1958 he moved to WMEX, where the train whistle on his "Night Train" show earned him the "Woo Woo" nickname; bells, whistles and sound effects were the trademark. The Massachusetts Broadcasters Hall of Fame, which inducted him in 2008, called him the most popular Boston disc jockey of the 1950s and '60s and noted that he even put humor in the commercials—"singing along with the Adventure Car Hop jingle" or working his record hops at the Surf Ballroom at Nantasket Beach.[^mbhof][^wbz]

He later had a brief on-air stint at WRKO in 1967, then shifted into sales there, became general manager of WBCN in 1970 and of WWEL in 1972, hosted a Saturday-night oldies show on WBZ from 1973, partnered in Kiss 108 FM in 1979, and with John Garabedian co-owned V-66, Boston's video music channel, in 1985.[^mmone][^wbz] He retired in 1986 and lived in Ogunquit, Maine, with his longtime partner, Carlos Alberto Vega. He died on June 26, 2020, at 93, at Vega's home in Framingham; he would have turned 94 on August 5.[^wbz][^ri]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see the bottom of the page">◐</a></sup>

Vega's notice said Ginsburg introduced the Beatles at both their Boston concerts and the Rolling Stones at their first New England show.[^wbz]

## The Ginsburger and the overpass

What ties him to this wiki is the parking lot. His Night Train ads sent listeners to Adventure Car Hop on Route 1 in Saugus, which served a "Ginsburger" on a 45 rpm record; on certain nights, anyone who said "Woo Woo Ginsburg" got a second one free. A hometown blogger wrote that on a summer night Arnie could draw a couple of thousand teenagers to the place. From the lot's exit to the first overpass was almost exactly a quarter mile, and that is where the hot rods raced. Full story: [Adventure Car Hop, Route 1](../places/route-1-saugus-adventure-car-hop.md).[^saugus]

The nearest sanctioned strips for that crowd were up the road at [Sanford, Maine](../drag-racing/sanford-cain-bannister.md) and, later, the club-built [New England Dragway in Epping](../drag-racing/new-england-dragway-epping.md). The radio made the street course famous first.
