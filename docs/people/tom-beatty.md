# Tom Beatty and the Glendale Strokers

## The club
Barney Navarro joined the Glendale club in 1940 along with Tom Beatty, George Hill and Doane Spencer.[^ahrf] **⚠ Name conflict:** AHRF writes "Glendale **Strokers**";[^ahrf] Kustomrama's club page is titled "Glendale **Stokers**." Kustomrama lists members including Beatty, Navarro, Spencer, Hill, Don Nicholson, Mac Schutt, Bob "Buzz" Buswell, Bill Davis and the Watson brothers.[^club] The spelling needs a period source, such as an SCTA entry list or a club plaque photo.

## The "Rust Bucket"
Beatty's channeled 1929 Ford roadster was unpainted and rusty, looking "gutted by a fire" (Hop Up, August 1951). It was clocked at 154.63 mph in 1951 with a '41 block, Navarro heads and manifold, and a GMC two-stroke diesel blower. It used an upside-down Model A front suspension, and the steering wheel was stripped to the wire core and wrapped in friction tape.[^rust] ◐ AHRF instead calls Beatty's Navarro-blown car a "'27."[^ahrf]

## The belly tank
- Navarro: "Tom lived next door to my shop and had a covered car port where he could do the welding and assembly work."[^ahrf]
- Beatty built a ¾-in. chrome-moly truss chassis with swing-axle independent rear suspension, a modified Ford gearbox, and an inverted Model A front axle. Hop Up (Dec 1951) wrote: "Finally an original tank has come along."[^tank]
- **⚠ Conflict, 1951 Bonneville:** AHRF gives a Class D Lakester *record* of 185.80 mph;[^ahrf] the RM Auctions text on Kustomrama gives a 188.284 mph *run*.[^tank] These may be a two-way record and a one-way best, which is unconfirmed.
- 1952: 203 mph one-way and a 197.17 mph two-way record per AHRF;[^ahrf] 203.61 one-way per RM. It was Hot Rod's "Hot Rod of the Month" (Nov 1952) with a Rex Burnett cutaway.[^tank]
- RM says it was SCTA Top Speed of the Season five straight years (1951–55), and again in 1959. It reached 211.267 mph in 1955 (the 16th member of the 200 MPH Club), switched to Olds power in 1958, and ran a 243.438 mph average in 1962.[^tank] ◐ *Auction-catalog source; check against SCTA/BNI records.*
- He opened **Tom Beatty Automotive Engineering**, Sun Valley, in 1958, selling 6-71 blower manifolds and multi-belt drives.[^tank]
- **Unresolved:** the RM text says Beatty "died later that year" after the tank was sold, but the sale date is unclear. **Death date unverified.**
