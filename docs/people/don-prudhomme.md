# Don Prudhomme: The Snake, from Van Nuys Body Shops to the Hawaiian and Hot Wheels (1941– )

*A painter who dropped out of Van Nuys High, a nickname he first hated, and a toy-company rivalry that put Funny Cars in living rooms*

## Van Nuys Boulevard

Don Prudhomme was born in Los Angeles in 1941. His parents, Newman and Ida, had moved from Louisiana in 1938; his father worked body shops, the one the son remembered most being Ray Brooks on Van Nuys Boulevard near Saint Elizabeth School. Don delivered papers, mowed lawns and worked on a nearby ranch to buy a minibike at 14. He dropped out of Van Nuys High before the 11th grade to paint cars. "I wanted to get out and make a living because I didn't have any money," he told the *Los Angeles Times* in 2007, which called leaving school his greatest regret. "I'm not proud of it, but I've done OK."[^hr06][^lat07][^hemm13]

He and Tom McCourry, his best friend since they were 12, raced junkers that Tom's dad parked behind a motel around its parking lot, went to the Rainbow Roller Rink, and cruised Van Nuys from Carl's Drive-In at one end to Bob's Big Boy at the other, "back and forth pretty much the entire night." Through Skip Togerson, a part-time actor who had brought Tommy Ivo into the Burbank Road Kings, Prudhomme found the club. The initiation was at the Bob's in Toluca Lake. "Prudhomme was in diapers and I was dressed as a woman," Bob Muravez remembered: Prudhomme pulled Muravez through the drive-in in a rickshaw a member's father had brought home from Japan, and "Ivo met us there and had a baby bottle that he tied around Prudhomme's neck."[^hr06]

Downtime went to the San Fernando and Saugus strips with Tommy Ivo. He bought a Buick-powered rail from Ivo, then moved up to a blown, fuel-burning Chrysler built with Dave Zeuschel; by the fall of 1961 he was campaigning a Kent Fuller chassis with Zeuschel. In March 1962, at 20, he won Top Fuel at the Smokers' March Meet at Famoso, from about 90 entrants, beating Ted Gotelli and Bob Leasher in the final. *Drag News* gave him the meet's top speed, 185.36 mph. Soon afterward Keith Black needed a driver for a car financed by Tommy Greer.[^nhra10][^hemm13][^hr06][^lat07]

## Greer-Black-Prudhomme

From its June 1962 debut win at Pomona through the end of 1964, the Greer-Black-Prudhomme dragster dominated Southern California Top Fuel. A Schiefer Clutch advertisement claimed a 236–7 win-loss record; NHRA's later count is closer to 81–8—still a terror whenever the car lost.[^nhra10][^book20]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> Black's combination smoked the tires less than most and stayed tunable. Crewmember Joel Purcell started calling him "Snake" because of the way he left the starting line. Prudhomme didn't like it at first. "I always had trouble with my last name. People couldn't pronounce it," he wrote later. "When I started becoming Snake, it was so easy."[^nhra10][^book20]

Greer's business went bankrupt; Black would not finance the car alone. About the same time Roland Leong crashed on his first Top Fuel pass at Lions and needed a driver. Black suggested Prudhomme. Prudhomme had met Leong in Hawaii, when the Greer-Black car was shipped over for a match race.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see the bottom of the page">⚠</a></sup> They debuted at the 1965 Winternationals, with Prudhomme two months short of 24 and Leong just 20, and won. A few months later they won the Nationals at Indianapolis. Of that summer's tour Prudhomme told Robert C. Post, as quoted in Post's book *High Performance*, "We were killers. We toured for four months and got beat only twice." He left after 1965 to prove the driving was his.[^nhra10][^hemm13][^book20][^leong]

## Snake and Mongoose

Tom McEwen took the "Mongoose" name—an animal that kills snakes—on Ed Donovan's suggestion. McEwen's mother, Sybil, was a secretary at Mattel, and his stepfather, Joe Ball, was an attorney whose clients included Mattel; that got them a meeting with vice president Art Spear. Prudhomme was skeptical. The deal put Hot Wheels money into both camps and pushed them into Funny Cars because Mattel sold toy cars, not rails. Kids staged Snake-versus-Mongoose races on orange tracks in living rooms; match races filled the rest of the calendar. It was not the first non-racing sponsor in the sport, NHRA notes, but it was the largest marketing plan the class had seen.[^nhra10][^hemm13]

In 1973 John Buttera built him a Barracuda; Carefree Sugarless Gum took the main sponsorship. At Indy that year Prudhomme beat Ed McCulloch and became the first driver with national-event wins in both Top Fuel and Funny Car. From 1975 through 1978, in the Army Monza and then a Plymouth Arrow, he won four straight Funny Car championships. In 1976 he took seven of eight national events; a 30-round win streak ended only in the U.S. Nationals final against Gary Burgin. In 1982 the Pepsi Challenger Firebird was the first Funny Car over 250 mph, and at Indy he ran a 5.637 that would have qualified fifth in Top Fuel.[^nhra10]

He returned to Top Fuel in 1990, called 1994 the Final Strike tour, and retired with 49 driver wins. As an owner through 2009, Larry Dixon and Ron Capps carried the Snake name to more Wallys and championships—112 event wins and six world championships by NHRA's team count. He was No. 3 on NHRA's Top 50 Drivers list in 2000.[^nhra10][^hemm13]

The Hawaii friendship that started the national career is told in full on [Roland Leong](roland-leong.md). The other Don of that generation is on [Don Garlits](don-garlits.md).

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **When did Prudhomme meet Leong: 1963 or 1964?** NHRA's "The 10" and Prudhomme's book put the Greer-Black-Prudhomme Hawaii match race—and the meeting—in 1963. The *Honolulu Star-Advertiser* ties the meeting to Hawaii Raceway Park's opening, which it dates 1963 and which other sources dispute as 1962–64. Full both-sides: [Leong Open questions q-1](roland-leong.md#q-1). <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **How many wins did Greer-Black-Prudhomme really have?** NHRA's Phil Burgess (2023): "While a famous Schiefer Clutch advertisement claimed that Prudhomme racked up a stunning 236-7 win-loss record in that time, the real number is closer to 81-8," for June 1962 through 1964. Dick Martin in *Rod & Custom* (2013) repeats the big number as fact and dates it differently: from June 1962 to May 1963 the car "won 236 drag races and lost just seven, a record that still stands today." Neither shows the count.[^nhra10][^hr06] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
