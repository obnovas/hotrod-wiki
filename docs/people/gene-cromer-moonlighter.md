# Named for the Nights in the Shop: Gene Cromer's Moonlighter and the Upstate Gassers (1960–2021)

*A Willys finished too late to be lettered, an Air Force base that held one drag race, a friend who kept the scrapbook, and a gasser that went back to the strip after 45 years in a shed*

## A Ford man's Willys

Gene Cromer came to drag racing from motorcycles, about 1960, with a Lincoln 368-powered rail. Next came a '55 Chevy like everybody else's, except that his had a Ford engine, and he remembered that it "broke a lot." In 1965 he built a Ford-powered Willys coupe and, in Marvin T. Smith's words, "terrorized A/G class." Smith, a Valdosta State sociologist who wrote up Georgia's racers, noted that Cromer "built all his own cars."[^gdr]

The name came from home. Melvin Craft started hanging around Gene's Speed Shop in the evenings and on weekends in 1964, when the Willys was being built, and became Cromer's right-hand man between rounds from 1965 to 1967. "All those nights in the shop explains why Gene's wife named the car 'The Moonlighter,'" *Hot Rod* wrote in 2016.[^hr16]

## One race at Donaldson

Its first race was an NHRA meet in Greenville in 1965, at Donaldson Air Force Base. The car was so fresh it had no lettering yet, and it won A/Gas.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^hr16][^dslsc] Cromer's friend Frank Taylor, who has the scrapbook, calls it "the only race there," and has the ad from *Drag Speed Illustrated* for May 15–16, 1965, a photo of Cromer leaning on his '58 Ranchero tow car, and one of the Willys outside his shop.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>[^dslsc] A photo posted on *RacersReunion* of a '65 Plymouth from Richmond, Virginia, is captioned "5-18-65 Donaldson AFB."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^rr10]

Rex O'Steen went that summer Saturday to watch Ken Cothran's yellow '65 Chevelle Malibu SS 327 four-speed. "My father had a Chevrolet dealership in downtown Greenville and Ken was a 'wild' parts salesman there."[^dslsc]

## The upstate's Saturday nights

Cromer ran a lot at Starlite 25, the strip everyone called Ware Shoals. Taylor wrote that it started as dirt in the '50s and was a paved quarter mile by the mid-'60s; in 2013 he found an old Starlite 25 Dragstrip truck in the woods at the end of the strip. Tommy Hill's father didn't like running there "because the shutdown was downhill!"[^dslsc] Cromer's scrapbook has the other upstate gassers too: Ed Skelton of Anderson in his Mustang, and David "Dougal" McDowell of Belton in the "Little Lemon" Henry J.[^dslsc]

In Georgia, Cromer match-raced the Willys gassers of Bunky Bobo, Fred Hurst, Lamar Walden and Bogan Renfroe. He built the headers for Renfroe's SOHC "Cat Skinner" Willys, and Renfroe called him his mentor. He ran A/Gas at the NHRA Nationals in 1965 and 1967. When the Willys went to nitro and kept wheelstanding, he took Skelton's advice and swapped the four-speed for a C-6 automatic, and the first time out it stood up again. At Calhoun, he told Smith, he "went through the lights backwards!"[^gdr] His '66 Mustang funny car roadster won the Atlanta $10,000 meet in 1967 over Huston Platt, and an AHRA national event in Competition Eliminator at Rockingham as a B/Fuel Altered. A Pro Stock Maverick followed, but with two daughters in school it cost too much, and after about 1972 he never got back to it.[^gdr]

## Forty-five years in a shed

Cromer kept the Willys. In 2012 he was grand marshal at the first Southeast Gassers race in Greer, and the next year he was back behind the wheel of the restored car, which had been stored in a shed in his backyard.[^cp21] Quain Stott, who first saw the Moonlighter around 1967 and went on to race Pro Mod, rebuilt the chassis to hide a modern cage; Craft and Cromer found the original parts and built the engine. The same Hilborn injection was still on it. By 2016 Cromer was touring eight to ten races a year with his wife Ruth, his daughters Julie and Robin and their families, and at 84 he had run a 6.10 in the eighth mile.[^hr16]

He was hurt in an accident away from the track on June 18, 2021, and died on July 14.[^cp21] On the *Drag Strip List*, Renee Whysong, who went from Ware Shoals' powder puff class to a dragster in the 1990s, summed up what the upstate strips had been: "We racers were like family, always ready to help each other."[^dslsc]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **A 1940 or a 1941 Willys?** *Hot Rod*'s 2016 story on the restored car calls it a 1941 Willys coupe; Frank Taylor's captions from Cromer's scrapbook call it a "1940 Willys A Gasser."[^hr16][^dslsc] Smith's profile says only "Willys coupe."[^gdr] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **May 15–16 or May 18?** The ad in Taylor's scrapbook gives May 15–16, 1965; the *RacersReunion* caption, posted in 2010, dates a photo "5-18-65."[^dslsc][^rr10] A Tuesday race on the 18th seems unlikely; the caption may give a processing date. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-3" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Open question.** **Was it really Donaldson's only drag race?** Taylor says so, and O'Steen remembered attending "one race."[^dslsc] We have not seen the base newspaper or the *Greenville News* for 1965 to confirm that there were no others, or who organized it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-3" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
