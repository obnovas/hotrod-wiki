# George DuVall (1913–1999): Plating Shop Designer, the V-Windshield, and the Hollywood Hubcap

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** secondary (census-based), oral · **Last verified:** 2026-10-13 (Round 3) · **Open issues:** Pattern sale date conflict · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

*Principal source: Kustomrama, drawing mainly on* Rod & Custom*, August 1990.*[^k] ◐

- **Dates (Round 3):** George H. DuVall Jr., born Aug 19, 1913, Los Angeles; died Feb 12, 1999, in Peggs, Oklahoma. His 1931 Hollywood High yearbook entry reads "Auto Body Designer 'Duv' Cal Tech."[^cb] In 1990 Dean Batchelor interviewed him for R&C ("Flying V's").[^cb] See also [Julian Doty](julian-doty.md) and [So. Cal. Plating](../companies/southern-california-plating.md).
- Graduated from Hollywood High in 1931 and studied mechanical engineering at UCLA. He saw a newspaper photo of a Southern California Plating Co. truck with the first Goodyear balloon tires sold in LA, and took a part-time delivery job there.[^k]
- Owner **Leonard DeBell** saw DuVall's work on his own cars and moved him to design: custom bumpers and grilles, mostly for Depression-era Fords, influenced by Harley Earl, all chrome-plated. DuVall left UCLA for So. Cal. Plating full time in 1933.[^k]
- DeBell believed in self-promotion, so the company trucks were always customized. The **1935 Ford phaeton delivery** (designed late 1935 with **Frank Kurtis**, finished 1936) was stretched 12 in. and had a Cord-inspired brass grille, hidden headlights and Sea Foam Green paint by Charles Remidi. It was the first vehicle with a **DuVall windshield**.[^k] A grille on an earlier So. Cal. Plating truck is said to have inspired the Hudson Terraplane's. ◐ *Unattributed claim.*[^k]
- The windshield was a five-piece bronze casting for 1935–46 Ford cowls, built by So. Cal. Plating, which held the pattern rights. Its first known hot rod use was Jimmy Summers fitting one to Jack Dorn's '32 roadster in the early 1940s.[^k][^summers]
- **⚠** In 1946 (Kustomrama) or "the late 1940s" (Coachbuilt), DuVall's nephew **Julian Doty** bought the windshield patterns, along with a DuVall-designed aluminum OHV head for the Willys Jeep.[^k]
- **Hollywood Hubcap:** called the first mass-produced aftermarket custom hubcap. Per Albert Drake, the first version had an S stamped in relief that appeared to spin, and a later one used a chromed bar.[^k]
- Xydias: "Jimmy Summers and George DuVall were two of the best-known guys before the war."[^summers]
