# Julian DuVall Doty (1922–2012): Seventy Years on the Lakes

*Sources: Coachbuilt.com (census and directory research) and an AHRF profile built on Doty's recollections.*[^cb][^ahrf]

- Born **December 10, 1922**; died **May 2, 2012**. His mother Evelyn was George DuVall's sister.[^cb]
- In 1934 he made his first trip to Muroc with his uncle George, in George's Cragar-powered '29 Model A roadster.[^ahrf]
- **⚠ Founding-era claims:** Coachbuilt says DuVall and Doty were **charter members of the Hollywood Throttlers** and "were present when the SCTA was founded in November of 1937," and that Doty was a founding member of the **Sidewinders**.[^cb] Doty would have been about 14 at the founding. ◐ *Single-sourced; AHRF does not repeat it.* AHRF places the Hollywood Throttlers clubhouse next door to the original B&S garage.[^ahrf]
- He bought a Miller-Schofield head for $120 at $5 a week. In 1940 his '31 Model A roadster topped 101 mph at Muroc, and he drove it on the street until 1972 (90,000 miles).[^ahrf]
- **B&S garage** (Baldwin & Sommerfeld), behind a Seaside station at Santa Monica & Spalding, built the Danny Sakai modified. It moved to Genesee Street in 1946 and ran until 1976.[^ahrf]
- He wrote to *S.C.T.A. News* from Germany in 1945 (Vol. II, No. 14): "I hope to have a car running next year."[^ahrf] This is a direct example of the wartime letters described on the [Veda Orr](veda-orr.md) page.
- **Stolen-signal starting lights:** as SCTA starters in 1947–48, Doty and Sidewinders president Kong Jackson "stole some traffic signals, set them up and voila! more efficient starting."[^ahrf] ◐
- In 1948 he crewed at Indy, helping Ed Winfield and Jean Marcenac with the Novis. On the way home, Ford men stopped them at a Detroit diner and showed them the unreleased flathead six on the dyno.[^ahrf]
- **The windshield patterns:** **⚠ Conflict.** Kustomrama says he bought them from So. Cal. Plating in **1946**;[^kr] Coachbuilt says "in the late 1940s." Coachbuilt quotes his ad: "Original DuVall V-Windshields For Any Car - $17.50, 6211 Banner Ave., Hollywood."[^cb]
- Late 1940s: did machine work at **Pacific Metal Products** (his uncle's employer) on intakes for Eddie Meyer, Tattersfield and Edmonds, plus the brightwork on the Muntz Jet.[^ahrf]
- The B&S wing-tank lakester won the SCTA points championship in 1956 and 1958. Its Tempest four used a DOHC head sketched on napkins by **Leo Goossen** and Arnold Birner at Edwards Steak House. Only five were cast. It set the F/L record of 165.13 mph in 1961.[^ahrf]
- From 1972 he made and signed official SCTA timing slips. Mike Cook: "if your time slip doesn't have Julian's autograph on it, it's not worth anything."[^ahrf]
