# Charles "Kong" Jackson: Sidewinders President and Ignition Maker

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** oral (Medley 1988 via Fuel Curve), contemporaneous list post · **Last verified:** 2026-10-13 (Round 4) · **Open issues:** Death year conflict (2000 vs 2002) · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

## Before the SCTA
Fuel Curve (2023) quotes Jackson from Tom Medley's interview in Tex Smith's *Hot Rod History*: "Before the Road Runners, Ninety Mile an Hour, Sidewinders, and Throttlers formed the SCTA in 1938, guys were getting killed at the lakes, running into each other. No organization. No nuthin'."[^fc] **⚠** Jackson's "1938" disagrees with the usual late-1937 founding date (see [Dry Lakes & the SCTA](../eras/1930s-dry-lakes-scta.md)). It is a recollection fifty years on.

- **Muroc, 1941:** "Here came a Jeep and out jumped these guys with bayonets. Man, we just split!" The Army Air Corps camp was downwind of the racers' dust. Jackson told Medley that about 90 percent of the men racing that day were in the service within two months.[^fc]
- **⚠ Chronology muddle:** Fuel Curve has him joining the Army as an Air Force mechanic, stationed at Mojave, *and then* back at the lakes by 1938 in a '32 highboy.[^fc] The order of events needs checking.

## Kong ignition
After the war he built a flathead distributor with dual high-speed points and a four-lobe breaker cam "ground by... Ed Winfield." It had a cable control so the driver could advance or retard the timing from the seat. Fuel Curve says Clay Smith used this feature on the Lincoln team in the 1953 Mexican Road Race.[^fc] ◐
Alex Xydias, quoted by Fuel Curve: "Kong was a really, really smart guy, who was president of the Sidewinders car club and timer at the lakes... I sold them at So-Cal Speed Shop. Think we charged 49 bucks." Xydias bought the whole business when Jackson was recalled for Korea.[^fc] See [Alex Xydias](alex-xydias.md). In later years **Kong Engineering** made flathead aluminum heads and intakes for the revival.[^fc][^ls]

## Death: ⚠ conflict
- A land-speed mailing-list post of **Sept 10, 2000**, by Katie Young Cortez, says he "passed away Monday (9/4)." She describes the funeral her father attended and says he left 8 children, 14 grandchildren and 2 great-grandchildren. It adds that he "began racing in 1938, I believe" and served in WWII and Korea.[^ls]
- Fuel Curve (2023) says he was at El Mirage "until his death in **2002**."[^fc]

The contemporaneous post (Sept 4, 2000) is the stronger evidence, and we treat 2000 as the working date. An obituary is still needed.
