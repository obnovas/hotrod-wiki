- Born **March 22, 1922**, Los Angeles; first hot rod a '29 roadster driven to Fairfax High.[^yum] Hung around Jimmy Summers' shop across the street ([see Summers](jimmy-summers.md)).[^sm]
- Entered the Army Air Corps in 1942. On furlough he watched San Fernando Valley street races: "I was really surprised at how fast the cars ran and I got the idea to open a speed shop."[^yum]
- He liked the name of a speed shop in Culver City (very likely Orr's, ◐ inference, not stated) and coined "So-Cal": "then I was the only one."[^css]
- Opened on Olive Avenue, Burbank, on his discharge day, **March 3, 1946**, with borrowed money.[^yum] "If I had 'speed shop' in the name I had to start to race."[^css]
- V8-60 belly tank lakester: 136 mph in 1948, cover of Hot Rod. A Batchelor-designed streamliner topped 210 mph in 1952. Closed the shop in 1961 as OHV engines overtook flatheads.[^fc]
- Later produced films, edited Car Craft and Hot Rod Industry News, helped start the SEMA Show, and revived So-Cal with Pete Chapouris after Bruce Meyer and Chapouris restored the belly tank.[^fc][^css]
