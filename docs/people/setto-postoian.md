# Setto Postoian and the Swamp Rat Insult

*Serop Postoian, 1922–1995: Detroit's first drag hero*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** <span class="hr-badge">Grassroots</span> small-town daily (*Dixon Evening Telegraph*, Dixon, Ill., 1958 and 1964 World Series previews); *Car Craft* (Oct. 1959); Michigan Motor Sports Hall of Fame biography; Autoweek; BangShift; search excerpts from two lending-library books ◐ · **Last verified:** 2026-10-22 (Round 21) · **Open issues:** the "Riverside Nationals" claim (Hall of Fame only) ◐; which engine won at Cordova ⚠; which two years he won (1956–57 or 1957–58) ⚠; the size of the 1957 field ⚠; Isky cams and M&H tire tests rest on search excerpts ◐ · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

!!! quote "In their own words"
    "The flag dropped. Garlits stomped on the throttle the same instant Postoian's foot shoved forward. The two cars roared from the line, nose to nose."

    — *Car Craft*, October 1959, on a Cordova final that Garlits lost when his clutch let go.[^cc5910]

The two cars left the line together at Cordova, Ill., and for three-quarters of the quarter-mile the Florida car led. Then, as *Car Craft* told it in 1959, "there was a quick, explosive sound. Postoian shot ahead. Garlits slowed. The second clutch, together with the lightweight aluminum flywheel had disintegrated." Don Garlits and his brother had replaced one clutch already that day, "feverishly," to make the call.[^cc5910] The man in the other lane was a Detroit speed shop owner named Serop "Setto" Postoian, and he was the reason a young Garlits was still having to prove himself.

## From Ford's engine shop to the Farmington Speed Shop

Before the Second World War, the Michigan Motor Sports Hall of Fame says, Postoian worked in Ford's engine-rebuilding division in Detroit. He served three and a half years overseas and came home to open a gas station in Farmington. He learned frame and front-end work from a former Bear Manufacturing school instructor, which led to an Indy pit-crew job and his first ride in a hot rod, Jack McGrath's "full-house" '32 roadster. In 1950 he spent six weeks at McGrath's Los Angeles shop and met Joe Pisano and Ed Iskenderian.[^mm] Back home he opened the **Farmington Speed Shop** and built a flathead roadster, then a DeSoto Hemi dragster, his first overhead-valve engine.[^mm]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

The California trip left a parts connection. In a 2017 book, seen by us only as a search excerpt, Iskenderian recalls that "Big John ran my cams, as did Seto Postoian."[^isky]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> Garlits later told NHRA that Iskenderian made two blower kits, "one for me and one for Setto Postoian."[^nhra] A 1960 hot rod annual, also seen only as an excerpt, says that "M&H's first attempt at dragster tires began with test runs by Don Garlits and Setto Postoian."[^mh]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> A Midwestern racer was testing the parts that eastern and western companies would sell to everyone.

## Cordova and a savings bond

The race that made him was the World Series of Drag Racing, which moved from an old airfield at Lawrenceville to Cordova, Ill., in 1957.[^dx640813] (For the track and its founder, see [Bob Bartel](bob-bartel.md).)

Online accounts have Postoian beating Garlits in the final in both 1957 and 1958; the full story of the 1957 meet, where Emery Cook helped the unknown Floridian with his fuel system, is told in [The Upset at Cordova](../drag-racing/detroit-cordova.md#1957-the-garlits-upset).[^bs]

The small-town paper up the road at Dixon covered it as a hometown event. In June 1958 the *Dixon Evening Telegraph* promised more than 500 hot rodders for that August's series and named the man to beat: "Serop Postoian, Detroit engineer who has won top eliminator honors the last two years in his Chrysler-powered belly-tank dragster, will return to defend his title."[^dx580628]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup> The same story noted that Garlits had been clocked at 176 miles an hour that spring "and is claiming that mark as a world record."[^dx580628] That Florida run, *Car Craft* said, set off a furor ("There must be something phony about the clocks"), and Garlits toured the Midwest to prove it. Cordova was where Postoian beat him.[^cc5910]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-5"><a href="#q-5" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>

Six years later the same paper looked back. "Serop Postoian of Detroit won top eliminator prize in that 1957 World Series, and repeated in 1958," it said. The prizes had been small: "Postoian won a $500 Savings Bond in 1957, and awards to most other class champions were nominal." In 1957, it said, "slightly more than 300 machines participated"; by 1964 the purse was $14,785.[^dx640813]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-6"><a href="#q-6" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>

## The insult, and the end

Postoian went west too. In the spring of 1959 *Car Craft* found him at a Central California meet, where "Ernie Hashim had run, clocked 161, and lost to Setto Postoian."[^cc5910] That year he placed a *Drag News* ad calling Garlits "a swamp rat." Garlits took the insult as the name of all his cars.[^aw] The Hall of Fame calls Postoian an early idol of the teenage Garlits.[^mm]

It ended suddenly. Postoian was seriously injured at Detroit Dragway in 1960 and never raced again. The Hall of Fame credits him with about 250 eliminator wins and inducted him in 1997.[^mm] The Swamp Rats kept running for decades. The name came from a Detroit speed shop owner who had beaten Garlits for a $500 savings bond.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-7"><a href="#q-7" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

<a id="q-4" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** *Which engine? The Hall of Fame says he won in 1957 and 1958 "after switching to a 392 cubic inch Chrysler."[^mm] BangShift describes the car that beat Garlits as an "unblown, carbureted 359-inch 354 Chrysler Hemi-powered nitro dragster."[^bs] The 1958 Dixon paper says only "Chrysler-powered belly-tank dragster." And which years? Read literally in June 1958, "the last two years" means 1956 and 1957, while the 1964 story, the Hall of Fame and BangShift all say 1957 and 1958. The first three World Series were held at Lawrenceville, Ill., per the 1964 story.*[^dx580628][^dx640813] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-4" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-5" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** *Car Craft doesn't date the race. It places the Cordova loss "early in the tour" after the Brooksville run, which the June 1958 Dixon story puts that spring, so the 1958 World Series fits best.*[^cc5910] <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-5" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-6" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** *How big was the 1957 field?
