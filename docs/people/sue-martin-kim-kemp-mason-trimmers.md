# "Why You Always Bring Me Junk?": Sue Martin of ASM Auto Upholstery and Kim Kemp-Mason (Dallas and Oak Harbor, Wash., 1984–2026)

*The most famous trimmer on television, the everyday repairs behind her, and a hometown trimmer known by her trophies*

## A Torino and a $4,500 shop

The best-known trimmer on television said the job was mostly not what television showed. Sue Martin's shop in Dallas lived on everyday repairs, car dealers and word of mouth, long before a camera came through the door. In Oak Harbor, Washington, Kim Kemp-Mason came to car interiors the long way too, and was known for the trophies her work helped win.

Yu-Lan Haiso Martin, known as Sue, was born in Taiwan to a family of farmers. She came to the United States in 1984 with her husband and children.[^hr13][^asm] In 1990, her shop's history says, she got into upholstery with her husband's 1968 Grand Torino, which she and a friend reupholstered in her garage.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> Then a chance came to buy an upholstery company that was going bankrupt, in a 1,000-square-foot garage in Dallas. She paid $4,500, and ASM Auto Upholstery was born.[^asm]

"A friend introduced me to the industry," she said in 2013. "I learned the craft by working side by side with my first employee, Adan De La Rosa, who has since passed away."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>[^hr13] For years she worked day in and day out, the shop says, sometimes 24 hours a day for three or four days running, "practically living at the shop, sleeping in shifts," to earn a name for dependability.[^asm] *The Hog Ring* wrote that she sometimes slept in her garage.[^hr13] ASM rented the 1,000 square feet next door, then leased 2,000 across the street with a parking lot, and three years after that she bought the whole 8,800-square-foot complex.[^asm] As a girl, she said in 2016, she had wanted to be a kung fu fighter. Her favorite vehicle was her own 1956 Ford pickup, and she worked about 74 hours a week.[^rm16]

## The shop down the street

Richard Rawlings's shop was down the street. He came by, saw ASM's work and started using it, about 12 years before 2013. On Discovery's *Fast N' Loud* she started as a guest and became a regular, and her line to Rawlings and Aaron Kaufman, "Why you always bring me junk?," became the show's catchphrase.[^hr13] Did they really? "Yes, since most of their vehicles are restorations, we get their projects at there worst," she said. The deadlines were real, because Rawlings's cars were often headed to auction; she put several people on them and worked late. ASM had two sides, so the shop kept working on one while Discovery filmed on the other.[^hr13]

The show, she said, did not change the business. "Since we are already a busy upholstery company before the show, with our accounts with many car dealerships, the business side hasn't changed with the show, but we now have a lot of fans from across the world." Most of ASM's work was general repairs, with some restorations and custom projects, and furniture, boats and even airplanes. "It would be nice to redo only hot rods and be able to make a living at my shop, but that's not the way it is."[^hr13] In 2018 she said what set ASM apart was turnaround: a customer could drop a car off between 8 and 9 in the morning and pick it up between 4 and 6 the same day. "Though I have been in the industry for over 27 years, most of my business was through word of mouth," she said.[^vd18] Her next dream, she said in 2016, was to retire and pass the business to her daughter.[^rm16]

## Oak Harbor

Kimberly Kemp-Mason was born at the naval air station hospital on Whidbey Island in 1957, one of five children in a Navy family; her father's career took them to California, Virginia, New Jersey, Italy and England. She came back to Oak Harbor for high school, was homecoming queen, sang in the madrigal choir and "mastered the art of sewing." She worked as a dental assistant in Seattle, then went to California and designed clothes for the Wet Seal line.[^wnt21] She came home again, "developed a new love of auto interior refurbishing," and opened Kim's Auto Upholstery. Her family wrote that her "masterful and meticulous attention to detail and design won her much local acclaim and contributed to multiple 'trophy winning vehicles.'" She died in Everett on September 12, 2021, at 64.[^wnt21] *The Hog Ring*, the upholstery trade's news site, reported her death.[^hr21]

## Junk and treasure

Martin died at home on October 30, 2026, after a battle with cancer. Her family's statement on the shop's page said that she had faced barriers "as a woman, an immigrant, and a minority."[^hr26] The trade site that had interviewed her in 2013 closed its obituary on her answer about what car people should know.[^hr26] She had built ASM on general repairs, dealer accounts and word of mouth before the cameras came, and the answer fit the shop as well as the show: one man's junk turned into another man's treasure.[^hr13]

## Open questions & disputed details

*From the detective's notebook: where the sources disagree, where we have only one, and what we are still looking for. Each item links back to its place in the story.*

<a id="q-1" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Disputed.** **Who taught Martin the trade?** In 2013 she told *The Hog Ring* that "a friend introduced me to the industry" and that she learned "by working side by side with my first employee, Adan De La Rosa."[^hr13] In 2016 she told a Dallas blogger, "A friend of mine, Adam De La Rosa, introduced me to the upholstery industry."[^rm16] Her shop's history says she and "her friend" reupholstered the Torino in her garage, without a name.[^asm] So the friend, the first employee and the teacher may be one man or two, and his first name is spelled two ways. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>

<a id="q-2" class="hr-q-anchor"></a>**Single source so far.** **The 1968 "Grand Torino."** The Torino, the 1990 start, the $4,500 price and the square footage all come from ASM's own history, which *Voyage Dallas* (2018) and a 2017 trade-show speaker page repeat word for word.[^asm][^vd18] *The Hog Ring* (2013) dates the shop's founding to 23 years earlier, which fits 1990.[^hr13] The page keeps the shop's spelling, "Grand Torino." <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-2" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
