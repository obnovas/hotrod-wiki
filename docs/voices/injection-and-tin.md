# Voices: Injection, Factory Racing & Detroit Tin

*Fuel injectors, factory racing and aluminum noses, told by the people who made them*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium–High · **Source types:** quoted interviews and obituaries we opened · **Last verified:** 2026-10-14 (Round 9) · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

These are first-hand and near-first-hand words from the Round 9 people: lakes injection, Lincoln-Mercury racing, Detroit aluminum work, and East Coast match racing. Full biographies: [Stu Hilborn](../people/stu-hilborn.md), [Fran Hernandez](../people/fran-hernandez.md), [Al Bergler](../people/al-bergler.md), [Malcolm Durham](../people/malcolm-durham.md), [Logghe Stamping](../companies/logghe-stamping.md).

## Stuart Hilborn — AHRF interview (told decades later)

> "I was very impressed that they had doubled the horsepower of the cars as they came out of Detroit with virtually no money at all, just work... I decided I was going to have a race car."

— Stuart Hilborn to the American Hot Rod Foundation, quoted by Mark Vaughn, Autoweek, Dec 15, 2013. Access 2026-10-14. https://www.autoweek.com/news/a1946706/fuel-injection-pioneer-stuart-hilborn-1917-2013/

**Label:** Told years later (AHRF interview late in life; Autoweek paraphrases the setting as "last year" relative to Dec 2013).

> "When the war ended, I started construction on my fuel injector, and also raced my streamliner at the dry lakes with considerably more success than I ever had with my roadster... The only bad scene was the day I got upside-down in it! The fuel injector was performing beautifully by that time, and after rebuilding the car, it became the first car to break 150 mph."

— Hilborn, company website text quoted in the same Autoweek obituary. Access 2026-10-14.

**Label:** Told years later / company-site memoir.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>

> "I began to build fuel injectors for the Offenhauser midget engine and these were doing so well, I eventually had to quit racing and start my manufacturing career."

— Same Autoweek quotation of Hilborn. Access 2026-10-14.

## Randy Hernandez — on Fran (obituary)

> "His work was everything... Cars, racing—he was just consumed by it. That was his passion."

— Randy Hernandez, quoted by Mark Hicks, Detroit News, Jan 20, 2011, as pasted on The H.A.M.B. by Harms Way, Jan 29, 2011. Access 2026-10-14. https://www.jalopyjournal.com/forum/threads/hot-rod-legend-from-the-greatest-generation-fran-hernandez-rip.555417/

**Label:** Family tribute in an obituary (affectionate by design).

> "He was a great engineer... He understood what the drivers wanted. He was a real big help for all of us, because he knew what needed to be done."

— Bud Moore, same Detroit News obituary text on H.A.M.B. Access 2026-10-14.

## Al Bergler — Hagerty shop interview (2024)

> "I always looked for the coolest car... and then I would drive that one back to the lot."

— Al Bergler, on ferrying trade-ins to a Gratiot used-car lot as a teenager, Paul Stenquist, Hagerty Media, Feb 21, 2024. Access 2026-10-14. https://www.hagerty.com/media/motorsports/al-bergler-first-ridler-award-winner-profile/

**Label:** Told years later (event ~1952; told 2024).

> "I made a bench on which I could clamp the pipe down. Still have it. Still using it."

— Bergler, on teaching himself aluminum noses over a 4-inch pipe when a Logghe tin man quoted $300. Same Hagerty article. Access 2026-10-14.

> "It was an emotional experience... It's like I was young and taking on the best at 200 mph with the sound of the exhaust pounding in my eardrums and flames shooting skyward to either side of me. I was overcome by memories of great times."

— Bergler, on his first cacklefest push-start (2003 Hot Rod Reunion, Bowling Green) in Ed Golden's Probe AA/FD. Same Hagerty article. Access 2026-10-14.

**Label:** Told years later (2003 event; 2024 interview). Tribute-adjacent emotion; the factual booking (Golden, 2003 Reunion) is in the same piece.

> "The match race money was drying up... and it was time to focus on my business."

— Bergler, on leaving Funny Car driving ~1983. Same Hagerty article. Access 2026-10-14.

## Malcolm Durham — on booking and the South

> "We encountered some problems in the South because those people didn't want to accept us. But for me, being black was actually a plus because it made me unique, and I tried to capitalize on it as much as possible. During the late 1960s, I averaged $800 per appearance, and that made me one of the highest paid drivers in the business."

— Malcolm Durham, quoted by Phil Burgess, "The Final Fred Files," NHRA.com, July 31, 2009. Access 2026-10-14. https://www.nhra.com/news/2009/final-fred-files

**Label:** Told years later (late-1960s fees recalled by 2009). Speaker's own framing of neighborhood/race experience; kept in his words per Voices method.

## Don Garlits — Freeway Drag Strip invitation (memoir)

> "A group of the West Coast fuel drag racers are doubting the times you've been setting there in the East, so everybody is figuring that it's time for a real showdown. Well, because our strip is about halfway between California and Florida, we'd like to invite both groups to see who really is the fastest drag racer..."

— Letter from a Freeway Drag Strip promoter to Garlits, as quoted in Garlits's memoir and reprinted on houstonfreewaydragstrip.com history page. Access 2026-10-14. https://www.houstonfreewaydragstrip.com/houston-international-speedway-history.htm
