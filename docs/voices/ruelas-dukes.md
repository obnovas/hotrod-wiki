# The Ruelas Brothers and the Dukes

*A South LA family shop*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** magazine feature with extended interviews (LA Times West, Lynell George, July 16, 2006) · **Last verified:** 2026-10-13 (Round 7) · **Open issues:** Club founding year not taken from this source; Coachbuilt profile not reachable this round · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

In the mid-1950s Josefina Ruelas brought her sons Julio, Oscar, Fernando and Ernie from Tijuana to 41st Street and Long Beach Avenue in South Los Angeles. In 2006, Lynell George found them still working on "primer-gray" cars in their yard on Saturday mornings, as they had "for the last 40 years and more." They had built cars for *Zoot Suit*, and Julio's '54 Chevy was on the cover of Tierra's 1980 album *City Nights*.[^lat] This is the custom tradition that grew alongside the [hot rod](../customs/index.md) story but is seldom told with it.

## In their words

The family story starts with an uncle:

> "It was Aunt Lucille's husband, Uncle Tinker ('Yes, really, Tinker! Frank C. Tinker!'), who tried to steer the boys away from the street gangs …"
>
> — The writer's narration, with **Fernando Ruelas'** aside, 2006.[^lat]

They started with bicycles:

> "For 50 cents, you'd get 20 or 30 because they'd sell it penny a pound."
>
> — **Fernando Ruelas**, on buying junk Schwinns at Albert & Albert Junkyard as boys. Uncle Tinker's shop on 41st Street started them on go-carts and minibikes "prepping them for cars." *Told about 50 years later.*[^lat]

The neighborhood had its lines, and cars crossed them:

> "There were a lot of African Americans that were across the tracks, and they would not dare cross into 38 territory, except for the ones who grew up in the neighborhood. They knew Spanish and everything."
>
> — **Fernando Ruelas**, 2006.[^lat] The writer adds that among those who crossed, some "were into custom cars too. That was the glue."

The music was part of it too:

> "Let's not forget the music, our African American musical artists. … We had the pleasure of meeting Don Julian and going to his dances."
>
> — **Richard "Chivo" Ceniceros**, Dukes sergeant-at-arms, 2006.[^lat]

## Other clubs

The feature names other LA clubs of the era: the Igniters, the Drifters, the Clique, the In Crowd, the Professionals and the Imperials.[^lat] Several anecdotes in the article are told in a joking group conversation, such as one about Barry White and stolen tires. We do not repeat them as fact.
