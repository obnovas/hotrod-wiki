# Voices: In Their Own Words

*The people who were there, telling it.*

Some of the best history in hot rodding was never written down by a historian. It was said into a recorder, printed in an obituary, or typed into a forum by someone's kid. This section collects **first-hand narratives**: interviews, oral histories, obituaries that quote family, club and forum posts by people who were there, and recollections that magazine writers recorded. Each page puts the speaker's words next to the documentary record.

*Short on time?* The [Quick Answers](../reference/quick-answers.md#how-we-know) explain in a few lines how we label a quote and how we know what we know, with links to the full stories.

## How we handle a quote

- **Verbatim only.** Every quote was copied from a page we actually opened and read (not from a search snippet) and checked against that page on 2026-10-13. The source is linked so you can read it in full.
- **Ellipses (…)** mark omitted words, including where the source breaks a quote for attribution ("he recalled").
- **Short excerpts.** We quote a few lines to about a paragraph per source, paraphrase the rest, and link the original. We never reproduce whole articles or transcripts. Photos in these sources are copyrighted, so we only link to them.
- **Each quote carries:** the speaker; when and in what setting they told it; who published it; the URL; and the access date.
- **Memory caveats.** Most of these stories were told decades after the events. We label each quote:
    - **Told years later:** the gap between event and telling.
    - **Secondhand:** the speaker relays someone else's account, or a writer quotes an earlier interview.
    - **⚠ Conflicts with the record:** where documents disagree, we say so and link the evidence.
- **Tribute pieces.** Obituaries and anniversary features are affectionate by design, so we treat their superlatives ("first," "only") as claims, not facts.
