# Peoria 1970: The People Who Drove There

*Why Peoria? Because it was nearly the center of the population.*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium · **Source types:** organizers' recollections recorded by Dick Martin, Rod & Custom (posted Aug 7, 2007) · **Last verified:** 2026-10-13 (Round 7) · **Open issues:** Told 37 years later; event size ⚠ (see article) · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

The first Street Rod Nationals (Peoria, Aug 1970) is covered in [1970s: Peoria](../eras/1970s-street-rod-nationals.md) and [NSRA](../street-rodding/nsra-early-nationals.md). In 2007 Dick Martin collected the memories of the Rod & Custom crew who organized the event: publisher Tom Medley, editor Bud Bryan, associate editor Jim "Jake" Jacobs, and freelancer Tex Smith.[^rc]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## Five voices, one road trip

Medley began with the choice of town.

> "We picked out a bunch of guys who were in this car club called the Slo-Pokes in Peoria, Illinois. The reason we picked Peoria was it was almost the center of the population of the U.S."
>
> — **Tom Medley**, 2007.[^rc]

He also said they went ahead without management's OK.

> "We couldn't get an OK from the 7th floor (Petersen Publishing upper management), but we were already into this thing so we went ahead and did it."
>
> — **Tom Medley**, 2007.[^rc]

Bryan described a sport split by region.

> "The sport, at that point, was fragmented nationally. The Midwest and the East Coast guys didn't know much about the West Coast guys, and visa versa. … Everybody was talking about cruises, but not many ventured out of state."
>
> — **Bud Bryan**, 2007.[^rc]

Jacobs drove there from California.

> "America was different then. … You could sleep by the side of the road without worrying about getting robbed or worse."
>
> — **Jim "Jake" Jacobs**, on driving his Model A from California, 2007.[^rc]

Smith remembered an improvised event.

> "Some of the guys went out and found traffic cones to set up a slalom course. … We called it a Streetkhana."
>
> — **Tex Smith**, 2007.[^rc]

### Who paid?

**Gary Medley**, Tom Medley's son, quoted by Autoweek (Mark Vaughn, June 19, 2015) after Tex Smith's death, gave a different account of how the Nationals were funded: "the pair filed an invoice to Tex for freelancing," because no budget line existed for the event.[^aw] This is **secondhand**. It fits with Medley's "7th floor" memory without proving it. <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
