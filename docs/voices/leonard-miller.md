## Context
Leonard Miller (born about 1934; WHYY says he was 5 in 1939) grew up on Philadelphia's Main Line. He raced at Bucks County strips in the 1960s, including Vargo, and in 1973 co-founded the **Black American Racers Association** with Wendell Scott, Malcolm Durham and Ron Hines (Smithsonian NMAH). He tells his story in his 2004 autobiography *Silent Thunder*. This complements [Midwest & East Threads](../regions/midwest-east.md). WHYY interviewed him in 2018 for the Mercer Museum's exhibit "Racing: A Need for Speed."[^why]

## In his words
> "I learned how wonderful the world was. … When they had a Memorial Day parade – this was before World War II – you'd see Stutz Bearcats, Cords, Duesenbergs in yellow, red and blue. I was taken in by it."
>
> — **Leonard Miller**, WHYY, 2018, recalling 1939. *Told about 79 years later.*[^why]

> "Bill Jones had a junkyard on Route 30 coming out of Paoli. He was a big African-American numbers writer and gambler. He had the junkyard as a front."
>
> — **Leonard Miller**, 2018, on where he found parts for his first car, a chopped '40 Ford club coupe with a Mercury engine and a Lincoln transmission.[^why] Jones remembered that Miller's father had always been civil to him and told the boy to take what he needed.

> "They were all Army vets like myself. Everyone got along. They were mature. You didn't have any racial animosities or silly stuff going on. … You're going close to dying, putting it all on the line. That was more important than anything else."
>
> — **Leonard Miller**, 2018, on racing at Vargo in the 1960s.[^why]

## Reading it carefully
- Miller describes **Vargo** as free of racial hostility. Elsewhere in the same interview he is sharply critical of corporate sponsorship and of NASCAR's record on Black driver development.[^why] One good local experience does not tell us what Black racers faced nationally.
- NMAH notes that Black drivers were barred from competing against white drivers in the 1920s, which led to the Colored Speedway Association (1924).[^nmah] ◐ We haven't been able to open that page directly yet (it blocked our fetch), so this point waits on confirmation.
- Malcolm Durham, the BARA co-founder and an East Coast Chevy match racer, has no first-person source in the wiki yet. He is on the [Research Trail](../research-trail.md).
