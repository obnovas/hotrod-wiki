# The Esparza Brothers: The "Mexican Express" at Lions

## Context
Rick and Fred Esparza, sons of a "second-generation Mexican-American Navy man" who moved to West Long Beach in 1958, built a **1941 Willys truck** into a drag car they called the *Mexican Express*. They raced it at **Lions Drag Strip** in Wilmington (1955–72) for seven years. Danny Miguel's documentary about them was reviewed by the Long Beach Post's *Hi-lo* on Aug 21, 2022.[^lb] They set no records, which is part of why their story is worth telling: it is about the many racers who never made the record books.

## In their words
> "If the wind was right, we could be at home and hear the announcer."
>
> — **Rick Esparza**, on Lions Drag Strip, 2022.[^lb]

> "I could have been in jail. … Sooner or later it would have happened."
>
> — **Rick Esparza**, on what drag racing kept him from, 2022.[^lb]

> "You never knew what was gonna show up on a Saturday night at Lions. You might see a twin-engine car. You might see them line up side-by-side, you might see a three-engine car, you might see an airplane engine car, you might see turbine jet cars—all kinds of machinery."
>
> — **Kenny Youngblood**, motorsports artist, in the film (2022).[^lb]

## Notes
- The reviewer notes that the film "doesn't dive as deeply into the cultural significance or challenges of the minority experience." Historian Claudine Burnett supplies some of the context on West Long Beach.[^lb]
- C.J. Hart managed Lions from the mid-1960s ([The Harts](harts-santa-ana.md)), which was the brothers' era.
