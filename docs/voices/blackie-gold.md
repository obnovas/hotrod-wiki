# Blackie Gold: Grease Monkey at Ten

*Muroc before the SCTA*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (one long first-person interview) · **Source types:** oral history (Pancho Barnes Trust Estate Archive, 2006) · **Last verified:** 2026-10-13 (Round 7) · **Open issues:** He was 92 when interviewed and made several unverifiable "first" claims (⚠ below) · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

## How a ten-year-old got a nickname

In **2006** the Pancho Barnes Trust Estate Archive interviewed Hyman "Blackie" Gold (born Feb 28, 1914), and published it as an excerpt from a planned book, *Voices from the Happy Bottom Riding Club*.[^g] His story runs from a Pasadena Ford agency in the 1920s to Muroc, the founding of the SCTA, and Pancho Barnes' ranch after the war. See [1930s Dry Lakes & SCTA](../eras/1930s-dry-lakes-scta.md).

> "My first job was at a Ford agency, Fortner and Loud, located on the northwest corner of Colorado and Hill in Pasadena, California. I started there as a grease monkey. I was 10 years old. And they said, 'We can't call you Hyman. We'll call you Blackie.' I have been Blackie ever since."
>
> — **Blackie Gold**, 2006 interview, recalling about 1924. *Told about 80 years later.*[^g]

## Waiting in line at Muroc

> "When you race on the dry lakes you have to get there very early, before the heat sets in. … There might be a 100 cars there waiting in line for their run, and the guys at the front of the line always ran faster than the guys toward the end of the line."
>
> — **Blackie Gold**, 2006, on late-1930s Muroc.[^g]

> "As a result of the activities of the Army Air Corps, by 1938 there were a lot of bomb holes out there on that Muroc Dry Lake and so when the desert got too beat up, we'd go to Rosamond and race there."
>
> — **Blackie Gold**, 2006.[^g] This matches [Kong Jackson's](kong-jackson.md) story of the Army chasing racers off.

## The rest, in brief

Gold says he started racing Model Ts and As in 1931. He bought a '32 roadster for $75 in 1935 and ran it at Muroc from 1936, reaching about 90 mph in 1939. Around 1936 he founded the **Night Flyers**, which he says was one of the first SCTA member clubs (SCTA founded 1937, in his account). He says Pancho Barnes brought racers "cold desert punch" and sandwiches, and that after the war the racers became honorary members of her Happy Bottom Riding Club.[^g]<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

### More from the notebook

⚠ **"Hot rod" etymology.** Gold says the term came from Model T connecting rods coming loose, and calls other explanations "malarkey." We have found no documentary support for this. It is a folk explanation, and we do not adopt it.

⚠ **"The very first person to race the Ford 1932 roadster on the dry lake at Muroc"** and the Knight Riders as "the very first hot rod club in the US": we cannot verify either. He spells the club both "Knight Riders" and "Night Riders" in the same interview.

⚠ **SCTA founding date:** he says 1937. Other sources give 1937 or 1938 (see [Kong Jackson](kong-jackson.md)). <a class="hr-q-back" href="#qr-1" title="Back to the story">↩</a>
