# Voices: Strip Owners & Door-Slammers

*The people who ran the tracks, and the people who filled them*

!!! abstract "Article record"
    **Confidence:** Medium (all quotes are verbatim from pages we opened on 2026-10-15) · **Source types:** NHRA bios and columns, *Hot Rod* (1980, 2015), *Street Rodder* anniversary feature, track-owner tribute, trade-blog event report · **Last verified:** 2026-10-15 (Round 10) · **Open issues:** no first-hand words from Peggy Hart herself; Bob Bartel's August 2009 newspaper interview could not be opened (rate-limited) · [How we rate this](../reference/source-standards.md)

These are first-hand and near-first-hand words from the families who **owned the strips** (the Harts, the Gays, Bob Bartel) and the **Midwest and Texas door-slammer racers** who filled them. Every quote is labeled by when it was told. Full articles: [Gene Snow](../people/gene-snow.md), [Bob Bartel](../people/bob-bartel.md), [Houston Freeway](../drag-racing/houston-freeway-dragstrip.md), [The Ohio Gassers](../places/ohio-gassers.md), [Akron Fulton Airport](../drag-racing/akron-fulton-airport.md), [Peggy Hart](../people/peggy-hart.md), [*Street Rod* magazine](../media/street-rod-magazine-portland.md).

## Gene Snow: used cars, Crowerglide and 200 mph (NHRA Top 50, 2001)

Snow came to racing through the used-car lot:

> "I did that because I could make more money than what engineers were making at General Dynamics."

— Gene Snow, on quitting electrical engineering at UT-Arlington to sell used cars, quoted by John Jodauga, "No. 26: Gene Snow," NHRA Top 50 (2001). Access 2026-10-15. https://www.nhra.net/50th/top50/G_Snow26.html

> "I would win a race with a dealership car on Sunday and usually sold the winning entry the next day."

— Snow, same NHRA bio. Access 2026-10-15.

*Told years later: 1960s Fort Worth lot; told about 2001.*

The racing got serious, and so did the hardware:

> "We worked with Crower to come up with a four-disc Crowerglide centrifugal clutch. Though it was sluggish off the line, it would mow down the automatic cars, which always nosed-over at about 185 mph."

— Snow, same NHRA bio. Access 2026-10-15.

> "The sea level location gave us extra horsepower, and we went 200.88 mph on the first pass. I didn't think it such a big deal at the time, but we kept running more than 200 mph at just about every race after that."

— Snow, on the first 200-mph Funny Car pass at the Gays' Dickinson track, same NHRA bio. Access 2026-10-15.<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-1"><a href="#q-1" title="Sources disagree: see Open questions">⚠</a></sup>

> "We carried a spare motor the whole year and never had to use it."

— Snow, on his 1970 season, same NHRA bio. Access 2026-10-15.

Eighteen years later, at Houston Raceway Park, he was still chasing numbers:

> "It was neat, it was fun and we've been trying for a long time to achieve this performance."

— Snow after his 4.997 at Houston Raceway Park, UPI, Baytown, Tex., Oct. 6, 1988. Access 2026-10-15. https://www.upi.com/Archives/1988/10/06/Top-Fuel-driver-Gene-Snow-of-Ft-Worth-Texas/4826592113600/

*Told the same day, in a wire report: the best-dated quote on this page.*

## Don Gay: the strip owner's son (NHRA, 2015)

Snow's first 200 came at the Gays' track, and the Gays' house was open to the racers:

> "They'd all stay at our house... We'd say, 'Hey, my mom has a two-story house. Why don't you stay?' We'd sit by the swimming pool on Friday night and drink and talk."

— Don Gay, on touring Funny Car racers at the family's Houston Drag Raceway, as quoted by Phil Burgess, "Drag racing's teen terrors," NHRA.com, Aug 14, 2015. Access 2026-10-15. https://www.nhra.com/news/2015/drag-racing-s-teen-terrors

*Told years later and secondhand. Gay died in 2007, so Burgess is quoting an earlier interview he does not date.*<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-2"><a href="#q-2" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup>

## Bob Bartel: Cordova (tribute, 2018)

Bob Bartel spent so much time at Cordova that he had one request:

> "I've spent so much time at this place I couldn't stand to be stuck here for eternity."

— Bob Bartel, asking not to be buried at the Cordova track, quoted in Bobby Bennett, "Quad Cities Drag Racing Legend Bob Bartel Passes," Competition Plus, Feb 20, 2018. Access 2026-10-15. https://competitionplus.com/quad-cities-drag-racing-legend-bob-bartel-passes/

*Secondhand (memorial tribute; date of the remark not given).*

## Otie Smith: Akron (NHRA, 2014)

The racers who filled those strips kept it simpler.

> "You got to be a little nuts. It was just fun. That's all."

— Otis "Otie" Smith, "asked once why he liked to drive fast," quoted by Phil Burgess, "Talking 'Otie,' terminology, skyjackings, and more," NHRA.com, May 30, 2014. Access 2026-10-15. https://www.nhra.com/news/2014/talking-otie-terminology-skyjackings-and-more

*Secondhand; when Smith said it is not given.*

## Randy Lipscomb: the Akron marker (2016)

Akron later put Smith and his friends on a state marker:

> "This is the spot where these local heroes became drag racing innovators back in the 50's and 60's... we believe that honoring these racing pioneers is long overdue."

— Randy Lipscomb, Akron Race Legends, in "Ohio to Honor Drag Legends Arfons, Smith & Vanke with Special Historical Marker," OnAllCylinders, June 15, 2016. Access 2026-10-15. https://www.onallcylinders.com/2016/06/15/ohio-to-honor-drag-legends-arfons-smith-vanke-with-special-historical-marker/

*Organizer's publicity statement (advocacy by design).*

## Ron Hassel: the Ohio gasser's creed (*Hot Rod*, 2015)

Another Ohio racer summed up the gasser budget in one line:

> "I'd pay a thousand bucks for a tenth of a second!"

— Ron Hassel, as quoted by Jim Hill, "Sportsman Racers Winning Class at Indy Was Their High Mark," *Hot Rod*, Nov 23, 2015. Access 2026-10-15. https://www.hotrod.com/news/sportsman-racers-winning-class-at-indy-was-their-high-mark

*Secondhand ("Hassel once confessed"), told years later by a fellow racer and friend.*

## C.J. Hart: Santa Ana, Lions, and Peggy (*Hot Rod*, January 1980)

Back west, the man who opened Santa Ana talked about where it all started:

> "I didn't invent drag racing... Heck, that sort of thing's been going on ever since there was cars!"

— C.J. "Pappy" Hart to Dave Wallace, "The Hart of Drag Racing," *Hot Rod*, January 1980. Access 2026-10-15. https://www.hotrod.com/features/the-hart-of-drag-racing-january-1980-982-1130-22-1

> "We used to run off seven, eight abreast. You always beat somebody — that was the nice thing about that arrangement!"

— Hart, on the 1950 informal meets at Mile Square before the Marines ran them off, same article. Access 2026-10-15.

> "Then we got real big, and anytime they went over 130 in the Top Eliminator final we'd give the winner a dollar a mile. Hell, the biggest purse I ever paid was $155!"

— Hart, on Santa Ana's lakes-car purses, same article. Access 2026-10-15.

> "I've always gone for the little guy... he's always been the backbone of drag racing; without him, you got nothin'."

— Hart, on Sunday bracket racing at Lions, same article. Access 2026-10-15.

Hart told all this years later (1950–71 events; told 1979). This is the earliest long interview with Hart we have opened. On Peggy, the article paraphrases him: "she ran a blown Cadillac dragster back in 1954 that went 130 mph, says Hart with obvious pride," and quotes him that she missed a Bonneville two-way record "when we lost Number Seven on the way back."<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-3"><a href="#q-3" title="Single source so far: see Open questions">◐</a></sup> The same article dates his Lions start to both "'65" and "'63." See [Santa Ana](../eras/1950s-santa-ana-drags.md).<sup class="hr-q" id="qr-4"><a href="#q-4" title="Open question: see the bottom of the page">?</a></sup>

## Jim Clark: losing the *Street Rod* title (2012/2014)

The last voice is from the magazines that covered the street-rod side:

> "We originally intended to call it Street Rod, but not long before we went to print Bruce Miller in Portland, Oregon, came out with Street Rod magazine. So we changed our name. It was released in May 1972."

— Jim Clark, *Street Rodder*'s first editor, in "Street Rodder's 40th Anniversary – Time Flies," hotrod.com, posted Apr 8, 2014. Access 2026-10-15. https://www.hotrod.com/features/street-rodders-40th-anniversary

Told about 40 years later, and confirmed by the *Street Rod* issue run (first issue October 1971). See [*Street Rod* magazine](../media/street-rod-magazine-portland.md).
